Researched 25 October 2026 (the Gazette scan of the Jerusalem order was read on 26 October 2026); a second pass on the [GAP] rows was made on the same day (sections 1.3, 1.4, 2.1, 2.4, 3, 4, 5 and the gaps table say what it added). ## Purpose

For each line of design section 7.3 between June 1967 and May 1974, give the dates, the instrument (with where its official
text is), and a map source, so that a later `lines.yaml` row (#63) can be dated and sourced. The 1949 lines before June 1967
are in [armistice-lines-1949.md](../Map%20borders/armistice-lines-1949.md); the 1975 Sinai II lines are not in this note. The
design is
[the 1957-1977 era design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1957-1977-design.md), section 7.3 (lines 554-559).
The format follows armistice-lines-1949.md.

## 1. The June 1967 cease-fire lines

### 1.1 What the instruments say

- SC resolution 233 (6 June 1967) "Calls upon the Governments concerned to take forthwith as a first step all measures for an
  immediate cease-fire and for a cessation of all military activities in the area" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check
  pending] (S/RES/233 (1967), Council volume page).
- Resolution 234 (7 June) "Demands that the Governments concerned should as a first step cease fire and discontinue all military
  activities at 2000 hours GMT on 7 June 1967" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/RES/234 (1967); the
  volume's OCR prints "1%7" for 1967, the date is in the resolution's heading).
- Resolution 235 (9 June) records that "the Governments of Israel and Syria have announced their mutual acceptance of the
  Council's demand for a cease-fire" and "Demands that hostilities should cease forthwith" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's
  check pending] (S/RES/235 (1967)).
- Resolution 236 (11 June) "Affirms that its demand for a cease-fire and discontinuance of all military activities includes a
  prohibition of any forward military movements subsequent to the cease-fire" and "Calls for the prompt return to the cease-fire
  positions of any troops which may have moved forward subsequent to 1630 hours GMT on 10 June 1967" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a
  person's check pending] (S/RES/236 (1967)).

### 1.3 Where the lines ran

- No instrument of June 1967 draws a line. The cease-fire held the forces where they stood. UNTSO observers were deployed on 11
  June to the Israeli-Syrian "Forward Defended Localities" (S/7930/Add.3, para 3), and General Bull said "the mapping of the
  positions of the forces on the ground has been the first and highest priority task for observers" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a
  person's check pending] (S/7930/Add.4, 12 June, para 8).
- The 1974 Syrian agreement's own report names the line it started from: "the area east of the 1967 cease-fire line" [V, agent read
  2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11302/Add.2, 6 June 1974, para 4; the text layer was checked on the page image).
- Suez Canal sector: the Council's consensus of 9-10 July 1967 asked the Secretary-General to have the UNTSO Chief of Staff "work
  out with the Governments of the United Arab Republic and Israel ... the necessary arrangements to station United Nations
  Military Observers in the Suez Canal sector". The UAR accepted on 10 July; Israel's reply of 11 July accepted talks on
  observation posts "on the basis of reciprocity ... on both sides of the line" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check
  pending] (S/8053, 13 July 1967, paras 1-2 and annexes I and II).
- **Suez sector, cease-fire of 15 July 1967.** Fighting there flared again: "Early in the afternoon of 15 July 1967, reports were received at United Nations Headquarters that fighting between Israel and United Arab Republic forces in the Suez sector was continuing on land and in the air and was heavy." General Bull "proposed a cease-fire to be effective at 2200 hours GMT on the same day. The cease-fire was accepted by both sides and went into effect as scheduled." Advance parties "were instructed by General Bull to begin observation operations on both sides of the sector at 1600 hours GMT on 17 July" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/7930/Add.23, 17 July 1967, paras 1-2, read on the page image). Before that, on 3 July: "No United Nations Military Observers are stationed in the Suez area" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/7930/Add.19, para 3, text layer only, the page image was not checked). No line is drawn in either.
- **Syria front, the line in coordinates.** The Secretary-General's report of 1 July 1967 (S/7930/Add.18, paras 4-5 and 11, annexes I and II, read on the page images) says the observers demarcated "the limits of the Forward Defended Localities (FDLs) occupied by the armed forces at the time of the cease-fire", and that "These limits would constitute the cease-fire line on each side across which the respective armed forces would not fire or move forward" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]. Two signed documents list the points (map references of 5 digits, a hyphen and 5 digits; annex II names the Palestine Grid, annex I does not name its grid and the two are not assumed to share one; the page images were read, the OCR layer has errors). Each bullet below is [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] for what it quotes and lists:

Signed at Kuneitra on 15 June 1967 ("1805Z") by a Major of the New Zealand Army, U.N.M.O., for the United Nations and by an I.D.F. officer of the rank "Sgan Aluf" for the Israel Defence Forces (the signers are named by role only). ## 2. Jerusalem's municipal boundary of 27-28 June 1967

### 2.1 Instruments and dates

The Secretary-General's Personal Representative (report S/8146 of 12 September 1967) was "supplied by the Israel authorities with
the text of certain laws and orders". It describes them as follows [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]
(S/8146, paras 38-40, printed page 8, PDF page 10, read on the image):

- 27 June 1967: "the Law and Administration Ordinance (Amendment No. 11) Law", under which "the law, jurisdiction and
  administration of the State should apply in any area of the State of Israel designated by the Government by order".
- 28 June 1967: "the Government issued an order dated 28 June 1967 which declared that a territory defined in an annex was an area
  in which the law, jurisdiction and administration of the State of Israel were in force. The area described in detail in the
  annex included the Old City, Sur Baher, Sheich Jarakh, the Kalandia airport, Mount Scopus and vicinity and Sha'afat." (The
  spellings are the report's.)
- 27 June 1967 (the same day as the law): "the Municipal Corporations Ordinance (Amendment No. 7)", under which "the Minister of
  Interior was empowered at his discretion to enlarge, by proclamation, the area of a particular municipal corporation by the
  inclusion of an area designated under the Law and Administration Ordinance as just amended." "By an order dated the following
  day, the Minister declared that the boundaries of the Jerusalem Municipal Corporation would be extended by the inclusion of the
  area described in the previous paragraph." The report's wording "the following day" makes the Minister's order 28 June 1967
  as well; the number "7" is the report's, not checked against the Israeli text. The page was read on its image.

The Government's order, as the Supreme Court describes it (HCJ 256/01, session date 9 January 2002, English translation by HaMoked, non-binding by its own disclaimer, read 2026-10-25, https://www.hamoked.org/files/2012/115170_eng.pdf [S]): "the government issued the Law and Administration Order (No. 1) on the next day, June 28, 1967. The Order stipulates: The territory of Eretz Israel described in the schedule is hereby declared a territory to which the law, jurisdiction and administration of the state apply." On the Minister's act the judgment says "On June 27, 1967, Section 8a was added to the Municipalities Ordinance [New Version]" and "The next day, the Minister of Interior proclaimed an extension of the jurisdiction of the Jerusalem Municipality. The schedule to the proclamation includes a description of the area that was added to the Municipality." [S] The ordinance is named "Municipalities Ordinance [New Version]" there and "Municipal Corporations Ordinance" in S/8146 (the report's wording above); both names are kept, the Israeli text was not opened. Basic Law: Jerusalem, section 5 as amended on 7 December 2000, refers to "the appendix of the proclamation expanding the borders of municipal Jerusalem beginning the 20th of Sivan 5727 (June 28, 1967)" [S] (same judgment, para 3).

The Order's own text and schedule, in the Gazette itself: Kovetz HaTakanot (the Israeli official gazette's regulations series), issue 2064, whose cover prints "כ' בסיון תשכ"ז" and "28 ביוני 1967" (28 June 1967), contents entry "Law and Administration Order (No. 1), 5727-1967" at page 2690; the order and its schedule run over pp. 2690-2691 (scan: https://olaw.org.il/takanot/takanot-2064.pdf, linked from the Hebrew Wikisource page), downloaded and read on the page images (200 and 300 dpi) on 2026-10-26 [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]. What is printed: the Order is made under section 11B of the Law and Administration Ordinance, 5708-1948; section 1 declares the territory of Eretz Israel described in the schedule to be an area in which the law, jurisdiction and administration of the State apply (my translation of the printed Hebrew); section 2 gives the name "Law and Administration Order (No. 1), 5727-1967"; the schedule describes a boundary by 63 map references (10 digits, 5 east and 5 north; the page does not name the grid) joined by wadis, roads, trig points (the Hebrew abbreviation for a trig point, with a height) and straight lines, and ends with the date line "כ' בסיון תשכ"ז (28 ביוני 1967)" and the signature of the Government's secretary (named by role only). The year in that date line is printed 5727, not 5726; the Hebrew Wikisource transcription of the same page (revision 2925636, read 2026-10-25, https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%A6%D7%95_%D7%A1%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%98%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%95%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%A4%D7%98_(%D7%9E%D7%A1%27_1)) [S] gives 5726 in that line, which is the transcription's slip. ## 5. The 1974 Egyptian-Israeli disengagement (January 1974)

- **Date and hour.** The Secretary-General's letter reported, on the Force Commander's report, that "at 1025 hours GMT on 18 January 1974, at a meeting held at kilometre 101
  on the Cairo-Suez road, an Agreement on the disengagement of forces in pursuance of the Geneva Peace Conference was signed by"
  the Egyptian chief of staff, Major-General Mohammad Abdel Ghani El-Gamasy, and the Israeli chief of staff, Lieutenant-General
  David Elazar, "and by the Force Commander as witness" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11198, 18 January
  1974). The JTA bulletin of Friday 18 January 1974, read for events-1970-77.md B8 [S], carries an item
  datelined "Jan. 17 (JTA)" (page 1, read on the page image 25 Oct 2026; http://pdfs.jta.org/1974/1974-01-18_013.pdf) under the
  headline line "Signing Due Friday", and says the agreement "would be signed ... at noon their time tomorrow", so noon on
  18 January, which agrees with the UN record (S/11198).
- **The lines.** Text (S/11198, annex, para B, read on the page image; the typewriter's "o" prints as a dot in places): "All Egyptian
  forces on the east side of the canal will be deployed west of the line designated as line A on the attached map. All Israeli
  forces, including those west of the Suez Canal and the Bitter Lakes, will be deployed east of the line designated as line B on
  the attached map." The area between is a zone of disengagement for UNEF. Line C "runs along the western base of the mountains
  where the Gidi and Mitla Passes are located"; the areas between the Egyptian line and the canal, and between line B and line C,
  are "limited in armament and forces" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending].
- **Timetable.** Disengagement "will begin within 48 hours after the completion of the work of the military representatives and

  of 24 January, disengagement "began on 25 January", and was "successfully completed, on schedule, on 5 March" [V, agent read
  2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11248, 1 April 1974, paras 39-40, read on the page image). Arithmetic: 25 January to 5

- **Marking on the ground.** Line B was surveyed and marked by UNTSO observers, with Israeli surveyors, by 2 February 1974; line A

## 6. The 1974 Israel-Syria disengagement (May-June 1974)

- **Announcement.** The Secretary-General's report of 29 May: the agreement "was announced at 1300 hours on 29 May 1974"; the
  signing "will take place in the Egyptian-Israeli Military Working Group of the Geneva Peace Conference on the Middle East, on
  Friday, 31 May 1974" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11302, para 1-2). **[C]** events-1970-77.md B11
  records the JTA report that Peres announced it "a few minutes after 9 p.m. local time" [S]; the UN report's "1300 hours" does not
  say which clock it uses. Both are kept.
- **Signing.** "At the meeting held on 31 May, the military representatives of Israel and Syria signed the Agreement on
  Disengagement and a map attached to it" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11302/Add.2, 6 June 1974, para
  2). The map showing the phases and the disengagement plan were "signed by the military representatives of Israel and Syria at
  the final meeting held on 5 June 1974" (para 3). FRUS 69-76 v26 d88 is headed "Geneva, May 31, 1974" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a
  person's check pending]. **[C]** The copy that `documents.un.org` returns for S/11302/Add.1 is the reprint in International
  Legal Materials vol. 13, p. 880, whose page header reads "[Done at Geneva, June 5, 1974]" [S]; the UN's own report puts the
  signing of the agreement on 31 May and the signing of the map and plan on 5 June. The UN report is followed.
- **The lines.** Text of the agreement, para B, as FRUS d88 prints it [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]:
  "All Israeli military forces will be west of the line designated as Line A on the map attached hereto, except in the Quneitra
  area, where they will be west of Line A-1."; "All territory east of Line A will be under Syrian administration"; "The area
  between Line A and the line designated as Line B on the attached map will be an area of separation."; "All Syrian military
  forces will be east of the line designated as Line B"; "two equal areas of limitation in armament and forces, one west of Line A
  and one east of Line B"; and, para C, "In the area between Line A and Line A-1 on the attached map there shall be no military
  forces." The UN copy (S/11302/Add.1, annex A) has the same words.
- **Resolution 350 (31 May 1974):** "Welcomes the Agreement on Disengagement between Israeli and Syrian Forces, negotiated in
  implementation of Security Council resolution 338 (1973) of 22 October 1973" and "Decides to set up immediately under its
  authority a United Nations Disengagement Observer Force" for "an initial period of six months"; adopted by 13 votes to none, two
  members (China and Iraq) not participating [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/RES/350 (1974), Council
  volume).
- **Implementation.** The plan: UNDOF positions between 6 and 8 June, "Separation of forces should be completed by 26 June"
  (S/11302/Add.2 para 5); phases 6-14 June, 15-18 June, 19-23 June and 24-26 June (S/11302/Add.3, plan and timetable, read on the
  page image). In fact, UNDOF "completed that process on 25 June in accordance with the time-table agreed to between the parties
  in Geneva"; Israeli forces handed area 2A to UNDOF on the afternoon of 18 June and area 3A on 23 June; the No. 4 areas
  (Quneitra and Rafid, the area of separation) on 24 and 25 June, with Syrian administration established in the evening of 25
  June; the limited-force zones were inspected on 26 and 27 June [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]
  (S/11310/Add.2, 27 June 1974, paras 1 and 4-7).
- **Map.** The map was first unpublished: "(which by agreement between the parties remains unpublished)" (S/11310/Add.2 para 4,
  27 June); the Secretary-General then published it on 9 July 1974: "The parties have now agreed to the publication of that map, a
  reproduction of which is attached" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11302/Add.3, 9 July 1974). It is a
  gridded map on 5 image pages (pages 2 to 6 of the PDF), with the phase table and the note "Scale and some place names added

  image; the other four pages were not read in detail). No coordinates are printed for the lines; the grid is the map's own.
- **Marking.** "Delineation and marking on the ground of line A has been completed with the assistance of the Israeli
  authorities"; line B's delineation "is in progress" [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] (S/11310/Add.2,
  27 June 1974, para 10).
