# Israeli localities and Nahal outposts founded in the Gaza Strip, June 1967 to 1977 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file (#61)
stays paused until after the MVP. Refs #130 (1957-77 18), the "Settlements" row of [the 1957-1977
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1957-1977-design.md) section 7.3: "settlements in the territories".
One topic of #130 only: the Israeli localities and Nahal outposts founded in the Gaza Strip between June 1967 and 31
December 1977. **The Rafah approaches (Hebrew Pithat Rafiah) and Sinai (PR #1657, note `sinai-localities-1967-77.md`),
the West Bank, the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines and the lines ([`lines-1967-74.md`](lines-1967-74.md))
are not in this note.** Which rows enter the game, and their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#118); this
note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no statement on the status of any place beyond what a source
says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and the conventions of
the sibling notes in this folder. Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which Israeli localities and Nahal outposts were founded in the Gaza Strip between June 1967 and 31
December 1977, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list cannot come from the CBS 2024 localities file.** These places were evacuated in 2005 and the file has no
  row for any of them. It has four rows with a similar name (Netzer Hazani, Karmei Katif, Shavei Darom, Bnei Netzarim),
  all later localities founded 2010 to 2017 elsewhere (section "The CBS file"). So the list is built from Haim Gvati's
  *100 Years of Settlement*: his narrative says "עד אמצע 1977 הוקמו ברצועת עזה שלוש היאחזויות נח״ל ושני מושבים" (by
  mid-1977 three Nahal outposts and two moshavim were set up in the Gaza Strip), and names them (Kfar Darom, Netzarim,
  Morag; the moshavim Netzer Hazani and Katif). Only one of the five, Netzer Hazani, has a row in his appendix. The
  list is Gvati's, cross-checked against Hebrew and English Wikipedia, the JTA Daily News Bulletin, UN General Assembly
  reports and a published album of the Gush Katif communities.
- **5 rows**: 3 Nahal outposts (Kfar Darom, Netzarim, Morag), 1 outpost that became the first civilian moshav (Nahal
  Gadish / Nahal Katif, later Netzer Hazani) and 1 moshav (Katif). Gvati's count of 3 + 2 matches if the
  outpost that became Netzer Hazani is counted once, as a moshav.
- **One more name, Tel Or [GAP].** A/31/218 para. 62 lists a sixth place, "Tel Or, near Deir Al Balah", that I could not
  identify (row note 6). It is not one of the 5 rows.
- **Founding days.** Every date below is [S], [C] or [GAP]; no government record, Knesset record, Official Gazette item
  or Statistical Abstract was opened (Knesset and the National Library newspaper archive blocked this box, see "What
  was not done"). 3 rows are [C] (Netzarim, Morag, Katif: the sources give different days or mark different events), 1 row
  is [S] (Kfar Darom, year agreed, its two days mark two events) and 1 row is [S] on the date and [C] on the name
  (Netzer Hazani, 29 May 1973: four secondary sources that are not independent agree).
- **Contemporaneous press** was read for all 5 rows (Morag through the plan of 17 Jan 1975 and UN A/9148 para. 57) (JTA, 8 bulletin PDFs opened and their datelines read; UN
  reports quoting the Israeli press, 5 pages read as images). The one JTA item that names Morag, of 17 Jan 1975, is forward-looking and gives no founding date.
- **Coordinates:** every row has a point from Hebrew Wikipedia, English Wikipedia and GeoNames; the sources agree to
  under 1.1 km for Kfar Darom and Katif, and disagree by 3.7 km (Netzarim), 11 km (Morag) and 7 km (Netzer Hazani,
  against GeoNames' separate "Nahal Katif" point). No coordinate comes from a map I opened, and each marks the later
  civilian site or a place name, not a surveyed founding site.

## Where the line between this note and the Rafah approaches falls

The next paragraph is copied word for word from `sinai-localities-1967-77.md` (merged in PR #1657); there "this note" is the Sinai note.

**Where the line between this note and the Gaza Strip note falls (same line as in `gaza-localities-1967-77.md`,
the Gaza Strip note of #130, `gaza-localities-1967-77.md`).** A locality is in the Gaza Strip note if the sources put it inside the Strip, the area Egypt
held between 1949 and June 1967 north and west of the Egyptian frontier that the armistice line of 24 February 1949
(Art. VI.1, "continuing thus to the Egyptian frontier"; `armistice-lines-1949.md`) stops at. It is in this note if
the sources put it in the Rafah approaches (Hebrew Pithat Rafiah), which Gvati describes as "פיתחת רפיח, המשתרעת בין
רפיח למבואות אלעריש לאורך חוף הים" and as "אזור חיץ בין רצועת עזה" ... "לבין מרחבי סיני" (a buffer zone between the Strip and the Sinai expanses) (Gvati, vol. 2,
Ben-Yehuda edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684, section "רצועת עזה ופיתחת רפיח", read 2026-10-25). By that
split the Gaza note has Kfar Darom, Netzarim, Morag, Netzer Hazani and Katif (Netzer Hazani's appendix row says "פיתחת רפיח"
but Gvati's narrative puts it in the Katif area of the Strip, so it goes to the Gaza note; Katif has no appendix row); this note has the
Rafah-approaches and Sinai rows of the table above. Nearest to the line: Morag (the southernmost
locality of the Strip: "between Khan Yunis and Rafah" in Hebrew WP, "3 km south of Khan Yunis" in Gvati) and, on this side, Dikla, Sadot
and Netiv HaAsara. No locality is on both lists. This is a reading of the sources named, not a surveyed boundary.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what the CBS 2024 file prints, read today. It says nothing
  about whether the printed value is right. Used only for the CBS file.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Gvati, Wikipedia, the Gush Katif album,
  the Bilig and Huberman paper and the JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). A UN report that quotes a
  newspaper is [S] with that chain stated; A/31/218 para. 62 (rows 2 to 4) is not a newspaper quote but a description
  the report attributes to two visitors to the Strip who gave testimony before the Special Committee, also [S]. Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper (Davar, Maariv), I give the citation as
  Wikipedia gives it and **do not count that source as read**; I opened none of them.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or date this note computed; the arithmetic or the tool is named.
- Hebrew quotes are given without vowel signs; translations are mine. English quotes from JTA and the UN reports are
  copied from the archive page text or read from the page image. Each quoted string was checked by script against the
  text I fetched, except the UN lines, which I read on the rendered page.

1. **Kfar Darom (Nahal outpost of 1970) [S].** Gvati's text: "ב־1970 הוקמה ההיאחזות בכפר־דרום. על־ידי כך חודש הישוב
   שהיה שם לפני מלחמת השחרור ונכבש על־ידי הערבים במהלך המלחמה" (in 1970 the outpost was set up at Kfar Darom, which
   renewed the locality that was there before the War of Independence). No appendix row. Hebrew WP (oldid 42998094):
   the site was "הוקם מחדש בשנת 1970"; an outpost of Bnei Akiva cores set up in October 1970 northeast of the first
   site, with "טקס חנוכת ההיאחזות נערך בדצמבר 1970" (it cites Maariv, 3 Dec 1970, not opened); the 1946 founding of the
   place, its fall in 1948 and the third settlement of 1989 are outside this window. English WP (oldid 1378265786):
   "a Nahal military outpost was established at the site in 1970". Album p. 36 (catalog 28): government decision of
   13 September 1970 (the album prints "13 בספטמבר 1970") to renew two outposts in the Strip, Kfar Darom and a second
   one the album calls "אבו מדיאן"; the going up on the land on 11 Tishrei 5731, printed "11.10.1970" (**derived** check:
   11 Tishrei 5731 = 11 Oct 1970), in a small ceremony; the official ceremony on 2 Kislev 5731, printed "30.11.1970"
   (derived: 2 Kislev 5731 = 30 Nov 1970), about a month and a half later. Bilig and Huberman p. 245: "החליטה הממשלה
   להקים שתי היאחזויות נח"ל ברצועה" on 13 September 1970 (they name Netzarim and Kfar Darom, citing Admoni 1992, p.
   213) and "כעבור חודש הוקמה היאחזות הנח"ל הראשונה ברצועת עזה – כפר דרום" (a month later the first Nahal outpost in
   the Strip, Kfar Darom, was set up); they date the civilianisation of the outpost to 16 December 1975, p. 246 (not
   a founding). JTA: bulletin 13 Oct 1970, dateline "TEL AVIV, Oct. 12 (JTA)": Minister Galili "spoke at Kfar Darom, a
   Jewish settlement on the outskirts of Gaza which was abandoned during the 1948 Arab-Israeli war and recaptured in
   1967" (no date for the speech, "last week" for his earlier call); bulletin 19 Oct 1971, dateline "TEL AVIV, Oct. 18
   (JTA)": "At present there is only one Jewish settlement in the Gaza Strip, Bnai Darom, which was established on the
   site of a settlement of the same name destroyed by the Egyptian Army in 1948" (as printed; my reading is that "Bnai Darom" is Kfar Darom, since the item
   says there is only one such settlement and gives the 1948 destruction; Bnei Darom of 1949 is a different place). UN A/8389, p. 38, item (vi): "The announcement carried on 3 December 1970 according to which a
   settlement, Kfar Darom, was re-established in the Gaza Strip." **Tag:** all sources agree on 1970; the album's two
   days mark two events (the land, the ceremony) and Hebrew WP's "December" and the UN's 3 December match the
   second. The day on which the outpost was founded is not settled by a record: [S].

   not settled.
