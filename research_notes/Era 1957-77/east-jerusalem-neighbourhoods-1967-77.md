# New Israeli neighbourhoods in East Jerusalem, June 1967 to 1977 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file (#61) stays
paused until after the MVP. Refs #130 (1957-77 18), the "Settlements" row of
[the 1957-1977 design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1957-1977-design.md) section 7.3. One topic of #130
only: the new residential neighbourhoods built inside the Jerusalem municipal boundary as extended on 27-28 June 1967
(the boundary is in `lines-1967-74.md`, section 2), from June 1967 to 31 December 1977, and the rebuilding of the Old
City's Jewish Quarter. Facts only: this note gives each source's dates and wording and takes no side on status or
legality; where a source states a legal position it is quoted and attributed. Which rows enter the game, and their
names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#118); this note makes no include, omit or naming choice.

**Where this note sits among its siblings (the boundary sentence agreed on #130).** Every Judea and Samaria row of the
CBS 2024 file with a founding year of 1967-1977 whose local authority is Arvot HaYarden or Megilot Yam HaMelah is in
`jordan-valley-localities-1967-77.md`; every other such row, including Ma'ale Efrayim, Kokhav HaShahar, Rimmonim and
Gvati's Mevo Shilo, is in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/west-bank-localities-1967-77.md` (PR #1663, merged). East Jerusalem
neighbourhoods inside the municipal boundary are in neither of those notes; they are in this note. Arab neighbourhoods
and villages, the lines, and localities outside the boundary are not in it.

It follows the format of `development-towns-1957-77.md` and of
[`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md). Every page below was opened on **25 October
2026** unless a row says otherwise; the second pass on the [GAP] rows was run on **27 October 2026** and its rows are marked.

The question: which new residential neighbourhoods were started inside the municipal boundary between June 1967 and
1977, and for each: the planning or land-taking decision if a source dates it, the start of construction, the first
residents, the place (coordinates from a named source), and the sources.

## Short answer

- **There is no official list in what I could open.** The list below is built from three named secondary sources (section
  "Which list"), not from the CBS localities file: the neighbourhoods are parts of Jerusalem (CBS code 3000), so the
  localities file has no rows for them. The choice of members is the owner's.
- **Nine rows:** Ramat Eshkol, Givat HaMivtar, French Hill (Giv'at Shapira), Ma'alot Dafna, Neve Ya'akov, Ramot (Ramot
  Alon), Gilo, East Talpiot, and the Jewish Quarter rebuild. Eight rows have a disagreement on the year or the first-residents date,
  kept as [C]: the sources differ by one to three years on when each neighbourhood was "founded"; the ninth (Jewish Quarter)
  has a disagreement on the size of the 1968 order.
- **Dated land-taking instruments found:** an order on 18 April 1968 (Jewish Quarter and the land of Kfar Ivri / Neve
  Ya'akov), reported by JTA from the gazette publication of that day; a JTA report of 2 September 1970 of about 2,700
  acres; and a large order of 11 January 1968 that Hebrew Wikipedia gives as 3,345 dunams (not confirmed in a primary
  source here). Second pass (27 Oct 2026): a
  US State Department telegram of 12 January 1968 (FRUS) relays a New York Times report of the same day of 838 acres taken
  in a crescent from Mount Scopus to the Sanhedrin area, and a United Nations report of 1971 relays a memorandum that cites
  Official Gazette No. 1656 of 30 August 1970 for a further order of 11,680 dunams (see "Dated instruments"). No Israeli
  official gazette or Knesset text was reached; those stay [GAP].
- **First-residents days:** one is dated by a contemporaneous report (Ramat Eshkol, 3 September 1969). For the others no
  day was found; they stay [GAP] with what was tried.

## Which list

Three named sources, all secondary, each opened 2026-10-25:

1. **[S, Wikipedia]** English "Ring Neighborhoods, Jerusalem" (oldid 1357154222) lists eight "Ring Neighborhoods": Ramot,
   Ramat Shlomo, Neve Yaakov, Pisgat Ze'ev, French Hill, East Talpiot, Har Homa and Gilo, and adds: "Another Jewish
   neighborhood in Jerusalem built beyond the Green Line, Ramat Eshkol (the first to be built), is not considered part of
   the Ring Neighborhoods." Of its eight, five carry a date of 1967-1977 in that article (Ramot 1974, Neve Yaakov 1970,
   French Hill 1968, East Talpiot 1973, Gilo 1971); Ramat Shlomo (1995), Pisgat Ze'ev (1982) and Har Homa (1997) are
   after the window and are not given rows.
2. **[S, Wikipedia]** Hebrew "גבעת המבתר" (oldid 44051578) and English "Ma'alot Dafna" (oldid 1324952154) describe four
   neighbourhoods near Mount Scopus as the "hinge" neighbourhoods (Hebrew "שכונות הבריח"): Ma'alot Dafna, Ramat Eshkol,
   Givat HaMivtar and French Hill. Ramat Eshkol, Givat HaMivtar and Ma'alot Dafna are not in source 1.
3. **[S, Wikipedia]** English "Jewish Quarter (Jerusalem)" (oldid 1381084421) and Hebrew "הרובע היהודי" (oldid 43944564)
   for the Quarter's rebuilding after 1967. It is not a new neighbourhood on empty land and is given its own row only
   because the lane brief names it.

The English "East Jerusalem" article (oldid 1378661048) says the extension "includes several West Bank villages ... that
are now considered neighborhoods of the city, as well as eight suburban neighborhoods that were built since then"; I used
it only for that context sentence. No official municipal list (Jerusalem Institute, municipality yearbooks) was opened;
see section "Gaps and what was tried".

## The boundary

The municipal boundary of 27-28 June 1967, its instruments (law of 27 June 1967, orders of 28 June 1967) and the map

### Ramat Eshkol

- **Land and plan.** English Wikipedia: "Ramat Eshkol ... was the first new neighborhood built in Jerusalem after the Six Day
  War, along the route to Mount Scopus, Hadassah Hospital and the Hebrew University"; it cites ARIJ for 416 dunams taken
  from Shuafat (ARIJ not read) [S, Wikipedia].
- **Start of construction.** Hebrew Wikipedia: building began in 1968 ("שבנייתה החלה ב-1968"); built 1968-1972; "officially
  inaugurated in 1969" (our translation); the main boulevard was named for Levi Eshkol in July 1969 [S, Wikipedia]. English
  Wikipedia: construction of Eshkol Boulevard began in 1968 [S, Wikipedia]. The Hebrew infobox gives "founding date 1967".
- **First residents [C].**
  - **3 September 1969** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 5 September 1969 (page 3, headline "New Housing Project Named For Late
    Premier Levi Eshkol Is Opened In Jerusalem"), dateline "JERUSALEM, Sept. 4 (JTA)": "Ramat Eshkol, a housing project in
    northeast Jerusalem, was opened yesterday when Housing Minister ... handed the keys to the apartments to their first
    residents. Some 96 persons moved into the apartments built on former Jordanian territory which had been unused."
    Dateline 4 September, "yesterday" = 3 September 1969. Read on the page image of
    `http://pdfs.jta.org/1969/1969-09-05_169.pdf`; web page https://www.jta.org/archive/new-housing-project-named-for-late-premier-levi-eshkol-is-opened-in-jerusalem.
  - **January 1970** [S, Wikipedia]: English Wikipedia: "In January 1970, the first occupants moved in."
  - **End of 1969** [S, Wikipedia]: Hebrew Wikipedia: the neighbourhood "was populated from the end of that same year" (1969; our
    translation; the sentence's citation is a newspaper issue of 1970 that the article names and that was not read).
  - All three are kept. They may mark different things (keys handed to a first group, then moving in); no source says so.
  - **Before the move-in, 14-15 May 1969** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 16 May 1969 (page 2, headline "Mark 2nd Anniversary Of
    Jerusalem's Reunification, 21st Year Of Statehood"), dateline "JERUSALEM, May 15 (JTA)": "The observance began with the
    naming yesterday of a main road leading to a new housing project in East Jerusalem for the late Premier Levi Eshkol. The
    housing project, for both Arabs and Jews, was named Ramat Eshkol. ... The first families are expected to move into Ramat
    Eshkol next September." Dateline 15 May, "yesterday" = 14 May 1969 (read on the page image, OCR-assisted at 200 dpi;
    page 2 of `http://pdfs.jta.org/1969/1969-05-16_094.pdf`; web page
    https://www.jta.org/archive/mark-2nd-anniversary-of-jerusalems-reunification-21st-year-of-statehood). This fits the
    3 September 1969 handing-over of keys above. **[C]** Hebrew Wikipedia dates the naming of the main boulevard to July 1969
    (above); this JTA item dates the naming of "a main road leading to" the project to 14 May 1969. They may be two
    different namings (the project, then the boulevard); no source says so. Both kept. Opened 2026-10-27.

### Givat HaMivtar

- **Planning and land.** Hebrew Wikipedia: the Prime Minister appointed a person in summer 1967 to head the development of
  East Jerusalem (role only); "the need for quick building forced the government to issue, on 11 January 1968, a large
  expropriation order (for an area of 3,345 dunams), which drew condemnation from the Security Council" (our translation)
  [S, Wikipedia; the order and the condemnation not read in a primary source here]. English Wikipedia: "In July 1967,
  Prime Minister Levi Eshkol ordered government clerks to bypass the ordinary procedures ... When purchasing land
  encountered difficulties, some tracts were expropriated" [S, Wikipedia].
- **Year [C].** Hebrew infobox: founded 1968. Hebrew text: "נוסדה בשנת 1970" (founded in 1970). English: "established in
  1970". The Hebrew article's 1968 may mark the order or the first plots; it does not say.
- **Form.** Both articles describe a "Build Your Own Home" area (private two-family houses); Hebrew Wikipedia says it was the
  first such area allocated in Jerusalem after 1967 [S, Wikipedia]. First residents: [GAP].
- **Derived.** 3,345 dunams = 3,345 / 4.04686 = about 826.6 acres. The New York Times article quoted in the section
  "Contemporary reporting of 1968" speaks of "slightly more than 800 acres" expropriated "in the Arab sector"; that is the
  same order of size, but the article does not name the 11 January order, so the two are not equated here.

### French Hill (Giv'at Shapira)

- **Plan.** Hebrew Wikipedia: the plan was approved by the Jerusalem city council in February 1969 as a neighbourhood for
  immigrants from North America at the initiative of an immigrants' association (our translation of the sentence); it was
  given the name Giv'at Shapira in 1971 [S, Wikipedia].
- **Start and year [C].** English Wikipedia: "In 1969, construction began"; its Ring list says "established in 1968"; the
  article cites ARIJ for land taken "in 1968" (394 dunams from Isawiya and 394 from Shuafat; ARIJ not read). Hebrew
  Wikipedia: "founded in 1971" (our translation). Three values: 1968, 1969, 1971. First residents: [GAP]. **Narrowed (27 Oct
  2026)**: JTA daily bulletin of 19 November 1974 (page 3, headline "West Bank Disturbances Spread To Jerusalem; 10 Injured,
  80 Arrested"), dateline "JERUSALEM, Nov. 18 (JTA)": "Yesterday, residents of a large apartment building on French Hill in
  the city's northern outskirts were evacuated after two bazooka rockets were found in a stairwell" [S]; "yesterday" = 17
  November 1974. A bulletin of 3 February 1975 (headline "Bomb on Jerusalem Bus", dateline not read; read as web text at https://www.jta.org/archive/bomb-on-jerusalem-bus, not on the page image) calls French Hill "a
  new suburb in the north of the city on formerly Jordanian land". So French Hill had residents by 17 November 1974 (an upper
  bound only). Page image (OCR-assisted, 200 dpi) of `http://pdfs.jta.org/1974/1974-11-19_218.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/west-bank-disturbances-spread-to-jerusalem-10-injured-80-arrested.

### Ma'alot Dafna

- **Land.** Hebrew Wikipedia ties this to the Arzei HaBira compound in the southern part of the neighbourhood, named after
  the company that built it, not to the neighbourhood as a whole: in that compound homes for new immigrants were built,
  "partly on private land expropriated in 1968" (our translation; the sentence cites a Davar item of 30 June 1972, not
  read) [S, Wikipedia].
- **Start and year [C].** Hebrew: "built from the end of 1971" (our translation). English: "established in 1972". First
  residents: [GAP].
- **Location note.** English Wikipedia says its sub-area Arzei HaBira lies on the Israeli side of the pre-1967 armistice
  line and "is now considered a separate neighbourhood"; it is not given a row [S, Wikipedia].

### Neve Ya'akov

- **Site.** English Wikipedia: a village of the same name was founded in 1924 and depopulated in 1948; "In 1972, a new
  Israeli neighborhood was constructed on the site of the original village" [S, Wikipedia]. The 1924 village is not in the
  window and is not given a row.
- **Land-taking, 18 April 1968** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 19 April 1968 (page 2, headline "Israel Expropriates Jewish Area
  in East Jerusalem for Slum Clearance Project"), dateline "JERUSALEM, April 18 (JTA)": "The second expropriation order
  covers land in Kfar Ivri (Neve Yaacov) which was officially incorporated into the Jerusalem municipality last year.
  ... Most of the land now being taken over by the State was occupied by an Arab Legion military camp. Some of it belongs
  to Jews and less than 10 percent belonged to Arabs, according to the officials." The same item
  says "The expropriation orders were published in the official gazette today", so the gazette date as JTA gives it is 18
  April 1968; the gazette itself was not opened. Page image of `http://pdfs.jta.org/1968/1968-04-19_076.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-expropriates-jewish-area-in-east-jerusalem-for-slum-clearance-project.
- **Year [C].** Hebrew Wikipedia: "founded in 1970", and "In August 1970 ... Neve Yaakov was founded as a new neighbourhood"
  (our translation). English Ring list: "Established in 1970". English text: built in 1972, "4,900 apartments". See the
  August 1970 announcement below.
- **Construction and residents.** JTA bulletin of 30 March 1971, dateline "JERUSALEM, March 29": 750 units "in the Neveh
  Yaacov area" in the first stage (see the 1971 row below). JTA bulletin of 6 July 1973, dateline "JERUSALEM, July 5":
  "Housing construction in Neve Yaacov and Gilo has already begun" and families "will settle in four new Jerusalem
  suburbs ... by the end of the year" [S]. First residents' day: [GAP]. **Narrowed (27 Oct 2026)**: JTA daily bulletin of
  31 January 1975 (page 4, headline "Chicago Jewish Leader Donates Money For Mother-And-Child Center"), dateline "JERUSALEM,
  Jan. 30 (JTA)": "A mother-and-child center was opened in the new north Jerusalem suburb of Neve Yaacov yesterday", and its
  users will include "Israeli young couples who have moved to Neve Yaacov, the northernmost section of the capital on the
  road to Ramallah" [S]. "Yesterday" = 29 January 1975. So the suburb had residents by 29 January 1975 (an upper bound;
  not the first day). Read on the page image of `http://pdfs.jta.org/1975/1975-01-31_022.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/chicago-jewish-leader-donates-money-for-mother-and-child-center.

### Ramot (Ramot Alon)

- **Land.** Hebrew Wikipedia: "the expropriation of the land for the neighbourhood took place in August 1970" (our
  translation); English: "built on land expropriated from Palestinian landowners in 1970" [S, Wikipedia]. See the August
  1970 announcement below.
- **Year [C].** Hebrew infobox: founded 1972. Hebrew text: "נוסדה בשנת 1974" (founded in 1974). English: "founded in 1974".
- **Plans.** JTA 30 March 1971 (dateline 29 March): 550 units in Ramot in the first stage. The Hebrew article says that in
  the 1968 master plan the area was to stay rural and that the initiative for the neighbourhood arose in the early 1970s
  (our translation) [S, Wikipedia]. First residents: [GAP]. **Searched (27 Oct 2026)**: JTA daily bulletin of 27 February
  1974 (page 3, headline "JNF Reports Green Belt Progress"), dateline "JERUSALEM, Feb. 26 (JTA)": "the two new housing
  projects of Ramot Eshkol and Ramot, also in the north" [S]. The item says "housing projects", not residents; so it dates
  the project, not the first residents. `http://pdfs.jta.org/1974/1974-02-27_040.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/jnf-reports-green-belt-progress. Note: "Ramot Eshkol" there is JTA's wording for the Ramat
  Eshkol of this note's first row; the item does not say so.

### Gilo

- **Land.** English Wikipedia: "In 1970, the Israeli government expropriated 12,300 dunams of land to build Ring
  Neighborhoods around Jerusalem" (no source named in the sentence as I read it) [S, Wikipedia]. See the August 1970
  announcement below for the other figure.
- **Year [C].** Hebrew Wikipedia: "founded in 1971 as one of the Ring neighbourhoods" (our translation); The English

- **Construction.** JTA 30 March 1971 (dateline 29 March): 700 units "in Giloh" in the first stage; JTA 6 July 1973 (dateline
  5 July): construction already begun. First residents: [GAP]. **Searched (27 Oct 2026)**: JTA, JNF item of 26 February
  1974 (as under Ramot above): "a further 1000 dunams near the Gilo housing estate in the south on the Bethlehem Road" [S];
  JTA daily bulletin of 16 August 1977 (page 4, headline "American Mizrachi Women Unveil Plan To Build Nursery School In
  Israel"), dateline "LOS ANGELES, Aug. 15 (JTA)": "The school, located at Gilo, a new residential area under construction
  in Jerusalem" [S]. So a source of 15 August 1977 still calls Gilo a residential area "under construction"; no residents'
  date. `http://pdfs.jta.org/1977/1977-08-16_156.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/american-mizrachi-women-unveil-plan-to-build-nursery-school-in-israel.

### East Talpiot

- **Land and start.** Hebrew Wikipedia: "construction of the neighbourhood began in the 1970s, according to the land
  expropriation order of August 1970" (our translation); English: constructed "as part of the 1968 Jerusalem Master
  Plan"; it cites ARIJ for 1,343 dunams from Sur Baher and 544 from Jabel Mukaber (ARIJ not read) [S, Wikipedia].
- **Year [C].** Hebrew: founded 1972. English: "established by Israel in 1973" and the Ring list "1973".
- **Plans.** JTA 30 March 1971 (dateline 29 March): 600 units in the first stage; JTA 6 July 1973: planned among the four
  suburbs. First residents: [GAP]. **Narrowed (27 Oct 2026)**: the JTA daily bulletin of 2 October 1975 (page 4, headline
  "Behind The Headlines: Arab Building Boom Around Jerusalem", a feature with the dateline "JERUSALEM, Oct. 1 (JTA)", seen on the page image) says an immigrant
  from the United States "now lives in the neighborhood of Talpiot-East" [S] (the article names him; he is given by role
  here). So the
  neighbourhood had residents by 1 October 1975 (dateline), as an upper bound only. `http://pdfs.jta.org/1975/1975-10-02_188.pdf`.
  JTA items of 9, 10 and 21 June 1971 that mention "Talpiot" concern the older "fashionable Talpiot" quarter, not this
  neighbourhood, and are not used.

### Jewish Quarter rebuild

- **Order of 18 April 1968 [C].** JTA bulletin of 19 April 1968 (page 2), dateline "JERUSALEM, April 18": "The Israel
  Government has expropriated 16 acres of land in the Jewish quarter of East Jerusalem for a slum clearance project ...
  The property in East Jerusalem includes buildings erected since 1948 which house 600 Arabs. ... Officials explained that the
  expropriation was necessary in order to re-plan the entire quarter" [S]. English Wikipedia: "On 18 April 1968, the Israeli
  minister of finance ... issued an order for the expropriation of 29 acres (116 dunums)" [S, Wikipedia]. Derived: 116 /
  4.04686 = 28.7 acres, so Wikipedia's two figures agree with each other and differ from JTA's 16. A third figure, [S]: the
  New York Times article in S/8667 (below) says "Some 30 acres in the old city were also expropriated" and does not say
  that this is the same order. All kept.
- **Company.** English Wikipedia: "In 1969, the Jewish Quarter Development Company was established" [S, Wikipedia]. Hebrew
  Wikipedia: in July 1968 the government decided to set up a state-controlled company for rehabilitating and developing
  the Quarter, and the company was founded in January 1969 (our translation) [S, Wikipedia].
- **First residents.** Hebrew Wikipedia: from 1968 members of a religious Nahal group settled in the Quarter's area (our
  translation) [S, Wikipedia]. The day: [GAP].
