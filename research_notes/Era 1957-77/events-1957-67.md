## Conventions

The same as [events-1952-56.md](../M4%20content%201952-56/events-1952-56.md):

- **[V]** read this session in a primary source: a FRUS document, a UN document or voting record, a Bank of Israel annual report. Every [V] here is an agent read; a person's check is pending.
- **[S]** single or secondary source only: a JTA bulletin, or Wikipedia as a pointer.
- **[C]** sources conflict; every value is given with its source.
- **[GAP]** not found. A gap stays a gap: no date, figure or name is filled in by inference.
- **JTA dates.** The date on a JTA archive page is the bulletin date. "Today" in an item normally means the day
  before the bulletin date. A day taken from JTA alone is [S]; where a weekday ("last Thursday") gives the day, the
  weekday was checked against the calendar.
- **Quotes** are copied from the cited text (its en dashes written as hyphens), short, and never put in a speaker's mouth for game text. Spelling inside
  quotes is the source's.
- Vote counts are checked where the source gives every component: in favour + against + abstaining + not voting =
  members, shown in each entry; where it does not, the missing part is said.
- Two lettered parts follow the design's two rows: A (1957 to Ben-Gurion's resignation in June 1963) and B (Eshkol to
  14 May 1967).

## A. After Sinai, 1 January 1957 to 25 June 1963

### A1. The withdrawal from Sinai, Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh (January to 8 March 1957)

- **Date(s).**
  - 14 January 1957: Eban told the US delegation that Israeli forces would be out of Sinai by 22 January, except at
    Sharm el-Sheikh [V: FRUS 1955-57 XVII, doc. 23]. The day the Sinai stage was actually completed is not in the
    documents read [GAP].
  - 2 February 1957: General Assembly resolutions 1124 (XI) and 1125 (XI) [V: UN Digital Library voting records].
    1124: 74 in favour, 2 against, 2 abstentions, 2 non-voting (74 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 80 members). 1125: 56 in favour,
    none against, 22 abstentions, 2 non-voting (56 + 0 + 22 + 2 = 80).
  - 11 February 1957: the US aide-mémoire handed to Eban [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 78].
  - 1 March 1957: Foreign Minister Meir's statement to the General Assembly (U.N. doc. A/PV.666) [V: cited by FRUS
    XVII, doc. 167, editorial note; the A/PV.666 text itself was not opened].
  - 4 March 1957: the Foreign Ministry told the US embassy at 13:00 local time that Ben-Gurion "has decided to
    withdraw" [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 189].
  - 7 and 8 March 1957: UNEF's commander, General Burns, cabled that Israeli forces and civil administration would be
    out of Gaza by 16:00 on 7 March, and out of Sharm el-Sheikh by 8 March, apart from a small group handling equipment
    [V: as planned dates, FRUS XVII, doc. 191]. On 7 March Ben-Gurion wrote to Eisenhower that Israel had "evacuated
    the whole of the Sinai Desert" and "also the Gaza Strip" [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 201].
- **Summary.** Israel withdrew from Sinai in stages from late 1956 and kept Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh while it sought
  guarantees on shipping through the Straits of Tiran and on Gaza's future. The General Assembly called on it on
  2 February to withdraw. The US aide-mémoire of 11 February held that withdrawal from Gaza "should be prompt and
  unconditional" and that the Gulf of Aqaba was international waters. After Meir's statement of 1 March in the
  Assembly, the government decided on 4 March to withdraw; Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh were handed to UNEF on 7 and
  8 March.
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS 1955-57, vol. XVII, doc. 23 (USUN telegram, New York, 14 January 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d23 . "Eban said by 22nd Jan Israeli forces will be
    entirely out of Sinai, and “UNEF will be up to Egyptian-Israeli frontier”, except for Sharm el Sheikh."
  - UN Digital Library, General Assembly resolution 1124 (XI) and 1125 (XI), 2 February 1957, voting records:
    https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/669321 and https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/669324 (votes as above).
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 78 (aide-mémoire, Washington, 11 February 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d78 . "we believe that Israeli withdrawal from Gaza
    should be prompt and unconditional".
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 167 (memorandum of conversation, 28 February 1957, with its note on the 1 March statement):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d167 .
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 189 (Tel Aviv telegram, 4 March 1957, 14:00):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d189 . "Foreign Ministry official telephoned Embassy
    at 1:00 p.m. local time to say Ben Gurion has decided to withdraw".
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 191 (Wilcox to Dulles, 4 March 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d191 . "the Israeli forces and civilian
    administration would be out of Gaza by 1600 hours March 7".
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 201 (Ben-Gurion to Eisenhower, Jerusalem, 7 March 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d201 .
- **Gaps.** [GAP] the day each Sinai stage ended (the design's "most of Sinai by January 1957" is still to verify:
  only the planned date of 22 January was found). [GAP] the Knesset address Ben-Gurion postponed from 4 to 5 March
  (doc. 189) was not read. [S] the 8 March Rafah date in the design rests on Wikipedia.

### A2. UNEF takes over Gaza; the Egyptian governor returns (7 to 14 March 1957)

- **Date(s).** UNEF takeover from 7 March 1957 [V: planned, FRUS XVII, doc. 191]. 11 March: Burns reported the
  Egyptian communiqué appointing an "administrative governor" of Gaza [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 210]. 14 March: the
  governor entered Gaza in the afternoon [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 224, Cairo telegram of 15 March, 02:00, reporting a
  meeting with Nasser "tonight"].
- **Summary.** The Secretary-General had told the General Assembly on 22 February that the takeover of Gaza "in the
  first instance would be exclusively by UNEF". Within a week of Israel's withdrawal Egypt named an administrative
  governor, who entered Gaza on 14 March; Nasser also told the US ambassador that Egyptian troops had moved forward in
  Sinai as far as El Arish, but not into Gaza. On 17 March Ben-Gurion told the US ambassador that Israel would "take
  no precipitate action".
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 137 (editorial note on the Secretary-General's statement of 22 February 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d137 . The quote above.
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 210 (USUN telegram, 11 March 1957): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d210 .
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 224 (Cairo telegram, 15 March 1957, 02:00): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d224 .
    "Not only did Governor of Gaza actually enter city this afternoon but (Nasser volunteered this) Egyptian troops
    have been moved into Sinai (but not into Gaza) and are now as far forward as El Arish."
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 230 (Tel Aviv telegram, 17 March 1957): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d230 .
    "we will take no precipitate action".
- **Gaps.** [GAP] the governor's name: doc. 210 prints it as "Hassan Abdul", apparently cut short in the source; not
  completed here.

### A3. A grenade in the Knesset (29 October 1957)

- **Date(s).** 29 October 1957, 17:15 local time [S: JTA bulletin of 30 October 1957, "today"; 29 October was a
  Tuesday].
- **Summary.** A man in the visitors' gallery threw a home-made bomb into the Knesset chamber. Five ministers were
  hurt: Ben-Gurion (hands and left leg), Meir (left foot), Moshe Shapira (Religious Affairs, the most seriously
  injured), Moshe Carmel (Transport, a broken arm) and Israel Barzilai (Health, slightly). The thrower, Moshe Douek (JTA's spelling),
  25, an immigrant from Iraq, was arrested at the scene; on 4 November JTA reported that he had been ruled fit to
  stand trial.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Five Cabinet Members Injured by Madman's Bomb in Knesset", bulletin of 30 October 1957:
    https://www.jta.org/archive/five-cabinet-members-injured-by-madmans-bomb-in-knesset . "The explosion occurred at
    5:15 P. M. local time."
  - JTA, "Douek Ruled Sane, Fit to Stand Trial for Knesset Bombing", bulletin of 4 November 1957:
    https://www.jta.org/archive/douek-ruled-sane-fit-to-stand-trial-for-knesset-bombing (title only).
- **Gaps.** [GAP] no Knesset record or court record was reachable; the sentence is not found.

### A6. Wadi Salib and the riots that followed (8 to 20 July 1959)

- **Date(s).**
  - Night of Wednesday 8 July 1959: police in Haifa shot and wounded a man, an immigrant from North Africa, who
    resisted arrest [S: JTA bulletin of 13 July 1959].
  - Thursday 9 July 1959: rioting in the quarter; arrests; 11 policemen hurt [S: same].
  - Monday 20 July 1959: riots in Beersheba, seven hurt and 26 arrested; earlier disturbances in Migdal HaEmek, 12
    policemen hurt [S: JTA bulletin of 23 July 1959]. The design's 11 July date for the spread rests on Wikipedia [S].
- **Summary.** After rumours that the wounded man had died, a demonstration marched with black flags and turned into
  a riot in the Wadi Salib quarter of Haifa; shops were damaged and cars burned. The Cabinet set up a commission of
  inquiry, enlarged to five members by the Knesset, under Judge Moshe Etzioni; the Police Minister, Bechor Shitrit,
  reported that the shooting that started it was not justified. The unrest spread to Beersheba and Migdal HaEmek.

### A8. Eichmann: the announcement and the Security Council (23 May to 23 June 1960)

- **Date(s).**
  - 23 May 1960: Ben-Gurion announces in the Knesset that Eichmann is under arrest in Israel [S: JTA bulletin of
    24 May 1960, "today"; the date is repeated as "May 23, 1960" in JTA's bulletin of 1 June 1962].
  - 22 June 1960: the Security Council invites Israel's representative to take part [V: resolution 138 text].
  - 23 June 1960: resolution 138 (1960), 868th meeting, adopted 8 in favour, none against, 2 abstentions (Poland,

- **Summary.** Israeli agents brought Adolf Eichmann from Argentina to Israel. Argentina complained to the Security

  said it should "in no way be interpreted as condoning the odious crimes of which Eichmann is accused".

### A9. The Eichmann trial, sentence and execution (11 April 1961 to 1 June 1962)

- **Date(s).**
  - 11 April 1961: the trial opens in Jerusalem, at Beit Ha'am [S: JTA bulletin of 12 April 1961, "today"].
  - Monday 11 December 1961: the three-judge District Court (presiding judge Moshe Landau) finds him guilty on all
    15 counts; the reading of the judgment runs over several sessions [S: JTA bulletin of 12 December 1961].
  - 15 December 1961: the death sentence [S: JTA bulletin of 31 May 1962, "issued last December 15 by a Jerusalem
    District Court"; JTA bulletin of 15 December 1961 announces it for "tomorrow morning"].
  - 29 May 1962: the Supreme Court upholds the verdict and sentence [S: JTA bulletin of 31 May 1962, the clemency plea
    "came 24 hours after the Israel Supreme Court upheld the Eichmann judgment"; the day is worked out from that].
  - 31 May 1962, late: President Ben-Zvi rejects the plea for clemency; Eichmann hanged at Ramla prison "a few minutes
    after midnight", early on Friday 1 June 1962 [S: JTA bulletin of 1 June 1962].
- **Summary.** Eichmann was tried in the Jerusalem District Court under what JTA calls "the law for the trial of Nazis
  and their collaborators", convicted on all counts and sentenced to death. The Supreme Court dismissed his appeal, the President refused
  clemency, and he was hanged at Ramla.

### A10. The Lavon affair reopened (September 1960 to February 1961)

- **Date(s).**
  - 29 September 1960: a communiqué from the Prime Minister's Office says the Chief of Staff, on Ben-Gurion's order as
    Minister of Defence, has appointed a three-man committee to examine a regular officer and a reserve officer
    [S: JTA bulletin of 30 September 1960, "last night"]. This is the Cohn committee of the design (§2.1); the name is
    not in the JTA item.
  - 22 December 1960: the seven-member Ministerial Committee reports, unanimously, that Lavon did not give the order
    for the 1954 "security mishap" [S: JTA bulletin of 23 December 1960, "issued today"].
  - 25 December 1960: the Cabinet accepts the committee's report [S: quoted in Ben-Gurion's resignation letter, JTA
    bulletin of 1 February 1961; the same item says the decision was unanimous].
  - Tuesday 31 January 1961: Ben-Gurion resigns, which brings down the government [S: JTA bulletin of 1 February 1961,
    "tonight"; the bulletin of 6 February says "last Tuesday"].
  - 4 February 1961: Mapai's central committee votes no confidence in Lavon as Histadrut secretary-general, 159 for,
    96 against, 5 abstentions [S: JTA bulletin of 6 February 1961, "yesterday"].
  - Thursday 9 February 1961: the Histadrut executive removes Lavon, 58 to 46 [S: JTA bulletin of 10 February 1961].
- **Summary.** A civil court case in 1960 cast doubt on testimony given in 1955 about the failed 1954 operation in
  Egypt. Ben-Gurion had the officers' conduct investigated; a ministerial committee of seven then cleared Lavon and
  the Cabinet adopted its report. Ben-Gurion refused responsibility for that decision and resigned. Mapai, under his
  conditions for forming a new government, voted to remove Lavon from the Histadrut. The Knesset was dissolved and
  the 1961 election followed (A12).
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Ben-gurion Orders Investigation of Army Aides Involved in Lavon Case", bulletin of 30 September 1960:

### B7. The German crisis and relations with West Germany (February-May 1965)

### B10. The slowdown and recession (mitun), 1965-1967

- **Date(s).** Bank of Israel Annual Reports 1964, 1965, 1966, chapter 1 (read); 1967 not retrieved (see Gaps).
- **Summary.** Real GNP growth, with each report's own figure because they differ by vintage [C]:
  - 1964: "10-11 percent" in the 1964 text; 10.6 in the 1964 report's Table 1-1 and 10.3 in the 1965 and 1966 reports' tables.
  - 1965: "only 7 percent" in the 1965 report (Table 1-1 value 7.0); 8.2 in the 1966 report's table ("the increase amounting to 8 percent" in its text).
  - 1966: "less than 1 percent" (Table 1-1 value 0.6; per capita -1.9).
  The 1966 report ties the slowdown to the end of the investment boom (capital formation up 21 percent at constant prices in 1964), the fall in net immigration (about 50,000 a year in 1961-64, 20,000 in 1965, 5,000 in 1966), the Government's restraint policy from late 1965, and a 20 percent fall in construction output. Employment did not grow while the labour force grew by about 36,000, so unemployment rose sharply. Policy turn: the Bank of Israel moved to expansionary monetary policy in the second half of 1966, and the Government and the Bank adopted expansionary steps that the report says were insufficient to turn the tide in 1966. Derived arithmetic: none beyond the figures quoted.
- **Sources.**
  - Bank of Israel Annual Report 1964, chapter 1, PDF p. 1: https://www.boi.org.il/boi_files/BankIsraelAnnualReport/Annual%20Report%201964/part%20one/pe_1.pdf . "The year 1964 showed further rapid growth of the national product".

"the increase came to only 7 percent in 1965". Also: "Aggregate demand went up by only 4.8 percent, compared with 12.8 percent in 1964."
  - Bank of Israel Annual Report 1966, chapter 1, printed p. 3 (PDF p. 1): https://www.boi.org.il/boi_files/BankIsraelAnnualReport/Annual%20Report%201966/part%20one/pe_1.pdf . "Real GNP increased by less than 1 percent in 1966". Also: "This caused unemployment to mount sharply".
  - Same report, printed p. 4: "In the latter part of 1965 and the beginning of 1966, the Government's policy of economic restraint began to make itself felt." and "the Government and the Bank of Israel adopted an expansionary policy in order to check these trends."
  - Same report, printed p. 5, Table 1-1 (garbled extraction, read as columns 1966/1965/1964/1963): GNP 0.6 / 8.2 / 10.3 / 11.5.
