## A. After Sinai, 1 January 1957 to 25 June 1963

### A1. The withdrawal from Sinai, Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh (January to 8 March 1957)

- **Date(s).**
  - 14 January 1957: Eban told the US delegation that Israeli forces would be out of Sinai by 22 January, except at
    Sharm el-Sheikh [V: FRUS 1955-57 XVII, doc. 23]. The day the Sinai stage was actually completed is not in the
    documents read [GAP].
  - 2 February 1957: General Assembly resolutions 1124 (XI) and 1125 (XI) [V: UN Digital Library voting records].
    1124: 74 in favour, 2 against, 2 abstentions, 2 non-voting (74 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 80 members). 1125: 56 in favour,
    none against, 22 abstentions, 2 non-voting (56 + 0 + 22 + 2 = 80).
  - 11 February 1957: the US aide-mémoire handed to Eban [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 78].
  - 1 March 1957: Foreign Minister Meir's statement to the General Assembly (U.N. doc. A/PV.666) [V: cited by FRUS
    XVII, doc. 167, editorial note; the A/PV.666 text itself was not opened].
  - 4 March 1957: the Foreign Ministry told the US embassy at 13:00 local time that Ben-Gurion "has decided to
    withdraw" [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 189].
  - 7 and 8 March 1957: UNEF's commander, General Burns, cabled that Israeli forces and civil administration would be
    out of Gaza by 16:00 on 7 March, and out of Sharm el-Sheikh by 8 March, apart from a small group handling equipment
    [V: as planned dates, FRUS XVII, doc. 191]. On 7 March Ben-Gurion wrote to Eisenhower that Israel had "evacuated
    the whole of the Sinai Desert" and "also the Gaza Strip" [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 201].
- **Summary.** Israel withdrew from Sinai in stages from late 1956 and kept Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh while it sought
  guarantees on shipping through the Straits of Tiran and on Gaza's future. The General Assembly called on it on
  2 February to withdraw. The US aide-mémoire of 11 February held that withdrawal from Gaza "should be prompt and
  unconditional" and that the Gulf of Aqaba was international waters. After Meir's statement of 1 March in the
  Assembly, the government decided on 4 March to withdraw; Gaza and Sharm el-Sheikh were handed to UNEF on 7 and
  8 March.

### A2. UNEF takes over Gaza; the Egyptian governor returns (7 to 14 March 1957)

- **Date(s).** UNEF takeover from 7 March 1957 [V: planned, FRUS XVII, doc. 191]. 11 March: Burns reported the
  Egyptian communiqué appointing an "administrative governor" of Gaza [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 210]. 14 March: the
  governor entered Gaza in the afternoon [V: FRUS XVII, doc. 224, Cairo telegram of 15 March, 02:00, reporting a
  meeting with Nasser "tonight"].
- **Summary.** The Secretary-General had told the General Assembly on 22 February that the takeover of Gaza "in the
  first instance would be exclusively by UNEF". Within a week of Israel's withdrawal Egypt named an administrative
  governor, who entered Gaza on 14 March; Nasser also told the US ambassador that Egyptian troops had moved forward in
  Sinai as far as El Arish, but not into Gaza. On 17 March Ben-Gurion told the US ambassador that Israel would "take
  no precipitate action".
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 137 (editorial note on the Secretary-General's statement of 22 February 1957):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d137 . The quote above.
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 210 (USUN telegram, 11 March 1957): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d210 .
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 224 (Cairo telegram, 15 March 1957, 02:00): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d224 .
    "Not only did Governor of Gaza actually enter city this afternoon but (Nasser volunteered this) Egyptian troops
    have been moved into Sinai (but not into Gaza) and are now as far forward as El Arish."
  - FRUS XVII, doc. 230 (Tel Aviv telegram, 17 March 1957): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d230 .

### A3. A grenade in the Knesset (29 October 1957)

- **Date(s).** 29 October 1957, 17:15 local time [S: JTA bulletin of 30 October 1957, "today"; 29 October was a
  Tuesday].
- **Summary.** A man in the visitors' gallery threw a home-made bomb into the Knesset chamber. Five ministers were
  hurt: Ben-Gurion (hands and left leg), Meir (left foot), Moshe Shapira (Religious Affairs, the most seriously
  injured), Moshe Carmel (Transport, a broken arm) and Israel Barzilai (Health, slightly). The thrower, Moshe Douek (JTA's spelling),
  25, an immigrant from Iraq, was arrested at the scene; on 4 November JTA reported that he had been ruled fit to
  stand trial.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Five Cabinet Members Injured by Madman's Bomb in Knesset", bulletin of 30 October 1957:
    https://www.jta.org/archive/five-cabinet-members-injured-by-madmans-bomb-in-knesset . "The explosion occurred at

  - JTA, "Douek Ruled Sane, Fit to Stand Trial for Knesset Bombing", bulletin of 4 November 1957:

### A6. Wadi Salib and the riots that followed (8 to 20 July 1959)

- **Date(s).**
  - Night of Wednesday 8 July 1959: police in Haifa shot and wounded a man, an immigrant from North Africa, who
    resisted arrest [S: JTA bulletin of 13 July 1959].
  - Thursday 9 July 1959: rioting in the quarter; arrests; 11 policemen hurt [S: same].
  - Monday 20 July 1959: riots in Beersheba, seven hurt and 26 arrested; earlier disturbances in Migdal HaEmek, 12
    policemen hurt [S: JTA bulletin of 23 July 1959]. The design's 11 July date for the spread rests on Wikipedia [S].
- **Summary.** After rumours that the wounded man had died, a demonstration marched with black flags and turned into
  a riot in the Wadi Salib quarter of Haifa; shops were damaged and cars burned. The Cabinet set up a commission of
  inquiry, enlarged to five members by the Knesset, under Judge Moshe Etzioni; the Police Minister, Bechor Shitrit,
  reported that the shooting that started it was not justified. The unrest spread to Beersheba and Migdal HaEmek.

### B13. The Eli Cohen affair (arrest early 1965; hanged in Damascus 18 May 1965)

### B14. Other major events for the Israeli government in the window (proposed additions)

#### B14a. Proposed addition: Harriman-Komer mission and the US-Israel arms understandings (25 February to 7 March 1965)

- **Date(s).** Talks in Tel Aviv from 25 February 1965 [V: FRUS d158, "met twice with Eshkol, Meir, and Bitan on February 25"]; telegrams from Tel Aviv continue to 1 March (d170) and 5 March (d177) [V]; the day the mission left is not found [GAP]. - **Summary.** Johnson sent Harriman and Komer to ask Eshkol to accept US arms for Jordan. The US sought Israeli undertakings on nuclear weapons, IAEA controls and not striking the Arab diversion works. On 28 February Komer reported no quick agreement; on 7 March the White House said it preferred a prompt understanding limited to the Jordan sale and did not want an open-ended obligation to supply arms to Israel. The US also tied arms to both sides: Johnson's recorded telephone call on 20 February.
- **Sources.**
