## Conventions

The same as [events-1957-67.md](events-1957-67.md) and [events-1952-56.md](../M4%20content%201952-56/events-1952-56.md):

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-24; a person's check pending]** read this session in a primary source: a FRUS document, a UN record, a primary text opened on the publisher's page. Every [V] here is an agent read; a person's check is pending (CONTENT_POLICY §2 item 6), which is why each [V] carries the full tag.
- **[S]** single or secondary source only: a JTA bulletin, or Wikipedia as a pointer, opened on the page itself.
- **[C]** sources conflict; every value is given with its source.
- **[GAP]** not found. A gap stays a gap: no date, figure or name is filled in by inference. A search snippet or summary is never a source and is never tagged [S].
- **JTA dates.** A JTA archive page shows the bulletin date. The event date comes from the dateline on the item in the
  original daily bulletin PDF (the page's "Original Daily Bulletin From This Date" link, `pdfs.jta.org`), usually one
  day earlier, and "today", "yesterday" and "last night" are resolved from that dateline. The PDFs are scanned images,
  so the datelines were read with OCR (RapidOCR) on 24 Oct; the OCR output is noisy and where it did not give the
  dateline of the item itself this is said in the entry and the day is not resolved from JTA alone. A day taken from
  JTA alone is [S]; where a weekday gives the day, the weekday was checked against the calendar (Python `datetime`).
- **Negative searches** are recorded in the "Searches that found nothing" section at the end, each with its exact query,
  date range and the day it was run.
- **Quotes** are copied from the cited text (its en dashes written as hyphens), short, and never put in a speaker's mouth for game text. Spelling inside
  quotes is the source's.
- Vote counts are checked where the source gives every component: in favour + against + abstaining + not voting =
  members, shown in each entry; where it does not, the missing part is said.
- Two lettered parts follow the design's two rows: A (8 August 1970 to 5 October 1973, with no decision scene) and B
  (6 October 1973 to 20 June 1977, with scenes 8, 9 and 10).

## A. After the ceasefire, 8 August 1970 to 5 October 1973

### A1. Nasser dies; Sadat takes over (28 September 1970)

- **Date(s).** 28 September 1970 [V, agent read 2026-10-24; a person's check pending] (FRUS). The JTA bulletin of 29 September carries the dateline WASHINGTON, Sept. 28 on the page's OCR.
- **Summary.** President Nasser of Egypt died of a heart attack at 52, seven weeks after the ceasefire of 7 August (forces-1967-73.md section 2). His Vice President, Anwar Sadat, took over as interim ruler.
- **Sources.**

  - JTA, "Nasser Dies of Heart Attack at 52; Death May Affect Middle East Situation", bulletin of 29 September 1970: https://www.jta.org/archive/nasser-dies-of-heart-attack-at-52-death-may-affect-middle-east-situation . ### A2. The Leningrad hijacking trial and the Soviet aliyah (June 1970 to 1973)

- **Date(s).**
  - 15 June 1970: the planned hijacking of a Soviet airliner at Leningrad's Smolny Airport, for which 11 people were tried [S] (JTA, "conspiring to hijack a Soviet airliner at Leningrad's Smolny Airport last June 15").
  - 24 December 1970 (a Thursday): sentences pronounced. The bulletin of 24 December is datelined LONDON, Dec. 23 on the OCR and says the verdict "is expected to be rendered tomorrow"; the bulletin of 30 December says "The Leningrad trial ended last Tuesday and sentences were pronounced on Thursday". Both agree on Thursday 24 December [S].
  - 30 December 1970 (a Wednesday): appeal hearing before the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation. The bulletin of 30 December is datelined NEW YORK, Dec. 29 on the OCR and says the hearing is "tomorrow", "ten a.m. Wednesday" [S].
- **Summary.** Eleven accused, nine of them Jews, were tried in Leningrad for conspiring to hijack an airliner to leave the USSR. Two were under sentence of death after the verdict; the appeal hearing was set within six days, "unexpected inasmuch as the normal time for setting an appeal date in the Soviet Union is six to eight weeks". The size of the Soviet Jewish emigration to Israel that followed (1.0 thousand in 1970, 13.0 in 1971, 31.4 in 1972, 33.3 in 1973) is in population-1957-77.md section 2a (see population-1957-77.md), not researched again.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Trial of Leningrad 11 Adjourns; Plea Entered for Reduced Sentences; Verdict Due", bulletin of 24 December 1970: https://www.jta.org/archive/trial-of-leningrad-11-adjourns-plea-entered-for-reduced-sentences-verdict-due . "The 11 accused, nine of whom are Jews, are charged with conspiring to hijack a Soviet airliner at Leningrad's Smolny Airport last June 15." [S]
  - JTA, "Appeal Hearings for Leningrad 11 Confirmed; Public Outcry Seen As Factor in Move", bulletin of 30 December 1970: https://www.jta.org/archive/appeal-hearings-for-leningrad-11-confirmed-public-outcry-seen-as-factor-in-move . "The 11 defendants, two of them under sentence of death, are to appear before the Supreme Court of the Russian Federation at ten a.m. Wednesday". [S]
- **Gaps.** [GAP] The appeal's result (whether the death sentences were commuted): the bulletin of 31 December ("No Decision on Leningrad Appeal; Court Recesses for Day", id 670786) was listed but not read, and the search for a commutation item found none (log). [GAP] The Soviet decision on exit visas of 1971 and its primary text.

### A4. The Black Panthers (March and May 1971)

- **Date(s).** The first reported demonstration and arrests: the bulletin of 4 March 1971 reports "Twelve self-styled 'Black Panthers' were arrested here yesterday" and a demonstration "this afternoon"; the item's own dateline was not legible in the OCR, and the neighbouring items of that bulletin are datelined 3 March, so the day is not resolved from JTA alone [S]. 18 May 1971 (a Tuesday): the "longest and most violent demonstration" in Jerusalem; the bulletin of 19 May is datelined JERUSALEM, May 18 on the OCR [S].
- **Summary.** A group of Jerusalem slum youths took the name of the American organisation and demonstrated against poverty. On 18 May police and demonstrators clashed in the city centre; the report counts 44 arrests including seven women and an unspecified number of injured. The title of JTA's report of 5 March calls the group "Miniscule" but says it reflects "Genuine Social Ills".
