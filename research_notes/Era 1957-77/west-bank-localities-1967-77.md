The question: which Israeli localities in the West Bank outside the Jordan Valley were founded between June 1967 and 31 December 1977, and for each: the founding date (and what event it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (Judea and Samaria, the file's own district name), whose local authority is neither "ערבות הירדן" (Arvot HaYarden) nor "מגילות ים המלח" (Megilot Dead Sea), with a year of founding of 1967 to 1977: **22 rows**. By the CBS year: 1967: 1, 1969: 1, 1970: 3, 1972: 2, 1975: 3, 1977: 12. By the CBS form code: 160: 1, 180: 2, 190: 7, 192: 1, 310: 1, 320: 1, 330: 3, 350: 1, 370: 5. Code 310, which the 2009 list prints "מושב (לרבות כפרי שיתופי)", is moshav, including kfar shitufi (a cooperative village; not the moshav shitufi of code 320). By local authority: Gush Etzion RC: 7, Mate Binyamin RC: 6, Shomron RC: 3, local council: 5, municipality: 1. The earliest CBS year is 1967 (Kefar Ezyon, the only row of that year). **Row 23, Mevo Shilo,** is added because the boundary sentence agreed on #130 (above) names it; it has no CBS row of its own and is a Gvati and Hebrew Wikipedia row, not part of the 22.
- **Dates.** 15 of the 22 rows carry a disagreement [C] on the year, the day or what the date marks (an attempt, a camp, an outpost, an inauguration, a move, a civil settling); 7 agree on the year across the sources read, with no source read disagreeing ([S]; for Elqana, Rehan and Shave Shomeron the two Wikipedia pages are not independent confirmations), and for those the day, where there is one, rests on one source or on a press item that agrees with Wikipedia. Row 23 is [C].
- **Contemporaneous press was read for 14 of the 22 rows and for Mevo Shilo not at all (the 9 Jun 1976 item cited under row 23 is on the Latrun area, not Mevo Shilo)** (Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) Daily News Bulletin, dateline rule applied on the PDF): Kefar Ezyon, Rosh Zurim, Allon Shevut, Qiryat Arba, El'azar, Ma'ale Adummim, Ma'ale Efrayim, Ofra (archive page only, item not found on the PDF), Kokhav HaShahar, Qedumim, Bet El, Neve Tsuf, Bet Horon and Migdal Oz, in 24 items (see "JTA items read"). **No government record, Knesset record or Official Gazette item was opened.** The Knesset site redirects this server to a maintenance page and the Israel Year Book is not open on the Internet Archive (see "What was not done"). The 1976 JTA items on Kochav Hashahar (11 May and 22 June) and the January to March 1978 items on Shiloh were not opened on PDF. Every founding date is [S] or [C]; the only verified items are what the CBS publications print.
- **A gap closed in a sibling note.** `events-1970-77.md` B14 has [GAP] "Kadum's founding day" and could not read the dateline of the bulletin of 9 December 1975. Read on the PDF it is "TEL AVIV, Dec. 8 (JTA)", and a JTA feature of 2 May 1976 says "On Dec. 8 ... The settlers were transferred to the Kadum military camp nearby" (Qedumim row). That is the day of the move to the camp, not a civil founding.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a CBS publication prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Gvati, Wikipedia and JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read; I did not open any Wikipedia citation. Wikipedia pages carry the revision (`oldid`) in the last table.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named. Derived values never carry the verified tag.
- Quotes are copied from the page read and were checked by script against the page text (Hebrew from Gvati without vowel signs and with its punctuation normalised for the check; Wikipedia as in the wikitext of the revision, reference tags and templates removed; JTA as on the archive page); translations from Hebrew are mine.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Territories", "administered areas", "West Bank", "legal status" and "illegal" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"),
  https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25 09:44 UTC, 370,654 bytes,
  SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet; the same hash as the copy the Jordan Valley note used). Filter: column "שם מחוז" (district name) = "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (154 rows), column "שם רשות מקומית" (local authority name) not "ערבות הירדן" and not "מגילות ים המלח", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1967 to 1977 (a script asserts that this returns exactly the 22 rows below). Rows of the district with no year (Avigayil, Giv'ot HaRo'e, Mishmar Yehuda, Asa'el, Shaharit and some statistical areas) are not in it. The district value is the CBS administrative label; the CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, opened today) says "קובץ היישובים כולל את היישובים הישראליים באזור יהודה ושומרון" (the localities file includes the Israeli localities in the Judea and Samaria area). The filter is that CBS category and nothing more.
- **What the form codes mean.** The 2024 file does not list the code meanings. I read the form printed for each symbol in the CBS publication "Local authorities in Israel 2009", list 03, settlements in regional councils (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, opened today), matching on the 4-digit locality symbol. **Derived** result: 16 of the 22 symbols are in that list (the regional-council rows); 6 are not (the local councils Ma'ale Efrayim, Qiryat Arba, Bet El, Elqana and Qedumim and the municipality Ma'ale Adummim): lists 01 and 02 of the same publication (opened today) print their names but no form. Code 190 prints as "יישוב קהילתי" (community settlement) for three symbols and as "יישוב יהודי" with a population band for two, so the 2009 label is not a function of the code; code 370 prints "יישוב קהילתי". The mapping is mine, read from that table; the CBS does not state it for the 2024 file.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine, not stated in the file. **Derived** check against English Wikipedia's own coordinates for the same 22 places (haversine, my computation): median difference 142 m, largest 1055 m (Ma'ale Adummim). No map source was opened; each coordinate is one CBS point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Mevo Horon 1974, Ma'ale Adummim, Rimmonim 1980, Neve Tsuf, Kokhav HaShahar) the point is the present locality.
- **Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, appendix "List of all Jewish rural settlements in Israel" and chapter 2, sections on the Jordan Valley, Gush Etzion and the territories, Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (downloaded and opened by me 2026-10-25 10:06 UTC, 1,348,249 bytes; every Gvati quote and the five appendix rows used were checked by script against that download). The appendix lists rural settlements only, so most rows have none. It is one secondary compilation and does not say what its date marks.
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch (about 09:35 to 10:00 UTC); each revision is named in the last table. It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S].
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches: the archive's wp-json endpoint `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive?search=<term>&after=<from>T00:00:00&before=<to>T23:59:59&per_page=100&page=<n>`, the window 1967-06-01 to 1978-12-31 unless a row of the Searches table says otherwise; pages up to 5 were read (500 items), so for the few terms with more results the later ones were not. Every result's text was searched by script for the exact string (case-insensitive substring). For every item used I opened the archive page, took the "See Original Daily Bulletin" PDF link (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read the PDF as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR in two column crops (the OCR drops spaces and mangles some words), and took the dateline from it. **Dates rule:** the archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF ("TEL AVIV, Oct. 10 (JTA)") and from "yesterday", "today" and "last night" read against it. Every dateline below except two was matched by script against the OCR of the PDF: J14 has none (the item is not on the PDF) and J16 prints a place but no date.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`. Hebrew dates were converted with the `convertdate` library (Python; Nisan = month 1); each conversion is asserted in the generating script.

## Rows

Each row: the CBS facts are in the table; here Gvati, Hebrew and English Wikipedia, JTA where read, and what the disagreement is. J-numbers refer to the JTA table below. Rows are in the same order as the summary table.

1. Kefar Ezyon (kibbutz, CBS 1967) [C]. Gvati appendix: 25.9.67, "הפועל המזרחי" (Hapoel Hamizrahi). Gvati narrative: "בכ״ה באלול תשכ״ז (27.9.1967) עלו עשרת החברים הראשונים והשתכנו במחנה הלגיון הערבי שהוקם על הריסות כפר עציון" (on 25 Elul 5727 (27.9.1967) the first ten members went up and settled in the Arab Legion camp that was built on the ruins of Kefar Etzion). **Derived** (`convertdate`): 25 Elul 5727 = 30 September 1967, so Gvati's Hebrew date and his civil date disagree with each other; 22 Elul 5727 = 27 September 1967. Hebrew WP: "ב-27 בספטמבר 1967 (כ"ב באלול ה'תשכ"ז) הוקם הקיבוץ מחדש על מקומו של קיבוץ כפר עציון החרב" (on 27 September 1967 (22 Elul 5727) the kibbutz was re-established on the site of the destroyed Kefar Etzion). English WP: "It was re-established again in 1967 after the Six-Day War". JTA J1 (bulletin of 26 September 1967), dateline "JERUSALEM, Sept. 25 (JTA)": "The reconstitution of four settlements in the so-called Etzion Bloc on the west bank of the Jordan River, established there before the war of 1948 and now again in Israel’s hands, will be entrusted to Nahal, the Cabinet here has decided". JTA J2 (bulletin of 28 September 1967), dateline "JERUSALEM, Sept. 27 (JTA)", read on the PDF: "An advance party of young settlers moved this morning into the former Jordanian Arab Legion camp on the site of Kfar Etzion and formally reestablished the Jewish settlement there"; the dateline makes "this morning" 27 September 1967. **Disagreement:** the Gvati appendix day, 25 September, against 27 September in JTA, Hebrew WP and Gvati's own narrative. The date marks the first group moving into the former Arab Legion camp on the site. The other Gush Etzion rows (Rosh Zurim, Allon Shevut, Har Gillo, El'azar, Migdal Oz, Teqoa) are below.

2. Rosh Zurim (kibbutz, CBS 1969) [C]. Gvati appendix: 15.9.69, "הפועל המזרחי". Gvati narrative: "שנתיים לאחר מכן עלה עוד גרעין של ״בני עקיבא״ והקים את קיבוץ, ראש־צורים" (two years later another Bnei Akiva gar'in went up and set up the kibbutz Rosh Zurim; "later" than 27.9.1967). Hebrew WP: "טקס העלייה לקרקע התקיים בט"ו באב ה'תשכ"ט, 31 ביולי 1969, אך העלייה הממשית למקום התבצעה בג' בתשרי תש"ל" (the ceremony of going up to the land took place on 15 Av 5729, 31 July 1969, but the actual going up to the place took place on 3 Tishrei 5730). **Derived** (`convertdate`): 15 Av 5729 = 30 July 1969, so Hebrew WP's Hebrew and civil dates differ by one day; 3 Tishrei 5730 = 15 September 1969, which is Gvati's appendix day. Hebrew WP also: "קיבוץ ראש צורים נוסד בשנת 1969 על חורבות קיבוץ עין צורים". English WP: "The settlement was established in 1969 by members of Bnei Akiva Religious Scouts and Nahal soldiers". JTA J4 (bulletin of 31 July 1969), dateline "TEL AVIV, July 30 (JTA)": "A settlement was inaugurated today on the west bank at Gush Etzion", so 30 July 1969; the item names "Rosh Tzurim, a commanding hill in the center of the Gush Etzion area". JTA J5 (bulletin of 15 October 1969), dateline "JERUSALEM, Oct. 14 (JTA)": "The settlements of Kfar Etzion and Rosh Tsurim, captured by the Jordanian Arab Legion in 1948, have already been revived". **Disagreement:** the inauguration is 30 July (JTA, and the Hebrew date of Hebrew WP) or 31 July (Hebrew WP's civil date); the actual settling is 15 September (Gvati, Hebrew WP). The CBS year 1969 fits both.

3. Allon Shevut (CBS 1970, form code 190; the 2009 list prints "יישוב יהודי, 2,000-4,999 תושבים", Jewish locality of 2,000 to 4,999 residents) [C]. Gvati has no appendix row for it (the appendix lists rural settlements). Gvati narrative: "כמו כן הוקם ב־1969 מרכז אזורי: אלון שבות" (also set up in 1969 a regional centre: Allon Shevut). Hebrew WP: "היישוב הוקם בשנת 1970 ביוזמת משה (מושקו) מושקוביץ" and "אלון שבות עלתה על הקרקע בכ' סיוון ה'תש"ל (1970)"; **derived** (`convertdate`): 20 Sivan 5730 = 24 June 1970. Hebrew WP also says "בנייתו החלה ב-1968" (its construction began in 1968). English WP: "The first settlers moved in on 25 June 1970, and the official founding ceremony was held on 5 July". The item does not name the village; the match to Allon Shevut rests on Gvati's "1969" and "regional centre", and is mine, not the item's. 4. Ma'ale Efrayim (CBS 1970, form code 350, own local council "מעלה אפרים"; the 2009 lists do not print a form for it) [C]. Not in the Jordan Valley note (#1658), which lists it as outside its filter because the CBS authority is not Arvot HaYarden; the CBS subdistrict is Jordan-Jericho.
