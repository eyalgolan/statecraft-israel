# Israeli localities founded on the Golan Heights from June 1967 to 1977 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file (#61)
stays paused until after the MVP. Refs #130 (1957-77 18), the "Settlements" row of [the 1957-1977
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1957-1977-design.md) section 7.3: "settlements in the territories".
One topic of #130 only: the localities founded on the Golan Heights. **The West Bank, Gaza and Sinai, the localities inside
the pre-1967 lines (PRs #1643, #1646, #1647) and the lines are not in this note.** Which rows enter the game, and their
names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#118); this note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no
statement on the status of any place beyond what a source says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and of the sibling notes in this
folder. Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which localities were founded in the Golan subdistrict between June 1967 and 31 December 1977, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024 has 43 rows in its Golan subdistrict. 33 carry a year of founding; none is earlier than 1967 and 11 are later than 1977. **22 carry a year of 1967 to 1977** (the CBS has no month, so every 1967 row is read as June 1967 or later): 7 kibbutzim, 8 moshavim, 4 moshavim shitufiyim, 2 community settlements and 1 urban centre (Qazrin).
- **Membership rests on the CBS year.** Three more rows have CBS year 1978 but sources read give 1974, 1975 or 1977 for their first settling (Ani'am, Avne Etan, Ortal); they are listed in "Rows at the edge of the window", not in the table.
- **17 of the 22 rows carry a disagreement [C]** on the year or on what the date marks (an outpost, a first camp, the present site, the civil settlement); 5 rows agree on the year across the sources read, and for those the day, where there is one, rests on Gvati or on one JTA item.
- **Contemporaneous press was read for 9 of the 22 rows** (Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) Daily News Bulletin, dateline rule applied where the item was found in the bulletin PDF). No government record, Knesset record or Official Gazette item was opened. Every date below is [S] or [C]; the only verified items are what the CBS file, the CBS intro and the CBS 2009 list print. That is the main [GAP] of the note, listed in "What was not done".

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a CBS publication prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Gvati, Wikipedia and JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read; I did not open any Wikipedia citation.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named.
- Quotes are copied from the page read (each was checked by script against the page text); Hebrew quotes from Gvati are given without vowel signs, and where I put a translation of Hebrew in brackets or mark "my translation", it is mine. Translations are mine.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"),
  https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25, 370,654 bytes,
  SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet). Filter: column "סמל נפה" (subdistrict code) = 29, "שם נפה" = "גולן", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1967 to 1977.
  Result: 43 Golan-subdistrict rows, 33 with a year, 22 in the window. The 10 rows without a year are 5 Arab and Druze local councils (CBS prints no year for them; forms 270, 280, 290), 4 regional-council area rows (form 530) and Nimrod (4035, form 510; no religion or population printed), which is neither a village nor a council row. The CBS year is its "year of Jewish settlement or founding"; its intro, section 12, says the year of founding or of settling by Jews is recorded, the last settling year after a long break, the original year after a short one.
- **The subdistrict.** The CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, opened today), section 4: "עד 1981 היו רק 14 נפות, מאז נוספה גם נפת גולן במחוז הצפון" (until 1981 there were only 14 subdistricts; since then the Golan subdistrict has also been added in the Northern district). The filter is that CBS administrative category and nothing more. Section 6 of the same intro says: "אין בקביעה זו כדי להשליך על מעמדו של יישוב על פי דין" (this determination does not imply anything about the legal status of a locality); I take over that sentence as the note's own rule.
- **What the form codes mean.** The 2024 file does not list the code meanings. I read the form printed for each row in the CBS publication "Local authorities in Israel 2009", list 03, settlements in regional councils (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, opened today), matching on the 4-digit locality symbol by word position in the PDF. **Derived** result for the 21 regional-council rows: 330 = kibbutz (7 of 7), 310 = moshav, printed in the 2009 list as "מושב (לרבות כפרי שיתופי)" (moshav, including kfar shitufi, a cooperative village; not the moshav shitufi of code 320; 8 of 8), 320 = moshav shitufi (4 of 4), 370 = community settlement (2 of 2). Qazrin is a local council and not in that list; its code 180 is printed in the file and I do not decode it beyond "urban"; the intro (section 6) splits urban localities by population size. The mapping is mine, read from that table; the CBS does not state it for the 2024 file.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine, not stated in the file (the intro's section 14 describes a different format, ten digits in hundreds of metres, which the 12-digit field does not match). **Derived** check against Wikipedia's own coordinates (haversine, my computation): Hebrew Wikipedia has a coordinate for 22 of 22 rows (median 13 m, largest 169 m, Mevo Hamma); English Wikipedia for 22 (median 85 m, largest 394 m, Qazrin). No map source was opened; the coordinates are a CBS point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Merom Golan, Ramat Magshimim, Yonatan and others) the first site is elsewhere.
- **Founding days: Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, appendix "List of all Jewish rural settlements in Israel", Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (opened 2026-10-25 08:01 UTC, 1,348,249 bytes; 807 appendix rows parsed by script from the page). Columns: name, region, date of founding, affiliation, origin of the name. It is one secondary compilation; it does not say whether its date is the day of going onto the land. Gvati was Minister of Agriculture (JTA, 11 May 1972, J1). Its misprints are visible (below, Ramot "5.5.67", and OCR-like spellings: "אלי על" for Eli-Ad, "אדם" for Odem, "זיון" for En Ziwan). Bene Yehuda, Haspin and Qazrin are not in the rural appendix; for them I cite the chapter "ההתיישבות מעבר ל״קו הירוק״", section "רמת-הגולן", which dates the settlements by year.
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S].
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches: the archive's wp-json endpoint `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive?search=<term>&after=1967-06-01T00:00:00&before=1978-12-31T23:59:59&per_page=100` (page 1 only) and the site search `https://www.jta.org/?s=<term>&post_type=archive` (result pages 1 and 2); every result dated June 1967 to 1978 was opened and searched for the exact string. Every query and count is in "Searches". For every item used I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read it as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR (the OCR drops spaces and mangles some words), and took the dateline from it. **Dates rule:** the JTA archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF ("TEL AVIV, Aug. 30 (JTA)") and from "yesterday", "today" and "last week" read against that dateline. Quotes in the rows are copied from the archive page text (the web transcription), which was checked by script to contain each quoted string. Where the dateline was not found, the row says so.
- **Internet Archive.** The Israel Government Year-Book and the Israel Year Book were looked for as a primary list of localities; see "What was not done" for the exact queries and the HTTP 401 on the full text.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`. Hebrew dates were converted with the `convertdate` library (Python).

## Rows

Each row: the CBS facts, Gvati, Hebrew and English Wikipedia, JTA where read, and what the disagreement is. J-numbers refer to the JTA table below.

1. Afiq (kibbutz, CBS 1967) [C]. Flag: the CBS year follows an earlier outpost, not the present kibbutz. Gvati appendix: 28.12.67, Ihud. Gvati narrative: three settlements went up in 1967, one of them "נח״ל-גולן", and "ב-1972 הקים נח״ל גולן, יחד עם חברי גרעינים אחרים, את קיבוץ אפיק" (in 1972 Nahal Golan, with members of other gar'inim, set up kibbutz Afiq). Hebrew WP: a Nahal outpost "נח״ל גולן" went up in 1967 near the abandoned village Jibin; civilianised in May 1972 and the permanent settlement was built at the site of the Syrian village Fiq. English WP: "was established near the site of the abandoned Syrian village of Fiq on 8 May 1972"; its infobox note says the Nahal outpost of 1967 stood about 4 km to the north-east and that the Israeli records still list the place as 1967. JTA, bulletin of 11 May 1972 (J1): "Nahal Golan, for four years a para-military outpost in the Golan Heights, officially became a civilian settlement and took the new name of Kibbutz Afik." That paragraph sits in a roundup and has no dateline of its own, so the day is not resolved from the bulletin. 1967 outpost (CBS, Gvati, both WP) against 1972 civil kibbutz (JTA, English WP, Hebrew WP); 28 December 1967 (Gvati) against no day in the others.

2. Merom Golan (kibbutz, CBS 1967) [C]. Gvati appendix: 15.7.67, Kibbutz Meuhad. Gvati narrative: "הם עלו למקום ב-15 ביולי 1967, חודשיים אחרי כיבוש הרמה" (they went up on 15 July 1967, two months after the capture of the Heights); the group first sat in an abandoned camp at Aleika and moved in 1972 to the permanent site. Hebrew WP: "הקיבוץ הוקם ב-14 ביולי 1967" (founded 14 July 1967), the first Israeli settlement on the Heights after the war; first named "Golan", renamed Merom Golan in November 1968; moved to the present site in March 1972. English WP: "Merom Golan was established on 14 July 1967". JTA, dateline "TEL AVIV, Aug. 17 (JTA)" in the bulletin of 18 August 1977 (J9): "Merom Hagolan, the first Israeli settlement on the Golan Heights after the Six-Day War, is marking its 10th anniversary this month." 14 July (WP) against 15 July (Gvati); JTA, in August 1977, dates the celebration of the 10th anniversary, not the founding, so it gives no August founding date; the first site and the present site (1972) are different places.

3. Ne'ot Golan (CBS form 310, moshav; Gvati: moshav shitufi) [C]. CBS 1967; every other source gives 1968. Gvati appendix: 10.9.68, HaOved HaZioni; Gvati narrative lists it among the moshavim that went up in 1968. Hebrew WP: a group from Hadera went up "ובספטמבר 1968 עלו להתיישבות בצומת פיק" (in September 1968, to the Fiq junction), later moved to the present site in 1973. English WP: "The moshav was built in 1968". The CBS year is not explained by any source read.

4. Eli-Ad (moshav, CBS 1968) [C]. The Gvati appendix row is spelled "אלי על" (Eli-Al): 21.1.68. Gvati narrative: "ב-1973 הוקם מושב אלי-על לזכרו של אלי כהן שהועלה לגרדום בסוריה. לפני כן, ב-1968 נוסד במקום זה מושב שיתופי בתוך הכפר הסורי אל-על ונקרא בשם זה" (in 1973 the moshav Eli-Al was founded in memory of Eli Cohen ... before that, in 1968, a moshav shitufi was founded at this place inside the Syrian village al-Al and was called that). Hebrew WP: "המקום יושב בפברואר 1968" (the place was settled in February 1968) in the buildings of an abandoned Syrian camp; the moshav was dissolved at the end of 1969; a Nahal outpost "נח״ל על" went up in early June 1970; a Nahal gar'in Eli-Ad went up in May 1973; settlers from Eliaflet came in July 1973 and again in July 1974. English WP: founded 1968 as a Nahal settlement, a moshav two years later. February 1968 (Hebrew WP), 21 January 1968 (Gvati), 1973 for the present name (Gvati).

5. En Ziwan (kibbutz, CBS 1968) [S]. Gvati appendix: 23.1.68, Kibbutz Meuhad; the narrative puts it in 1968 in the north of the Heights. Hebrew WP: "הגרעין הראשון של הקיבוץ שהיה מורכב מבני קיבוצים וכן מתנדבים מחו"ל התיישב ב־23 בינואר 1968 בבתיו הנטושים של הכפר הצ'רקסי" (the first gar'in settled on 23 January 1968 in the abandoned houses of the Circassian village). English WP: "The kibbutz was founded in 1968". Gvati and Hebrew WP agree on 23 January 1968; the two may share one source, so I count the day once.

6. Giv'at Yo'av (moshav, CBS 1968) [S]. Gvati appendix: 13.3.68, Tnuat HaMoshavim; narrative: 1968. Hebrew WP: "היישוב הוקם בשנת 1968" (founded in 1968). English WP infobox 1968; its text says "The settlement was built in the 1960s". The year agrees; the day rests on Gvati alone.

7. Mevo Hamma (kibbutz, CBS 1968) [C]. Gvati appendix: 22.1.68, Ihud; narrative: first at El-Hamma by the hot springs, after a short time went up to the Heights (my translation). Hebrew WP: in January 1968 eight kibbutz members "התאספו ... והחליטו להתיישב" (gathered and decided to settle) at the site of a Syrian post; the kibbutz was first built at the El-Hamma railway station. English WP: "In January 1968, several members of the surrounding kibbutzim gathered and decided to establish a new settlement in the area and in September had settled the ruins of the base". January 1968 (Gvati; WP for the decision) against September 1968 (English WP for settling the base); JTA: three items mention the name (shelling, an attack, a road), none its founding.

8. Ramat Magshimim (moshav shitufi, CBS 1968) [C]. Gvati appendix: 1.7.68, Hapoel Hamizrahi. Hebrew WP: "רמת מגשימים עלה על הקרקע באוגוסט 1968" (went onto the land in August 1968) as a Nahal outpost of Bnei Akiva in abandoned houses of the village Fiq; after eight months it moved to an abandoned camp next to the present site, and in summer 1972 to the present site. English WP: "The community was established in 1968". 1 July (Gvati) against August (Hebrew WP).

9. Ramot (moshav, CBS 1970) [C]. Gvati appendix: 5.5.67, a date earlier than the June 1967 war and so not a settling date on the Heights; I cannot tell what was meant. Gvati narrative: "ב-1969 עלה רק מושב אחד, רמות של תנועת המושבים" (in 1969 only one moshav went up, Ramot) and at first it lived in the village Sqoufiya. Hebrew WP: "המושב הוקם בשנת 1969" (founded 1969), first in the abandoned houses of Sqoufiya, a temporary site two years later, the present site in 1973. English WP: "The moshav was founded in 1969". CBS 1970 against 1969 against the printed 5.5.67.

10. El-Rom (kibbutz, CBS 1971) [S]. Gvati appendix: 15.7.71, Kibbutz Meuhad (its note: the highest settlement in the country, my translation); narrative: 1971 in the north. Hebrew WP: "הקיבוץ נוסד ביולי 1971" (founded July 1971) by the Machanot Haolim gar'in. English WP: "The kibbutz was founded in 1971". Year and month agree; the day rests on Gvati alone.

11. Geshur (kibbutz, CBS 1971) [C]. Flag: the CBS year sits between an outpost of 1968 and a civil kibbutz of 1975-76. Gvati appendix: 8.4.68, Kibbutz Artzi; narrative: it began as an outpost and built its permanent settlement later in the south of the Heights; a Geshur B was still being set up in 1977. Hebrew WP: the Nahal outpost was established "ב-8 באפריל 1968" (8 April 1968); civilianising was decided in March 1973; the Kibbutz Artzi council approved settling at Geshur on 30 June 1974; "הגרעין של מקימי היישוב האזרחי גשור התיישב במחנה פיק ב־15 באפריל 1975, ועבר למקומו הנוכחי ב־22 בנובמבר 1976" (the civil nucleus settled in the Fiq camp on 15 April 1975 and moved to its present site on 22 November 1976). English WP: founded 1971 by Hashomer Hatzair. JTA, bulletin of 3 July 1974 "The Kibbutz Haartzi Council voted 200-100 last night to establish a kibbutz on the Golan Heights" (J5; dateline "TEL AVIV, July 2 (JTA)", so "last night" is the night of 1 July 1974, derived; Hebrew WP says the council approved on 30 June 1974). JTA, dateline "TEL AVIV, Nov. 25 (JTA)" in the bulletin of 26 November 1976 (J8): "The first Mapam kibbutz on the Golan Heights, the 25th settlement on the Heights, was inaugurated Tuesday"; 25 November 1976 was a Thursday, so Tuesday is 23 November 1976 (derived, weekday by Python `datetime`); JTA spells the name "G Shor". 8 April 1968, 1971, 15 April 1975, 22 or 23 November 1976: what each marks differs, and this note does not choose.

12. Bene Yehuda (community settlement, CBS 1972) [S]. Flag: form 370, a regional centre, not a kibbutz or moshav. A Jewish moshava of the same name stood nearby from 1891 to 1920 (Hebrew and English WP); the CBS year is the 1972 settling, not the earlier one. Gvati narrative: "ב-1972 הוקמו שני מרכזים אזוריים: בני-יהודה" (in 1972 two regional centres were set up: Bene Yehuda), named after the settlement abandoned in 1920. Hebrew WP: first houses occupied in August 1972, the first residents were Israel Aircraft Industries workers, the plant opened in November 1972. English WP: "Modern Bnei Yehuda was founded in 1972". JTA, dateline "TEL AVIV, Aug. 30 (JTA)" in the bulletin of 31 August 1972 (J2): "A new urban and manufacturing center, Bnei Yehuda, was officially established yesterday in the southern Golan Heights", which gives 29 August 1972 (derived: dateline minus one day), and "Bnai Yehuda is the 17th Israeli settlement established on the Golan Heights since the region was captured from Syria in the Six-Day War." Year, and August, agree; the day rests on JTA alone.

13. Newe Ativ (moshav, CBS 1972) [C]. Gvati appendix: 23.4.69, HaOved HaZioni, listed among the moshavim shitufiyim (the CBS code is 310). Gvati narrative: "ב-1971 הוקמו שני ישובים בצפון הגולן" (in 1971 two settlements were set up in the north of the Heights), Newe Ativ one of them, and "עוד ב-1969 הוקם בסביבה זו ישוב בשם רמת-שלום" (already in 1969 a settlement called Ramat Shalom was set up near it), which broke up, and in its place Newe Ativ was set up (my translation). Hebrew WP: "היישוב עלה לקרקע במיקומו הנוכחי ב-20 בפברואר 1972" (went onto the land at its present site on 20 February 1972); an earlier Ramat Shalom group tried in 1968-69. English WP: "The Israeli settlement was founded in 1972". 23 April 1969, 1971 and 20 February 1972.

14. Haspin (community settlement, CBS 1973) [C]. Flag: form 370, a regional centre. Gvati narrative: "ב-1972 הוקמו שני מרכזים אזוריים: בני-יהודה" and then "המרכז האזורי השני, חספין" (the second regional centre, Haspin). Hebrew WP (article "חיספין"): in 1973 an industrial Nahal gar'in settled at the Fiq junction and was called "כפר גנ"ת"; the permanent settlement was built later at "Merkaz Hispit" and renamed Haspin. English WP: "The modern Haspin was established in 1978". 1972 (Gvati), 1973 (CBS, Hebrew WP), 1978 (English WP). JTA: none read for this name.
