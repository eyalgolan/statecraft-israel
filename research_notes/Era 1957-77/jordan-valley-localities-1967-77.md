Dead Sea regional councils, Nahal outposts included). **The rest of the West Bank, Gaza, Sinai and the Golan, the

folder. Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which Israeli localities, kibbutz, moshav or Nahal outpost, were founded in the Jordan Valley between June 1967 and 31 December 1977, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (Judea and Samaria, the file's own district name), whose local authority is "ערבות הירדן" (Arvot HaYarden) or "מגילות ים המלח" (Megilot Dead Sea), with a year of founding of 1967 to 1977: **16 rows** (10 moshavim, 1 moshav shitufi, 5 kibbutzim; 13 in Arvot HaYarden and 3 in Megilot). The earliest CBS year is 1968, so no row depends on the month of 1967. All 16 are in the subdistrict "ירדן-יריחו" (Jordan-Jericho).
- **The council name.** The task named "Bik'at HaYarden". The 2024 file and the 2009 CBS list print "ערבות הירדן" (Arvot HaYarden). English Wikipedia's article "Bik'at HaYarden Regional Council" (oldid 1379722839) says "also Aravot HaYarden" (lit. Jordan Plains), and Hebrew Wikipedia (oldid 44001418) says that Arvot HaYarden is its name "במסמכים רשמיים של הממשלה" while it is called "מועצה אזורית בקעת הירדן" "במסמכיה של המועצה האזורית עצמה". I took the CBS spelling and treat them as one council. Both councils were set up after the window: Hebrew WP says of Arvot HaYarden "המועצה האזורית הוקמה בתחילת 1980" (the regional council was set up in early 1980) and of the Dead Sea council "למרות הקמת המועצה האזורית ב־1979" (despite the setting up of the regional council in 1979); so the council field is the 2024 membership, not a 1967-77 fact.
- **Gvati's own count.** Gvati's chapter on the Jordan Valley says "בסך הכל הוקמו בבקעת הירדן ובמורדות הרי אפרים ויהודה 21 ישובים" (in all, 21 settlements were set up in the Jordan Valley and on the slopes of the Ephraim and Judea hills), among them, as he lists them, 6 kibbutzim, 10 moshavim and moshavim shitufiyim, 4 Nahal outposts and one regional centre. **Derived (one possible assignment: Gvati names the regional centre, "ומרכז אזורי אחד: מעלה אפרים" (and one regional centre: Ma'ale Efrayim), but does not list which places are the 6 kibbutzim, the 10 moshavim and moshavim shitufiyim and the 4 outposts):** counting the places its narrative names gives 21 (Mehola, Qalya, Argaman, Ma'ale Efrayim, Gilgal, Massu'a, Pe'za'el, Mizpe Shalem, Yitav under its outpost name Na'aran, Hamra, Beqa'ot, Gittit, Mekhora, Netiv HaGedud, Kokhav HaShahar, Mevo Shilo, Ro'i, the kibbutz Na'aran, Almog, Rimmonim, Tomer), of which 16 are the rows of this note and five are outside the CBS filter (Ma'ale Efrayim, Tomer, Kokhav HaShahar, Mevo Shilo, Rimmonim; section "Rows outside the filter"). The split by type also matches: kibbutzim Qalya, Gilgal, Mizpe Shalem, Yitav, Na'aran, Almog (6), moshavim Mehola, Argaman, Massu'a, Pe'za'el, Hamra, Beqa'ot, Gittit, Mekhora, Netiv HaGedud, Tomer (10), outposts Kokhav HaShahar, Mevo Shilo, Ro'i, Rimmonim (4), regional centre Ma'ale Efrayim (1). The split matches only if Yitav is counted with the kibbutzim (its Gvati appendix row is of the Kibbutz Meuhad); the CBS file and the 2009 list print a moshav shitufi.
- **Dates.** 13 of the 16 rows carry a disagreement [C] on the year, the day or what the date marks (an outpost, an inauguration, a move, the civil settling); 3 agree on the year across the sources read (Hamra, Beqa'ot, Almog), and for those the day, where there is one, rests on one source or two that may share a source.
- **Contemporaneous press was read for 9 of the 16 rows** (Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) Daily News Bulletin, dateline rule applied in the PDF): Argaman, Mehola, Qalya, Gittit, Mekhora, Mizpe Shalem, Na'aran, Almog, Peza'el (as "Patzael", existing by 13 May 1975); Yitav only through a name that Gvati, not JTA, ties to it. **No government record, Knesset record or Official Gazette item was opened.** The Knesset site redirects this server to a maintenance page and the Israel Government Year Book is not on the Internet Archive (see "What was not done"). Every founding date is [S] or [C]; the only verified items are what the CBS publications print.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a CBS publication prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Gvati, Wikipedia and JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read; I did not open any Wikipedia citation.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named.
- Quotes are copied from the page read and were checked by script against the page text (Hebrew from Gvati without vowel signs; Hebrew Wikipedia as in the wikitext of the revision named in the last table); translations from Hebrew are mine.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Territories", "administered areas" and "West Bank" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"),
  https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25 09:03 UTC, 370,654 bytes,
  SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet). Filter: column "שם מחוז" (district name) = "אזור יהודה ושומרון", column "שם רשות מקומית" (local authority name) = "ערבות הירדן" or "מגילות ים המלח", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1967 to 1977 (a script asserts that this returns exactly the 16 rows below). The district value is the CBS administrative label; the CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, opened today) says in its general section: "קובץ היישובים כולל את היישובים הישראליים באזור יהודה ושומרון" (the localities file includes the Israeli localities in the Judea and Samaria area). The filter is that CBS category and nothing more. The intro, section 12, says the year recorded is the year of founding of the locality or of its settling by Jews, by the circumstances of the case. The file's own authority name for the Dead Sea council is "מגילות ים המלח"; no row in the 1967-1977 window has any other authority name in the subdistrict except Ma'ale Efrayim (its own local council, 1970), which the filter leaves out and which is listed in "Rows outside the filter".
- **What the form codes mean.** The 2024 file does not list the code meanings. I read the form printed for each of the 16 symbols in the CBS publication "Local authorities in Israel 2009", list 03, settlements in regional councils (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, opened today), matching on the 4-digit locality symbol. **Derived** result: 16 of 16 symbols are in that list; 310 = "מושב (לרבות כפרי שיתופי)" (moshav, including kfar shitufi (a cooperative village; not the moshav shitufi of code 320)) for 10 of 10, 320 = "מושב שיתופי" (moshav shitufi) for Yitav, 330 = "קיבוץ" (kibbutz) for 5 of 5. The 2009 list prints symbol 3620 as "נירן" (Niran), where the 2024 file has "נערן" (Na'aran). The mapping is mine, read from that table; the CBS does not state it for the 2024 file.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine, not stated in the file (the intro's section 14 describes a different format, ten digits in hundreds of metres, which the 12-digit field does not match). **Derived** check against English Wikipedia's own coordinates (haversine, my computation): English Wikipedia gives a coordinate for 16 of the 16 rows (Ro'i's is 32°14'50"N 35°29'20"E, 54 m from the CBS point); median difference 84 m, largest 233 m (Gilgal). Hebrew Wikipedia's coordinates were not extracted. No map source was opened; each coordinate is one CBS point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Peza'el, Netiv HaGedud, Mizpe Shalem, Yitav, Gittit) the first site is elsewhere.
- **Founding days: Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, appendix "List of all Jewish rural settlements in Israel" and chapter 2, section "בקעת הירדן", Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (opened 2026-10-25 09:03 UTC, 1,348,249 bytes). Appendix columns: name, region, date of founding, affiliation, origin of the name. It is one secondary compilation; it does not say whether its date is the day of going onto the land or of something else. Its appendix and narrative disagree for at least Gittit (24.8.72 against 1973) and Peza'el (3.75 against 1970); I give both. On the plan behind these settlements, the chapter says of what it calls the "Allon plan": "תכנית זו, אמנם לא אושרה רשמית על־ידי הממשלה, אולם בפועל הינחתה את פעולות ההתיישבות" (this plan was not officially approved by the Government, but in practice guided the settlement actions), and "רוב הישובים הוקמו כהיאחזויות נח׳׳ל, עד שנוצרו התנאים לאזרוחם על־ידי גרעיני התיישבות קבועים" (most of the settlements were set up as Nahal outposts until the conditions arose for civilianising them by permanent settling gar'inim). Those are Gvati's statements, tagged [S]; this note takes no position on them.
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S].
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches: the archive's wp-json endpoint `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive?search=<term>&after=<from>T00:00:00&before=<to>T23:59:59&per_page=100&page=<n>` (every page up to 5 was read; for terms with more results the later pages were not), the window 1967-06-01 to 1978-12-31 unless a row says otherwise. Every result's text was searched by script for the exact string. Every query and count is in "Searches". For every item used I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read it as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR (the OCR drops spaces and mangles some words), and took the dateline from it. **Dates rule:** the JTA archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF ("TEL AVIV, Oct. 10 (JTA)") and from "yesterday", "today" and "last night" read against that dateline. Quotes in the rows are copied from the archive page text (the web transcription), which a script checked to contain each quoted string; where the transcription and the OCR differ I say so. Where the dateline was not found, the row says so.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`. Hebrew dates were converted with the `convertdate` library (Python), month numbers as that library numbers them.

## Rows

Each row: the CBS facts, Gvati, Hebrew and English Wikipedia, JTA where read, and what the disagreement is. J-numbers refer to the JTA table below. Rows are in the same order as the summary table.

1. Argaman (moshav, CBS 1968) [C]. Flag: the CBS year follows the Nahal outpost; the civil moshav is later. Gvati appendix: 20.11.68, "מושבי ״חירות״" (the Herut moshavim), name from the initials of two IDF officers who fell in the area. Gvati narrative: "הישובים הראשונים עלו לבקעה ב־1968" (the first settlements went up to the Valley in 1968), and "היאחזות נח״ל ארגמן עלתה בצפונה של הבקעה על אדמת נפקדים" (the Nahal outpost Argaman went up in the north of the Valley on absentees' land). Hebrew WP: "בשנת 1968 עלה כח חלוץ למקום להקים היאחזות נח"ל מטעם תנועת המושבים"; "באמצע 1969 נחנכה ההיאחזות באופן רשמי" (the outpost was officially inaugurated in mid-1969); later passed to the Herut-Betar movement and "אוזרח ב-18 במאי 1971" (civilianised on 18 May 1971). English WP: "Initially established in 1968 as a Nahal settlement by the Betar movement, Argaman was converted to a civilian moshav in May 1971". JTA, bulletin of 11 October 1968, no. 194 (J2), dateline "TEL AVIV, Oct. 10 (JTA)": "A new Nahal settlement soon to be established in the Jordan Valley will be called Argaman in memory of Lt. Col. Aryeh Regev and a Capt. Manellah", so on 10 October 1968 the outpost was still to come. JTA, bulletin of 8 May 1969, no. 88 (J3), dateline "TEL AVIV, May 7 (JTA)": "The third Jordan Valley outpost manned by settlers serving in the Israeli armed forces was inaugurated yesterday", which gives 6 May 1969 (derived: dateline minus one day); the same item places it "on the Beisan-Jericho road east of Nablus". **Disagreement:** 20 November 1968 (Gvati, no event named) against an outpost not yet established on 10 October 1968 and inaugurated on 6 May 1969 (JTA), mid-1969 (Hebrew WP), and the civil settling of May 1971 (both WP). The CBS year 1968 agrees with the Gvati, WP and JTA year of the first plans or outpost; the note does not choose.

2. Mehola (moshav, CBS 1968) [C]. Gvati appendix: 6.2.68, "הפועל המזרחי" (Hapoel Hamizrahi). Gvati narrative: "בצפון הבקעה הוקם המושב השיתופי מחולה של ״הפועל המזרחי״" (in the north of the Valley the moshav shitufi Mehola of Hapoel Hamizrahi was set up), listed with the settlements of 1968. Hebrew WP (article Mehola): "היישוב הוקם כהיאחזות נח"ל ב-6 בפברואר 1968, ז' בשבט תשכ"ח, ואוזרח ב-13 בנובמבר 1969" (civilianised 13 November 1969); the same article says the Bnei Akiva gar'in went up on 6 February 1968 and "ב-14 בפברואר 1968, ט"ו בשבט תשכ"ח, חגגו בני הגרעין את העלייה לקרקע במסיבה פנימית ויום זה מצוין על ידי בני עקיבא כיום העלייה על הקרקע" (on 14 February 1968, 15 Shevat 5728, the gar'in celebrated going up on the land at an internal party; Bnei Akiva marks that day as the day of going up). Derived check (Python `convertdate.hebrew.to_gregorian`): 7 Shevat 5728 = 6 February 1968 and 15 Shevat 5728 = 14 February 1968. Hebrew WP, article on the regional council: "ראשון היישובים היה מחולה, שהוקם בשנת 1967" (the first of the settlements was Mehola, founded in 1967). English WP: "The village was established in 1967 by Bnei Akiva members". JTA, bulletin of 1 July 1971, no. 125 (J6), dateline "TEL AVIV, June 30 (JTA)": "Mcholah, a religious settlement in the Jordan Valley, celebrated its first ritual circumcision today" (the spelling of the archive transcription; the PDF print reads "Meholah"), so "today" is 30 June 1971 (derived: the dateline). JTA, bulletin of 13 June 1978, no. 114 (J12), dateline "TEL AVIV, June 12 (JTA)": "Meholah was founded 10 years ago as a Nahal (para-military) settlement, the first in the Jordan Valley after the Six-Day War". **Disagreement:** 1967 (English WP and the Hebrew WP council article) against 6 February 1968 (Gvati, Hebrew WP), 14 February 1968 (the day Bnei Akiva marks, Hebrew WP), "10 years ago" from June 1978, i.e. 1968 (JTA), and the civil settling of 13 November 1969 (Hebrew WP). JTA of 8 May 1969 calls Argaman the third Jordan Valley outpost (J3), which fits Mehola being an earlier one; it names no other.

3. Qalya (kibbutz, CBS 1968) [C]. Flag: a kibbutz of the same name stood at the north end of the Dead Sea until 1948 (English WP); the CBS year is the 1968 outpost. Gvati appendix: 1.2.68, "האיחוד" (Ihud), name from the initials "קם לתחיה ים המוות". Gvati narrative: "הוקמה היאחזות נח״ל קליה במחנה של הצבא הירדני" (the Nahal outpost Qalya was set up in a camp of the Jordanian army), near the former Qalya hotel, in the settlements of 1968. Hebrew WP: "בפברואר 1968, אושרה הקמתה של היאחזות נח"ל קליה" (in February 1968 the setting up of the Nahal outpost Qalya was approved); "במאי 1974 הוקם יישוב הקבע" (in May 1974 the permanent settlement was built). JTA, bulletin of 27 February 1968, no. 39 (J1), dateline "JERUSALEM, Feb. 26 (JTA)": "the new strong-point will be called Nahal Kallia. It is one of 17 approved by the Government out of 24 applications by Nahal groups" (spelling as printed); the Government decision was announced on the evening of 26 February 1968, so Gvati's 1 February precedes the approval JTA reports. **Disagreement:** 1 February 1968 (Gvati, no event named) against approval on 26 February 1968 (JTA), "February 1968" for the approval (Hebrew WP), the camp settled by civilians in 1972 and homes populated in 1974 (English WP), May 1974 (Hebrew WP). The CBS year 1968 agrees with every source on the year of the outpost.

4. Gilgal (kibbutz, CBS 1970) [C]. Gvati appendix: 15.1.70, "הקיבוץ המאוחד" (Kibbutz Meuhad). Gvati narrative: "ב־1969 הוקמה היאחזות גלגל במרכז הבקעה" (in 1969 the outpost Gilgal was set up in the middle of the Valley), meant for a kibbutz of the Kibbutz Meuhad. Hebrew WP: "הקיבוץ הוקם בט"ז בכסלו ה'תש"ל (26 בנובמבר 1969) כהיאחזות נח"ל גלגל ואוזרח בט"ו באייר ה'תשל"ג (17 במאי 1973)".
