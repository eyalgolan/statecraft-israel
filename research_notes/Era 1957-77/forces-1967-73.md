## Purpose

Collect the sourced facts that war mode (#21), the intelligence estimates (#22, design §6) and the war-start anchors
(D12) would need for the 1957–1977 era: the war-start dates, the fronts, orders of battle at symbolic scale (commands,
divisions and named brigades, not battalions), the operations with their dates and aims, the historical intelligence
estimates, and the equipment in service. The design is
[the 1957–1977 era design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1957-1977-design.md) (§2.2, §3.3, §5.6, §6).
The format follows [war-1948.md](../M4%20war%20mode/war-1948.md).

## Source caveat

- **Israeli tier 1 sources were mostly out of reach.** gov.il (MFA chronology and *Israel's Foreign Relations, Selected
  Documents* 1947–1974) returned HTTP 403; the Wayback Machine was offline ("Temporarily Offline") for the whole session;
  idf.il returned a 212-byte stub; knesset.gov.il is geo-blocked from this box. The JVL Agranat page renders by
  script and returned no text.
- **The MoD/IDF Archives Agranat pages opened**, but the three reports are photographed page albums with no text layer.
  Only the archive's own page text and item metadata are used (sections 1, 2, 5.3, 7.3). The wording of the reports is a [GAP].
- **The primary backbone is therefore American:** FRUS volumes 1964–68 XVIII, XIX, XX and 1969–76 XXIII, XXV, read
  from the Office of the Historian's TEI files (https://github.com/HistoryAtState/frus, `volumes/<id>.xml`; each
  document is also at `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/<volume>/<dN>`). UN Security Council resolution
  dates come from the UN Digital Library catalogue records (field 992, the adoption date).
- **(24 Oct) Contemporaneous press, JTA.** The JTA archive (https://www.jta.org/archive) opened on 24 Oct 2026 with its full article text, through the site's own search (`/wp-json/wp/v2/archive`). JTA dates each article by the day of the bulletin, which is one day after the event it describes ("this morning" in a bulletin dated 6 Jun is 5 Jun). A newspaper report is a primary source for what was announced and said that day, so it is [V] for the statement, not for the battlefield fact. The FRUS TEI files were read again from the same repository. gov.il was still 403 on 24 Oct; the Wayback Machine, idf.il and the Knesset site were not retried. The Agranat reports are still unreadable as text.
- **The orders of battle are [S] throughout.** No primary order of battle for either war was reachable. The en.wiki
  "Six-Day War order of battle" page (oldid 1258316296) lists the 37th and 45th Armoured Brigades under both Northern
  and Central Command, so it is internally inconsistent and is used with care.
- SIPRI's arms-transfer register has moved to a script-only application (armstransfers.sipri.org) that could not be
  queried from here. Equipment is therefore given from FRUS sales records and from the types named in operations
  (section 6); a year-by-year inventory is a [GAP].

## 3. Fronts

**June 1967.** Three fronts, one per Israeli command: Southern (Sinai and the Gaza Strip, against Egypt), Central (the
West Bank and Jerusalem, against Jordan) and Northern (the Golan, against Syria). [S] en.wiki Six-Day War order of
battle. The fronts opened in sequence: Egypt on 5 June; Jordan from 5 June, with "near total control of the West Bank
by the evening of 7 June"; the Golan on 9–10 June after the debate of 7–8 June. [S] en.wiki Six-Day War.

**War of Attrition.** Mainly the Suez Canal line (the Bar Lev Line, "completed in spring 1970" [S] en.wiki Bar Lev
Line), with raids into the Gulf of Suez and deep air raids into Egypt (Priha, 7 Jan to 13 Apr 1970 [S] en.wiki War of
Attrition). The same timeline has the Jordanian and Syrian lines: Karameh on 21 Mar 1968 (section 7.2); in February 1970 "Four days of fighting took place between Israeli and Syrian forces"; and "June 1970: An Israeli armored raid on Syrian military positions" [S]. Formations on those two lines are a [GAP].

(27 Oct) The formations on the Jordanian and Syrian lines in 1968-70 are still a [GAP]. Read this pass: the Karameh passages of FRUS 64-68 v20 d119 and d148 (section 7.2), which name no formation beyond "Units of the Israel Defense Forces", "IDF aircraft" and "several thousand Israeli troops" (d128 mentions the raid but was read only in part); JTA, 22 Mar 1968, which names "helicopter-borne paratroops" and "infantry and armored units" without unit numbers (section 7.2). No JTA search was run for the February and June 1970 Syrian-front actions, so those two entries stay [S].

**October 1973.** Two fronts: the canal (Southern Command, against the Egyptian Second and Third Armies) and the Golan
(Northern Command, against Syria, joined by Iraqi and Jordanian forces). [S] en.wiki Yom Kippur War. Jordan's own
line stayed quiet: "on the night of 12–13 October Jordanian troops deployed to the Jordanian-Syrian frontier to
buttress Syrian troops, and Jordanian forces joined Syrian and Iraqi assaults on Israeli positions on 16 and 19
October." [S]
