# Era 1957-77 research: the economy, 1957 to 1977

Researched 2026-10-23. All URLs were opened on that date. Research only, for issue #116: no rules file, test, golden or `content/` change goes with it (the era's rules file and calibration tests, #119, stay paused).

**Purpose.** #116 asks for Bank of Israel and CBS anchors for the 1957-77 era: the 1962, 1967 and 1974 devaluations, the 1965-67 slowdown and recession, growth after 1967, defence spending's share of GDP, and aid and loans after 1973, with proposed calibration anchors for #119. This note gives each figure with its report, table and printed page, and gives both values where the sources disagree. Section 7 lists the values at the years the era design names for the calibration test; which of them become targets is #119's decision.

**Conventions** (the same as `new-economic-policy-1952.md` and `economy-series-1952-56.md`)
- **[V]** verified on the cited page, which was opened. Every table below was read on a rendered image of the page, because the text layer of these scans jumbles table columns; for rotated tables the text layer was then used as a second check.
- **[S]** single source, or a search-result summary that was not opened directly.
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- Nothing is estimated here. A figure "computed here" is plain arithmetic on the cited figures and is marked as such. "Our reading" marks an interpretation that the source does not state.
- Page numbers are the printed page numbers of the report. "PDF p." is the page of the chapter file, where it helps to find a table.

Notes on the measures [V]:
- BoI 1975 and BoI 1977 print GDP "Valued at the effective rate of exchange" (footnote b to Table I-1). The earlier reports (to BoI 1971) print GNP. Our reading: the 1966-71 values therefore differ partly in what they measure and partly by revision; no opened source splits the difference.
- The basis changed over time. BoI 1977, footnote a to Table I-1 (p. 5): "Until 1968 the percentage changes were calculated on the basis of the previous year's prices. From 1969 to 1975 the rates of real change were calculated on the basis of 1970 prices, and since 1976 they have been calculated on the basis of 1975 prices." BoI 1975, footnote a to Table I-1 (p. 5), says "since 1969 the rates of real change have been calculated on the basis of constant 1970 prices".
- BoI 1975 and BoI 1977 each print 1971-75 in their Table I-1, "Main economic indicators", with the different values above. Suggestion for step 2: take the later report (BoI 1977) as the revised series for 1971-77 and BoI 1975 as the second value; for 1966-70, BoI 1975 (GDP, constant 1970 prices from 1969) with BoI 1971 (GNP, the report nearest after those years) as the second value; for 1957-65, the report nearest after the year, as listed.

### 1.2 Growth after 1967 in the reports' words [V]

- BoI 1968, p. 3: "The strong upsurge in economic activity, which began in the middle of 1967, carried over through the year reviewed. Gross national product expanded by 13 percent at constant prices, employment rose steeply, and unemployment contracted sharply." The report puts the main thrust of the 1967 upturn on "the huge increase in Government spending and its deficit financing during and immediately after the Six Day War" (same page).
- BoI 1968, Table I-1, p. 4: gross investment 1968, +44.0 percent; public consumption 1967, +36.0 percent.

## 2. The 1965-67 slowdown and recession

### 2.1 What the Bank wrote [V]

BoI 1966, p. 3: "Real GNP increased by less than 1 percent in 1966, while per capita the figure declined somewhat." On the same page, the slowdown is laid partly to "deliberate Government policy" against the background of the earlier boom, and partly to the fall in immigration: net immigration of "some 50,000 per annum during the years 1961-64" dropped "to only some 20,000 in 1965 and to about 5,000 in 1966". The same page names the decline in private restitution payments from West Germany and the fall in capital transfers for investment among the other factors.

### 3.2 Each devaluation in the annual reports

- **1962** [V, C on the day]. BoI 1962, p. 3: "On February 9, the official rate of exchange was raised from IL 1.80 per dollar to IL 3.00, and simultaneously the majority of import levies and export subsidies, which in recent years could be regarded as a substitute for a higher rate of exchange, were abolished." The same page gives the effect on effective rates: the return per dollar of exports rose 20 percent, from an average effective IL 2.50 to IL 3.00, and the average price of imports rose 44 percent, from an effective IL 2.31 to IL 3.00 plus customs duties at an average of 10 percent. The day differs: 9 February in the report, 10.2.62 in BoI rates. Our reading: an announcement on the 9th taking effect on the 10th, which neither source says.
- **1967** [V]. BoI 1968, p. 466: "the devaluation of the Israeli pound on November 19, 1967", the same day as BoI rates. BoI 1967, p. 544, states both rates: "$ 612.3 million at the previous exchange rate of IL 3/$ 1" and "($ 694.7 million according to the present rate of IL 3.50/$ 1 )". The same page gives the Bank's gold as $68.4 million at the end of both 1966 and 1967, valued at IL 205.1 million and IL 239.5 million (computed here: IL 3.00 and IL 3.50 per dollar, which matches BoI rates). BoI 1968, p. 5, says the devaluation "had only a slight impact on the domestic price level", and p. 6 links the rise in exports in 1968 mainly to "the larger return on exports after the devaluation in November 1967".
- **1971** [V for the month]. BoI 1971, chapter I (p. 3), calls it "the devaluation of August 1971"; chapters I and III, the only ones opened, do not state the official rates in words; the day and rate (22.8.71, 4.20) are from BoI rates only [S for the day].
- **1974** [V, C on the day]. BoI 1974, p. 549: "the change in the rate of exchange of the Israeli pound on November 9, 1974"; p. 552: "Until the devaluation of the Israeli pound on November 9, foreign currency reserves had declined by $ 816 million (IL 3,427 million)" (computed here: IL 4.20 per dollar, the pre-devaluation rate in BoI rates). BoI rates gives 10.11.74. Our reading, as for 1962: announced on one day, effective the next; no opened source says so.
- **1975 onwards** [V]. BoI 1975, pp. 52-53: "In 1975 a more flexible exchange rate system was introduced, with a view to making it an instrument of current balance of payments policy and to diminish the need for large devaluations with all their unsettling effects." BoI 1975, chapter X, puts the average official rate at IL 4.5 per dollar in 1974 and IL 6.3 in 1975, with the average import levy down from 27.2 to 15 percent (p. 209).
- **October 1977** [V]. BoI 1977, p. 3: "At the end of 1977 an economic reform was introduced, the main features of which were the liberalization of foreign currency control, the unification of exchange rates, and the floating of the Israeli pound." The last official rate in BoI rates is 10.78 on 17.10.77; the page's sources switch to Bank of Israel circulars from 31.10.1977. The exact day of the float is [GAP] in the opened sources (our reading of the table is late October 1977).

## 5. Aid, loans, reserves and foreign debt

### 5.1 Before 1973 [V]

- **German reparations ended in 1966.** BoI 1966, p. 72: "The outstanding change in unilateral transfers in 1966 was the complete cessation of receipts on account of German reparations, which for many years after 1952 were one of the main component items." Table III-17, p. 74: German reparations, net, $17 million in 1964 and 1965, $-2 million in 1966. Personal restitution from Germany continued: $134, 113 and 110 million in 1964, 1965 and 1966 (same table).
- **US aid before 1967 was mostly loans.** BoI 1966, p. 75: "U.S. Government aid is extended entirely in the form of loans, and the $ 3 million listed as a direct grant in 1966 represents the sum drawn on under former agreements." US Government grant-in-aid: $8, 5 and 3 million in 1964-66 (Table III-17, p. 74). BoI 1968, p. 72: "Since 1968 all U.S. Government loans have to be repaid in dollars".
- **Total capital imports** (unilateral transfers plus long- and medium-term capital): $625, 590 and 493 million in 1964, 1965 and 1966 (BoI 1966, Table III-17, p. 74; source as printed: CBS).
