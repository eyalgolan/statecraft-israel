# Arab forces at 15 May and 11 June 1948: positions and strengths

Researched 2026-10-13. Every URL below was opened on that date unless the entry says otherwise. Research for issue #226 and
its sub-issues #227 to #233. Feeds GDD Phase 5 (#385): the Historical starting positions of the Arab forces, and their
strength when the first truce began.

## Purpose

Give, for each Arab force, the formations, where they stood and how strong they were on two dates: the evening of
15 May 1948 (the invasion) and 11 June 1948 (the first truce). Sources from the three historiographies are set side by
side: Israeli (Lorch, the Ben-Gurion diary), Arab (the Arab Legion and ALA commanders' own accounts, Walid Khalidi, the
Arab armies' joint headquarters), Western and "new historian" (Glubb, O'Ballance, Dupuy, Morris, Shlaim, Pollack), and
contemporary US records (FRUS, CIA).

This note is research, not a design. It adds no event text and changes no content file. It builds on the branch
`arab-units-research` (`research_notes/Units 1948/arab-armies-1948.md`, 2026-10-11), which covers the whole war as
map tokens; this note narrows to the two dates and adds strength figures and Arab sources.

## Conventions

- **[V]** read on 2026-10-13 in the cited text: a page, a PDF, a full-text file, or a full-text search snippet with
  the words quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **[S]** single weak source, or carried from the `arab-units-research` note (Google Books snippets read on
  2026-10-11, not re-read here because Google Books returned HTTP 429 all day on 2026-10-13). Marked "[S, carried]".
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given with its source.
- **not found** or **unverified**: searched and not found, or seen only in a source that cannot carry the claim.
- **Commanders are named by role only** ("commander of the Egyptian expeditionary force"). Authors of sources are named
  in citations, including where the author was himself a commander (Glubb, al-Tall, al-Qawuqji).
- **Coordinates** are decimal degrees from the place's English Wikipedia article (MediaWiki API, `prop=coordinates`)
  or Wikidata (Ramallah Q158119, Antipatris Q682612). They mark the named place, not the unit. Treat every one as
  approximate, within a few kilometres. Majdal is given by the coordinates of modern Ashkelon.
- **Unit names.** The Arab Legion called its battalions "regiments" in English. Al-Tall's Arabic text calls them
  battalions (كتيبة). Both mean the same units here: 1st to 6th.
- **Wikipedia** is used only for coordinates and as a pointer to sources. No figure in this note rests on Wikipedia.
- **Sensitive events** near these positions (Kfar Etzion, the Jewish Quarter, Nitzanim, Lydda and Ramla) are not
  described here. The branch note `arab-armies-1948.md`, "Sensitivity review", lists them for the owner.
- Dates are given as the source gives them. Where sources give different dates for the same move, both are listed.

## 1. Egyptian Expeditionary Force (#227)

**Command.** Commander of the expeditionary force, a major general; a brigadier as his deputy, who commanded the
coastal brigade group directly [V] Dupuy p. 55. The eastern (inland) force was commanded by a colonel who had led the
volunteers [V] Dupuy pp. 54, 58.

**Formations at entry.**
- Two brigade groups of unequal size [V] O'Ballance pp. 89 to 90; Dupuy p. 55.
- Coastal brigade group: "a reinforced brigade group, composed of the First, Sixth, and Ninth battalions" [S, carried]
  Morris p. 233.
- Inland column: two regular infantry battalions plus Muslim Brotherhood volunteers [V] Dupuy p. 55. Volunteers of
  the Brotherhood had been in the Gaza and Khan Yunis area since early 1948 in two small battalion-sized contingents
  [V] Dupuy p. 54.
- Support: tanks (Dupuy: Mark VI and Matilda; O'Ballance: Sherman and Matilda) [C], a medium machine gun battalion, a
  field regiment of 25-pounders (sixteen guns, Dupuy), 6-pounder guns, anti-aircraft guns [V] O'Ballance p. 89;
  Dupuy p. 55. Air: 15 fighters, 5 converted bombers as transports, a few light aircraft [V] both.
- Saudi troops served under Egyptian command: first two companies, later six [V] O'Ballance p. 78.

**Strength at 15 May** [C]
- About 10,000 [V] O'Ballance pp. 77, 90 ("hard put to raise 10,000 men for the initial invasion force"); [V] Glubb
  (snippet, field strength 15 May); [V] CIA, week ending 19 May (10,000 near the border).
- 10,000 to 12,000 sent to the frontier [V] FRUS d270, US ambassador in Cairo, 14 May.
- About 7,000 in five infantry battalions: coastal group about 5,000, inland group under 2,000 [V] Dupuy p. 55.
- About 6,000: the force "initially consisting of some six thousand troops" crossed at Rafah on 15 May [V] Morris,
  snippet.

Dupuy prints the occupation of Iraq Suwaydan as "October 14" in a passage about 12 to 14 May 1948. Read as 14 May (a
misprint in the book). [V] Dupuy p. 55.

**Moves between the dates** (for a scripted advance)
- Beersheba [C]: advance guard 17 May, main body 20 May [V] Dupuy p. 58; entered 20 May [V] O'Ballance p. 93; 17 May
  (Tal) or 19 May (Morris) [S, carried].
- Bethlehem [C]: handed over by the Legion on the morning of 22 May [V] O'Ballance p. 93; link-up 20 May and control
  21 May [V] Dupuy p. 58; 20 May (Tal) or about 22 May (Morris) [S, carried].
- Majdal reached about 21 to 24 May; Isdud entered 29 May [V] Dupuy p. 58. A small column went east through Iraq
  Suwaydan, Faluja and Bayt Jibrin to link with the eastern force [V] Dupuy p. 58.

The Arab armies' joint headquarters gave the Egyptian truce line as: "Beginning at Gaza and running northward to Majdal
and Isdud, southeastward to Iraq Suweidam and Taluja, eastward to Beit Jibrin and Deir Nakh Khas, northwestward to
Aijun, Artuf on the Jerusalem-Lydda road, and Tarqumiva, thence southeastward to Hebron and Beersheba", with "one
spearhead to Artuf and another to Bethlehem" [V] AP, Amman, 13 June 1948. Spellings are as in the transcription:
"Suweidam" and "Taluja" are read here as Iraq Suwaydan and Faluja, "Tarqumiva" as Tarqumiya; "Aijun" is not
identified. The CIA's summary for the week ending 16 June agrees on Gaza to Isdud and a link to the Beersheba to
Jerusalem area [V].
- Reported in the JTA's Tel Aviv dispatch of 10 June (bulletin of 11 June): "the Egyptians trapped in the
  Ishdud-Yibneh pocket for over a week made a new attempt to break out" [V] JTA bulletin, 11 June 1948, p. 1 (press;
  matches the Isdud pocket above). The same dispatch has two Egyptian Spitfires over Rehovoth, one shot down.

**Strength on 11 June** [C]
- At Isdud: about 2,500 men under the deputy commander [V] Dupuy p. 58. The Givati Brigade's estimate on 31 May:
  2,000 to 3,000 soldiers with many guns and tanks [V] Ben-Gurion diary, 31 May 1948.
- Whole force: no figure dated 11 June found [GAP]. O'Ballance: stepped up during the truce to about 18,000, with more
  Sudanese, North African and Saudi volunteers [V] p. 137. CIA, 27 July: 5,000 in Palestine and 8,000 near it [V].

## 2. Arab Legion (#228)

**Command.** The Legion's commander (a British officer in Jordanian service); a British officer commanding the
division in the field; British officers commanding the 1st and 3rd brigades; an Arab officer commanding the 4th
Brigade [V] O'Ballance p. 136; [V] al-Qawuqji part II, pp. 29 to 30 (the 4th Brigade commander with his staff in
Ramallah on 18 May). Al-Tall lists which battalions had British and which Arab commanders: 1st, 2nd and 3rd British;
4th, 5th and 6th Arab [V] al-Tall pp. 180 to 182.

**Formations.** Two brigades in the field, the 1st (1st and 3rd battalions) and the 3rd (2nd and 4th battalions), and a
4th Brigade (5th and 6th battalions) at Ramallah, plus garrison companies, an artillery battalion with two batteries,
and services [V] al-Tall pp. 84 to 85. Al-Tall says there was no 2nd Brigade: the number was skipped [V] al-Tall p. 85.
O'Ballance describes one mechanized brigade of three battalions of about 500 each, with 17 independent companies of about
200 each [V] p. 80. [C] The branch note records en.wiki's table, which puts the 5th and 6th in a "2nd Brigade"; al-Tall
and Tal (p. 205, [S, carried]) both have them in the 4th.

**Strength at 15 May** [C]
- 4,500 "men of the Arab Legion" in the field [V] Glubb, snippet.
- About 9,000, including Jordanian volunteers: "nine thousand soldiers approximately is the strength of the Arab Army
  which entered" [V] al-Tall p. 85 (his table of the parts is unreadable in the OCR).
- 8,000 men and fifty British armoured cars at the outbreak of war [V] Pollack, snippet.
- About 6,000 in all, about 5,000 in the field [V] O'Ballance pp. 80, 137.
- About 5,000 in Palestine on 20 May by Israeli count: 1,500 between Jerusalem and Bayt Jibrin, 300 towards Latrun,
  1,500 to 2,000 in the Triangle, 300 to 400 in the Jordan valley, 400 to 500 in Ramla and Lydda [V] Ben-Gurion diary,
  20 May 1948 (report of the IDF chief of operations).
- Before the British left, about 1,500 of the Legion were in Palestine under British command, and all but two companies
  were to leave by 12 May [V] FRUS d206 (3 May). About 3,000 had been on duty with the British in Palestine [V] UP,
  *New York Times*, 15 May (press).

**Positions on 13 May (before entry)** [V] al-Tall pp. 38 to 39, a table of units and stations "until the evening of
13 May 1948": 3rd Brigade and 2nd Battalion at Irbid; 4th Battalion at Mafraq; 4th Brigade and 5th Battalion at
Ramallah, with the 5th Company at Nablus; 6th Battalion at Jericho; the 8th Company at the Allenby Bridge, Jericho and
Khan al-Ahmar; the 12th Company at Hebron; the 13th Company at Jisr al-Majami. The 1st Brigade's entries are not
readable in the OCR column layout. [S] for any single line: the OCR mixes the columns.

**Positions on the evening of 15 May** [V] al-Tall pp. 84 to 85 ("the positions where the army's units settled on

  O'Ballance p. 137 ("reduced from about 5,000 to 4,000").
- The 3rd Brigade at Latrun: 2,300 men plus 800 auxiliaries (Morris p. 221, [S, carried]).
- No other figure dated 11 June found [GAP]. CIA, 27 July: 6,000 in Palestine, 4,000 near it [V].

## 3. Syrian Army (#229)

**Command.** Commander of the 1st Brigade (a colonel, replaced by a brigadier general on or before 18 May); the Syrian
chief of staff directed operations on the upper Jordan from late May [V] Dupuy pp. 47 to 49.

**Formations and strength at 15 May** [C]
- 1st Infantry Brigade: two infantry battalions, an armoured car battalion and a tank company, with an artillery
  regiment firing from the Golan [V] Dupuy p. 47.
- 2nd Infantry Brigade further north, near the Bnot Yaakov bridge [V] Dupuy p. 49.
- Army of 7,000 to 8,000, with one effective mechanized brigade [V] O'Ballance p. 82.
- 3,000 in the field [V] Glubb, snippet. About 4,500 "available" [V] FRUS d270 (British estimate of 11 May).

**Positions on 15 May**

## 4. Iraqi expeditionary force (#230)

**Command.** Commander of the Iraqi expeditionary force, a general [V] Dupuy p. 50; O'Ballance p. 115 (the two give
different names for him; not used here).

**Formations and strength at 15 May** [C]

## 6. Arab Liberation Army (#232)

**Command.** Commander of the ALA ("Commander, Northern and Central Front" in his cables), reporting to the Arab League's
inspector-general in Damascus [V] al-Qawuqji parts I and II.

**Formations.** Eight battalions, entered from January 1948 [V] al-Qawuqji part I, editor's note, p. 27. Named in the
memoirs: First, Second and Third Yarmuk, Hittin, Ajnadin, Qadisiya, a Lebanese battalion and an Alawite battalion
[V] al-Qawuqji parts I and II. Morris lists eight: Yarmuk 1, 2 and 3, Ajnadin, Husayn, Qadisiya, Hittin and one more
(snippet cut off) [V] Morris, snippet. By 8 May the Qadisiya Battalion had been disbanded and the Ajnadin Battalion had
broken up in Jaffa [V] al-Qawuqji part II, pp. 5, 7.

**Strength at 15 May** [C]
- Almost 4,000, including about 1,500 Palestinians [V] JPS editors, al-Qawuqji part I, p. 27; about 4,000 [V]
  Shlaim seminar page.
- 7,000 by the British GOC's estimate on 2 May, with other estimates of 10,000 [V] FRUS d206.
- About 10,000 in all, nearly 6,000 inside Palestine: about 2,500 Syrians, 2,500 Iraqis, 500 Lebanese and "a handful of
  Yugoslav Moslems" [V] Dupuy p. 41. The same make-up "by the end of May" [V] O'Ballance pp. 83 to 84.

**Positions on 15 May.** The ALA was withdrawing from central Palestine as the regular armies entered.
- HQ at Jaba', central Palestine, from March [V] al-Qawuqji part I, p. 27. Jaba' 32.3239, 35.2222.
- "Practically all our units had left the area" of Jerusalem on the King's orders by 18 May; the artillery was withdrawn
  to "assembly positions east of Nablus" [V] al-Qawuqji part II, p. 30.
- A force under one of the ALA's lieutenant-colonels, strengthened with a company of the Alawite Battalion and
  Palestinian and Jordanian volunteers, fought in Sheikh Jarrah on 18 to 19 May and was withdrawn that night [V]
  al-Qawuqji part II, pp. 31 to 33.
- Units reached Qalqilya on 15 May by Israeli report [V] Ben-Gurion diary, 16 May. Qalqilya 32.1903, 34.9686.
- Al-Malikiyya: the Second Yarmuk Battalion (branch note, from he.wiki and en.wiki, [S, carried]); see section 5.
- [C] Dupuy: the ALA commander "withdrew to Lebanon from Galilee and ordered his units that had been operating north of
  the Jerusalem Corridor to concentrate in the Samarian 'triangle'" [V] p. 60. O'Ballance: when the Iraqis came to
  Nablus "the Arab Liberation Army was moved farther north to look after Galilee" [V] p. 115.

**Positions on 11 June**
- The ALA commander reached Nazareth as the truce began (Tal p. 266, [S, carried]). O'Ballance puts the reorganised ALA
  HQ at Nazareth in July [V] p. 155. Nazareth 32.7019, 35.3033.
- Al-Malikiyya, from 6 June, with the Lebanese (see section 5).
- During the truce the ALA "was contracted and withdrawn still farther northwards" [V] O'Ballance p. 136.

**Strength on 11 June.** Reduced during the truce to about 2,000 and placed under Lebanese command [V] O'Ballance
p. 138. No figure dated 11 June found [GAP].

## 9. Open questions and gaps

- No source found gives the Arab armies' strengths dated 11 June. The nearest are O'Ballance's truce-period figures and
  the CIA's 27 July table.
- Lorch, Morris, Pollack and Rogan and Shlaim were read only through short search snippets, without page numbers. The
  printed pages should be checked before any figure is used.
- Al-Tall's two tables (13 May and 15 May) need the printed page: the OCR mixes the columns. The 1st Brigade's 13 May
  stations are unreadable.
- Lebanese, Syrian and Egyptian brigade and battalion numbers on 11 June are mostly missing. The JTA daily
  bulletins of 10 to 17 June 1948 (scans at pdfs.jta.org, OCR'd locally 2026-10-17) were searched for Syrian,
  Egyptian, Legion and Iraqi formation numbers and for any strength dated 11 June: none found - the bulletins
  carry operational mentions only (the items added to the per-army 11 June blocks above).
- The Iraqi brigade at Gesher on 15 May (1st or 2nd) is unresolved.
- The Arab Legion's 1st Brigade between 23 May and 11 June: only the 1st Battalion's hills are sourced.
- The Holy War outside Jerusalem and Ramla (Hebron, Bethlehem, the Triangle) on these dates: not found.
- Saudi, Sudanese and Yemeni contingents on 15 May and 11 June: only O'Ballance's "two companies, later six" for Saudi
  Arabia.
- Arab sources still to read: the Iraqi parliamentary inquiry report (1949), the Egyptian commander's and deputy's
  memoirs, the Lebanese official account, and Walid Khalidi's "Selected Documents" (JPS 1998), which could not be
  opened.

  19 May it is certain. 11 June: Latrun, Bab al-Wad, Jerusalem's Old City and Sheikh Jarrah, Ramallah, with small
  detachments at Lydda and Ramla (al-Tall, AP, Dupuy, Khalidi, Ben-Gurion diary).

  the Mishmar HaYarden bridgehead (Dupuy, Lorch, AP, CIA). Brigade number at the bridgehead is contested.
- **#230 Iraq.** 15 May: the Gesher and Jisr al-Majami crossing (Dupuy, Ben-Gurion diary, Morris carried). 11 June:
  Nablus HQ, Jenin, Tulkarm, Qalqilya and the line south to Ras al-Ayn (AP, Khalidi, O'Ballance, Dupuy).
