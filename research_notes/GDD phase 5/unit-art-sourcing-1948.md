# Unit art sourcing, 1948: flags, uniforms, the gun and the armoured car

Researched 2026-10-16. Every URL below was opened on that date, except where the entry says it is a search snippet,
refused or not opened. Research for GDD
Phase 5 (#385), plan PR 17 ("art for the new units", `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-15-gdd-phase-5.md`), its
sourcing half. The searches used the built-in web search and fetch tools (no Tavily key on the machine this was
written on).

## Purpose

PR 17 asks for era-correct figures and models "sourced to the same standard as the IDF units" (`docs/units.md`,
"Sources") for artillery, the Israeli armoured car and each Arab force D-G1 names. Its markers follow Q-ART-1
(decided 15 October 2026, "2+3"): a neutral pennant now, replaced by a sourced emblem when one is found. Today
`docs/units.md` draws Israel's flag only, because no Arab state's 1948 flag is sourced there.

This note gives, per force:
- the flag or emblem in force on 15 May 1948, with its legal text where one was reached;
- the uniform and headgear references, with each photograph's date and licence;
- the shape references for the gun and the armoured car that `research_notes/M4 war mode/war-1948.md` section 8
  ("Q-ART-2") names.

This note is research, not a design. It adds no event text, no art and no content row. Q-ART-2 (which vehicle and gun
the models show) stays the owner's question. The sensitivity review (Q-ART-3) applies to anything built from this
note.

## Conventions

- **[V]** read on 2026-10-16 on the cited page, words quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **[S]** single or secondary source, or a search snippet only (said where so).
- **[C]** sources conflict; every version is given.
- **[G]** not found; the searches tried are listed in "Searches tried".
- **Photographs are references only**, as in `docs/units.md`: no image is copied into the repository. No photograph was
  looked at for this note. What a photograph shows is given only from its own file description ("per the file
  description"), so no garment is claimed from a picture.
- Licences are the file page's own tags. "PD-Israel" means the page's Israeli copyright-expiry tag; Commons notes that a
  US status is also needed. That matters for reuse, not for a reference.
- The note's places and subjects follow `arab-forces-1948.md` "Conventions": events at the sensitive places it lists
  are not described here. Two 1948 Arab Legion photographs on Commons whose subjects are among them are left out as
  figure references.
- Commanders are named only where they are the subject or author of a cited file.

### Transjordan

- **[V]** Organic Law of Transjordan, 16 April 1928, Art. 3, in the French translation on the Digithèque MJP, which
  names its source as *L'Europe nouvelle*, no. 589, 25 May 1929, p. 684:
  https://mjp.univ-perp.fr/constit/jo1928.htm (the site's certificate fails some clients; read with a plain HTTP
  client). The text: the length "sera double de la largeur", "trois bandes (qita') parallèles et égales ; la bande
  supérieure sera noire, la bande centrale blanche et l'inférieure verte"; at the hoist "un triangle rouge dont le plus
  grand côté sera égal à la largeur du drapeau et dont la hauteur sera égale au quart de la longueur du drapeau"; in
  the triangle "une étoile blanche à sept pointes (musabba')" inscribed in a circle of "1/24e de la longueur du
  drapeau".
- **[S]** Still the flag on 15 May 1948: a search snippet of Britannica says no change was made to the flag at
  independence in 1946. The flag article of the 1946/47 constitution was not reached [G].
- **[C]** Flags of the World (https://crwflags.com/fotw/flags/jo_his.html) carries a contributor's report, citing
  Sulaiman Musa in *The Flag Bulletin* 123 (1987), that the long triangle "was not introduced in 1928, but only some
  years later". The 1928 text gives the triangle a height of a quarter of the flag's length. Which length flew in 1948
  is not settled here.
- **[S]** The Arab Legion's own colours: Flags of the World (https://crwflags.com/fotw/flags/jo^1939.html) shows an
  Arab Legion flag of about 1939, from the *Flaggenbuch* of 1939. Nothing there is dated 1948.

### Iraq

- **[V]** "Constitution (Organic Law) of the Kingdom of Iraq", 21 March 1925 as amended 29 July 1925, Art. 4, in the
  translation on constitution.org, which gives its source as *British and Foreign State Papers*, 1926, vol. CXXIII:
  https://constitution.org/1-Constitution/cons/iraq/iraqiconst19250321.html. The text: "The length of the flag shall
  be double its breadth. It shall be divided horizontally into three colours of equal size ... the upper section being
  black, the others white and green respectively. On the side of the staff there shall be a red trapezoid, the greater
  base of which shall be equal to the breadth of the flag and the lesser base equal to the breadth of the white
  section, the height being equal to one quarter of the length of the flag. In the centre there shall be two white
  stars of seven points each, in a perpendicular position, parallel to the staff."
- **[S]** The same wording, citing League of Nations document C-49-1929-VI, is on Flags of the World:
  https://www.fotw.info/flags/iq_kingd.html. It gives the flag's use as up to 1958/59 ([C] on the end year; either way
  after 1948).

### Lebanon

- **[V]** Constitution of Lebanon, Art. 5, WIPO Lex text "(As amended by the Constitutional Law of December 7, 1943)":
  https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/565753. "The Lebanese flag shall be composed of three horizontal stripes, a
  white stripe between two red ones. The width of the white stripe shall be equal to that of both red stripes. In the
  center of and occupying one third of the white stripe is a green Cedar tree with its top touching the upper red
  stripe and its base touching the lower red stripe."
- **[C]** WIPO's text is the consolidated constitution with later amendments. English Wikipedia's "Flag of Lebanon"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Lebanon) says that before the amendment of 21 September 1990 the article
  "did not specify the color of the cedar tree", and that the cedar was "commonly depicted with a brown trunk" before
  then. So the 1943 wording of the cedar's colour is not verified here, and a 1948 token should not claim an
  all-green cedar on this text alone. The text gives no overall length to height.

### Syria

- **[V, transcription]** Constitution of the State of Syria, promulgated by Decree No. 3111 of the French High
  Commissioner, 14 May 1930. The English translation is in the *League of Nations Official Journal*, vol. 11,
  September 1930, pp. 1112-1122, with Art. 4 on p. 1113. Its words, as transcribed on the Wikimedia Commons page
  for the flag (<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_Syria_(1930%E2%80%931958,_1961%E2%80%931963).svg>):
  "The Syrian flag shall be composed as follows, the length shall be double the height. It shall contain three
  bands of equal dimensions, the upper band being green, the middle band white, and the lower band black. The white
  portion shall bear three red stars in line, having five points each." The *Official Journal* itself was not
  opened. Upgrade: read p. 1113.
- **[S]** English Wikipedia's "Flag of Syria" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Syria) gives this flag's use as
  1930-1958 (and 1961-63). It also cites a first use in 1932 ([C] with 1930); the design is the same either
  way.

### Egypt

- **[G]** The legal text of the 1923 flag was not reached. No primary scan of the Egyptian gazette was found.
- **[C]** What made the flag law: English Wikipedia's "List of Egyptian flags"
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Egyptian_flags) says "Law No. 47 of 1923", citing el Ansary (2001), p. 144.
  The State Information Service, as quoted on Flags of the World (https://www.crwflags.com/fotw/flags/eg.html), says
  "a Royal Decree". Flags of the World's page on the kingdom (https://crwflags.com/fotw/flags/eg-kingd.html) dates
  it to 10 December 1923, citing Whitney Smith.
- **[S]** The design, in all three: green, with a white crescent and three white stars. The 2:3 proportion is only in
  the *Flaggenbuch* of 1939, as cited on Flags of the World. The flag was in use on 15 May 1948 in every version.
- Lead not opened: manshurat.org, node 73466, a decree of 10 December 1923 (search snippet; the page timed out).

### The Arab Liberation Army and the local forces

- **ALA, [G]:** no flag or emblem with a source was found. English Wikipedia's "Arab Liberation Army" shows an
  "emblem" from a Commons drawing whose own page
  (<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arab_Liberation_Army_(bw).svg>) says "Historical status as a flag is
  unknown, possibly dubious" and gives no references; its one source is a dead link. It is not a sourced emblem.
  A 1948-49 photograph of an ALA armoured car with an emblem is on Commons, but its page says the emblem "may have
  been added at some point *after* the photo was taken" (section 2). Neither should be drawn.
- **Local forces, [G]:** no flag or emblem of the Army of the Holy War (al-Jihad al-Muqaddas) was found. English
  Wikipedia's article on it shows a "Palestinian flag of 1938" with no footnote. Search snippets tie the Arab Revolt
  flag to the All-Palestine Government, at dates after 15 May 1948 ([C], none opened). Nothing ties a flag to the
  force on 15 May 1948.

## 3. The gun: the 65 mm "Napoleonchik"

`war-1948.md` section 8 names the 65 mm gun from Ben-Gurion's diary. This adds what it was and its shape.

- **[V]** The type: "Napoleonchik" redirects on English Wikipedia to "Canon de 65 M (montagne) modèle 1906", a French
  mountain gun. The page: "used by the Israel Defence Forces in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, and was nicknamed
  Napoleonchik by the Israelis due to its old look", citing Luttwak and Shamir (2023), p. 17. It gives a weight of
  400 kg, calibre 65 mm, elevation −9° to +35°, a 4.4 kg round, 330 m/s and 6.5 km. Tier 3; Luttwak and Shamir not
  read. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_de_65_M_(montagne)_mod%C3%A8le_1906>
- **[S]** A collectors' site (https://www.armedconflicts.com/-t82778) gives the barrel as 1,313 mm overall and the bore
  as 1,032 mm. It gives the elevation as −10° ([C] with −9°) and shells of 4.5 kg shrapnel and 3.8 kg HE ([C] with
  4.4 kg).
- **[G]** Wheel diameter, track and overall length. A 900 mm wheel figure that came up in search belongs to a different
  gun (the 75 mm of 1907), so it is not used. A model would scale the wheels from the barrel in the photographs and
  mark them as an estimate.
- **[V]** The one dated photograph in use: "Napoleonchik.jpg", the battle for Beersheba, 20 October 1948, Hugo
  Mendelson, GPO D284-064. PD-Israel. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Napoleonchik.jpg

## 4. The armoured car: the "sandwich"

`war-1948.md` section 8 names the "sandwich" armoured truck, from Tank Archives. This adds the build and the
photographs.

- **[S]** Construction, from Tank Archives' "Israeli Sandwiches" (https://www.tankarchives.com/2017/08/israeli-sandwiches.html),
  the one source for these details: "A 38 mm thick layer of wood was placed in between 5 mm and 3 mm plates of steel.
  The side and rear of the armoured cars was made out of these 'sandwiches', and the front was made from solid 14 mm
  plate." "The most common type of 'sandwich' [was] built on the Ford F15 15 CWT chassis." "127 such vehicles were
  built from December 28th, 1947, to April 15th, 1948", by six companies. The most were "a supply truck that also
  performed the duties of an APC". Variants were also built on 1.5 and 3 ton chassis and on the Chevrolet G-7117.
- **[V]** The steel-wood-steel build in a second source: English Wikipedia's "Bab al-Wad" (Sha'ar HaGai),
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al-Wad: "regular trucks with improvised armour made of two sheets of steel and a
  layer of wood in between". The Latrun museum photograph's Hebrew description says the same.
- **[G]** Overall length, width and height. A model would take them from the donor chassis and mark them as an
  estimate.
- **[V]** Photographs:
  - "Sandwich" armoured vehicle at the Armoured Corps museum, Latrun, 27 April 2012, Avishai Teicher (PikiWiki). CC BY
    2.5. <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PikiWiki_Israel_19387_'Sandwich'_armored_vehicle_in_armored_corps_museum,_Latrun.jpg>
  - "Sandwich" armoured car at Batey HaOsef, Tel Aviv, October 2005, Bukvoed. GFDL, CC BY-SA 3.0 or CC BY 2.5.
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Armored-car-batey-haosef-1.jpg
  - The remains at Sha'ar HaGai, 29 April 2009, CC BY-SA 3.0.
    <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sha'ar_hagai_armored_vehicle1.JPG>
  - 1948: "The first supply convoy reaches the outskirts of Jerusalem ... April 1948", from Herzog, *The Arab Israeli
    Wars* (1982), credited to the Jerusalem Post. PD-Israel. The caption does not name the vehicles' type.
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jerusalem_convoy.jpg
- **[G]** A dated 1947-49 photograph whose own description names a "sandwich". Commons' "Improvised armored vehicles
  of Israel" category (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Improvised_armored_vehicles_of_Israel) holds about
  140 files, many from 1948 (National Library of Israel and Palmach archive scans). They were not opened one by one.
  That is the next step.
- **[V]** Other cars in Israeli hands: an Otter armoured car "captured by the Haganah from the Arab Liberation Army in
  1948" (English Wikipedia's "Otter light reconnaissance car", its photograph's caption).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otter_light_reconnaissance_car. Others (Marmon-Herrington, Humber) are [S], from a
  book review and Commons category names. No source was found for a Staghound in Israeli service in 1948 [G].

## 5. What PR 17 can use

- **Flags.** On these texts, Iraq's flag is drawable from its 1925 law, and Transjordan's from its 1928 law, with the
  triangle's length an open [C]. Lebanon's is drawable, with the cedar's colour in 1948 an open [C]. Syria's is
  drawable on a transcription, upgraded when the *Official Journal* page is read. Egypt keeps the neutral pennant
  until its 1923 text is reached. The ALA and the local forces keep the pennant (Q-ART-1); no emblem of theirs was
  found.
- **Figures.** No dress detail of any force has a dated, primary text source. The nearest is the Arab Legion's
  red-and-white shemagh, in a magazine essay about the 1950s [S]. Every force's dress is [S] or [G]. A figure for those forces should wait for the Osprey plates, or for a person
  to look at the photographs listed above and record what they show.
- **The gun and the car.** The photographs above give the shape. Dimensions beyond the gun's weight and barrel are [G],
  so the models scale from the photographs and say so.

**Decisions for the owner:**
1. **Flag on Lebanon's tokens.** Draw the cedar green (today's text) or follow the pre-1990 practice of a brown trunk?
   The 1948 practice was not sourced here.
2. **Flag on Transjordan's tokens.** Fly the national flag, or wait for a sourced Arab Legion colour? Note that the
   1928 flag's continuity to 1948 rests on one search snippet [S].
3. **Syria on a transcription.** Is a Commons transcription of the *Official Journal* enough to draw its flag, or must
   the page be read first?
