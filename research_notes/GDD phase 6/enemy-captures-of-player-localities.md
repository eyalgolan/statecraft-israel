# Enemy captures of player localities: research note (DRAFT)

**DRAFT for the owner's sensitivity review (CONTENT_POLICY; impact plan §4.2).** Research date: 18 October 2026.
Research only: this note adds no card or other record to `content/`, and nothing here is player-facing text until the
owner has reviewed it.

## Scope and method

This note answers the research part of Q-TGT-2(c) (#783 and #1015): enemy units may capture the player's localities,
and a place tied by sources to a dated loss receives a sourced card. “Player locality” here means a player-side place
in the approved MVP location roster (`docs/locations.md`), plus the Jewish Quarter because the Phase 6 plan and #1015
name it explicitly within the East Jerusalem / Old City location. The date must be after the campaign starts on
15 May 1948; the cutoff is exclusive. A loss can be a surrender or an evacuation followed by enemy control, but the
table preserves that distinction instead of calling every case a battlefield capture.

Conventions:

- **[C]**: sources conflict; the table retains each date.
- **High confidence**: an Israeli official source states both the place and date directly.
- **Medium confidence**: the sources establish the date of evacuation and subsequent enemy control, but do not give a
  separate date for enemy entry. (No row uses it after the 18 October source check.)
- `massacre` appears only if a cited source characterizes the loss as a massacre. Absence of the tag is not a finding
  about every act during the fighting.

### Boundary checks and exclusions

- The Etzion bloc fell before the cutoff and is not in the loss table. The Ministry of Defense memorial page says the
  bloc was overcome and its defenders surrendered on the high command's order on 13 May 1948 (4 Iyar 5708)
  ([Gush Etzion settlements memorial](https://www.izkor.gov.il/monument/en_300178f2726f6f62b27f5c113a605cf0/),
  checked: 2026-10-18); #1015 records 14 May for part of the bloc. **[C]** on the day, but both dates are before
  15 May, so the exclusion holds either way. Whether a Kfar Etzion card would carry `massacre` is out of this note's
  scope; the visitor-centre page first cited for it (landmarks.gov.il) could not be reached on 18 October, so this
  note makes no claim about it, and no tag is transferred from the Etzion bloc to any row above.
- Atarot's remaining defenders were ordered out on 14 May, also before the cutoff. The Eretz Israel Museum records
  that date ([Atarot exhibition](https://www.eretzmuseum.org.il/en/exhibitions/atarot/), checked: 2026-10-18).
- Naharayim is a distinct place from the roster's Gesher. It is not substituted for Gesher.
- Masada, Sha'ar HaGolan, Gezer, Mishmar HaYarden, Kfar Darom, Neve Yaakov, Beit HaArava, Kalia and Hartuv are useful
  comparison cases in the wider history, but they are not approved player-locality rows in `docs/locations.md`.
  They are therefore not silently added to the game scope here.
- Ramat Rachel and the radar camp are not localities in the approved roster and are not added here.

This scope check found no fourth approved player-side roster locality tied by the sources read to a dated loss after
15 May 1948. That is a roster-scoped conclusion, not a claim that only three Jewish localities were lost in the war.

## What-if capture cards

### Safe factual structure

A card for a locality that was not lost historically may contain four clearly separated kinds of statement:

1. **Simulation fact:** “Enemy forces have taken {locality}.” This reports the current game state and must not be
   presented as recorded history.
2. **Fixed sourced fact about the place:** its period name, kind, position or documented strategic setting, cited to
   the locality's approved source. Do not infer a garrison, resident count, destruction or civilian outcome from that
   background.
3. **Comparable recorded pattern:** in a separate sentence, say that recorded 1948 losses included surrender
   (Nitzanim), withdrawal followed by enemy control (Yad Mordechai), and the fall of an urban quarter after a siege
   (the Jewish Quarter). The cited comparison must keep its own place and date. It is context, not a prediction for
   the captured locality.
4. **Design consequences:** any effects, choices or numbers are labelled design assumptions, “not a historical figure
   and not a moral judgement,” and require the owner's approval. No positive reward may attach to civilian harm.

The card may ask a sober present-tense decision such as whether to organize a counterattack, stabilize a nearby line,
or account for missing defenders only when that option exists in approved simulation rules. It may not assert that
residents fled, were expelled, were killed, were taken prisoner, or that buildings were destroyed unless a rule or
the current simulation state independently establishes that exact fact. A comparable historical outcome cannot be
transplanted to the alternate locality.

### Alternate-timeline provenance and gates

The alternate-timeline plan permits sourced statements about what a party publicly threatened or promised and what
officials recorded that they feared or expected; it forbids stating what “would have happened.” Applied here:

- use conditional attribution (“Officials warned that …”) with the view-holder and date, never omniscient prediction;
- never invent an action, quotation or fate for a real person;
- keep each historical comparison's source and `checked:` date on the eventual card;
- mark any hypothetical numerical effect as a design value, not evidence;
- add `massacre` only when the card itself states a source-supported massacre claim; never inherit it merely because
  another loss is used as a comparison;
- submit the draft to the owner before it enters `content/`; a sensitive card also needs the owner's sensitivity
  review; and
- under R-ALT-011, a consequence card or a card with alternate-timeline variants cannot enter a shareable build
  without a person's `reviewed_by` sign-off. An agent never writes that sign-off.

### Minimal card pattern for owner review

> **{Locality} has fallen**  
> Enemy forces have taken {locality}. [Simulation state, not historical narration.] In the recorded 1948 war,
> Yad Mordechai was evacuated on 24 May, the Jewish Quarter fell on 28 or 29 May [C], and Nitzanim surrendered on
> 7 June. Those cases do not establish what has happened to this locality's residents or defenders. What orders
> should be given under the approved capture rules?

This is a structure, not final player-facing prose. The eventual card should normally use only the closest relevant
comparison, not all three, and must cite that comparison directly. Its options and effects wait for the capture PR's
approved rules and the owner's wording and sensitivity review.
