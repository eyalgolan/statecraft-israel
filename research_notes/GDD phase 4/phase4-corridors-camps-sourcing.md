## 1. The Tulkarm bypass, 1949 (Q-RAIL-1, Task 2.0)

The corridors note records: "The bypass's own alignment west of Tulkarm is not given by any source read [U]." What a dated press pass adds:

- **[V] The bypass was completed by 4 August 1949.** Haaretz, 4 August 1949, headline read on the scan: "Train services to Haifa in September - the railway section bypassing Tulkarm completed" (הושלם קטע המסילה העוקף את טול כרם); trains were to run Tel Aviv-Haifa via Petah Tikva-Rosh HaAyin from mid-September. https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/haretz/1949/08/04/01/article/12, checked 2026-10-15.
- **[V] The line opened 20 September 1949.** Ma'ariv, 20 September 1949, page one, headline read on the scan: "The railway line along the Shfela was opened" (נפתח קו הרכבת לאורך השפלה); the OCR transcript has the first train Haifa-Tel Aviv running that morning with a ceremony. https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/mar/1949/09/20/01/article/1, checked 2026-10-15.
- **The alignment in words:** the new track ran right along the Green Line south of Hadera (התוואי החדש, מדרום לחדרה, עבר ממש על הקו הירוק) - this phrasing appears in HE Wikipedia "היסטוריה של רכבת ישראל" **without a citation [U]**; it is NOT in "רכבת ישראל". A section of about 2 km near Tulkarm station stayed in Jordanian hands [S: HE Wikipedia "תחנת הרכבת טולכרם", citing the dated press, checked 2026-10-15]. **[C]** the two wiki articles also clash on dating: "רכבת ישראל" dates the bypass to September 1949 (the opening), "היסטוריה של רכבת ישראל" to August (the completion) - the dated press supports both as written here (completed by 4 August, opened 20 September). A surveyed alignment is in no source read; whether the alignment-in-words claim is usable at all, and how fine the corridor's trace may be, stays with the owner (Q-RAIL-1).
- **[C]** EN Wikipedia "Palestine Railways" says Israel "bypassed Tulkarm with a short stretch of new track" in August 1948 (a war-time track?); the dated press records completion 4 August 1949 and service from 20 September 1949. Recorded, not resolved. The review of this note adds that Cotterell, "The Railways of Palestine and Israel" (1984), p. 86, traces both dates - recorded from the review, not read here [S].
- **Still [U]:** the 7 October 1948 Latrun-road bus agreement (corridors note) - not checked; no plan row waits on it.

## 2. Remez Junction to Hadera by end of 1951

The corridors note's [U] on the Davar citation clears. **[V]** Davar, 29 November 1951, "The Tel Aviv-Herzliya line near completion": the article itself says the rails were already laid from Remez Junction to the new Hadera station, and the laying to Herzliya would finish within weeks. https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/dav/1951/11/29/01/article/49, checked 2026-10-15. ## 3. Camp-site candidates (Task 1.0, Q-CAMP-2)

Candidates, each with its place and date sourced; the plan's rule applies: "If a candidate has no source for its place and its date, it is not a site."

- **Tzrifin (Sarafand), near Rishon LeZion - the strongest candidate.** The British left 15 May 1948; Givati captured the camp 18-19 May 1948 [S: HE Wikipedia "צריפין"; the state's memorial page at https://www.izkor.gov.il/monument/en_ce95202c4ac303d9789b983d629a887b; both checked 2026-10-15]. It became an IDF camp at once, and Bahad 4, the national basic-training base, sat there **from 1949 [U: HE Wikipedia "בה"ד 4" gives the year without a citation, checked 2026-10-15]**. A prisoner-of-war camp on the site held Arabs during the war (over 1,100 in December 1948; closed summer 1949) - any card text built from this goes through D-F1 review.
- **Schneller camp, Jerusalem.** The British evacuated 17 March 1948 and handed the camp to Etzioni ("permanent camp no. 1"; cited there to Yanait and to Avrahami and Etzion, "HaHagana BeYerushalayim", vol. 2, pp. 96, 98); it was the Jerusalem district headquarters, and Harel's fifth battalion based there for operations in the city, 1948 [S: HE Wikipedia "בית היתומים שנלר", checked 2026-10-15; a palmach.org.il archive caption, https://palmach.org.il/archive/galleries/galleryphoto/?itemId=35129]. Its description as Etzioni's training camp rests on a later source [U: inn.co.il, https://www.inn.co.il/news/182116, checked 2026-10-15] - no dated source read. **Owner ruling needed:** the camp was held before 15 May 1948; #608 excluded Tel HaShomer on exactly that ground, so Schneller's candidacy under Q-CAMP-2 is the owner's call.
- **Julis camp, near Gedera - weaker anchor.** Golani camped there from December 1948 (before Operations Assaf and Horev) - an operational presence, not a training-base claim; the row is Wikipedia-only in `content/map/units_1948.yaml` (checked 2026-10-11 by that PR; re-verified 2026-10-15 on HE Wikipedia "חטיבת גולני").
- **Ein Shemer, near Hadera - weaker anchor.** The 7th Brigade "organised for a time" there in June 1948 - operational, Wikipedia-only (same file; re-verified 2026-10-15 on HE Wikipedia "חטיבה 7").

## 4. The Hula swamp site against Task 5.0 criterion 4

Recorded factually for the owner, per the criterion's exception clause: the Hula marsh's papyrus economy was worked by the Ghawarna villages. Al-Salihiyya, in the marsh area four km north of the lake, was depopulated on 25 May 1948 in Operation Yiftach [S: HE Wikipedia "א-סאלחיה" (the mainstream-citable Israeli source); PalQuest, https://www.palquest.org/en/place/16877/al-salihiyya; Zochrot, https://www.zochrot.org/villages/village_details/49353/en; EN Wikipedia; all checked 2026-10-15], as were neighbouring marsh villages (al-Dawwara, al-Muftakhira, others). The lake bed itself was concession land, but the swamp site's surroundings include depopulated villages whose lands met the marsh. Whether the site's hex clears criterion 4 is the owner's call.
