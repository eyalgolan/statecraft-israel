# Israeli localities founded in the Jordan Valley from 1978 to 1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays paused
until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of the [1977-1993
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) sections 7.3 and 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the
localities of the CBS rows of the Arvot HaYarden and Megilot Dead Sea regional councils with a founding year of 1978 to 1993,
Nahal outposts included. **The rest of the West Bank, Gaza, Sinai and the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines and
the lines are not in this note.** Every Judea and Samaria row of the CBS 2024 file with a founding year of 1978-1993 whose local authority is Arvot HaYarden or Megilot Yam HaMelah is in this note; the other 68 Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 (recounted from the CBS file, see "How it was done") are not covered here and are not in any note yet. East Jerusalem neighbourhoods are not covered. Which rows enter the game, and their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#118); this note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no statement on the status of any place beyond what a source says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and of the sibling note `../Era 1957-77/jordan-valley-localities-1967-77.md` (PR #1658, not on main when this note was written). Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise. The second pass on the [GAP] rows (Refs #165) opened the sources marked U1 to U7, C1 and K1 in the section "Second-pass sources" on **26 October 2026** (UTC, the box's clock); its additions are marked "Second pass" in each row and are all [S] or [C] except C1, which carries the verified tag with the day it was read.

The question: which Israeli localities, kibbutz, moshav, community settlement or Nahal outpost, were founded in the Jordan Valley between 1978 and 1993, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (Judea and Samaria, the file's own district name), whose local authority is "ערבות הירדן" (Arvot HaYarden) or "מגילות ים המלח" (Megilot Dead Sea), with a year of founding of 1978 to 1993: **10 rows** (7 in Arvot HaYarden, 3 in Megilot). By CBS form code: 4 of code 310, 1 of 320, 1 of 330, 4 of 370. By CBS year: 1978 (1), 1979 (1), 1980 (4), 1982 (1), 1983 (2), 1986 (1). **No row has a year of 1987 to 1993**; after 1986 the next row of the two councils is 2019 (Mevo'ot Yeriho). All 10 are in the CBS subdistrict "ירדן-יריחו" (Jordan-Jericho).
- **The council name.** The task named "Bik'at HaYarden". The CBS file prints "ערבות הירדן" (Arvot HaYarden); I took the CBS spelling and treat the two as one council, as the sibling note does (English Wikipedia, oldid 1379722839, titles the article "Bik'at HaYarden Regional Council").
- **Dates.** **None of the 10 founding days is closed from a record of the day.** Every row carries a disagreement [C] on the year or on what the date marks (an outpost, a planned site, a move, a name change, the civil settling), except Shadmot Mehola (1979) and Na'ama (1982), where the sources read in the first pass agreed on the year (the second pass found a different year for both, see the second-pass bullet below); for those two the day is [GAP]. The only primary documents I could open are three Official Gazette name lists (Yalkut HaPirsumim 2633, 3165 and 4012), which date a name, not a founding, and give a map grid for 8 of the 10 sites (a ninth entry, spelled "רֹתֶם" in the Gazette, is the same name as Rotem; that it is the same place is not shown).
- **Coordinates.** All 10 rows have CBS coordinates (derived to WGS84, method below). For 8 rows the Gazette grid is an independent cross-check; the largest gap is Bet HaArava (about 1.8 km), and English Wikipedia says that the settlement moved 2 km west in 2000.
- **Press.** JTA (Jewish Telegraphic Agency) coverage of these places is thin: five items are used (Tomer, Vered Yeriho, Na'ama), none gives a founding day. **No government record, Knesset record or newspaper of the day was opened** (the National Library of Israel returns HTTP 403 to this server, so every Maariv and Davar article that Hebrew Wikipedia cites is unopened). Every founding date is [S] or [C]; the verified items are what CBS and the Gazette print.
- **Second pass (26 Oct 2026).** The [GAP] rows were searched again in sources reachable from here: seven UN Special Committee reports (A/33/356, A/34/631, A/35/425, A/36/579, A/37/485, A/38/409, A/39/591; read from the page images; [S], because each paragraph relays Israeli or Arab press whose date it prints), the State Comptroller's report 30 (one printed primary fact, verified-tagged: a fourth regional council, Bik'at HaYarden, with 16 localities, was set up in January 1980), the CBS 2010 key of localities, and 17 more JTA queries. **What changed:** Shadmot Mehola and Na'ama are now [C] (a UN annex says 1978 for Shadmot Mehola; press relayed by the UN says Na'ama was already established on 31 December 1980, and that settlers moved in between 2 December 1981 and 7 January 1982, the range of press dates printed); press dates are added for Bet HaArava (15 and 19 Aug 1980), Wered Yeriho (3 to 15 Nov and 31 Dec 1980), Yafit (Aug 1980) and for three planning mentions of 1982 (Hemdat, Maskiyyot, Mul Nevo); a 1978 item names a place "Rotem" near Pe'za'el. **No founding day is closed** and no source I could open gives one; every disagreement of year stays [C].

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a primary document (a CBS publication, an Official Gazette page) prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Wikipedia, Gvati and JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named. A derived value is never given the verified tag.
- Quotes are copied from the page read. Hebrew Wikipedia quotes are from the wikitext of the revision named in the last table, with link brackets removed; quotes from the Gazette were read from the page image (the PDF's text layer is OCR and wrong in places); JTA quotes are from the archive page text, with the PDF dateline read from the scan. Translations from Hebrew are mine.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Settlement", "territories" and "West Bank" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"), https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25, 370,654 bytes, SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet). Filter: column "שם מחוז" (district name) = "אזור יהודה ושומרון", column "שם רשות מקומית" (local authority name) = "ערבות הירדן" or "מגילות ים המלח", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1978 to 1993 (a script returned exactly the 10 rows below). **Derived:** the file has 154 Judea and Samaria rows, 78 of them with a year of 1978 to 1993, so 68 such rows (recounted again in the review pass: 154, 78, 10 and 68) are in other councils and not in this note, nor in any note yet; with the two councils named but no year the file has the rows "נפת ירדן מ"א 74", "נפת ירדן מ"א 75" and "שלומציון" (see "Rows outside the filter"). The CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, opened today) says in its general section that the file "כולל את היישובים הישראליים באזור יהודה ושומרון" (includes the Israeli localities in the Judea and Samaria area); the filter is that CBS category and nothing more. Its section 12 says: “לפי נסיבות המקרה, נרשמה שנת הייסוד של היישוב או שנת אכלוסו בידי יהודים” (as the case may be, the year of founding of the locality or the year it was populated by Jews was recorded) and “אם ההפסקה בהתיישבות הייתה ארוכה, צוינה שנת ההתיישבות האחרונה” (if the break in settling was long, the last year of settling was recorded), which is the rule behind Bet HaArava's 1980 although the 1939 kibbutz of that name stood on a different site.
- **What the form codes mean.** The 2024 file does not list the code meanings. I read the form printed for each symbol in the CBS publication "Local authorities in Israel 2009", list 03 (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, opened today), matching on the 4-digit locality symbol. **Derived:** 8 of the 10 symbols are in that list: 310 = "מושב (לרבות כפרי שיתופי)" for Tomer, Wered Yeriho, Yafit and Na'ama; 320 = "מושב שיתופי" for Shadmot Mehola; 330 = "קיבוץ" for Bet HaArava; 370 = "יישוב קהילתי" (community settlement) for Hemdat and Avenat. Rotem (3782) and Maskiyyot (3785) are not in the 2009 list (the symbol string is not in the extracted text), so their code 370 is read by analogy only. **[GAP]** The mapping is mine; the CBS does not state it for the 2024 file. Second pass (26 Oct): I also read appendix C ("מפתח יישובים במועצות אזוריות", 2010) of the CBS "Local authorities in Israel 2010" (K1 in the second-pass sources): it prints "יישוב קהילתי" for symbol 3786 (Avenat, code 370 in the 2024 file) and "מושב (לרבות כפרי שיתופי)" for 3713 (Na'ama, code 310), and neither 3782 (Rotem) nor 3785 (Maskiyyot) is in its text layer; the CBS files of 2019 and 2022 print the same code and year for the two rows as the 2024 file (370, 1983 and 370, 1986). The mapping stays [GAP] for Rotem and Maskiyyot.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine (the intro's section 14 describes a different format, ten digits in hundreds of metres, which the 12-digit field does not match). **Derived** cross-checks: (a) English Wikipedia's own coordinates for the same 10 articles (MediaWiki API `prop=coordinates`, revisions in the last table), haversine distance, median 90 m, largest 248 m (Yafit); (b) the Official Gazette grid, see the next bullet. No map was opened; each coordinate is one point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Tomer, Bet HaArava, Hemdat) the first site is elsewhere.
- **Official Gazette (Yalkut HaPirsumim), the one primary source opened.** The Government Names Committee's lists are published there. The publisher nevo.co.il serves a captcha page to this server in place of the PDFs, so I read the Internet Archive copies of the same files: G1 = issue 2633, 10 June 1980 (26 Sivan 5740), https://web.archive.org/web/20131224094153id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-2633.pdf (704,197 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `e3bcd6b7`); G2 = issue 3165, 24 February 1985 (3 Adar 5745), https://web.archive.org/web/20131005000648id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf (566,094 bytes, `f40db934`); G3 = issue 4012, 4 June 1992 (3 Sivan 5752), https://web.archive.org/web/20160303170912id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-4012.pdf (729,916 bytes, `5cfae6ef`). Each issue states the period of the names it lists: G1 from 1 January 1969 (11 Tevet 5729) to 26 June 1979 (1 Tammuz 5739), signed 31 December 1979 (11 Tevet 5740); G2 from 26 June 1979 to 22 June 1984 (22 Sivan 5744); G3 from 22 June 1984 to 6 February 1992 (2 Adar I 5752). **Derived** (Python `convertdate.hebrew.to_gregorian`, checked for all the dates here). So a name printed in an issue was fixed within that issue's period; the issue does not say on which day. Each entry gives a grid "נ"צ" of two numbers. **Derived:** the old Israel grid is, to within some hundreds of metres, the ITM less (50,000 m, 500,000 m); I used that rule of thumb (mine, not an official conversion) to compare the grid with the CBS point, the difference is in the table "Coordinates".
- **Founding days: Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (opened 2026-10-25, 1,348,249 bytes). Its narrative of the Valley ends with the settlements of 1977 (the passage closes with its count of 21) and its appendix (name, region, date of founding, affiliation, origin of the name) has no row for any of the 10 places: a full-text search of the file for each Hebrew name returned one place hit, Tomer in the narrative (below), and four hits of "בית־הערבה" that are all about the 1939 kibbutz. So Gvati does not date any of these founding days. [GAP]
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S].
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches through the archive's wp-json endpoint, every query and count in "Searches". For every item used I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read it as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR (the OCR drops spaces and mangles some words), and took the dateline from it. **Dates rule:** the JTA archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF and from "yesterday", "last night" or a weekday read against that dateline.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`.
