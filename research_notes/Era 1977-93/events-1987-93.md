# 1977-93 events research: 9 December 1987 to 12 September 1993

Researched 23 October 2026. Research only, for issue #163: nothing here changes `content/`, no event
is drafted, and there are no proposed tags, options, effects or card text. Every URL was opened on
23 October 2026 unless it is marked as not opened.

**Purpose.** A sourced list of the events of the window the issue sets, 9 December 1987 to
12 September 1993. For each: the date or dates, a short factual summary, the sources, and both
values where sources disagree. Which rows enter the game, how they are shown, and their sensitivity
treatment are the owner's, and nothing is proposed about them here.

**On the exemplar.** `events-1952-56.md` is the format this issue names, and its sourcing
conventions are followed. Its proposed sensitivity tags and candidate decisions are **not** copied:
the issue rules them out. That omission is deliberate.

**Conventions** (as in `events-1952-56.md`)
- **[V]** verified: the cited page was opened, and the fact does not rest on Wikipedia alone.
- **[S]** single source, a page not opened in full, a day worked out from a JTA bulletin date, or a
  fact resting only on Wikipedia. Wikipedia, Hebrew and English, is used as a pointer; it is cited
  by `oldid` so the revision read is fixed, but a fact carried only by it is [S].
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given with its own source, and none is picked.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- **JTA dates.** The date on a JTA archive page is the bulletin date. "Today" in an item means its
  dispatch day, usually the day before. A weekday named in a bulletin is resolved against the
  bulletin date and the result marked **computed here**.
- **No quotes are invented.** The short phrases in quotation marks are copied from the cited page.
- **computed here** marks arithmetic or a date resolution done for this note.
- **our reading** marks an interpretation, and is not attributed to a source.

**Casualty figures.** This window is the most contested in the project. Every figure below carries
the source that gives it, and where sources differ all the figures are listed rather than
reconciled. No figure here should be used without the source beside it.

## A1. Outbreak of the first intifada, 8 to 9 December 1987

**The trigger, 8 December 1987.** An Israeli lorry hit Palestinian vehicles near the Erez
checkpoint and four Palestinian workers were killed. **[C] on the place and on where the four
lived.** he.wiki `האינתיפאדה הראשונה`, oldid **43899795**: "נהג משאית ישראלי פגע בשתי מוניות מדרום
למחסום ארז, בארבע אחר הצהריים ב-8 בדצמבר 1987. בתאונה נהרגו ארבעה פועלים פלסטינים ממחנה הפליטים
ג'באליה". [S] en.wiki `First Intifada`, oldid **1378812902**, places it at the Erez checkpoint:
"Four Palestinians, three of them residents of the Jabalya refugee camp ... were killed and seven
others seriously injured". [S]

**Whether it was deliberate.** he.wiki says rumours spread that day that the collision was not an
accident but deliberate revenge for the murder of an Israeli. en.wiki: "Palestinians
charged that the collision was a deliberate response for the killing of an Israeli in Gaza days
earlier", and "Israel denied that the crash, which came at time of heightened tensions, was
intentional or coordinated". Both are recorded and neither is adopted. [S]

**The first day, 9 December 1987.** he.wiki states it directly: "יום זה, 9 בדצמבר 1987, נחשב ליום
הראשון של האינתיפאדה". [S] The article attributes this to Schiff and Ya'ari, *Intifada*, p. 13,
which was not opened. The unrest reached East Jerusalem on 19 December 1987. [S]

**[C] on the age band of the first person killed.** he.wiki describes the Palestinian killed by
gunfire as a young adult; en.wiki describes the victim as an older teenager, footnoted to the New
York Times of 10 December 1987, which was not opened. Both give 16 wounded. Both age bands are
recorded; neither is picked.

**[C] on the totals for the whole intifada.** Four figures, each with its source.
- he.wiki, citing B'Tselem: "נהרגו באינתיפאדה, מפריצתה ועד סיומה בשנת 1993 165 ישראלים ו-1,160
  פלסטינים", and separately more than a thousand Palestinians killed by other Palestinians. [S]
- en.wiki infobox: 179 to 200 killed by Palestinians, citing Nasrallah in Newman and Peters; and
  1,962 Palestinians killed, citing Kober 2005, of whom 771 to 942 by Palestinians. [S]

The two rest on different secondary works and cannot be reconciled from the pages read. Neither
Wikipedia's figures are corroborated by a non-Wikipedia source read here, so both are [S].

**Contemporary wire.** JTA, bulletin 7 February 1988, "Three Arabs Killed, As Unrest Spreads to
Jewish Neighborhoods", https://www.jta.org/archive/three-arabs-killed-as-unrest-spreads-to-jewish-neighborhoods:
"Unrest has continued almost unabated for nearly two months", and "about 170,000 Palestinians are
living under tight curfew". [V] for the state of the unrest in early February 1988. No JTA bulletin
for the December 1987 outbreak itself was found. [GAP]

## A2. Mothers' Bus attack, 7 March 1988

**Date.** he.wiki `אוטובוס האמהות`, oldid **42504168**, infobox `תאריך = 7 במרץ 1988`, and the body
"בליל יום שני, 7 במרץ 1988, חדרו לישראל ממצרים שלושה מחבלים פלסטינים חמושים". en.wiki
`1988 Negev bus hijacking`, oldid **1374876671**, gives the same date. [S]

**Summary.** Three armed men crossed from Egypt, seized a car, ran a police roadblock, and near
Ar'ara in the Negev took over a bus carrying workers to the nuclear research centre near Dimona,
holding eleven passengers. A Yamam unit stormed the bus and all three attackers were killed.

**Casualties, where both pages agree:** three Israeli civilians killed and eight wounded, plus the
three attackers. The three killed are described here by role as a male passenger and two female
passengers. [S]

**[C] inside and between the pages on the sequence.** he.wiki's lead says one passenger was murdered
before the assault and two hostages died during it; its own body says the male passenger was
executed at 10:25 and the two women were killed during the storming. en.wiki's lead says two were
executed before the assault and one hostage died during it; its body says the male passenger and
one of the female passengers were executed at 10:25. All versions are recorded.

**[C] on who ordered the assault.** he.wiki: Alik Ron. en.wiki: Mordechai. Both given.

**A quote that should not be used.** Both pages carry a remark attributed to Shamir, in Hebrew on
one and English on the other, with no footnote attached to the sentence on either. It is therefore
not a sourced quotation and is not reproduced here.

**[GAP].** No JTA bulletin for this attack was found.

## A3. Kach barred from the 1988 election, 5 to 18 October 1988

**[C] on the date of the Central Elections Committee decision. Three values.**
- **5 October 1988**: he.wiki `כך`, oldid **44098882**: "ב-5 באוקטובר 1988 נפסלה המפלגה על ידי ועדת
  הבחירות המרכזית לכנסת מלהתמודד ב... בחירות לכנסת השתים עשרה לאור העובדה שמצעה קרא להגביל נישואים
  בין ערבים ליהודים". No footnote on that sentence. [S]
- **4 October 1988**: en.wiki `1988 Israeli legislative election`, oldid **1379921032**, timeline
  row. [S]
- **6 October 1988**: JTA, bulletin 19 October 1988, "a ban imposed on his party Oct. 6 by the
  Central Election Committee",
  https://www.jta.org/archive/court-ruling-disqualifying-kahane-seen-as-boon-to-likud. [V] as a
  contemporary report.

JTA's own two bulletins disagree. The bulletin of 7 October 1988,
https://www.jta.org/archive/elections-committee-votes-not-to-ban-left-wing-party, places the Kach
vote on a Wednesday, which is 5 October 1988 (computed here): "The committee acted Thursday only a
day after it voted overwhelmingly to bar Rabbi Meir Kahane's extremist Kach Party from
participating in the elections, on grounds that it engages in incitement to racism."

**The High Court, 18 October 1988.** JTA, 19 October 1988: "a decision by Israel's highest court
Tuesday to disqualify Rabbi Meir Kahane's Kach party", a unanimous five-judge panel, with the court
ruling 3 to 2 that the Progressive List for Peace could run. Tuesday is 18 October 1988 (computed
here). [V] en.wiki `Kach`, oldid **1381507628**, renders the finding as the aims and action of Kach
being "manifestly racist". [S]

**The amendment that allowed disqualification** is Basic Law: The Knesset (Amendment No. 9),
passed 31 July 1985 per the official text, `knessets-1977-93.md` source L-BL-9. [V] he.wiki `כך`
also gives 1985. JTA of 7 October 1988 says "an amendment to the Basic Law, which the Knesset
adopted in 1984"; that report is wrong against the primary record.

**The Progressive List for Peace vote.** he.wiki `הבחירות לכנסת השתים עשרה`, oldid
**43586719**: the application to bar it was rejected "ברוב של 20 מול 19 מחברי הוועדה". JTA of
7 October 1988 says the committee chairman "cast the tie-breaking vote". The two are consistent:
a 19-19 tie broken by the chairman, Justice Eliezer Goldberg, as JTA names him. [S]

**1992.** JTA, bulletin 4 June 1992,
https://www.jta.org/archive/kahanes-son-barred-from-running-in-israels-upcoming-elections: the
committee barred Kahane Chai on Wednesday, 3 June 1992 (computed here), "25-5, with two
abstentions". [V] **[GAP]:** no page read gives a date for the parallel 1992 Kach disqualification
or the outcome of its court review.

## A5. Bus 405 attack, 6 July 1989

**Date.** he.wiki `הפיגוע בקו 405`, oldid **44033833**: "ביום חמישי, 6 ביולי 1989". en.wiki
`1989 Tel Aviv-Jerusalem bus attack`, oldid **1381081051**, same date. JTA's bulletin of 7 July 1989
reports it as the previous day. [V]

**Summary.** On the Egged intercity line 405 from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem, a passenger seized the
wheel and forced the bus into a ravine near Kiryat Ye'arim; the bus caught fire. The attacker
survived and was sentenced to sixteen life terms.

**[C] on the number killed. Two tolls, in three sources.**
- **16 killed, 27 wounded**: he.wiki, "16 מנוסעי האוטובוס נרצחו בפיגוע ו-27 נפצעו", and the infobox
  `הרוגים = 16`. [S]
- **16 killed, 27 wounded**: en.wiki, adding that two were Canadian and one American. [S]
- **14 killed, 27 wounded**: JTA, bulletin 7 July 1989, the day after, headline "14 Killed, 27
  Wounded After Arab Forces Israeli Bus into a Ravine",
  https://www.jta.org/archive/14-killed-27-wounded-after-arab-forces-israeli-bus-into-a-ravine, and
  "Fourteen people were killed and 27 injured Thursday". [V] as a contemporary wire count. The same
  item records 42 passengers and the driver, and that "two passengers were unaccounted for", which
  may be why the later figure is higher. **Our reading:** the gap is consistent with a day-after
  count being revised, but no page read says so.

**On the "first suicide attack" description.** he.wiki calls it the first suicide attack "על אף
שהמחבל לא נהרג בו". en.wiki records both that description and a contrary reading, that it has been
called a "commandeered crash" with "definitional ambiguities". Both are characterisations carried
by the articles and are recorded, not adopted. [S]

**The attacker's name.** he.wiki spells it two ways in the same article, and en.wiki has its own
transliteration. Under the owner ruling, he is referred to here by role only and those spellings
are not reproduced. [C]

## A6. The unity government falls, 13 March to 11 June 1990

From he.wiki `התרגיל המסריח`, oldid **43885916**, he.wiki `ממשלת ישראל העשרים וארבע`, oldid
**43253517**, and two JTA bulletins.

- **13 March 1990.** Shamir dismissed Peres during a cabinet meeting. [S] JTA, bulletin 14 March
  1990: "Israel's national unity government took a decisive step toward dissolution Tuesday, when
  Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir informed the Cabinet he was dismissing Vice Premier Shimon Peres.
  All other Labor Party ministers responded immediately by announcing their collective
  resignation", with a 48 hour statutory delay,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/shamir-dismisses-peres-from-cabinet-prompting-mass-defection-from-labor.
  Tuesday is 13 March 1990 (computed here). [V]
- **15 March 1990.** The Knesset carried a motion of no confidence "ברוב של 60 ח"כים מול 55", which
  he.wiki says was the first time in Israel's history a government fell that way. [S]
- **20 March 1990.** Herzog gave Peres the mandate. **11 to 12 April 1990.** Peres presented a
  government and the confirmation failed when two Agudat Yisrael members stayed away.
  **26 April 1990.** Peres returned the mandate. [S]
- **11 June 1990.** Shamir presented the 24th government. he.wiki `ממשלת ישראל העשרים וארבע`:
  `תחילת כהונה = 11 ביוני 1990`, 19 ministers, 59 coalition MKs. [S]
- **Support and coalition size are different measures.** JTA, bulletin 8 June 1990, says Shamir
  formed the government "shortly before his mandate expired at midnight Thursday" and that a
  defection "gives Shamir a 62-vote majority, just one vote more than the minimum needed to win a
  Knesset vote of confidence",
  https://www.jta.org/archive/shamir-says-he-has-now-assembled-government-with-62-vote-majority.
  [S] The confidence vote of 11 June 1990 was 62 for, 57 against and 1 abstention, per the plenum
  record in `offices-1977-93.md`. [V] he.wiki counts 59 coalition MKs from 11 June 1990, rising to
  60 on 2 July 1990. [S]
- **The name.** he.wiki dates Rabin's coining of "התרגיל המסריח" to an interview on 9 May 1990 and
  quotes him: "הבלוף הזה וכל ההסתאבות שבאה לחיים הפוליטיים בישראל בניסיון להרכיב ממשלה צרה, נכשל לא
  רק באופן טקטי אלא גם קונספטואלית". [S]

## A7. Temple Mount, 8 October 1990

**Date.** he.wiki `מהומות הר הבית (1990)`, oldid **42443327**: "ביום שני 8 באוקטובר, י"ט בתשרי, ד'
חוה"מ סוכות תשנ"א". en.wiki `1990 Temple Mount killings`, oldid **1370599709**: "at 10:30 am on
Monday, October 8, 1990". [S]

**Names.** he.wiki titles the article `מהומות הר הבית (1990)` and opens "מהומות הר הבית היו
מהומות". en.wiki titles it `1990 Temple Mount killings` and gives "the Al Aqsa Massacre" as another
name. All are recorded; none is adopted.

**Summary as the pages give it.** The Temple Mount Faithful announced a cornerstone ceremony; police
barred them from the Mount and the ceremony was held at the Western Wall plaza. Per the Zamir
commission findings as reported on the page, "ב-5 באוקטובר 1990 קרא המואזין של אל אקצה בדרשת יום
השישי שלו למוסלמים לבוא להר ולהגן עליו". About 3,000 gathered on the Mount against about 44 police.
A gas grenade slipped from a policeman's hand; stones were thrown down at the Western Wall plaza;
the Mount police post was overrun; the district commander ordered a breach and police fired live
rounds. [S]

**[C] on the casualties. Every figure with its source.**

| Figure | Source as the page gives it |
|---|---|
| 17 killed, about 200 Muslims wounded, about 20 police wounded | he.wiki body: "נהרגו 17 ונפצעו כ-200 מוסלמים" |
| 17 killed, "יותר ממאה" wounded, 20 police wounded, 34 Jewish worshippers hurt by stones | he.wiki lead |
| "בפרסומים מוסלמים דובר על 20 או 21 הרוגים" | he.wiki body |
| 17 killed, more than 150 Palestinians and more than 20 Israelis wounded | en.wiki infobox and lead |
| "All accounts originally reported anywhere between 19 and 21 deaths", lowered to 17 by PHRIC on 15 October | en.wiki footnote |
| "They killed at least 21. There were no Israeli deaths." | en.wiki, quoting Anthony Lewis, New York Times, 12 October 1990, not opened |
| more than twenty killed, more than 150 injured | UN Security Council Resolution 672, as rendered on the he.wiki page |
| 17 killed, "wounding scores more" | JTA, bulletin 19 July 1991 |

**The inquiries.** The Zamir commission reported on 26 October 1990: the riot was not pre-planned,
the police were inadequately prepared, the breach and the use of live fire were justified once lives
were at risk, but there was "השימוש המופרז בנשק חם". [S] The investigating judge Ezra Kama decided
in July 1991 that no officer should be prosecuted, and found the event began when a tear gas grenade
slipped from a Border Police officer's hand. [S] JTA, bulletin 19 July 1991, reports the same
outcome: "An investigative judge announced Thursday that there is insufficient evidence available to
incriminate any of the police officers",
https://www.jta.org/archive/police-bungled-temple-mount-riots-but-did-not-break-law-judge-finds.
With JTA alongside the article, the no-prosecution finding is [V]; its date, Thursday, is 18 July
1991 (computed here).

**UN.** The Security Council met on 12 October 1990 and adopted Resolution 672; Israel refused to
receive the Secretary-General's mission. [S]

## A8. Gulf War missile attacks on Israel, from 17 January 1991

**End dates.** en.wiki `1991 Iraqi missile attacks against Israel`, oldid **1379196818**:
missiles were fired into Israel "from 17 January to 23 February 1991". he.wiki
`ישראל במלחמת המפרץ`, oldid **43864006**, gives `תאריך סיום העימות = 28 בפברואר 1991`, the end of
the war. Different endpoints, not a conflict. [S]

**Start.** he.wiki: "מהלילה שבין 17 ל-18 בינואר 1991". [S]

**[C] on the number of missiles. Four figures, three of them inside one sentence.** he.wiki reads
literally "שיגר צבא עיראק ... 38 או 39 או 43 טילי סקאד לעבר ישראל", footnoting 38 to the Israel
MFA, 39 to the air force site and to Maariv of 26 February 1991, and 43 to a ynet piece of
6 January 2021. en.wiki gives "approximately 42". None of the underlying sources was opened. [S]

**[C] on the casualties.**
- he.wiki: three killed by missile strikes, named in the article's footnotes, and 77 recognised in
  total as casualties of hostile action during the war. [S]
- en.wiki: two civilians killed directly, and 11 to 74 killed "from incorrect use of gas masks,
  heart attacks, and incorrect use of atropine", with 230 injured. [S]

**[C] on property damage.** he.wiki: 1,302 houses, 6,142 flats, 23 public buildings, 200 shops and
50 cars. en.wiki: 4,100 buildings damaged, at least 28 destroyed, Ramat Gan worst hit. Both given.

**Restraint.** Both pages record that Israel did not retaliate, he.wiki attributing it to American
insistence out of concern for the coalition, en.wiki to Jordan and the United States. [S]

## A9. Madrid Conference, 30 October to 1 November 1991

**Dates.** he.wiki `ועידת מדריד (1991)`, oldid **43960036**: "בין 30 באוקטובר ל-1 בנובמבר 1991".
en.wiki `Madrid Conference of 1991`, oldid **1380962009**, agrees, and dates the letter of
invitation to 30 October 1991. JTA, bulletin 31 October 1991, reports Bush's opening speech
"Wednesday", 30 October 1991 (computed here), and names a "14-member Israeli delegation",
https://www.jta.org/archive/bush-urges-territorial-compromise-as-mideast-parley-opens-in-madrid.
[V]

**Summary.** Co-sponsored by the United States and the Soviet Union and hosted by Spain. The
Palestinians were represented within the Jordanian delegation. Shamir led the Israeli delegation in
person; Foreign Minister David Levy stayed away, which JTA's bulletin of 25 October 1991 reports as
a government crisis: "Levy, who had expected to be in charge, saw this as a gesture of no confidence
and announced that he would stay home",
https://www.jta.org/archive/shamir-decision-to-go-to-madrid-creates-israeli-government-crisis. [V]
Bush is quoted in the 31 October bulletin: "We believe territorial compromise is essential for
peace." [V]

**A caution about the Hebrew article.** It carries a maintenance banner,
`{{מקורות|סיבה=אין כמעט מקורות בערך}}`, meaning it is largely unfootnoted. Facts taken from it alone
are [S] and should be treated as weaker than the tag alone suggests.

**Coalition consequences, inside the window.** he.wiki `ממשלת ישראל העשרים וארבע`: Tsomet left the
coalition on 31 December 1991; Tehiya and Moledet on 21 January 1992, taking it from 66 to 59 MKs.
[S]

## A12. Thirteenth Knesset election, 23 June 1992, and Rabin's government

**Date.** he.wiki `הבחירות לכנסת השלוש עשרה`, oldid **43170738**: 23 June 1992, threshold 1.5 per
cent. The election was brought forward from 3 November 1992. [S]

## A13. Deportation of 415 activists to Lebanon, 16 to 17 December 1992

he.wiki `גירוש פעילי חמאס ללבנון`, oldid **44100428**. A Border Police officer was abducted on
13 December 1992 and his body found two days later. About 1,200 Hamas and Palestinian
Islamic Jihad activists were arrested. On the morning of 16 December 1992 the cabinet decided to
deport 415 of them to Lebanon without prior notice or a prior hearing; the deportation was carried
out on 17 December 1992 after a day's delay from a High Court petition. [S]

Lebanon refused them entry on 18 December 1992 and they camped in no man's land near Marj al-Zuhour.
[S] The page records that the Justice Minister and the Attorney-General supported the measure while
the State Attorney and a colleague held it unlawful, and that all ministers supported it except one
who abstained. [S] It also records, citing B'Tselem, that the deportees were not told where they
were being taken and that families were not notified. [S] The largest group, 181, returned on the
day the Declaration of Principles was signed. [S]

## A15. Oslo, 20 August to 12 September 1993

The window closes on 12 September 1993. The public signing is 13 September 1993, one day outside it,
and is recorded here only so the sequence is complete.

**Initialling, the night of 19 to 20 August 1993.** he.wiki `הסכם אוסלו א'`, oldid **43876238**:
"בלילה שבין 19 ל-20 באוגוסט 1993, נחתם ההסכם בראשי תיבות על ידי אבו עלא, חסן עספור, אורי סביר ויואל
זינגר". [S] en.wiki `Oslo I Accord`, oldid **1379210356**, says the negotiation was "completed on
20 August 1993". [S]

**Cabinet approval, 30 August 1993.** he.wiki: "ב-28 באוגוסט דיווח פרס על ההסכם למזכיר המדינה וורן
כריסטופר, וב-30 באוגוסט אושר ההסכם בישיבת הממשלה". [S]

**[C] on the mutual recognition sequence.** This is the sharpest conflict in the window.
- **A single date, 9 September 1993**: en.wiki
  `Israel-Palestine Liberation Organization letters of recognition`, oldid **1352596626**: "The
  Letters of Mutual Recognition were exchanged between Israel and the Palestine Liberation
  Organization (PLO) on 9 September 1993", with the letters dated 9 September 1993. [S]
- **A two-day sequence**: JTA, bulletin 10 September 1993,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-agrees-to-recognize-plo-in-a-historic-reversal-of-policy:
  Arafat signed "Thursday night" and "Rabin, in turn, was due to formalize Israel's recognition of
  the PLO by signing a letter to Arafat on Friday morning". Thursday is 9 September and Friday
  10 September 1993 (computed here). [V]
- **The inner cabinet, 9 September 1993**: JTA, bulletin 10 September 1993, same URL: "the pact
  was approved unanimously Thursday by the 10-member 'inner Cabinet' of senior ministers". [S]
- **A third cabinet step on the window's last day**: JTA, bulletin 13 September 1993,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/israeli-cabinet-endorses-plan-giving-plo-formal-recognition: "The
  Cabinet on Sunday endorsed Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin's decision Friday to extend formal
  Israeli recognition to the PLO". Sunday is 12 September 1993 (computed here), the last day of this
  window. [V]

**Our reading:** the inner cabinet's approval (9 September), Rabin's signing (10 September per
both JTA bulletins) and the full cabinet's endorsement (12 September) are separate acts. That does
not explain why en.wiki dates both letters, Rabin's included, 9 September. Whether that is the date
written on the letters rather than the day Rabin signed is a [GAP]; the [C] stands.

**Outside the window.** The signing in Washington on 13 September 1993, and the Knesset confidence
vote of 23 September 1993, 61 to 50 with 8 abstentions per en.wiki. [S]
