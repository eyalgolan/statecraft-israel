# Israeli localities and Nahal outposts founded in the Gaza Strip, 1978 to 1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays paused
until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), "settlements founded 1977-93" in [the 1977-1993
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) section 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the Israeli
localities and Nahal outposts founded in the Gaza Strip proper between 1 January 1978 and 31 December 1993. **The Rafah
approaches (Hebrew Pithat Rafiah) and Sinai, the West Bank, the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines and the
lines are not in this note, and neither is the 1967-77 Gaza note (`research_notes/Era 1957-77/gaza-localities-1967-77.md`) which this note
cross-references and does not redo.** Which rows enter the game, and their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list
(#150); this note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no statement on the status or legality of any
place beyond what a source says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and the conventions of the
sibling notes in this folder and in `Era 1957-77` (`research_notes/Era 1957-77/sinai-localities-1967-77.md`, `research_notes/Era 1957-77/development-towns-1957-77.md`). Every page
below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which Israeli localities and Nahal outposts were founded in the Gaza Strip between 1978 and 1993, and for
each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Where the line between this note and the Rafah approaches falls

The next paragraph is copied word for word from `research_notes/Era 1957-77/sinai-localities-1967-77.md` (on main); there "this note" is the Sinai note and "the Gaza Strip note" is `research_notes/Era 1957-77/gaza-localities-1967-77.md`, not this one.

> **Where the line between this note and the Gaza Strip note falls (same line as in `gaza-localities-1967-77.md`,
> the Gaza Strip note of #130, `gaza-localities-1967-77.md`).** A locality is in the Gaza Strip note if the sources put it inside the Strip, the area Egypt
> held between 1949 and June 1967 north and west of the Egyptian frontier that the armistice line of 24 February 1949
> (Art. VI.1, "continuing thus to the Egyptian frontier"; `armistice-lines-1949.md`) stops at. It is in this note if
> the sources put it in the Rafah approaches (Hebrew Pithat Rafiah), which Gvati describes as "פיתחת רפיח, המשתרעת בין
> רפיח למבואות אלעריש לאורך חוף הים" and as "אזור חיץ בין רצועת עזה" ... "לבין מרחבי סיני" (a buffer zone between the Strip and the Sinai expanses) (Gvati, vol. 2,
> Ben-Yehuda edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684, section "רצועת עזה ופיתחת רפיח", read 2026-10-25). By that
> split the Gaza note has Kfar Darom, Netzarim, Morag, Netzer Hazani and Katif (Netzer Hazani's appendix row says "פיתחת רפיח"
> but Gvati's narrative puts it in the Katif area of the Strip, so it goes to the Gaza note; Katif has no appendix row); this note has the
> Rafah-approaches and Sinai rows of the table above. Nearest to the line: Morag (the southernmost
> locality of the Strip: "between Khan Yunis and Rafah" in Hebrew WP, "3 km south of Khan Yunis" in Gvati) and, on this side, Dikla, Sadot
> and Netiv HaAsara. No locality is on both lists. This is a reading of the sources named, not a surveyed boundary.

For 1978 to 1993 the same line is used. Rows with an event before 1978 and a later one: this note keeps **only the 1978-1993
events** of Morag, Katif, Netzarim and Kfar Darom (rows 1, 2, 12 and 15, marked "overlap"), and the founding events of 1967 to
1977 stay in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/gaza-localities-1967-77.md` (rows 1 to 5 there). Netzer Hazani (Nahal outpost 29 May 1973, civilian February 1977) is
only in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/gaza-localities-1967-77.md` (row 4) and has no row here. Nothing in the Rafah approaches or Sinai is a row.

## Short answer

- **The list cannot come from the CBS 2024 localities file or from Gvati's appendix.** These places were evacuated in August
  2005 (Hebrew WP, Gush Katif page, oldid 43690979) and the CBS file has no row for any of them: it has rows with similar names (Bnei Netzarim, Ganei Tal, Karmei
  Katif, Netzer Hazani, and Bne Dkalim, symbol 1368, year 2009, Ashkelon), all later localities with years of founding 2009 to 2016 (section "How it was done"). Gvati's
  appendix and narrative stop at 1977: his only Gaza Strip row is Netzer Hazani (29.5.73), and none of the 14 new rows
  is in his text (Morag, Katif, Netzarim and Kfar Darom, rows 1, 2, 12 and 15, are in his narrative). So membership is built from Hebrew Wikipedia's pages on Gush Katif and on each locality (the list of 16
  in the bloc and the five outside it, 21 in all, as the Gush Katif page counts them) and cross-checked against English
  Wikipedia and JTA. English WP's Gush Katif page (oldid 1371988823) counts 17 places in the bloc, Kfar Darom included, and its "Date of establishment" table lists 17 names: 16 of the 18 rows plus Netzer Hazani (Slav and Tel Katifa are not in it).
- **18 rows**, 14 new and 4 overlap rows (Morag, Katif, Netzarim, Kfar Darom), 13 of them on Hebrew WP's list of 16 places in the Gush Katif bloc and 5 outside it (Netzarim, Kfar Darom in the centre; Nisanit, Elei Sinai and Dugit in the north). Three places of that list of 21 are outside the window and have no row: Netzer Hazani (1977),
  Kerem Atzmona (2000 or 2001) and Shirat HaYam (2001); see "Outside the window".
- **Tags.** 12 rows are [C] (the sources disagree on the year, or give one year for two different events) and 6 are [S]. Four of the 12 (rows 3, 8, 12 and 15) are [C] only because of the date values in English WP's Gush Katif table; without it they would be [S]. No row is settled between [C] values.
  The only items tagged [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] are what the CBS file prints.
- **Press.** JTA's archive has few items that name these places for 1978 to 1993: of the 1344 items returned by the 29 queries of
  the first run (the same item can come back for several queries), 36 distinct items contain a row name (the rule is under "Searches"), and the earliest of them that names a Gaza Strip place is dated 17 Aug 1984. Eight
  bulletin PDFs were opened and their datelines read. They support: three new settlements formally established in the Strip on 22 Jul 1984
  (no names; Bilig and Huberman (2019) p. 249 name Netzarim and Nisanit among the places civilianised that day, see rows 9, 12, 13 and 17), and the state of Dugit in November 1989 and April 1990 (row 17). **No government record, Knesset record, Official Gazette
  item or Statistical Abstract was opened**; every founding date below is [S], [C] or [GAP]. That is the main gap of the note.
- **A count I could not reconcile (derived).** JTA, bulletin of 19 Apr 1990 (dateline 18 Apr), says Dugit "will be the 16th
  settlement in the Gaza Strip since it was captured from Egypt in 1967", and on 1 Feb 1990 (dateline 31 Jan) puts "15 Jewish
  settlements" in Gush Katif. Of Hebrew WP's 21 places, 17 had a founding event before April 1990 (21 minus Dugit, Tel Katifa,
  Shirat HaYam and Kerem Atzmona), so 17 against JTA's 15 existing: a difference of 2, not reconciled. Slav (a camp from 1982, resettled 2001) and
  Pe'at Sadeh (1989, inside that camp) might be the two; that is a guess, not checked.
- **Coordinates:** every row has a Hebrew WP point and an English WP point (from the MediaWiki GeoData `coordinates` property of each page);
  GeoNames has a point for 16 of the 18 rows (none under the name Slav, see "How it was done"; for Tel Katifa only a hill, class T). No coordinate comes from a map I opened, and none is a survey of a founding site.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what the CBS 2024 file prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Hebrew and English Wikipedia (each revision named by oldid), Gvati, Bilig and Huberman (2019), the UN reports and the JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record; the UN reports quote the press).
  Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper (Davar, Maariv), a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read; I opened none of them.
- **Tag rule used throughout:** [S] where the sources agree on the year of the locality's founding event even if a page also records later stages; [C] where they give different years, or one year for different events.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or date this note computed; the arithmetic or the tool is named (Hebrew calendar dates: Python `convertdate` 2.5.1; distances: haversine, 6,371 km, computed from the unrounded source coordinates, so a distance can differ in the last digit from one recomputed from the five-decimal points shown).
- Hebrew quotes are given without vowel signs; translations are mine. English quotes from JTA are copied from the archive page text, and the dateline
  and event day come from the bulletin PDF; English quotes from Wikipedia are from the wikitext of the named revision. Each quoted string was checked by script against the page text I fetched.
- A search snippet or an unopened source is never used as a source: places and dates known only from one are [GAP].

## What was not done

- **No primary list and no primary founding record.** No Official Gazette notice, Knesset record, Government decision, State Archives or Jewish Agency record, and no Statistical Abstract of Israel was opened for any row. The Statistical
  Abstract was not looked for in this pass; `research_notes/Era 1957-77/sinai-localities-1967-77.md` ("What was not done") records that its Internet Archive search for the Abstract of 1978 to 1981 returned 0 volumes.
  The founding days therefore rest on Hebrew Wikipedia (which cites Davar and Maariv for several of them) and the state's own record of each founding is the main gap.
- **Official Gazette.** Hebrew WP cites Yalkut Hapirsumim 3165 (24 Feb 1985, p. 1491) for the Government Names Committee's list that cancelled the name "Amber" for Neve Dekalim. `http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf` answered HTTP 301 with an empty body; the
  Internet Archive's copy `web.archive.org/web/20260702061406if_/https://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf` answered HTTP 429 on the one try. The notice was not read.
- **Davar and Maariv** (which Hebrew WP cites on the Gush Katif page for 1977 to 1982 items, for example Davar 24 Aug 1977, Maariv 17 Nov 1978, 4 Jan 1980, 7 Jan 1980, 5 Sep 1980, 31 Dec 1982) and the two Maariv items of 23.7.1984 that Bilig and Huberman cite on p. 249 were not opened; the National Library's newspaper archive and the Historical Jewish Press were not searched.
- **JTA coverage is thin** for 1978 to 1983 and the search is capped at 100 returned items per query; see "Searches". JTA items returned but not opened (archive text only, used for no date): 30 of the 36 items that contain a row name, which are about attacks, visits or politics, not foundings.
- **The Gush Katif album and the Katif.net community pages** that Hebrew WP links (and the printed album that `research_notes/Era 1957-77/gaza-localities-1967-77.md` read) were not opened; the 1980s community dates in them are not checked.
- **No map.** No coordinate comes from a map I opened; Wikipedia GeoData points and GeoNames points are not surveys, and for the Nahal outposts that later became localities the first site is not given by any source I read.
- **No row was settled between [C] values.** The note does not choose one date.
- **No naming or status decision.** Place names follow Hebrew Wikipedia's transliteration as I read it in Latin letters (several variants exist); this is not a naming proposal.
