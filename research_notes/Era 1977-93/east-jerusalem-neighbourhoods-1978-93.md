# New Israeli neighbourhoods in East Jerusalem, 1978 to 1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays
paused until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of the
[1977-1993 design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) sections 7.3 and 7.4. One topic of
#165 only: the new Israeli residential neighbourhoods built, planned or started inside the Jerusalem municipal boundary
as extended on 27-28 June 1967 (the boundary is in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/lines-1967-74.md`, section 2), from 1
January 1978 to 31 December 1993. Facts only: this note gives each source's dates and wording and takes no side on
status or legality; where a source states a legal position it is quoted and attributed. Which rows enter the game, and
their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#150); this note makes no include, omit or naming choice.

**Where this note sits among its siblings.** The 1967-77 neighbourhoods inside the boundary are in
`research_notes/Era 1957-77/east-jerusalem-neighbourhoods-1967-77.md` (PR #1683, open when this note was written; its
rows are not redone here, only their 1978-93 later stages). East Jerusalem neighbourhoods inside the municipal boundary
are in none of the Judea and Samaria notes of 1978-93: not in
`research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (PR #1665, merged) nor in the notes of the councils
listed in it, so none of the 68 Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 that #1665 lists as in no note yet is taken by this
note; they are not in this note. Arab neighbourhoods and villages, the lines, and localities outside the boundary
(Ma'ale Adummim, Givat Ze'ev and the rest of the West Bank) are not in it.

It follows the format of `research_notes/Era 1957-77/development-towns-1957-77.md` and of
[`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md). Every page below was opened on **25 October
2026** unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which new residential neighbourhoods were planned, expropriated for, started or first settled inside the
municipal boundary between 1978 and 1993, and for each: the planning or land-taking decision if a source dates it, the
start of construction, the first residents, the place (coordinates from a named source), and the sources.

## Short answer

- **There is no official list in what I could open.** The list is built from named secondary sources (section "Which
  list"), not from the CBS localities file: the neighbourhoods are parts of Jerusalem, so the localities file has no
  rows for them (the 1967-77 sibling note says the same and gives CBS code 3000; I did not reopen the CBS file for this
  note). The choice of members is the owner's.
- **Four new neighbourhoods and four later stages.** New: Pisgat Ze'ev, Ramat Shlomo (planned as Reches Shuafat),
  Har Homa and Givat HaMatos (a caravan site). Later stages of rows already in the 1967-77 note: Ramot, Neve Ya'akov,
  Gilo, East Talpiot.
- **The decisions that are dated by a document I opened.** (1) The Cabinet meeting of 9 March 1980 where the Housing
  Minister raised the land-taking between French Hill and Neve Ya'akov, and (2) the expropriation order signed on the
  night of 11 March 1980 for about 1,000 acres on the same stretch, both from JTA bulletins whose datelines I read on
  the page images; (3) Jordan's letter of 14 March 1980 to the Security Council (UN S/13844), read as a printed page,
  which gives a different size and a different number of dwellings; (4) the Ministerial Absorption Committee's plan of
  Sunday 14 October 1990 for 17,000 apartments, from a JTA bulletin of 16 October 1990.
- **Disagreements kept as [C].** Pisgat Ze'ev's sub-neighbourhood years (English against Hebrew Wikipedia); the year
  of the Har Homa land-taking (1991 in English Wikipedia, 1992 in Hebrew); the size and the units of the March 1980
  land-taking; Neve Ya'akov's eastern stage (1992 against "early 1990s"); the two coordinate values for Har Homa.
- **First-residents days: none found.** Pisgat Ze'ev's first houses are given as 1985 by Hebrew Wikipedia and no day is
  given by any source I opened. Every other first-residents day and every construction-start day is [GAP].

## Which list

Named sources, all secondary, each opened 2026-10-25:

1. **[S, Wikipedia]** English "Ring Settlements, East Jerusalem" (oldid 1357154222): "The first neighborhoods built
   after 1967 were Ramot, French Hill, Neve Yaakov, Pisgat Ze'ev, East Talpiot, and Gilo. In the 1990s, Ramat Shlomo and
   Har Homa were added to the list." Of its eight, the ones after 1977 are Pisgat Ze'ev ("Established in 1982"), Ramat
   Shlomo ("established in 1995") and Har Homa ("established in 1997"). Ramat Shlomo (1995) and Har Homa (1997) are
   after the window as "established" years; they are given rows because the planning and land-taking steps are in it.

   later stages (1978-93): Ramot, Neve Ya'akov, Gilo, East Talpiot.
3. **[S, Wikipedia]** Hebrew "גבעת המטוס" (oldid 42719989) and English "Givat HaMatos" (oldid 1372720532): a caravan
   site of 1991. It is not a Ring neighbourhood and is given a row only because it is a place where residents were
   placed inside the boundary in the window.

No official municipal list (Jerusalem Institute for Policy Research yearbook, municipality publications) was found;

### Pisgat Ze'ev

- **Purpose and location.** English Wikipedia: established "as one of the city's five Ring Neighborhoods, meant to
  create a contiguous Jewish link with Neve Yaakov in the city's north"; it lies "south of Neve Yaakov" [S, Wikipedia].
  Hebrew Wikipedia: "הרעיון המרכזי שעמד מאחורי הקמת השכונה היה יצירת רצף אורבני יהודי בין הגבעה הצרפתית לשכונת נווה
  יעקב" (the central idea was an urban Jewish continuum between French Hill and Neve Yaakov; our translation) [S,
  Wikipedia].
- **Land-taking, March 1980.**
  - **Cabinet, 9 March 1980** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 11 March 1980 (No. 49, page 2, headline "Levy Proposes
    Confiscation Of Arab-Owned Land In East Jerusalem"), dateline "JERUSALEM, March 10 (JTA)": "Housing Minister David
    Levy has proposed the large-scale confiscation of Arab-owned land in East Jerusalem for the construction of some
    10,000 new housing units for Jews on the eastern borders of the city. Levy raised the matter at yesterday's Cabinet
    meeting but was told to refer it to the special expropriations committee headed by Finance Minister Yigal Hurwitz."
    Dateline 10 March, "yesterday" = 9 March 1980 (derived; 9 March 1980 was a Sunday). The item adds that the project
    "should be built between the existing French Hill and Neve Yaacov neighborhoods which are presently separated by Arab
    villages" and "would create a 'Jewish territorial continuum' around the northern and eastern fringes of the city",
    and that the plan "received a cool reception from Jerusalem officials". Page image of
    `http://pdfs.jta.org/1980/1980-03-11_049.pdf`; web page
    https://www.jta.org/archive/levy-proposes-confiscation-of-arab-owned-land-in-east-jerusalem.
  - **Order, night of 11 March 1980** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 14 March 1980 (No. 52, page 1, headline "U.S. Rebuke
    Of Israel On Land Expropriation Termed One-Sided"), dateline "JERUSALEM, March 13 (JTA)": "The Ministerial
    Expropriations Committee, headed by Finance Minister Yigal Hurwitz, signed the expropriation order Tuesday night.
    The action followed a proposal by Housing Minister David Levy that 1000 acres be seized for the purpose of
    constructing 10,000 housing units for Jews. The projects would link two Jewish neighborhoods, French Hill and Neve
    Yaacov which are now separated by mostly unused land, the bulk of which is owned by Arabs." Dateline Thursday 13
    March, so "Tuesday night" = 11 March 1980 (derived). The item also reports the State Department spokesman's statement
    "yesterday" (12 March) that the US "deplores the decision" to expropriate some 1000 acres of land "in occupied
    territories", and Mayor Kollek's reply that "the expropriation was within the city limits, was within the law and
    was intended to prevent individuals from illegally taking possession of the land". Both positions are the item's
    wording. Page image of `http://pdfs.jta.org/1980/1980-03-14_052.pdf`; web page
    https://www.jta.org/archive/u-s-rebuke-of-israel-on-land-expropriation-termed-one-sided. The item does not name
    Pisgat Ze'ev; that this land became Pisgat Ze'ev rests on Hebrew Wikipedia's "האדמות לצורך הקמת השכונה הופקעו בתחילת
    1980" (the land for the neighbourhood was expropriated at the beginning of 1980; our translation) [S, Wikipedia] and
    on the two items' shared description of the stretch between French Hill and Neve Yaacov. No source I opened says
    the two are the same.
  - **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]** United Nations document S/13844, "Letter dated 14 March
    1980 from the Permanent Representative of Jordan to the United Nations addressed to the President of the Security
    Council", issued 17 March 1980, https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/12320/files/S_13844-EN.pdf (read on the page
    image). It says: "the latest seizure by the Israeli occupation authorities of four thousand dunums (four million
    square meters) of land, to the north of occupied Arab Jerusalem, in Palestinian Arab residential areas, located
    between Karm Louisse (the French Hill) and Qalandia Jerusalem airport" and that "Radio Israel has announced that the
    seizure was a prelude to the construction of 12,000 new Israeli dwellings and four factories, astride a new road to
    link two earlier illegally established quarters". That is the Arab Group's position as put by Jordan's
    representative, quoted as a position, not a finding.
  - **[C] size and units.** JTA: 1,000 acres and 10,000 housing units. Derived: 1,000 acres = 1,000 x 4.04686 = 4,046.86
    dunams, about the 4,000 dunums Jordan's letter gives, so the two size figures are of the same order; no source
    equates the two orders. Units: 10,000 (JTA) against 12,000 dwellings and four factories (Jordan's letter, citing
    Radio Israel). Both are kept. The Hebrew Wikipedia sentence that the expropriation was delayed "after a long delay
    by the finance minister who feared high compensation costs" (our translation of "לאחר עיכוב ארוך על ידי שר האוצר
    שחשש לעלות פיצויים גבוהה") is Hebrew Wikipedia's, citing a Maariv item of 1 October 1985 headed "יגאל הורביץ מודה ומתוודה" that was not read; the clause "the land was expropriated at the beginning of 1980" cites a Maariv item of 14 March 1980, also not read.
- **Decision to build.** Hebrew Wikipedia: "ועדת השרים לענייני ירושלים, קיבלה את החלטה הרשמית על הקמת השכונה ב-3
  בדצמבר 1980" (the ministerial committee for Jerusalem affairs took the formal decision to establish the
  neighbourhood on 3 December 1980; our translation) [S, Wikipedia]; the citation is a Maariv item of 4 December 1980
  headed "שכונה חדשה תוקם בשטח שהופקע בבירה" and a Kol HaIr item of 5 December 1980, neither read (the National Library's newspaper site returned HTTP
  403 to this box, see "Gaps and what was tried"). No source I opened gives the day independently.
- **Start and first residents.** Hebrew Wikipedia: "בניית השכונה החלה ב-1982 וראשוני הבתים אוכלסו ב-1985" (construction
  began in 1982 and the first houses were occupied in 1985; our translation); a Davar item of 15
  November 1981 ("דנים בהתחלת עבודות התשתית לשכונה החדשה ...", on starting infrastructure work; the headline goes on "בי־ם ליד הגבעה הצרפתית") is cited there for the
  sentence on the aim of a Jewish "רצף אורבני" (urban continuity) between French Hill and Neve Yaakov, not for the 1982
  start of building; it was not read [S, Wikipedia]. English Wikipedia: "established in
  1982", and "five districts: Center (1984), West (1988), East and North (1990), and South (1998)" [S, Wikipedia].
- **[C] sub-neighbourhood years.** English: Center 1984, West 1988, East and North 1990, South 1998. Hebrew: Center
  "first populated (1985)", West 1990, East 1990, North 1994, South 1998 ("אוכלסה"; populated). The two articles differ
  for Center (1984 against 1985), West (1988 against 1990) and North (1990 against 1994); what each year marks is not
  stated. Hebrew Wikipedia adds that in March 1990 the cornerstone of Pisgat Ze'ev East was laid (citing Maariv of 14
  March 1990, not read) and that about 6,500 residents lived in the neighbourhood by early 1990 [S, Wikipedia].
- **Land by village.** English Wikipedia, citing ARIJ (not read): 1,458 dunams from Beit Hanina, 686 dunams from Beit
  Hanina for Pisgat Amir, 239 from Shuafat, 89 from Hizma [S, Wikipedia]. Derived: 1,458 + 686 + 239 + 89 = 2,472 dunams.
  That is not the 1980 order's size and no source says how the two relate.
- **[S] A contemporaneous count, 1993.** JTA daily bulletin of 8 July 1993 (No. 126, page 2, headline "Jews Now Outnumber
  Arab Population In Ex-Jordanian Section Of Jerusalem"), dateline "JERUSALEM, July 7 (JTA)": Deputy Mayor Avraham
  Kahilla "said Jews became a majority in eastern Jerusalem sometime during the last year and a half, mainly due to the
  development of the northern Jewish suburb of Pisgat Ze'ev, situated between the Jewish suburbs of Neveh Ya'acov and
  French Hill", and "6,000 additional housing units in Pisgat Ze'ev are approaching completion". The item gives
  "exceeds 160,000" Jews and "about 155,000" Palestinians in those areas; these counts are the speaker's. Page image of
  `http://pdfs.jta.org/1993/1993-07-08_126.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/jews-now-outnumber-arab-population-in-ex-jordanian-section-of-jerusalem.
- **First residents' day:** [GAP]. Construction-start day: [GAP]. The text of the 1980 order and its Official Gazette
  publication: [GAP] (see below).

### Ramat Shlomo (Reches Shuafat)

- **Before the neighbourhood.** Hebrew Wikipedia: a national sports village with a stadium was planned on the "Shuafat
  ridge" from 1971; "עבודות התשתית באזור החלו בשנת 1979" (infrastructure works in the area began in 1979; our
  translation), with ultra-Orthodox protests; in January 1980 the city council resolved that the Shuafat site was
  preferred for the stadium, but the works were not resumed [S, Wikipedia; every citation is a Davar or Maariv item, none
  read].
- **The neighbourhood.** Hebrew Wikipedia: in 1989 the Finance Minister asked a developer group to take on the project;
  "במאי 1990 חתמו על ההסכם עם מינהל מקרקעי ישראל להקמת 2,400 יחידות דיור בשכונה" (in May 1990 they signed the agreement
  with the Israel Land Administration for 2,400 housing units in the neighbourhood; our translation); "הקמת השכונה החלה
  בסוף 1990" (the establishment of the neighbourhood began at the end of 1990; our translation); Arab landowners tried
  to prevent it, claiming the land was taken for a stadium and not for a Jewish neighbourhood [S, Wikipedia].
- **Year [C].** English: "Ramat Shlomo was founded in 1995", initially called "Reches Shuafat (Shuafat Ridge)", later
  named for a rabbi. Hebrew: construction from the end of 1990. The two may mark different things; neither says.
- **Land by village.** English Wikipedia, citing ARIJ (not read): 1,494 dunams from Shuafat, 232 from Beit Hanina, 53
  from Beit Hanina el Balad [S, Wikipedia]. Derived: 1,494 + 232 + 53 = 1,779 dunams.
- **Not equated.** The JTA item of 16 October 1990 (see "The plan of 14 October 1990") speaks of two new neighbourhoods
  of 2,400 units in the north of East Jerusalem. Hebrew Wikipedia's figure for Ramat Shlomo is also 2,400. The item names
  no neighbourhood, so the two are not equated here.
- **Planning and first-residents days:** [GAP]. The 1995 land orders belong to the next window.

### Har Homa (Homat Shmuel)

- **Land.** English Wikipedia says it is built on 1,850 dunams of land that Israel expropriated in 1991; "In 1991, Israeli cabinet
  minister Yitzhak Moda'i approved expropriation of the land, which was owned by a variety of private owners, both
  Israeli and Palestinian, on the basis of eminent domain"; plans "were drawn up in the 1980s" and were opposed by
  Israeli environmental groups [S, Wikipedia]. Hebrew Wikipedia: "ב-1992 פורסם צו הפקעה לאדמות הפרטיות בהר חומה" (in 1992
  an expropriation order was published for the private lands at Har Homa; our translation), that about 30 per cent was
  in Arab hands, and that Jewish landowners petitioned the High Court (cases 736/93 and 3956/92), which rejected the
  petition [S, Wikipedia; the order and the judgments not read].
- **Year of the land-taking [C].** 1991 (English), 1992 (Hebrew). The English article's 1,850 dunams (derived: 1,850 /
  4.04686 = 457 acres) carries citations to a New York Times article of 8 November 2010, a BBC page of 10 December 2009 and a
  BBC page of 9 November 2010 (none read). The Hebrew article's case number 3956/92 points
  to a petition filed in 1992; it is not evidence of the order's day.
- **Earlier stadium proposals.** Hebrew Wikipedia (Har Homa): in 1979 a football stadium was proposed at the site after the
  Shuafat ridge plan was cancelled, and in 1989 the Interior Minister proposed it again [S, Wikipedia]. The Hebrew Ramat
  Shlomo article says the Interior Ministry's director general proposed the Har Homa area for the stadium, citing Davar of
  29 November 1979 (not read) [S, Wikipedia].
- **After the window.** Planning in 1995, a cabinet decision of 19 February 1997 and the start of building in March 1997
  are in both articles; they are outside 1978-93 and are not researched. Hebrew Wikipedia's "founded 1997".
- **JTA.** No JTA item of 1978-93 names the place under any spelling I tried (see "Gaps and what was tried").
  The only 1990 item I opened that may touch the area is the plan below, which says "Wall Mountain" (the English
  meaning of "Har Homa", per English Wikipedia's "lit. Wall Mountain"): "Another 4,500 units would go up on the Wall
  Mountain and along a stretch of the Bethlehem Road in southern East Jerusalem". The item does not say Har Homa, so
  the two are not equated.
- **Planning, order and construction days:** [GAP].

### Givat HaMatos

- **Caravan site, 1991.** Hebrew Wikipedia: "ב-1991 הוקם על הגבעה אתר קרוואנים במטרה לקלוט את גלי העלייה מאתיופיה
  במבצע משה ובמבצע שלמה, וכן לפתור בעיות דיור נוספות" (in 1991 a caravan site was set up on the hill to absorb the waves of immigration from Ethiopia
  in Operation Moses and Operation Solomon, and also to solve additional housing problems; our translation); about 170 dunams and about 400 caravans, with the
  intention that all would move to permanent buildings within two years [S, Wikipedia]. English Wikipedia: "The caravans
  were built on a hill in 1991 to house a large influx of Ethiopian Jews airlifted to Israel" [S, Wikipedia].
- **What the sources do not say.** Neither article gives the day, the deciding body or the first residents. The
  Hebrew article's phrase "Operation Moses" and "Operation Solomon" are the article's words, and no dates for them are taken here;
  the 1991 year is the only date given and may mark the caravans' arrival; no source says.
- **Not a Ring neighbourhood.** The place is not in the English Ring list. It is here because residents were placed
  inside the boundary in the window. Whether the land lies inside the 1967 line was not checked (see "The boundary").
- **JTA:** a bulletin item of 26 November 1992 ("Sharon Charges Government Neglect Of Caravan Parks For New Olim", web
  page https://www.jta.org/archive/sharon-charges-government-neglect-of-caravan-parks-for-new-olim) is the only item the
  quoted-name search "Givat Hamatos" returned (1 result); its web text speaks of "15,000 prefabricated units" at caravan
  parks and does not, in the part the page shows, name the site. Its PDF link
  (`http://pdfs.jta.org/1992/1992-11-26_223.pdf`) was opened and all four pages were read: the item is not in that
  bulletin, so the item is **not used**. [GAP] whether the site is named in the item.

## The plan of 14 October 1990 and the land policy of 1980-93

- **14 October 1990** [S]: JTA daily bulletin of 16 October 1990 (No. 192, page 1, headline "Sharon Announces Plan To
  Construct Thousands Of Homes In East Jerusalem"), dateline "JERUSALEM, Oct 15 (JTA)" (printed without a period): "Housing Minister Ariel Sharon
  ... announced plans to build 17,000 new apartments for Soviet Jewish immigrants in Jerusalem, all but 2,000 of them in
  the Arab-populated eastern part of the city. The plan, adopted Sunday by the Ministerial Absorption Committee, which
  is headed by Sharon, is believed to have the tacit support of Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir and will be presented to
  the Cabinet for approval." "Sunday" = 14 October 1990 (derived; the dateline is Monday 15 October). The item continues:
  "The plans call for two new Jewish neighborhoods consisting of 2,400 units in the northern reaches of East Jerusalem,
  between the heavily Jewish French Hill and Pisgat Ze'ev neighborhoods. Another 4,500 units would go up on the Wall
  Mountain and along a stretch of the Bethlehem Road in southern East Jerusalem. Other flats would be built in existing
  Jewish parts of East Jerusalem." Derived: 17,000 - 2,000 = 15,000 units in the east, which is the figure the item gives
  for "East Jerusalem" in a later paragraph; the page image was read for these sentences. The item reports an
  American objection and the Israeli reply; both are the item's wording and are not repeated here. Page image of
  `http://pdfs.jta.org/1990/1990-10-16_192.pdf`; web page
  https://www.jta.org/archive/sharon-announces-plan-to-construct-thousands-of-homes-in-east-jerusalem. Whether the
  Cabinet approved it: [GAP].
- **Other land-taking steps.** Hebrew Wikipedia gives the Har Homa order as 1992 and the Ramat Shlomo agreement as May
  1990 (above). English Wikipedia (Gilo) says "In 1970, the Israeli government expropriated 12,300 dunams" (1967-77 window,
  in the sibling note). No other dated order of 1978-93 was found.
