# Israeli localities of the Gush Etzion and Har Hevron Regional Councils founded 1978-1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays paused
until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of the [1977-1993
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) sections 7.3 and 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the
localities of the CBS rows of the Gush Etzion and Har Hevron Regional Councils with a founding year of 1978 to 1993, Nahal
outposts that became localities included. **Other councils, Gaza, Sinai, the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines
and the lines are not in this note.** Every Judea and Samaria row of the CBS 2024 file with a founding year of 1978-1993 whose local authority is Gush Etzion or Har Hevron is in this note; the Mateh Binyamin rows are in `binyamin-localities-1978-93.md`; the Arvot HaYarden and Megilot rows are in `jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`, and the other Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 are not in any note yet. East Jerusalem neighbourhoods are not covered. **Of the 68 Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 that #1665 lists as in no note yet, this note takes 19** (the list is under "Short answer"). The Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 that are local councils or towns in the CBS file (Ariel, Efrat, Betar Illit, Immanuel and six others, ten rows in all) are not yet in any note; Ma'ale Adummim (CBS 1975) is by its year in `../Era 1957-77/west-bank-localities-1967-77.md`. The question: which Israeli localities of the Gush Etzion and Har Hevron Regional Councils were founded between 1978 and 1993, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (Judea and Samaria, the file's own district name), whose local authority is "גוש עציון" (Gush Etzion) or "הר חברון" (Har Hevron), with a year of founding of 1978 to 1993: **19 rows** (7 in Gush Etzion, 12 in Har Hevron). By CBS form code: 4 of code 320, 12 of code 370, 3 of code 190. By CBS year: 1981 (3), 1982 (5), 1983 (5), 1984 (3), 1985 (2), 1989 (1). **No row has a year of 1978 to 1980 or 1986 to 1988 or 1990 to 1993**; the next Har Hevron row after 1985 is Negohot (1999) and the next Gush Etzion row after 1989 has no year in the file. All 19 are in the CBS subdistricts Bethlehem (Hebrew: בית לחם) or Hebron (חברון): 14 Hebron, 5 Bethlehem.
- **Which of the 68 rows this note takes.** The sibling note `jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (#1665) lists 68 Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 as in no note yet (78 such rows in the file, less its 10; my recount of the same file gave 154, 78, 10 and 68). **This note takes 19 of the 68**: Har Hevron (12): Karmel (1981), Ma'on (1981), Eshkolot (1982), Pene Hever (1982), Telem (1982), Bet Yattir (1983), Tene (1983), Suseya (1983), Otni'el (1983), Adora (1984), Haggay (1984), Shim'a (1985); Gush Etzion (7): Ma'ale Amos (1981), Newe Daniyyel (1982), Noqedim (1982), Asfar (1983), Karme Zur (1984), Qedar (1985), Bat Ayin (1989). The other 49 are Mateh Binyamin (19, `binyamin-localities-1978-93.md`, #1669), Shomron (20, `shomron-localities-1978-93.md`, PR #1671, open) and ten rows of ten single-locality authorities (Ariel and Betar Illit are cities in the CBS file, so "authorities" fits better than "councils"; Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Efrat, Ariel, Beit Arye-Ofarim, Betar Illit, Giv'at Ze'ev, Har Adar, Immanuel, Karnei Shomron), which are in no note yet. The 1967-77 rows of Gush Etzion are in `../Era 1957-77/west-bank-localities-1967-77.md` (#1663); the Arvot HaYarden and Megilot rows are in `jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (#1665). Arithmetic: 78 - 10 = 68; 19 + 19 + 20 + 10 = 68.
- **Dates.** **None of the 19 founding days is closed from a record of the day.** The primary documents I could open are three Official Gazette name lists (Yalkut HaPirsumim 3165 of 24 February 1985, 4012 of 4 June 1992, 2633 of 10 June 1980), which date a name and show that an outpost or a locality existed by the date of the issue, and give a map grid for 18 of the 19 rows; Bat Ayin has no entry under its own name, but G2 has an entry "צורף" (Tzoref, a Nahal outpost in the Etzion bloc) that Hebrew Wikipedia ties to Bat Ayin's first gar'in (row 19). Every date is [S] or [C]. The one dated list is the council list of 7 October 1984 in G2 (printed p. 1507, a printed date that dates a name and a council membership, not a founding); it names nine of the 19 rows (see "Council lists, council tables and UN reports"). In the first pass ten rows were [C] (the sources differ on the year or on what the date marks: a Nahal outpost, a move, an ascent to the land, an inauguration or a civil settling) and nine were [S] (the sources I read agree on the year). **Second pass (27 Oct):** fourteen rows are [C] and five are [S]: Ma'on, Pene Hever, Tene and Otni'el moved from [S] to [C]; Haggay stays [C] (the day is resolved to 19 July 1984, the year is not: 1983 in the government table and in Hebrew Wikipedia's outpost, 1984 in the CBS), see "Second pass on the [GAP] rows". Still no founding day is closed from a record of the day, but press days are added (Qedar first settled over the weekend of 6-8 July 1984, Haggay turned civilian 19 July 1984, Asfar handed over 16 July 1984, Tene inaugurated by 30 May 1984, Tzoref settled by the dateline of Sun 7 May 1989, dedication planned for Wed 10 May, establishment reported for 10 May) and a government table dates the approvals of all 19 rows.
- **Press.** JTA (Jewish Telegraphic Agency) items were opened as scanned bulletins for 7 rows (Karmel, Bet Yattir, Telem, Haggay, Shim'a, Eshkolot, and Bat Ayin through the name Tzoref, T9); for the other 12 rows no JTA item with a date was found. Two give a day that bears on a row: Telem dedicated on 28 April 1982 and Eshkolot settled on 5 August 1991. **No government record, Knesset record or newspaper of the day was opened** (the National Library of Israel and nevo.co.il block this server, see "What was not done"). **Second pass:** three more JTA bulletins were read to the dateline (T10 to T12: the Neot Adumim decision of 25 April 1982, Neot Adumim set up over the weekend of 6-8 July 1984, Tsoref of 7 May 1989), eight reports of the UN Special Committee were read as page images, and two documents on gov.il (a Cabinet decision and a Government Names Committee protocol) were read as page images.
- **Coordinates.** All 19 rows have CBS coordinates (derived to WGS84, method below). For 18 rows the Gazette grid is a cross-check: 13 rows agree within 1 km (the grids have 1 km or 100 m units); 5 do not (Adora 5.0 km, Qedar 4.8 km, Karme Zur 2.7 km in total, Telem 1.5 km, Haggay 1.1 km), see "Coordinates". **Second pass:** an OpenStreetMap point (via Nominatim, [S]) for all 19 rows lies within 0.61 km of the CBS point, and the grid printed in a government table (the "Spiegel copy", [S]) for all 19 rows lies within 1.4 km of it; see the second-pass section.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a primary document (a CBS publication, an Official Gazette page) prints, read today by an agent. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Wikipedia and JTA bulletins are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named. A derived value is never given the verified tag.
- Quotes are copied from the page read. Hebrew Wikipedia quotes are from the wikitext of the revision named in the last table, with link brackets removed; quotes from the Gazette were read from the page image (the PDF's text layer is OCR and wrong in places); JTA quotes are from the archive page text, with the PDF dateline read from the scan. Translations from Hebrew are mine.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Settlement", "territories" and "West Bank" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"), https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25, 370,654 bytes, SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`, the same file and hash as `jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet). Filter (a script, `openpyxl`): column "שם מחוז" (district name) = "אזור יהודה ושומרון", column "שם רשות מקומית" (local authority name) = "גוש עציון" or "הר חברון", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1978 to 1993; it returned exactly the 19 rows below. **Derived:** the file has 154 Judea and Samaria rows, 78 of them with a year of 1978 to 1993; 19 are in these two councils (7 + 12). Rows of the two councils outside the filter are listed under "Rows outside the filter". **What the CBS year marks.** The CBS intro to the file (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, 302,077 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `f45b920f`, opened 2026-10-25; item 12, printed page 11, read from the page image) says of the column "שנת ההתיישבות היהודית/שנת ייסוד": “לפי נסיבות המקרה, נרשמה שנת הייסוד של היישוב או שנת אכלוסו בידי יהודים” (case by case, the year of founding of the locality or the year it was settled by Jews was recorded). So the CBS year is not one fixed step; where this note says that a CBS year "matches" a step (outpost, move, civil settling), that is this note's comparison with the source named, not a statement of the CBS. [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]
- **What the form codes mean.** The 2024 file does not list the code meanings. I read the form printed for each symbol in the CBS publication "Local authorities in Israel 2009", list 03 (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, SHA-256 `cec0b3f2...`, opened today), matching on the 4-digit locality symbol; in the extracted text the form is printed before the symbol and the name after it (I checked this on kibbutz Migdal Oz, symbol 3561, which is preceded by "קיבוץ"). **Derived:** all 19 symbols are in that list. The 2009 form of Karmel, Ma'on, Telem and symbol 3745 (printed there as "מצדות יהודה", the Gazette's official name of Bet Yattir) is "מושב שיתופי" (cooperative moshav), which I map to code 320; the 2009 form of the 12 code-370 rows is "יישוב קהילתי" (community settlement), which I map to 370. Both are 2009 forms, as the 190 rows are, and not the 2024 file's own meaning of the codes. The three code-190 rows (Newe Daniyyel, Noqedim, Bat Ayin) are also printed "יישוב קהילתי" in the 2009 list, so that list does not explain why the 2024 file gives them another code. The CBS intro (item 6, printed pages 5 to 7) says the current form ("צורת יישוב שוטפת") is set by the size of the population at the end of each year, and that settlements of 2,000 residents or more are classed as urban. **Derived** from the 2024 file's population column: the three code-190 rows are the only ones of the 19 with 2,000 residents or more (Bat Ayin 2,034, Newe Daniyyel 2,418, Noqedim 3,498; the other 16 have 575 to 1,605), which fits the size rule but is my reading; the file does not print the code meanings. **[GAP]** The mapping is mine; the CBS does not state it for the 2024 file.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine. The Gazette grid is the old Israel grid; the "CBS in the old grid" values in the table below are the `pyproj` EPSG:2039 to EPSG:28191 conversion of the CBS point (Karmel is 167,397 / 93,284). The rule of the sibling notes, the CBS point less (50,000, 500,000) m, gives values that differ by at most 2.3 m on these 19 rows (Karmel 167,399 / 93,284); either convention serves a 100 m or 1 km grid. **Derived** cross-checks: (a) English Wikipedia's own coordinates for the same 19 articles (MediaWiki API `prop=coordinates`, revisions in the last table), haversine distance, median 105 m, largest 674 m (Noqedim); (b) the Gazette grid, see "Coordinates". No map was opened; each coordinate is one point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Bet Yattir, Noqedim, Qedar) the first site is elsewhere.
- **Official Gazette (Yalkut HaPirsumim), the one primary source opened.** The Government Names Committee's lists are published there. nevo.co.il serves a captcha page to this server in place of the PDFs, so I read the Internet Archive copies of the same files (re-downloaded today; sizes and hash prefixes equal those of `jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`): G1 = issue 2633, 10 June 1980, https://web.archive.org/web/20131224094153id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-2633.pdf (704,197 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `e3bcd6b7`); G2 = issue 3165, 24 February 1985 (3 Adar 5745), https://web.archive.org/web/20131005000648id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf (566,094 bytes, `f40db934`); G3 = issue 4012, 4 June 1992 (3 Sivan 5752), https://web.archive.org/web/20160303170912id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-4012.pdf (729,916 bytes, `5cfae6ef`). I searched the text layer of all three for the 19 names and the temporary names, then read every entry cited below from a page image (130 dpi at the first reading; the entries that the owner's review questioned were re-read at 200 dpi). G2 has the entries of 18 of the 19 rows (and the entry "צורף" that Hebrew WP ties to Bat Ayin); G3 has two entries that bear on rows (Doran, the earlier name of the Adora outpost, and Yonadav). I did not read G1 (the 1980 issue) for these rows beyond the text-layer search, which found no entry. An entry the OCR garbled may exist, so a negative from the text layer alone is weak.
- **Founding days: Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (opened 2026-10-25, 1,348,249 bytes, the same size as in the sibling notes). A full-text search of the file for the Hebrew name of each of the 19 rows (in the plain and the hyphenated spellings, 28 strings) returned no hit that is one of these places: Eshkolot hits once as the name of a moshav near Ashkelon ("המושב כרמון (אשכולות) בסביבת אשקלון"), Karmel 27 times (a Carmel region and other places), Ma'on 6 times (the Hevel Ma'on regional council in the Negev), Noqedim once (a society of sheep breeders), Shim'a once (the verb), Telem 2 times (the word "להשתלם"), Qedar 2 times (other words). None of the 19 places is in it, so it dates none of these founding days. [GAP]
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S]. The Hebrew title of CBS "אספר" is "מיצד" (the disambiguation page "אספר" does not describe the place; the Hebrew infobox carries the CBS symbol 3754).
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches through the archive's wp-json endpoint, every query and count in "Searches". For every item used for a date I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read it as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR (the OCR drops spaces and mangles some words), and took the dateline from it. **Dates rule:** the JTA archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF and from "yesterday", "tomorrow" or a weekday read against that dateline.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`.
