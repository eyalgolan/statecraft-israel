The conventions follow `research_notes/M4 war mode/war-1948.md`, including the separation of an agent's reading from a person's check:

- **[V, agent read 23 October 2026; human check pending]**: the identified source text was opened and read for the narrow stated claim. This does not verify the whole topic.
- **[S]**: an official retrospective, forwarded press account, limited-access extraction or locator whose limits are stated. It is not promoted to a contemporary primary record.
- **[C]**: differing versions are recorded without choosing one or silently ordering undated material.
- **[GAP]**: the requested evidence was not established in this bounded pass. This does not mean no source exists.

No sensitivity tag, include/omit or how-to-show recommendation, game value, office mapping, portrait, living/legal flag, content record or human review field is supplied. Source terminology and judgments remain attributed. No casualty total or order-of-battle count is chosen. An operation date, report date, announcement and actual completion are separate fields.

### Intelligence: keep the uncertainty in the source

**I1.** The Defence Archives exhibit describes General Staff discussions dated 21 and 28 November 1977, after the visit, with differing views of Sadat's intentions. This is an official retrospective (S07), not a pre-visit assessment or the protocols themselves. The page's chronology also calls the 21 November meeting three days after a visit it dates to 19 November; that wording is not used to calculate a date. Its unrelated reference to March 1979 is not used here. The CIA page (S08) identifies a collection, but no individual 1977 estimate was opened from it. I1 remains GAP.

**I2 / I3a / I4.** The requested estimates remain GAP. A resolution, a later operational result or a retrospective conclusion cannot supply an original assessment on a particular earlier date. S05 retains its attribution as a dated observation/report. The later commission account relevant to I3b is recorded separately below; it is not an original assessment opened here.

**I3b [attributed later report; original records GAP].** On PDF/browser-print page 6, S12 contrasts Military Intelligence emphasis on danger in the relationship, including lack of reliability, with the Mossad approach. The report describes intelligence officer A as having served as Military Intelligence's liaison in the Mossad representation at Phalangist headquarters at the beginning of the war. It then describes his negative assessment of Phalangist wartime policy and future aims (exhibit 171), and the Mossad's rejection of the criticism (exhibit 172). The role's early-war setting does not establish the writing date of either exhibit. Neither original exhibit, its exact date or the officer's identity was independently checked here. This is not a blanket prohibition on cooperation. [V, attributed commission account] S12, page 6, paragraphs beginning “As we have said” and “From the documents submitted to us”.

The report says that when the war began in June 1982, a prevailing view among Mossad agents maintaining the contacts was that earlier atrocities belonged to the past and that political maturity would prevent their recurrence. This supplies a reported institutional view and period, not an individually authored original estimate with a precise day. The same passage also reports warnings and contrary apprehensions, so it is not treated as a uniform view of all agencies. [V, attributed commission account] S12, final paragraph of page 7 continuing into the first paragraph of page 8; the remainder of that paragraph supplies the contrary warning context.

Page 8 cites testimony pages 16, 17 and 168 and exhibit 85 of **30 June 1982**, clause 14, section 2 of Appendix B, in connection with what the report says Mossad heads heard about the Phalangist leaders' intentions. That is the date attributed to the cited exhibit, not a proven date for each meeting or for the entire preceding favourable assessment. The exhibit, testimony and Appendix B were not opened here; their authors, recipients and original contents were not independently verified. [V, report locator only] S12, page 8, first paragraph, sentences beginning “During the meetings”. **I3b remains open:** this is new report context and a set of precise follow-up locators, not a completed original-estimate record, a responsibility finding or a sensitivity/presentation decision.

**I5 [C, limited access].** The official GulfLink text extraction for an information paper dated 8 August 1990 discusses Scud/FROG chemical-warhead use conditionally (S10, section 2). Another official extraction explicitly marked **“Not Finally Evaluated Intelligence”** claims chemical warheads had been manufactured (S11), but provides no original document date. These are differing statements, not a proven change of assessment in chronological order. The 1996-looking URL components are not used as issue dates.
