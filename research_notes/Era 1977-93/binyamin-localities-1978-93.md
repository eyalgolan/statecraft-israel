# Israeli localities of the Mateh Binyamin Regional Council founded 1978-1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays paused until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of the [1977-1993 design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) sections 7.3 and 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the localities of the CBS rows of the Mateh Binyamin Regional Council with a founding year of 1978 to 1993. **Other councils, Gaza, Sinai, the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines and the lines are not in this note.** Every Judea and Samaria row of the CBS 2024 file with a founding year of 1978-1993 whose local authority is Mateh Binyamin is in this note; the Arvot HaYarden and Megilot rows are in `research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`, and the other Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 are not in any note yet. East Jerusalem neighbourhoods are not covered. Which rows enter the game, and their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list (#118); this note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no statement on the status of any place beyond what a source says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and the conventions of the sibling notes `research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (PR #1665, open when this note was written) and `research_notes/Era 1957-77/jordan-valley-localities-1967-77.md`. Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise.

The question: which Israeli localities of the Mateh Binyamin Regional Council were founded between 1978 and 1993, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** The Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון" (Judea and Samaria, the file's own district name), whose local authority is "מטה בנימין" (Mateh Binyamin), with a year of founding of 1978 to 1993: **19 rows**. By CBS year: 1978 (1), 1979 (2), 1980 (1), 1981 (5), 1982 (1), 1983 (2), 1984 (3), 1985 (2), 1988 (1), 1989 (1). By CBS current-form code (printed in the file, no legend in the file; the 2009 labels are in "How it was done"): 4 of code 180, 8 of code 190, 1 of code 320, 6 of code 370. By CBS subdistrict: Ramallah 17, Jordan-Jericho 1 (Mitzpe Yeriho), Shechem 1 (Eli). **No row has a year of 1986, 1987 or 1990 to 1993**; the council's next row after 1989 is Ganei Modi'in, 1996.
- **Which of the 68 rows this note takes.** The sibling note of #1665 (`research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`) lists 68 Judea and Samaria rows of 1978-1993 as in no note yet (my recount of the same file: 78 rows less the 10 Arvot HaYarden and Megilot rows). **This note takes 19 of the 68**, all of local authority Mateh Binyamin: Mitzpe Yeriho (1978); Kfar Adumim (1979); Shilo (1979); Giv'on HaHadasha (1980); Ma'ale Mikhmas (1981); Mattityahu (1981); Nili (1981); Ateret (1981); Pesagot (1981); Almon (Anatot) (1982); Dolev (1983); Ma'ale Levona (1983); Geva Binyamin (Adam) (1984); Nahali'el (1984); Eli (1984); Hashmona'im (1985); Kokhav Ya'akov (1985); Na'ale (1988); Talmon (1989). Once this note and #1665 are both in, the "68 ... in no note yet" of that note is 49 (68 less these 19); whichever of the two lands second should change that figure and point here. The other 49 (Shomron 20, Har Hevron 12, Gush Etzion 7, ten single-row councils and towns) are not in this note; by their council they are for the sibling notes `research_notes/Era 1977-93/shomron-localities-1978-93.md` (in progress), `research_notes/Era 1977-93/gush-etzion-har-hevron-localities-1978-93.md` (#1670) and `research_notes/Era 1977-93/js-local-councils-towns-1978-93.md` (in progress); which row each of them takes is for those notes. The 1967-77 rows of the same region are in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/west-bank-localities-1967-77.md` (#1663).
- **Dates.** **No founding day is closed from a record of the day.** Only the cabinet's approval of Shilo (22 April 1979), the cabinet's decision of 20 March 1983 that names Ma'ale Levona and the settlers' attempt of 7 March 1989 at Talmon rest on a document I opened with a resolved date, each a JTA bulletin read from the PDF image with its dateline (press, tagged [S]). Eight rows carry a disagreement [C] on the year or on what the date marks (Mitzpe Yeriho, Shilo, Giv'on HaHadasha, Ma'ale Levona, Nahali'el, Hashmona'im, Kokhav Ya'akov, Talmon); the other eleven are [S], the sources I read agree on the year and the days, where given, are one source's (almost always Hebrew Wikipedia citing newspapers and government decisions I could not open). The primary documents I opened are the CBS file and three Official Gazette name lists (Yalkut HaPirsumim 2633, 3165 and 4012), which date a name, not a founding.
- **Coordinates.** All 19 rows have CBS coordinates (derived to WGS84, method below). For all 19 rows the Gazette prints a map grid that is an independent cross-check (20 grids: Eli has two that disagree); the differences from the CBS point, with the precision of each grid, are in "Coordinates".
- **Press.** JTA coverage of these places is thin: three items carry a fact about a founding (Shilo, Ma'ale Levona, Talmon), plus the items named at the end of "JTA items read" (a dig permit of 1978, a Gush move of Dec 1978 that also names a camp near Giv'on, two items of April 1985 and two of May and June 1991 that name Giv'on HaHadasha under other spellings, and a feature of 1993). Two reports of the UN Special Committee (A/34/631 of 1979 and A/35/425 of 1980) were read as page images; they compile press reports, so they are [S] and add days that no other source read gives. The National Library of Israel returns HTTP 403 to this server, so every Maariv and Davar article that Hebrew Wikipedia or those reports cite is unopened. **No government record or Knesset record was opened**; every founding date is [S] or [C].

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a primary document (a CBS publication, an Official Gazette page) prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right.
- **[S]** secondary: one source, or several that plainly copy one another. Wikipedia, JTA bulletins, the UN Special Committee reports (they compile press reports) and the Gazette-independent press are [S] (JTA is press, not a record). Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, I do not count that source as read.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the routine is named. A derived value is never given the verified tag.
- Quotes are copied from the page read. Hebrew Wikipedia and English Wikipedia quotes are copied from the wikitext of the revision named in the last table, with link brackets removed. Quotes from the Gazette were read from the page images (the PDFs' text layer is OCR and wrong in places) and are typed here without the vowel points the print has; where a page number is not visible in the crop I read, it is inferred from the sequence and says so. JTA quotes are from the archive page text, with the PDF dateline read from the scan where stated. Translations from Hebrew are mine; a Hebrew quote is a whole sentence of the wikitext, and the translation after it renders the part that carries the date or the fact, not always the whole sentence.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Settlement", "territories" and "West Bank" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"), https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25, 370,654 bytes, SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`, the same file as in the sibling note of #1665; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet; read with the Python standard library, since the box has no spreadsheet package). Filter: column "שם מחוז" (district name) = "אזור יהודה ושומרון", column "שם רשות מקומית" (local authority name) = "מטה בנימין", **and** column "שנת ייסוד" (year of founding) from 1978 to 1993; a script returned exactly the 19 rows below. **Derived counts:** the file has 154 Judea and Samaria rows; 78 of them have a year of 1978 to 1993; of these 19 are Mateh Binyamin (this note), 10 are Arvot HaYarden and Megilot Yam HaMelah (`research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`, same count as that note) and 49 are in other councils (Shomron 20, Har Hevron 12, Gush Etzion 7, and one row each in Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Efrat, Ariel, Beit Arye-Ofarim, Beitar Illit, Giv'at Ze'ev, Har Adar, Emanuel and Karnei Shomron). The council has 31 rows of the file in all; those outside the 1978-1993 window are in "Rows outside the filter".
- **What the CBS code means.** The 2024 file prints a "current form" code (column "צורת יישוב שוטפת": 180, 190, 320 or 370 for these rows) and does not list its legend. The CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, opened 2026-10-25, SHA-256 prefix `f45b920f`) says that the current form "נקבעת על פי גודל האוכלוסייה ביישוב בסוף כל שנה" (is set by the size of the population of the locality at the end of each year), so for the size classes the code is not a record of what the place was when founded. I also opened "Local authorities in Israel 2009", Appendix C, list 03 (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `cec0b3f2`; PDF page 9 of 16, printed page 100, read as a page image). Its Mateh Binyamin block prints a form label next to each locality symbol, and all 19 symbols of this note are in it, so I matched them by symbol (the match is derived). The labels are the **2009 form** of each place; they are not a decoding of the 2024 code. [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] Code 370: "יישוב קהילתי" for Giv'on HaHadasha, Ateret, Almon, Dolev, Ma'ale Levona and Nahali'el. Code 320: "מושב שיתופי" for Mattityahu. Code 180: "יישוב יהודי 2,000-4,999 תושבים" for Kfar Adumim, Shilo, Geva Binyamin and Talmon. Code 190 splits into four labels: "יישוב קהילתי" for Mitzpe Yeriho, Ma'ale Mikhmas, Nili and Pesagot; "יישוב יהודי 2,000-4,999 תושבים" for Eli and Hashmona'im; "יישוב יהודי 5,000-9,999 תושבים" for Kokhav Ya'akov; "יישוב כפרי אחר, יהודי" for Na'ale (the size ranges are as the print shows them, right to left). **Derived:** the 2009 label does not follow from the 2024 code (190 carries four different labels), and the 2009 form is not the form at founding, so the codes stay as printed and carry no meaning in this note beyond the 2009 labels above.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` and the sibling notes I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each; EPSG:2039 parameters) and converted it to WGS84, rounded to five decimals. **Derived:** the box has no `pyproj`, so I wrote the inverse transverse-Mercator series and the seven-parameter Israel-1993-to-WGS84 shift myself; it reproduces to within 0.00001 degrees (about 1 m) the five-decimal values printed in the sibling note for Tomer, Shadmot Mehola, Bet HaArava, Wered Yeriho and Avenat (computed from the same raw fields), which were made with `pyproj`. That reading of the field is mine; the CBS intro describes a different format (ten digits in hundreds of metres). No map was opened; each coordinate is one point per locality, not a survey of the founding site. **[GAP]** (no named map source).
- **Official Gazette (Yalkut HaPirsumim), the primary source opened.** The Government Names Committee's lists are published there. nevo.co.il serves a captcha page (HTTP 200, 3,184 bytes) in place of the PDFs on this server, so I read the Internet Archive copies of the same files (the same three as in the sibling note): G1 = issue 2633, 10 June 1980 (26 Sivan 5740), https://web.archive.org/web/20131224094153id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-2633.pdf (704,197 bytes, SHA-256 `e3bcd6b7`, 24 pages); G2 = issue 3165, 24 February 1985 (3 Adar 5745), https://web.archive.org/web/20131005000648id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf (566,094 bytes, `f40db934`, 20 pages); G3 = issue 4012, 4 June 1992 (3 Sivan 5752), https://web.archive.org/web/20160303170912id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-4012.pdf (729,916 bytes, `5cfae6ef`, 48 pages). Each issue states the period of the names it lists (read from its opening page): G1 from 11 Tevet 5729 (1 January 1969) to 1 Tammuz 5739 (26 June 1979), signed 11 Tevet 5740 (31 December 1979); G2 from 1 Tammuz 5739 (26 June 1979) to 22 Sivan 5744 (22 June 1984); G3 from 22 Sivan 5744 (22 June 1984) to 2 Adar I 5752 (6 February 1992). So a name printed in G2 was fixed between 26 Jun 1979 and 22 Jun 1984, and one in G3 between 22 Jun 1984 and 6 Feb 1992 (the issue date is the publication date, not the date of the Committee's decision). The grid in an entry is two numbers (3-digit numbers are kilometres, 4-digit numbers hundreds of metres, 5-digit numbers tens of metres); my rule of thumb, used by the sibling note, is that the CBS point less (50,000, 500,000) m is in the same old grid. The entries were found by searching the OCR text layer for each name and read from the page image; I did not read every entry of the three issues, and the text layer missed several names, so a name I did not find may exist. Dates in Hebrew are converted with my own Hebrew-calendar routine (checked on 1 Tishri 5779 = 10 Sep 2018, 1 Tishri 5700 = 14 Sep 1939, 1 Tishri 5784 = 16 Sep 2023 and 15 Nisan 5785 = 13 Apr 2025).
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S].
- **UN Special Committee reports** A/34/631 (1979) and A/35/425 (1980), the same two reports as in the Gaza notes of #165: fetched from `https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&t=pdf&s=<symbol>` on 2026-10-25 (6,762,152 and 4,475,171 bytes). A/34/631 pp. 20, 31, 34 and 98 and A/35/425 PDF p. 28 were read as page images. They compile press reports (the papers named in each paragraph; none opened), so they are [S].
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches through the archive's wp-json endpoint, every query and count in "Searches". For the three items whose event date this note uses (T1 to T3) I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`, scanned page images, no text layer) and read the page; **Dates rule:** the JTA archive page shows the bulletin date, the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF and from "yesterday", "today" or a weekday read against that dateline. For every other JTA item named the archive page text was read and the PDF was not opened, so the event day is not resolved.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`.
