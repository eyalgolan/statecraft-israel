# Israeli localities of the Shomron Regional Council founded from 1978 to 1993 (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file stays paused until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of the [1977-1993 design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) sections 7.3 and 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the localities of the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) 2024 localities file whose local authority is Shomron (שומרון) and whose founding year is 1978 to 1993. **The other councils, Gaza, Sinai and the Golan, the localities inside the pre-1967 lines and the lines are not in this note.**

Every Judea and Samaria row of the CBS 2024 file with a founding year of 1978-1993 whose local authority is Shomron is in this note; the Mateh Binyamin rows are in `research_notes/Era 1977-93/binyamin-localities-1978-93.md` (PR #1669); the Arvot HaYarden and Megilot rows are in `research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (PR #1665); the Har Hevron and Gush Etzion rows are in `research_notes/Era 1977-93/gush-etzion-har-hevron-localities-1978-93.md` (PR #1670); the ten rows that are single local councils and towns are in `research_notes/Era 1977-93/js-local-councils-towns-1978-93.md` (PR #1672). East Jerusalem neighbourhoods are not covered.

**Which rows this note takes.** 20 rows (list in the summary table): Kefar Tappuah, Mevo Dotan, Elon More, Barqan, Hinnanit, Yaqir, Enav, Shaqed, Hermesh, Ale Zahav, Berakha, Yizhar, Migdalim, Qiryat Netafim, Itamar, Pedu'el, Nofim, Zufim, Avne Hefez and Revava. **How it fits the neighbours** (**derived** from the same CBS file, counts below): the file has 154 Judea and Samaria rows, 78 of them with a year of 1978 to 1993. `research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md` (PR #1665) takes 10 of the 78 and lists the other 68 as in no note yet; this note takes 20 of those 68; `research_notes/Era 1977-93/binyamin-localities-1978-93.md` (PR #1669) takes the 19 Mateh Binyamin rows; `research_notes/Era 1977-93/gush-etzion-har-hevron-localities-1978-93.md` (PR #1670) takes the 19 Har Hevron (12) and Gush Etzion (7) rows; so 68 - 20 - 19 - 19 = **10 rows** of 1978-1993 remain, one row each of ten local councils and towns: Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Efrat, Ariel, Beit Arye-Ofarim, Betar Illit, Giv'at Ze'ev, Har Adar, Immanuel and Karnei Shomron (the separate local council Karnei Shomron has the founding year 1978). The question: which Israeli localities of the Shomron Regional Council were founded between 1978 and 1993, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **The list.** CBS localities file 2024, rows whose district is "אזור יהודה ושומרון", whose local authority is exactly "שומרון" and whose year of founding is 1978 to 1993: **20 rows**. By CBS year: 1978 (2), 1979 (1), 1981 (5), 1982 (2), 1983 (4), 1984 (2), 1987 (1), 1989 (1), 1990 (1), 1991 (1). **No row has a year of 1980, 1985, 1986, 1988, 1992 or 1993.** By CBS subdistrict (נפה): Tulkarm 10, Shechem (Nablus) 6, Jenin 4. In the 2009 CBS list of localities by form, 18 of the 20 are "יישוב קהילתי" (community settlement) and 2 (Nofim, Zufim) are "יישוב כפרי אחר, יהודי" (other rural settlement, Jewish).
- **Dates.** **None of the 20 founding days is closed from a record of the day.** The sources I could open are Hebrew and English Wikipedia ([S]), 15 JTA bulletin items ([S], press), two UN Special Committee reports (press as the UN cites it), and two Official Gazette name lists (Yalkut HaPirsumim 3165 and 4012, a Government Names Committee list each, which date a name and give a map grid, not a founding). **10 rows carry a disagreement [C]**: Mevo Dotan, Elon More, Barqan, Hermesh, Ale Zahav, Berakha, Yizhar, Migdalim, Qiryat Netafim, Nofim. **10 rows are [S] on the year** (CBS, both Wikipedias and the table of the council's localities in Hebrew Wikipedia agree): Kefar Tappuah, Hinnanit, Yaqir, Enav, Shaqed, Itamar, Pedu'el, Zufim, Avne Hefez, Revava. Days that one source gives are tagged as that source's, not closed.
- **Coordinates.** All 20 rows have CBS coordinates (derived to WGS84, method below). 18 rows have an Official Gazette grid as an independent cross-check; 16 of the 18 differ from the CBS point by under 1.1 km on each axis on the rule of thumb below, Yaqir (1,281 m) and Berakha (1,190 m) by more. Ale Zahav and Revava have no Gazette entry I could find. English Wikipedia's own coordinates are within 160 m of the CBS point for 17 rows; Hinnanit (465 m), Yaqir (353 m) and Ale Zahav (601 m) are further.
- **Primary sources.** The only primary documents opened are the CBS file and its notes, the Gazette pages, and two UN Special Committee reports (which print what the press said and cite it; none of those papers was opened). No government decision, Knesset record, Reshumot item or contemporaneous Hebrew newspaper was opened: the National Library of Israel and the State Archives returned HTTP 403 to this server (tested 25 October 2026, see "Searches"), so every Maariv and Davar article that Hebrew Wikipedia cites is unopened. **[GAP]**

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a primary document (a CBS publication, an Official Gazette page, a UN report page) prints, read today. It says nothing about whether the printed value is right. A Gazette entry is read from the page image; the PDF text layer is OCR and wrong in places.
- **[S]** secondary: Wikipedia (Hebrew and English, always with the revision), JTA bulletins (press, not a record), Gvati. Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper, a book or a government publication, that source is not counted as read.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or a date this note computed; the arithmetic or the library is named. A derived value is never given the verified tag.
- Quotes are copied from the page read (Hebrew Wikipedia from the wikitext of the revision named in the last table, link brackets removed; JTA from the archive page text, with the date from the dateline of the bulletin PDF). Translations from Hebrew are mine. People without a Wikipedia article are named by role only; where a quote contains such a name the quote stops before it.
- Status words are quoted only as a source prints them. "Settlement", "outpost", "territories" and "West Bank" are the sources' words, not this note's.

## How it was done

- **The list, the CBS year and the coordinates.** CBS, localities file 2024 ("קובץ יישובים 2024"), https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (downloaded and opened 2026-10-25, 370,654 bytes, SHA-256 `a021c6a151863ded624d2f674f9bbcf90ba9f3660c8d55aebe6526ff7f5d2ad0`, the same file as `research_notes/Era 1977-93/jordan-valley-localities-1977-93.md`; 1,490 locality rows, one sheet). Filter: column "שם מחוז" = "אזור יהודה ושומרון", column "שם רשות מקומית" = "שומרון" (exact match; "קרני שומרון" and "שער שומרון" are other authorities and are not in the filter) **and** column "שנת ייסוד" from 1978 to 1993; a script returned exactly the 20 rows below. **Derived:** the file has 154 Judea and Samaria rows, 78 with a year of 1978 to 1993; of those 78, the authority counts are Shomron 20, Mateh Binyamin 19, Har Hevron 12, Gush Etzion 7, Arvot HaYarden 7, Megilot Dead Sea 3 and one each for Oranit, Alfei Menashe, Efrat, Ariel, Bet Aryeh-Ofarim, Beitar Illit, Givat Ze'ev, Har Adar, Immanuel and Karnei Shomron. The CBS intro (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/intro.pdf, December 2025, 14 pages, opened today) says the file "כולל את היישובים הישראליים באזור יהודה ושומרון" (includes the Israeli localities in the Judea and Samaria area) and, in its section on the founding year, that the year recorded is, "לפי נסיבות המקרה", the year of founding or the year the place was populated by Jews; the filter is that CBS category and nothing more. [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]
- **Form.** The 2024 column "צורת יישוב שוטפת" (current form) is, by the intro, set by population size, so it does not say what a place was when founded; I give it in the raw table below only as the number (190 for 9 rows, 370 for 9, 350 for Nofim, 180 for Ale Zahav). For the type I read the 2009 CBS list of localities by form (https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2011/local_authorities09/pdf/list03_h.pdf, 159,648 bytes, 16 pages, opened today; page 15), matching each of the 20 4-digit symbols: 18 are listed as "יישוב קהילתי" and Nofim (3790) and Zufim (3791) as "יישוב כפרי אחר, יהודי". **[GAP]** The 2024 file does not list its own code meanings and the 2009 list is an earlier year.
- **Coordinates.** The CBS field "קואורדינטות" has twelve digits; as in `settlements-1949-51.md` I read it as an Israel Transverse Mercator easting and northing in metres (six digits each) and converted it with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), rounded to five decimals. That reading is mine. **Derived** cross-checks: (a) English Wikipedia's own coordinates for the same 20 articles (MediaWiki API `prop=coordinates`, revisions in the last table), haversine distance; (b) the Official Gazette grid. No map was opened; each coordinate is one point per locality, not a survey of the founding site, and for rows that moved (Elon More, Mevo Dotan) the first site is elsewhere.
- **Official Gazette (Yalkut HaPirsumim).** The Government Names Committee's lists are published there. I read the Internet Archive copies of the same PDFs (nevo.co.il was not tried; the sibling note reports a captcha page): G2 = issue 3165, 24 February 1985 (3 Adar 5745), https://web.archive.org/web/20131005000648id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-3165.pdf (566,094 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `f40db934`; printed pages 1490 to 1498 are PDF pages 2 to 10); G3 = issue 4012, 4 June 1992 (3 Sivan 5752), https://web.archive.org/web/20160303170912id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-4012.pdf (729,916 bytes, `5cfae6ef`; PDF page 3 is printed page 3393, **derived** from the printed 3391 on page 1). The text of G2 states the period of its names as 26 June 1979 (1 Tammuz 5739) to 22 June 1984 (22 Sivan 5744) and G3 as 22 June 1984 to 6 February 1992 (2 Adar I 5752) (read from the text layer of their first pages; the Hebrew dates are the issues' own, the Gregorian ones are printed beside them). So a name printed in an issue was fixed within that issue's period; the issue does not say on which day. G1 (issue 2633, 10 June 1980, https://web.archive.org/web/20131224094153id_/http://www.nevo.co.il/Law_word/law10/yalkut-2633.pdf, `e3bcd6b7`) was downloaded and searched: it has none of the 20 names in the text layer except as other places. Each entry gives a grid "נ"צ" of two numbers. **Derived:** the old Israel grid is, to within some hundreds of metres, the ITM less (50,000 m, 500,000 m); I used that rule of thumb (mine, not an official conversion). A 4-digit number is read as hundreds of metres and a 3-digit one as kilometres, as in the sibling notes.
- **UN Special Committee reports.** The reports of the Special Committee to Investigate Israeli Practices Affecting the Human Rights of the Population of the Occupied Territories, transmitted by the Secretary-General: A/34/631 (13 November 1979), https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/8810/files/A_34_631-EN.pdf (6,762,152 bytes, SHA-256 prefix `8b32ffc4`, 141 pages; paragraphs 52 and 64 are on PDF pages 17 and 20), and A/35/425 (6 October 1980), https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/16165/files/A_35_425-EN.pdf (4,475,171 bytes, `17d02295`, 89 pages; paragraphs 105 and 108 are on PDF page 28). Each paragraph quoted was read on the page image (the text layer is OCR and wrong in places, for example "1919" for 1979). A report prints what the press said and cites it (Ma'ariv, Ha'aretz, Jerusalem Post, Al Quds, Asha'b), none of which I opened: the report is [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending] for what it prints, and the facts in it are press.
- **Founding days: Gvati.** Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (opened 2026-10-25, 1,348,249 bytes). The sibling note reports that its narrative and appendix end with the settlements of 1977. A full-text search of the file (niqqud removed, maqaf and hyphens normalised) for the 15 names that are not ordinary words (Avne Hefez, Itamar, Elon More, Barqan, Hinnanit, Hermesh, Yizhar, Yaqir, Kefar Tappuah, Mevo Dotan, Nofim, Ale Zahav, Pedu'el, Qiryat Netafim and Revava) returned one hit, for Elon More, and none for the other 14; the names Berakha, Migdalim, Enav, Shaqed and Zufim are ordinary words with many hits that I did not read as place hits. The one hit is “אלון־מורה, שהוקם על־ידי ״גוש אמונים״ בשומרון כעשרה ק״מ ממערב לשכם על־יד מחנה קדום” (Elon Moreh, set up by Gush Emunim in Samaria about ten km west of Nablus, by the Kadum camp): that is the Kadum group of 1975-77 (the same sentence is quoted in `research_notes/Era 1957-77/west-bank-localities-1967-77.md` row 17), so it dates nothing for this row. A bare substring search for "יקיר" also matches three times inside "קירור" (cooling), which I did not count. So Gvati dates none of these places. [GAP]
- **Wikipedia** (Hebrew and English) was fetched through the MediaWiki API as wikitext of the current revision at the fetch; each revision is named in the last table (`oldid`). It is a lead and a cross-check, always [S]. Where an article's infobox or category gives a year that its own text does not, I give both.
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Searches through the archive's wp-json endpoint, every query, count and day in "Searches". For each item used I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`), read it as scanned page images through PyMuPDF at 250 dpi and RapidOCR (the OCR drops spaces and mangles words), and took the dateline from it; quotes are from the archive page text. **Dates rule:** the archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the dateline of the item in the PDF and from "yesterday", "last night" or a weekday read against that dateline, and marked derived.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`.
