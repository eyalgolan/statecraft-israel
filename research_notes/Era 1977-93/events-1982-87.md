**Purpose.** A sourced list of the events of the window the issue sets, 6 June 1982 to 8 December

sources disagree. This fills the gap between `events-1977-82.md` (#1428, ends 5 June 1982) and

**Boundary check, 24 October 2026.** The three windows were compared on `origin/main`:

**Sensitive material.** Most of this window is sensitive: the September 1982 chain including Sabra
and Shatila, Bus 300, and the Jewish Underground. For those, official findings get their own
sourced lines, a casualty figure is given only with the source that states it and that source's
basis, and disagreement is recorded as disagreement with every version and no compromise number.
Nothing is said about intent beyond what a cited source states. How any of this is shown in the
game is the owner's call and nothing is proposed about it.

**Conventions** (as in `events-1952-56.md`, and as used in the two sibling notes)
- **[V]** verified: an authoritative primary source, or two independent sources that agree. The
  Knesset's own record is treated as primary.
- **[S]** single source, a page not opened in full, a day worked out from a bulletin date, or a
  fact resting only on Wikipedia. Wikipedia is a pointer, cited by `oldid`, and a fact carried only
  by it is [S].
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given with its source, and none is picked.
- **[GAP]** not found, or the source could not be reached.
- **No quotes are invented.** Short phrases in quotation marks are copied from the cited page.
- **JTA dates.** The date on a JTA archive page is the **bulletin** date. "Today" in an item means
  its **dispatch day**, which is usually the day before the bulletin. A weekday named in an item is
  resolved against the dispatch day, and the result is marked **computed here**. This rule is
  applied throughout and was not stated in an earlier draft of this note, although the marker it
  produces was already in use.
- **computed here** marks arithmetic or a date resolution done for this note, not something a
  source states. Every such resolution in this note was checked with a date library rather than by
  eye.
- **Pages of the Kahan report are read as rendered images**, never from the PDF text layer. The
  text layer is used only afterwards, to check that what is quoted here matches the page. Where the
  page misprints something, it is quoted as printed and flagged (see C9b, C9e).
- **Private people are referred to by role, not by name** (the owner ruling applied across the
  sibling notes in #1492). Office holders are named. Victims, attackers, captives and convicted
  individuals are described by role even where the cited source names them, and their names are
  not reproduced here. **The Kahan report does the same thing**: it names ministers and generals but
  introduces a junior officer as "intelligence officer A, whose full name appears in the list of
  names in section I of Appendix B" (C0). This note's practice follows the source rather than
  departing from it.
- **our reading** marks an interpretation and is not attributed to a source.

## The September 1982 chain

Sourced from the JTA archive, each item opened 24 October 2026. **The date on a JTA archive page is
the bulletin date**; "today" inside an item means its dispatch day, usually the day before, as the
sibling notes record. Dates below say which they are.

### C0. The window's own start date, and the run-up [V]

Read from **page 6 of 64** as a rendered image. **This section exists because the note's window
began unsourced.** The header takes 6 June 1982 from the issue, and until now no section cited
anything for it; the first dated content was C1, in September. The report supplies it.

**The war's start:** "The 'Peace for the Galilee' war (henceforth the war) began on **6.6.82**".
That is the first day of this note's window, now on a primary source rather than on the issue's
say-so.

**The run-up, as the report sets it out**, with its own printing kept:

- "On 12-14 June, **J.D.F.** forces took over the suburbs of Beirut and linked up with the Christian
  forces who controlled East Beirut." "J.D.F." is the page's, for I.D.F.
- "On **25 June** the encirclement of West Beirut was completed and I.D.F. forces were in control of
  the Beirut-Damascus road."
- Then "approximately one and a half months of negotiations on the evacuation of the terrorists and
  the Syrian forces from West Beirut", during which "various targets in West Beirut were
  occasionally shelled and bombed".
- "On **19.8.82** the negotiations on the evacuation ... were completed"; "On **23.8.82** Bashir
  Jemayel was elected president of Lebanon. **His term of office was supposed to begin on 23
  September 1982.**"
- "On **21-26 August**, a multi-national force arrived in Beirut, and the evacuation of the
  terrorists and the Syrian forces began. The evacuation was completed on **I September**", the
  "I" for 1 is the page's, "however ... the terrorists did not fulfill their obligation to evacuate
  all their forces from West Beirut and hand their weapons over to the Lebanese army but left in
  West Beirut, according to various estimates, **approximately 2,000 fighters**, as well as many
  arms caches".

The election date is worth setting beside C1: on the report's own account he was elected on 23
August and his term was to begin on 23 September, and he was killed on **14 September** (C1), nine
days before it would have started. **Computed here**, and offered as arithmetic only.

**The basis for "obligated to foresee as probable", which C6a and C9c rest on.** The same page, on
the Phalangists' aims: they wanted "removing a large portion of the Palestinian refugees from
Lebanese soil, whether by methods of persuasion or other means of pressure. **They did not conceal
their opinion that it would be necessary to resort to acts of violence in order to cause the exodus
of many Palestinian refugees from Lebanon.**" That is the report's evidence for the foreseeability
it later imputes, and it is quoted here so the finding in C9c does not rest on an assertion the note
never shows.

**A disagreement between the two services, recorded because it bears on C9 and C9b.** "there were
differences of opinion between the Mossad and Military Intelligence with regard to the relations
with the Phalangists." The Mossad "felt positively about strengthening relations with that
organization, though not ignoring its faults and weaknesses", while "Military Intelligence was to
emphasize in its evaluations **the danger in the link with the Phalangists**, primarily because of
this organization's lack of reliability, its military weakness, and other reasons we need not
specify here." A Military Intelligence officer's "negative evaluation, from Israel's standpoint, of
the Phalangists' policy during the war and their aims for the future ... **was vigorously rejected
by the Mossad**."

**And a sentence that settles how this note names people.** The report introduces that officer as
"henceforth **intelligence officer A, whose full name appears in the list of names in section I of
Appendix B**". So the published report deliberately withholds junior officers' names and keeps them
in the unpublished appendix. **This note's practice of giving junior officers and private
individuals by role only is therefore the report's own practice**, not a departure from it, and it
is a second place where the published text points into Appendix B (the first is at C9e).

### C1. Bashir Gemayel killed, 14 September 1982 [V]

JTA bulletin **16 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-troops-enter-west-beirut-following-gemayels-assassination:
Gemayel "succumbed to injuries sustained when a massive bomb explosion destroyed the head quarters
of his Christian Phalangist Party in east Beirut where he was attending a meeting". The same item:
"Eight other persons were reported dead and at least 50 injured", and it calls him "the 34-year-old
Phalangist leader".

The day is in the sources, and an earlier draft of this note wrongly called it a **[GAP]**. Three
items give it between them:

- 16 September bulletin: "Israeli troops and armor entered west Beirut in force **last night**
  after the death of President-elect Bashir Gemayel in a bomb blast **yesterday** was confirmed."
- 17 September bulletin,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/some-cabinet-members-critical-of-decision-to-order-israeli-troops-into-west-beirut:
  "the assassination of Lebanon's President-elect Bashir Gemayel **Tuesday**".
- 4 October bulletin,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/two-high-court-justices-and-retired-army-officer-to-comprise-commission-of-inquiry:
  "the decision for the Israeli army to enter west Beirut on **September 15**".

**Computed here:** 14 September 1982 was a Tuesday, so the blast is **14 September 1982** and the
entry into West Beirut **15 September 1982**. The 16 September bulletin's "yesterday" agrees, its
dispatch day being 15 September. That computation alone would be [S], resting on the wire's
wording rather than on a
record of the event.

### C1a. The report's own account of 14 and 15 September, and a boundary with the forces note [V]

Read from **page 8 of 64** as a rendered image, after the owner's coordination note on #1529 pointed
out that `forces-1977-93.md` (row O5, source S12) reads **the same mirror**. It does, and our
readings agree; where they overlap this note links rather than restates.

**The bombing, which upgrades C1 from a computation to the report's own words.** "On **Tuesday
afternoon, 14.9.82**, a large bomb exploded in a building in Ashrafiyeh, Beirut, where Bashir
Jemayel was [meeting] with a group of commanders and other Phalangists." The square brackets are
the translation's. C1 had reached 14 September by computing across three JTA items; the report
states it, and 14 September 1982 was a Tuesday. **C1 is therefore [V], not [S].**

The report adds what the wires could not: "For the first few hours after the explosion, it was not
clear what had happened to Bashir, and there were rumors that he had only been slightly wounded",
and "**Word of Bashir's death reached Israel at about 11.00 p.m.**"

**The entry, which upgrades C2 the same way.** "Execution of the I.D.F.'s entry into West Beirut
began during the **early morning hours of 15.9.82**." **C2 is therefore [V].**

**The decision, and who was not told.** The decision to enter "was taken in conversations between
the Prime Minister and the Minister of Defense and between the Prime Minister and the Chief of
Staff". Then: "In one of the consultations between the Minister of Defense and the Chief of Staff,
there was mention of including the Phalangists in the entry into West Beirut. **The question of
including the Phalangists was not mentioned at that stage in conversations with the Prime
Minister.**" That is the earliest point in the report at which the Prime Minister is left out of
the Phalangist question, and it belongs beside C9d and C9e.

**The written orders, and the one that mentions the camps.** "Order Number I was issued at 12.20
a.m. on the night between 14.9.82 and 15.9.82, Orders Number 2 and 3 were issued on **Wednesday,
15.9.82**, and Order Number 4 was issued that same day at 2.00 p.m.; Order Number 5 was issued at
3.00 a.m. on 16.9.82; and Order number 6 was issued on the morning of 16.9.82." The capital "I"
for 1 is the page's, as at C0. Then: "**The first five orders said nothing about entering the
refugee camps**, and only in Order Number 6 were the following things stated":

> "The refugee camps are not to be entered. Searching and mopping up the camps will be done by the
> Phalangists/ Lebanese Army."

The same order's clause 7 states that the Lebanese Army "is entitled to enter any place in Beirut,
according to its request."

**Three pages call 15.9.82 a Wednesday.** Pages 8, 49 and 59 all do; page 51 alone calls it a
Friday (C9b). Three to one now, and 15 September 1982 was a Wednesday.

**Page 12 corroborates C3 independently of page 29.** It has the Phalangist commanders' first
coordinating session "at about 11:00 a.m. on **Thursday, 16.9.82**", an agreement that "a company
of **150 fighters** from the Phalangist force would enter the camps", and then: "The entry of the
Phalangists into the camps began at about **18.00 on Thursday, 16.9.82**", the same hour C3 takes
from page 29.

**The boundary with the sibling notes**, as the owner proposed on #1529 and recorded here so the
three stay in step: this note keeps the **chronology and dates**; `forces-1977-93.md` keeps the
**operational and intelligence side** (its row I3b reads page 6 on the Military Intelligence and
Mossad disagreement, which C0 also quotes, and its row O5 reads pages 8 and 12 for the same
sequence); and `sensitivity-list-1977-93.md` (#1525, row A14) keeps **what is included or left
out**. **Our readings of pages 6, 8 and 12 agree, so there is no [C] to record**, the dates, the
hour of entry and the account of the two services' views match across both notes.

### C2. The IDF enters West Beirut, 15 September 1982 [V]

Same bulletin, **16 September 1982**. An IDF spokesman is quoted: "The IDF forces' entry was
accomplished without incident." The item then reports that "by mid-morning, reports from Beirut
said leftwing forces and Palestine Liberation Organization remnants left behind when the PLO
evacuated west Beirut last month had opened fire on Israeli troops", with "stiff fighting involving
tanks, bazooka, mortar and machinegun fire".

JTA bulletin **17 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/some-cabinet-members-critical-of-decision-to-order-israeli-troops-into-west-beirut:
Begin "called an urgent special session of the Cabinet for later tonight". On the argument made for
the entry, the item reports what Begin and Sharon "are expected to argue": that forces "had to move
swiftly into west Beirut to prevent Moslem leftists and a residual PLO force there from stepping
into the vacuum created by the assassination and to prevent Gemayel's Christian Phalangists from
wreaking bloody vengeance for their leader's death". **our reading:** that is the JTA's account of
an argument expected to be made, not a finding, and it is recorded here as such.

### C3. The massacre at Sabra and Shatila, the wires disagreed, the report settles it [V]

The three items read for this note give three different spans, and none is picked:

| Bulletin | What it says |
|---|---|
| 29 September | "the massacre of Palestinian civilians in the Shatila and Sabra refugee camps in west Beirut **September 16-17**" |
| 30 September ([link](https://www.jta.org/archive/begin-says-he-regards-himself-as-the-premier-as-fully-responsible-for-beirut-massacre)) | "The massacre of the Palestinians at two refugee camps in west Beirut **reportedly began the night of Thursday, September 16**" |
| 4 October | "the massacre of Palestinian civilians by units of the Lebanese Phalangist militia in west Beirut **September 16-18**" |

"Reportedly" is the 30 September item's own word and is kept. All three agree the 16th is the
start; they differ on the end, 17 or 18, and one gives no end at all.

**The Commission's report settles it, and it is read here as rendered images of two pages. [V]**

From **page 2 of 64**, stating the period the inquiry covers: the Cabinet's resolution set the task
as "to investigate all the facts and factors connected with the atrocity which was carried out by a
unit of the Lebanese Forces against the civilian population of the Shatilla and Sabra camps." And
then, in the Commission's own words: "**These acts were perpetrated between Thursday, 16 September
1982, and Saturday, 18 September 1982.**"

From **page 29 of 64**, under the heading "The Direct Responsibility", with the hours: "all the
evidence indicates that the massacre was perpetrated by the Phalangists between the time they
**entered the camps on Thursday, 16.9.82,. at 18:00 hours**, and their **departure from the camps on
Saturday, 18.9.82, at approximately 8:00 a.m.**" The stray comma-stop after the date is the page's.

**Checked here:** 16 September 1982 was a Thursday and 18 September 1982 a Saturday, so the
Commission's weekdays are right, and the span is **16-18 September 1982**.

**So the wires can now be scored against a primary source**, which is why all three were kept:

| Bulletin | Against the report |
|---|---|
| 4 October, "September 16-18" | **Agrees** with the Commission's span. |
| 30 September, "reportedly began the night of Thursday, September 16" | **Agrees on the start**, and the report's 18:00 entry on the Thursday makes "the night of Thursday" apt. |
| 29 September, "September 16-17" | **Ends a day early** against the Commission. |

This note now gives **16-18 September 1982** on the Commission's authority. The earlier draft left
it as a conflict because no primary source had been read; the wire conflict is kept above because it
is a fact about the reporting, not because the dates are still open.

The same page 29 passage adds two things this note had not recorded: "The victims were found in
those areas where the Phalangists were in military control during the aforementioned time period. No
other military force aside from the Phalangists was seen by any one of the witnesses in the area of
the camps where the massacre was carried out, or at the time of the entrance into or exit from this
area." And on the perimeter: "The camps were surrounded on all sides: on three sides by I.D.F.
forces, and on the fourth side was a city line (that", the sentence runs past the text read here and
the rest is **not quoted**.

**The toll is no longer a [GAP].** It is now taken from the Commission's own report, which is
reachable after all (see the routes table). The report does not give "a" figure, and this note does
not either: it sets out several, each with whose count it is, and the Commission's own warning
against precision. See C6 below.

### C4. Begin on his responsibility; the commission is decided [S]

JTA bulletin **30 September 1982**, same item. The responsibility is stated in the item's opening
sentence and is narrower than a bare *responsible for the massacre*: "Premier Menachem Begin said
today he regarded himself, **as Prime Minister, as fully responsible for any negligence or
wrongdoing that might have happened in connection with the Beirut massacre**." Only then:
addressing the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Security Committee, Begin "said he accepted this
responsibility even though he had actually learned of the massacre only after it was over — on
Saturday afternoon, September 18", and the item adds in parentheses: "(Begin first heard of the
massacre in a BBC world service broadcast.)" He "firmly denied that he or Defense Minister Ariel
Sharon had sought to duck an inquiry."

JTA bulletin **29 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/unanimous-cabinet-decision-a-judicial-commission-will-conduct-full-formal-inquiry-into-massacre-of:
a unanimous Cabinet decision for a full judicial inquiry. Cabinet secretary Dan Meridor's statement,

### C6a. What the report means by "indirect responsibility" [V]

Read from **page 38 of 64** as a rendered image. This is the passage the whole affair turns on, and
the note had been using the phrase without the report's own definition of it.

The Commission puts it in two limbs. First, the decision:

> "the decision on the entry of the Phalangists into the refugee camps was taken **without
> consideration of the danger** - which **the makers and executors of the decision were obligated to
> foresee as probable** - that the Phalangists would commit massacres and pogroms against the
> inhabitants of the camps, and **without an examination of the means for preventing this danger**."

Second, the response once reports came in:

> "when the reports began to arrive about the actions of the Phalangists in the camps, **no proper
> heed was taken** of these reports, **the correct conclusions were not drawn** from them, and **no
> energetic and immediate actions were taken** to restrain the Phalangists and put a stop to their
> actions."

And then the limit, which is as important as the finding: "**This both reflects and exhausts
Israel's indirect responsibility** for what occurred in the refugee camps." The word is the
report's; the Commission is marking the outer bound of what it attributes to Israel, having found
in C3 that the killing itself was done by the Phalangists.

**On who could be held personally responsible**, from the same page: notices under "Section 15(A)
of the Commissions of Inquiry Law" went to "the Prime Minister and two ministers, and in this
matter **no distinction was made between Cabinet ministers and officeholders and other officials**."
The reason given: "in principle, in the matter of personal responsibility, **no distinction should
be made between Cabinet members and others** charged with personal responsibility for actions or
oversights", and "any claim that calls for a distinction of this sort is **wholly untenable**."

**This explains the restraint in C9's wording on the Minister of Defence, which C9a flags as weaker
than the wire's.** The Commission grounds its approach on the earlier Agranat Commission, quoting
its partial report as reflecting "the essence of the correct approach, from a legal and public
standpoint, to the problem of the personal responsibility of the political echelon". The quoted
passage ends:

> "the question of the possible resignation of a Cabinet member in cases of this kind is
> **essentially a political question par excellence**, and therefore we believe that we should not
> deal with it..."

The ellipsis is the page's. So when C9 records the Commission recommending that the Minister of
Defence "draw the appropriate personal conclusions" rather than calling for dismissal, that is
consistent with the principle it had just adopted. **This note does not argue that the Commission
was right to frame it that way**, only that C9a's gap between the report and the wire has a stated
reason in the report itself, which is better than leaving it as an unexplained discrepancy.

### C9b. The findings behind the two operative recommendations [V]

Read from **pages 51, 55 and 57 of 64** as rendered images. C9 has the recommendations and C11 has

**On naming in this section.** The senior officers are named because C9 names them by office, as the
report does. The pages below also name a lieutenant, a captain, two lieutenant colonels, a colonel
and the Defence Minister's personal aide; **those are referred to by role only here**, as elsewhere

**The Director of Military Intelligence (page 51).** The report quotes his own explanation of why he
did not warn, in his words: "I am labelled as one who has always opposed the Phalangists, not from
today, [but] for four years already. In the morning, I read that the Phalangists were inside the
camps; and I know that this is as per the Defense Minister's orders ... So what could I say now? Why
did you send it [sic] in without asking me? Or should I act insulted? No, I simply step aside in
this matter. That's all." The "[but]" and "[sic]" are the report's.

The Commission's reading of that: "We believe that in these remarks Major General Saguy revealed the
main reason why he 'stepped aside' regarding the whole issue". Then the finding: "it was the duty of
the director of Military Intelligence, as long as he occupies this post, to **demonstrate
alertness** regarding the role of the Phalangists in the entry into Beirut after Bashir's
assassination, to **demand an appropriate clarification**, and to **explicitly and expressly warn**
all those concerned of the expected danger even prior to receipt of the report on Friday, and
certainly after receipt of the report. The fear that his words would not receive sufficient
attention and be rejected **does not justify total inaction**. **This inaction constitutes breach of
the duty incumbent on the director of Military Intelligence in this capacity.**"

The same page records what he did do on being told of the killing of 300: "All he did was give an
order to check the veracity of this report, and nothing else. He made no attempt to contact the
Chief of Staff or the Defense Minister".

**The Division Commander (pages 55 and 57).** On what he was told: "Brigadier General Yaron received
reports of acts of killing in the evening and night hours of 19.9.82. He received the first report
from" a lieutenant, "and from it it should have been clear to him that the Phalangists were killing
women and children in the camps." A second came that evening from the division intelligence officer
"concerning the fate of the group of 45 people who were in the Phalangists' hands", and "A third
report was delivered by the Phalangists liaison officer ... about 300 killed, a number which was
later reduced to 120."

On why he did not act, and the Commission's judgement of it (page 57): his "desire was to save
I.D.F. soldiers from having to carry out the operation in the camps, and this appears to be the main
reason for his insensitivity to the dangers of the massacre in the camps. This concern of a
commander for the welfare of his men **would be praiseworthy in other circumstances**; but
considering the state of affairs in this particular instance, it was a **thoroughly mistaken
judgment** on the part of Brigadier General Yaron, and a **grave error** was committed by a
high-ranking officer of an I.D.F. force in this sector." The finding: "**We determine that by virtue
of his failings and his actions, detailed above, Brigadier General Yaron committed a breach of the
duties incumbent upon him by virtue of his position.**"

Page 57 also records his own words at a Senior Command Meeting, which the Commission credits: he
"admitted with **laudible candor** that this was an instance of 'insensitivity' on his part and on
the part of others concerned." The spelling "laudible" is the page's.

meeting on Thursday 16.9.82", and the Commission concludes "The testimony of the head of the Mossad
should therefore be accepted, that only at the cabinet meeting of Thursday evening did he hear of
the decision regarding the role of the Phalangists and of their entry into the camps, **which by
then had already taken place**." That is consistent with C9, where he draws no further
recommendation.

### C9c. The finding on the Minister of Defence [V]

Read from **pages 43 and 44 of 64** as rendered images. C9 records the Commission's conclusion that
"the Minister of Defense bears personal responsibility"; this is the reasoning, and the operative
sentence runs across the page break, so both pages were read to quote it whole.

**The finding, in full:**

> "It is our view that responsibility is to be imputed to the Minister of Defense for **having
> disregarded the danger** of acts of vengeance and bloodshed by the Phalangists against the
> population of the refugee camps, and **having failed to take this danger into account** when he
> decided to have the Phalangists enter the camps. In addition, responsibility is to be imputed to
> the Minister of Defense for **not ordering appropriate measures for preventing or reducing the
> danger of massacre as a condition** for the Phalangists' entry into the camps. **These blunders
> constitute the non-fulfillment of a duty with which the Defense Minister was charged.**"

**And what he was expressly not held responsible for, which is part of the same finding.** Page 44:
"We **do not believe** that responsibility is to be imputed to the Defense Minister for **not
ordering the removal of the Phalangists** from the camps when the first reports reached him about
the acts of killing being committed there." The reason: reports "initially reached the Defense
Minister on Friday evening; but at the same time, he had heard from the Chief of Staff that the
Phalangists' operation had been halted, that they had been ordered to leave the camps and that
their **departure would be effected by 5:00 a.m. Saturday**. These preventive steps might well have
seemed sufficient to the Defense Minister at that time, and it was **not his duty to order
additional steps**".

**A number worth not misreading.** The 5:00 a.m. here is what he was *told* would happen; C3's
8:00 a.m. from page 29 is when the Phalangists *actually* left. The two are not in conflict, and
this note does not treat them as a discrepancy.

**The reasoning behind the first limb.** Page 43: "Had it become clear to the Defense Minister that
no real supervision could be exercised over the Phalangist force that entered the camps with the
I.D.F.'s assent, **his duty would have been to prevent their entry**. The usefulness of the
Phalangists' entry into the camps was **wholly disproportionate to the damage** their entry could
cause if it were uncontrolled." The Commission rejects the defence that the force looked
disciplined: "We **do not accept** the contention that the Defense Minister did not need to fear
that the Phalangists would commit acts of killing because in all outward aspects they looked like a
disciplined and organized army", and holds that "The **routine warnings** that I.D.F. commanders
issued to the Phalangists, which were of the same kind as were routinely issued to I.D.F. troops,
**could not have had any concrete effect**."

**The Chief of Staff's own testimony, which the Commission quotes against the decision.** Asked
whether he would have acted differently had a massacre been expected, he is quoted (p. 1677): "No,
if I had expected that this was liable to happen, or if someone had warned me that this was liable
to happen, **they would not have entered the camps**." And: "**They would not have entered the
camps; I would not have allowed them to enter the camps.**" And, on the aim of sparing I.D.F.
losses: "Then maybe we should have acted differently, by closing the camps, by surrounding them, or
bringing them to surrender in another week or in another few days, or shelling them with all our
might from the air and with artillery. As for me, if I had anticipated that this is what would
happen, or if such a warning had been given, they would not have entered the camps." The Commission
says why it quotes him: "in order to show that **despite the usefulness** of having the Phalangists
enter the camps, that step **should have been abandoned** if a massacre could not have been
prevented using the means in the I.D.F.'s hands."

**One passage about the killing itself**, from page 43, which is the sharpest thing the report says
about how it began: "a few hours after the Phalangists entered the camps, **soldiers at the site
asked what to do with the people who had fallen into their hands**, and the replies they were given
**not only did not bar them from harming those people, but even urged them to do so**." The
Commission adds that had those commanders heard "a clear and explicit order barring harm to
civilians" from the Defence Minister or their own superiors, "their reply to these questions would
have been different."

**And a remark the Commission makes in passing**, which bears on C4: "it is **ostensibly puzzling**
that the Defense Minister did not in any way make the Prime Minister privy to the decision on
having the Phalangists enter the camps."

**Read with C6a and C9a this closes the loop on the Sharon question.** The finding here is as
strong as "blunders" and "non-fulfillment of a duty"; the recommendation in C9 is still only that
he "draw the appropriate personal conclusions", with removal left for the Prime Minister to
"consider". C6a shows the Commission had just adopted the principle that a minister's resignation
is "essentially a political question par excellence" and not for it to decide. So the gap C9a
noticed between the report and the wire, which reported a call for the Minister's "resignation
... or his dismissal", is **not** a softness in the findings, it is the Commission declining, on a stated principle, to turn a finding into a demand.

### C9d. The findings on the Prime Minister, the Foreign Minister and the Chief of Staff [V]

Read from **pages 41 and 45 of 64** as rendered images. With C9c these complete the political
echelon, and they let C9a's wire account be checked line by line.

**The Prime Minister (page 41).** What he was *not* faulted for: "the Prime Minister first heard
about the Phalangists' entry into the camps **about 36 hours after the decision** to that effect was
taken, and did not learn of the decision until the Cabinet session. When he heard about the
Phalangists' entry into the camps, **it had already taken place**." Given the "**rosy**" reports he
had from the Defence Minister and the Chief of Staff, "the Prime Minister was entitled to assume at
that time that all the operations in West Beirut had been performed in the best possible manner and
had nearly been concluded. We believe that in these circumstances **it was not incumbent upon the
Prime Minister to object** to the Phalangists' entry into the camps or to order their removal." The
Commission is also "prepared to believe the Prime Minister that, being preoccupied at the Cabinet
session with formulating the resolution, he **did not pay heed to the remarks of Minister Levy**".

What he *was* faulted for: "we find **no reason to exempt** the Prime Minister from responsibility
for **not having evinced, during or after the Cabinet session, any interest** in the Phalangists'
actions in the camps." No report reached him "except perhaps for the complaint regarding the Gaza
Hospital, **until he heard the BBC broadcast towards evening on Saturday**. For **two days** after
the Prime Minister heard about the Phalangists' entry, he showed **absolutely no interest** in their
actions in the camps." The finding: "**The Prime Minister's lack of involvement in the entire matter
casts on him a certain degree of responsibility.**"

**Checked here, as arithmetic only:** the decision was discussed "on Wednesday morning, 15.9.82"
(page 49, quoted in C9b), and about 36 hours later falls on the evening of Thursday 16 September,
which is when the report says the Cabinet sat. The two statements are consistent. The Saturday of
the BBC broadcast is **18 September 1982** (C3).

**The Foreign Minister (page 45).** The finding: "In our view, **the Foreign Minister erred in not
taking any measures** after the conversation with Minister Zipori in regard to what he had heard
from Zipori about the Phalangist actions in the camps." The reasoning is sharper than the finding:
"It is difficult to find a justification for such **disdain for information that came from a member
of the Cabinet**", and "The Foreign Minister **should at least have called the Defense Minister's
attention** to the information he had received and not contented himself with asking someone in his
office whether any new information had come in from Beirut". The Commission says this holds "even if
we were to accept unconditionally his statement that the point under discussion was only the
'unruliness' of the Phalangists."

**The Chief of Staff (page 45).** He "stated in his testimony before us that **it had never occurred
to him** that the Phalangists would perpetrate acts of revenge and bloodshed in the camps",
justifying this by past experience and by "the disciplined conduct of the Phalangists". The
Commission's answer is flat: "**We are not prepared to accept these explanations.** In our view,
none of these reasons had the power to cancel out the serious concern that in going into the refugee
camps, the Phalangist forces would perpetrate indiscriminate acts of killing."

**Now C9a's wire account can be scored against the report, and it comes out well on two of three.**

| What the 9 February 1983 wire said | Against the report |
|---|---|
| it "accepted Begin's testimony that he knew nothing of the massacres until he heard of them from a foreign radio broadcast after the fact" | **Accurate.** Page 41 has the BBC broadcast on the Saturday evening and accepts he had not learned of the decision until the Cabinet session. |
| "it faulted the Premier for his detachment from events, though it found mitigating circumstances" | **Accurate.** "Lack of involvement ... casts on him a certain degree of responsibility", against "not incumbent upon the Prime Minister to object" and the "rosy" reports. |

So the wire was reliable on the Prime Minister, the Foreign Minister and the Chief of Staff, and on
the fourth it reported what the document's own summary reported. **That is worth recording because

### C9e. The systemic flaws, and what they explain [V]

Read from **pages 59 and 60 of 64** as rendered images. This is the chapter between the personal
findings and the recommendations, and it is what C9's general recommendation, an investigation of
"the shortcomings and the manner of correcting them", comes out of.

**The Commission's own framing, including its limits and a balance it insists on.** It sets out "to
dwell briefly on the flaws in the functioning of these organizational establishments", dealing
"only with the functioning of the various establishments from the time the decision was taken on
the entry of the Phalangists into the camps until their departure". And: "Unquestionably, there
were **many establishments that functioned properly, even excellently**; but in the nature of
things our attention is directed toward those establishments in which were revealed flaws". Its two
topics are "the flaws in the course of decision-taking by the policy-making institutions" and "the
flaws in the manner of handling the information which was received."

**A clean chronology, and a third page agreeing on the date page 51 gets wrong.** Page 59: "The
decision on the entry of the Phalangists into the refugee camps was taken on **Wednesday (15.9.82)
in the morning**. The Prime Minister was not then informed of the decision. The Prime Minister
heard about the decision, together with all the other ministers, in the course of a report made by
the Chief of Staff at the **Cabinet session on Thursday (16.9.82)** when the Phalangists were
already in the camps. Thereafter, no report was made to the Prime Minister regarding the excesses
of the Phalangists in the camps, and the Prime Minister learned about the events in the camps from
a **BBC broadcast on Saturday (18.9.82) afternoon**."

That settles the misprint flagged in C9b: pages 8, 49 and 59 all call 15.9.82 **Wednesday**, page 51
alone calls it Friday, and 15 September 1982 was a Wednesday. **Two pages against one**, and the
note keeps recording the odd one rather than deleting it.

It also tightens a small difference the note should own: page 41 has the broadcast "towards evening
on Saturday" and page 59 has it on "Saturday (18.9.82) **afternoon**". Same day, different wording within one
report. C4 quotes the Prime Minister's own account as "on Saturday afternoon, September 18", which
matches page 59.

**What the Commission says about that state of affairs:** the decision "required that the decision
on having the Phalangists enter the camps be made with the **prior approval of the Prime
Minister**", and once taken without him, "orderly processes of government required that the
decision be made known to him at the earliest possible moment. It is **not proper procedure** for
the Prime Minister to hear about this decision in an incidental manner along with the other Cabinet
ministers during a Cabinet session, when the Phalangists were already in the camps." And: "It is
**inconceivable** that the Prime Minister should receive his information about this from a foreign
radio station."

**No appraisal was made before the entry.** "When this decision was taken **its ramifications were
not examined, nor were its advantages and disadvantages weighed**. This is explicable in that the
decision was taken under pressure of time. Nonetheless, enough time existed before the Phalangists'
entry on Thursday evening (16.9.82) to carry out a situation appraisal ... **No such deliberation in
fact took place.**" The Defence Minister's Thursday discussion, in which he said "**I would move the
Phalangists into the camps**", "cannot be regarded as a situation appraisal in the usual sense of
the term", and a consultation held on the Wednesday "was a briefing and not a situation appraisal".
The Commission's remark on method: "**Experience and intuition are very valuable, but it is
preferable that they not constitute the sole basis on which decisions are taken.**"

**The information flaws, page 60.** "The information about the decision was **not transmitted in an
orderly fashion** to all the parties who should have known about it." Examples it gives: Command
Intelligence officers "were first briefed ... about the fact that the Phalangists would enter the
camps on Thursday, **some two hours after the operation had already commenced**"; the head of the
Mossad "learned of the decision only at the Cabinet session", and although Mossad personnel were in
Beirut and "maintained ongoing contacts with the Phalangist commanders, **no report was received
from them** regarding the special role of the Phalangists in the camps prior to their entry, **nor
did they collect any data at all** on events in the camps after the Phalangists had entered." More
generally: "**Matters that should have been reported were not reported at all, or were reported
late and in fragmentary fashion.**"

On the reports of the killing specifically: "the reports in the possession of Divisional Operations
about the **300 killed (or the 120 killed)** were not transmitted at all to Command Operations", and
the report about the 300 killed, received on Thursday evening in Command Intelligence, "was not
transmitted to Command Operations, and ipso facto was not brought to the knowledge of the G.O.C.,
either that evening or the following day." One exception is recorded: the report reached Military
Intelligence only "as the result of the **fine initiative** of Intelligence officer A."

**Two things on that page bear on how this note is written, and both are the report's own doing.**

- **The published report cites the unpublished appendix.** Page 60 ends a sentence with the
  citation "(**Secion 6, Appendix B**)", the misspelling is the page's. That is a concrete instance
  of the published text resting on material **no reader can reach**, which is the point C5a makes in
  the abstract. Anyone checking a figure through that citation hits the withheld part of the report.
- **The report anonymises a junior officer by letter**, "Intelligence officer A", while naming
  generals and ministers. This note's practice of giving junior officers and private individuals by
  role only therefore follows the report's own handling rather than cutting across it.

### C9f. The closing passage, the signatures and the terms of publication [V]

Read from **page 63 of 64** as a rendered image. Page 64 carries only the word "Close", a print
artefact, so page 63 is the report's last page of text.

**How the Commission ends.** On the army: "the I.D.F. should continue to foster the [consciousness
of] basic moral obligations which must be kept even in war conditions, without prejudicing the
I.D.F.'s combat ability", and then the sentence the report closes that thought on: "**But the end
never justifies the means, and basic ethical and human values must be maintained in the use of
arms.**" The square brackets are the translation's.

**It answers the objection to holding the inquiry at all**, which is worth recording because it is
the Commission describing the argument against itself: "Among the responses to the commission from
the public, there were those who expressed dissatisfaction with the holding of an inquiry on a
subject not directly related to Israel's responsibility. The argument was advanced that in previous
instances of massacre in Lebanon, when the lives of many more people were taken than those of the
victims who fell in Sabra and Shatilla, world opinion was not shocked and no inquiry commissions
were established." Its answer: "**We cannot justify this approach** to the issue of holding an
inquiry, and not only for the formal reason that it was not we who decided to hold the inquiry, but
rather the Israeli Government resolved thereon. The main purpose of the inquiry was to bring to
light all the important facts relating to the perpetration of the atrocities; it therefore has
importance from the perspective of Israel's moral fortitude and its functioning as a democratic
state that scrupulously maintains the fundamental principles of the civilized world."

And on its own reception: "**We do not deceive ourselves** that the results of this inquiry will
convince or satisfy those who have prejudices or selective consciences, but this inquiry was not
intended for such people. We have striven and have spared no effort to arrive at the truth".

**The terms of publication, which correct C5a.** Under the heading "Publication of the Report":
"this report and the attached Appendix A will be published after the report is submitted to the
Government. **Appendix B to this report will not be published**, since we are convinced that this
is necessary to protect the security of the state and its foreign relations."

But it is not sealed from everyone. The same page: "the right to examine the transcripts from those
sessions which were held in camera, **as well as Appendix B to the report, will be given to** all
members of the cabinet, all members of the Knesset Defense and Foreign Affairs Committee, the
General Staff of the Israel Defense Forces, and any person or class of persons which may be
determined by the Ministerial Defense Committee. Similarly, the right to examine Appendix B is
given to **those persons who received a notice in accordance with section 15(a) of the law**, and to
their representatives who appeared before the commission."

**So an earlier draft of this note was wrong to say Appendix B cannot be read by anyone.** It is
unpublished, and it is out of reach from here, but the report defines who may examine it. C5a is
corrected.

**The signing date, and a third confirmation of the membership.** "This report was signed on **7
February 1983**", which is the day before the date the document carries (C6). The signature block sets
out each name on its own line above its role: **Yitzhak Kahan** as "Commission Chairman", then
**Aharon Barak** and **Yona Efrat** each as "Commission Member". (The run-together rendering in an
earlier draft of this line was mine, not the page's.) They are the same three named on page 1 and in
the October 1982 wire, so C5's membership now rests on three places.

JTA bulletin **2 March 1983**,

### E2. The decision to support the bank shares [S]

JTA bulletin **13 October 1983**,

version.**" That sentence is what G4 and G7 later take for granted, and it is now sourced inside
1984 rather than inferred from the 1986 and 1987 items.

Two details the finding adds beyond G2: the men died **under interrogation**, not at the scene, and
the manner is given as bludgeoning, which the 25 August 1986 item (G3b) independently calls
"beating deaths".

**The Premier's response.** Same item: "The killing of two captured bus hijackers was a deviation
from the normal behavior of Israeli security forces when they deal with terrorists, Premier Yitzhak
Shamir told the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Security Committee today", **computed here, 29 May
1984**. "Shamir said the inquiry was set up to **prevent similar behavior in the future**."

**The Defence Minister's response, and a name this note cannot confirm.** The same item has him
saying on television that the report "**cleared him and Chief of Staff David Levy** of any
responsibility for the actions of some security personnel on the spot", quoting him: "Neither the
Chief of Staff nor I was at the site when what happened happened. Neither the Chief of Staff nor I
knew what had happened." It also records that he "established the Zorea committee after the media
claimed that two of the four slain hijackers were taken alive".

**On the Chief of Staff's name: the item prints "David Levy", and a second source contradicts it.
[C]** The Hebrew Wikipedia article on the post, read at a pinned revision,
https://he.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=43910926, lists the holders in order: the Chief of Staff
named in C9 from **16 April 1978 to 19 April 1983**, and then **משה לוי (Moshe Levi)** from **19
April 1983 to 19 April 1987**, under Defence Minister Moshe Arens. On that list the post-holder in
April 1984 was **Moshe Levi**, the same surname as the wire's, a **different forename**.

**Why that row is worth some weight, and still only some.** The predecessor row agrees with the
primary source this note already has: the Kahan report (C9) says of the Chief of Staff that he "is
about to complete his term of service in **April, 1983**", and the list ends his term on 19 April
1983. So the encyclopaedia agrees with the report on the one date the report states. That is
corroboration of the list, not proof of the forename.

**A third source, and it is the JTA archive contradicting itself.** The 2 October 1985 bulletin
already cited in I1, the raid on the PLO headquarters, attributes a casualty figure to "Israel's
**Chief of Staff Gen. Moshe Levy**". That is the same archive as the 30 May 1984 item, naming a
different man in the same post, seventeen months later and still inside the term the pinned
revision gives (19 April 1983 to 19 April 1987).

| Source | The Chief of Staff |
|---|---|
| JTA, 30 May 1984 | "Chief of Staff **David Levy**" |
| JTA, 2 October 1985 | "Israel's Chief of Staff Gen. **Moshe Levy**" |
| he.wikipedia, pinned revision 43910926 | **משה לוי (Moshe Levi)**, 19 April 1983 to 19 April 1987 |

So the count is **two sources to one**, the dissenting item is from the same archive as one of the
two, and the two that agree are independent of each other. **This note still does not adopt a
name**, because it does not need one: what the May 1984 item is cited for, that the Defence
Minister said the report cleared him and the Chief of Staff, does not depend on which man held the
post. But a reader should know the weight is against "David Levy", and that the disagreement is
**inside the JTA archive**, not merely between a wire and an encyclopaedia.

**This was found by reading this note's own sections against each other**, not by a new search: the
Moshe Levy attribution had been sitting in I1 since that section was written.

**The reception, across parties.** The 29 May item: a former Premier and "other Knesset members
representing both the Labor Alignment and the Likud coalition had high praise today for the
report". He is quoted on the commission: "took their job very seriously. They did not try to hide
the terrible reality. They exposed everything that happened without fear or any attempt to whitewash
the situation." On the IDF: the killing of captured prisoners "was not typical of the behavior of
Israeli fighting men", and "I believe that this moral value... must be retained by us at all costs."
The ellipsis is the page's. A Labor MK called the committee a "credit to the IDF and to Israeli
society", and "Only speakers of the ultra-nationalist Tehiya Party refused to comment on Zorea's
findings."

**And a claim for compensation.** Same item: "the lawyer representing the families of the two slain
terrorists, said today that they would demand compensation. She praised the press for playing the
role of 'the national conscience' in this instance. Otherwise, the entire matter would have been
concealed, she claimed." The lawyer is named in the item and is referred to here by role, as the
correspondent in G3 is.

**A date outlier, recorded and not adopted. [C]** The 29 May item puts the hijacking "**last April
12**". The 30 May item, the 26 April item and the 30 April item all have **April 13** (G1), and 13
April 1984 was the Friday those items name. So this note keeps **13 April 1984** and records the
single outlier rather than dropping it.

### G3b. The pardons, 1986, part of the gap, filled from a 1987 item [S]

JTA bulletin **17 April 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shin-bet-appears-to-be-involved-in-a-new-scandal. The bulletin is
mainly about a different matter (see G5), but in passing it supplies a piece of the Bus 300
sequence that G3 and G4 leave open.

It describes a former senior Shin Bet operative as "**one of three who received a Presidential
pardon last year** in connection with the murder of two captured Arab bus hijackers by Shin Bet
agents in 1984", and adds: "**None of the three was ever formally charged or tried**."

Two things follow, and the note states them as the source does and no further:

- By 1987 the wire describes the deaths flatly as "**the murder of two captured** Arab bus
  hijackers by Shin Bet agents", with no hedge. That is the same shift already noted at G4 against
  the 1984 official version in G2.
- People were pardoned "last year", so during 1986, **without charge or trial**. The exact date is
  **not in this item** and is not inferred from "last year".

The operatives are referred to here by role, under the owner ruling; the bulletin names one of
them.

**The date, and a correction to what this section first said [S].** JTA bulletin **25 August
1986**, https://www.jta.org/archive/seven-more-shin-bet-members-pardoned: "President Chaim Herzog
Sunday pardoned another **seven** members of the General Security Services (Shin Bet), for
involvement in the **beating deaths** of two Palestinian bus hijackers and in the coverup which
followed." **Computed here:** the Sunday before a 25 August 1986 bulletin, a Monday, is **24 August
1986**.

An earlier draft of this section read the 1987 item's "one of three who received a Presidential
pardon last year" as meaning that **three** people were pardoned in 1986. **That was wrong**, and
the count is now a **[C]**. The same 25 August item: "The decision followed the granting of
immunity by Herzog to **Avraham Shalom**, who recently resigned as head of the Shin Bet, and
**three of Shalom's aides** for crimes connected with the 1984 killing of the two Palestinians,
after the hijacked bus had been retaken. **None of those pardoned had been charged with any
crimes.**" So on this item's account there were **four** earlier (the service head and three aides,
whom it later calls "the four most senior Shin Bet officials") and **seven** on 24 August, at least
**eleven** in 1986, not three. The 1987 item's "three" is most plausibly the three aides, but this
note does **not** decide that; it records that the figure cannot be the total.

The former service head is named here as he is in G4: a head of the service is an office holder.
The seven are not named by the item at all, "The names of the seven pardoned Sunday have not been
disclosed."

On how the second round was decided, in the President's own words as the item gives them: he "said
he had **not consulted** with either the Prime Minister or the Cabinet, as he had done in the case
of Shalom and his senior aides", acting instead "at the recommendation of the Justice Minister, on
the basis of the precedent he had set in granting the earlier pardons"; he "had pardoned the seven
for **reasons of state security and the public good**"; and "**It would not be right to
differentiate between ranks and echelons**," he said.

**The first four are dated too, 25 June 1986 [S].** JTA bulletin **26 June 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shin-bet-chief-resigns-gets-presidential-pardon-probe-dropped: "Avraham
Shalom, head of Israel's internal security services, Shabak, or Shin Bet, **resigned Wednesday and
was granted a pardon** by President Chaim Herzog for any acts on his part or failure to act in
connection with a bus hijack incident in the Gaza Strip in April, 1984." **Computed here:** the
Wednesday before a 26 June 1986 bulletin, a Thursday, is **25 June 1986**. And: "**Three of
Shalom's aides accused with him were also pardoned.**"

So the 1986 pardons are **four on 25 June and seven on 24 August**, eleven in all, which is what
the correction above says and what makes the 1987 item's "three" impossible as a total.

The grounds, in the President's words as the item gives them: he went on television that night "to
explain that his decision was made in '**the national interest**' and '**to protect Shin Bet from
further damage**.'" He "cited judicial and legal opinions to the effect that the President's
exercise of pardon was **not merely an act of mercy** but could in some circumstances be an exercise
of **supreme national interest**", and "noted pointedly that he made the decision **after hearing
from the Inner Cabinet** which had convened with the Attorney General." The item also notes the
objection plainly: the pardon "immediately raised questions in legal circles and drew strong
criticism from some political quarters inasmuch as Shalom was **never tried or convicted** of any
offense."

**What the pardon stopped.** Same item: it "would seem to **preclude any investigation** of charges
that Shalom engaged in an elaborate cover-up amounting to obstruction of justice with respect to the
unexplained deaths of two captured Arab bus hijackers while in custody of security agents." The
Attorney General, who "had promised to announce Tuesday night his decision on whether to proceed
with an investigation", "told reporters that in light of the Presidential pardon **there was no point
in pursuing the investigation**", and "said he had **not been aware of the approach to the President
for a pardon until after it was made**."

With this, **G3b is complete**: both rounds of 1986 pardons are dated and their stated grounds
recorded.

### G3c. Between the pardons and the Harish report: 1986 [S]

Three items fill the months between G3b's pardons and G4's December report, and they explain how
one led to the other.

**The pardon did not end the demands, 26 June 1986.** JTA bulletin **27 June 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/demands-persist-for-probe-of-shin-bet-chief-despite-his-resignation-and-pardon:
"Demands for a full scale investigation of charges against Avraham Shalom **mounted within the Labor
Party and other coalition factions Thursday** despite Shalom's resignation Wednesday as chief of
Shin Bet ... and the pardon given him and three of his senior aides by President Chaim Herzog."
**Computed here:** the Thursday before a 27 June 1986 bulletin, a Friday, is **26 June 1986**, the
day after the resignation and pardon dated in G3b.

The item states the suspicion in the open: "The pardon, supported by **Premier Shimon Peres and a
majority of the Inner Cabinet**, was seen by many as a **deliberate ploy to avoid an investigation**
which Attorney General Yosef Harish said he had been prepared to launch." That is the wire's
reporting of how it was seen, not a finding, and is recorded as such.

**Who asked for the pardon, which G3b could not say.** Herzog, on Army Radio that Thursday, said the
pardon "did not mean that any further inquiry was ruled out", and is quoted: "It was not my
business... It is for the government... the government did not ask anything of me. **The Shin Bet
asked, and I had to decide**." The ellipses are the page's. So on the President's own account the
request came from the service, not from the government, which G3b's 25 August item gestures at when
it says he had consulted the Inner Cabinet the first time and not the second.

Two ministers, named as office holders, "said Thursday they would introduce a proposal at this
Sunday's Cabinet meeting to set up a **judicial commission of inquiry**", which is not what
followed in 1986; the judicial commission came in June 1987 (G6), over a different case.

**The Supreme Court upheld the pardon, 6 August 1986, and a police investigation began the next
day.** JTA bulletin **8 August 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/police-begin-questioning-people-inconnection-with-the-killings-of-two-palestinian-hijackers:
"Police began **Thursday** to summon for questioning people allegedly connected to the April 1984
killing of two Palestinian bus-hijackers in the custody of Shin Bet intelligence agents. This
follows the **Wednesday ruling by the Supreme Court upholding President Chaim Herzog's pardon**."
**Computed here:** on an 8 August 1986 bulletin, a Friday, the Wednesday is **6 August** and the
Thursday **7 August 1986**. **That the pardon was challenged in the Supreme Court and upheld is new
here** and nothing earlier in this note records it.

On the investigation: "The special investigations team, headed by police Inspector General David
Kraus, will question army officers, Shin Bet officials and politicians, according to local press
reports." Its limits were plain from the start: "The probe is expected to be brief, with findings
handed soon to Attorney General Yosef Harish, who will decide whether to take legal action.
**Shalom and his three aides are immune from such action.**" And its stated purpose was not
prosecution: "the prime purpose of the investigation is 'to clarify the picture'", so that those
responsible "in the political echelons and Shin Bet leadership" could "draw the necessary
conclusions" to avert "similar incidents in the future."

**This is the line into G4.** The findings were to go to the Attorney General, and it is the Attorney
General who heads the committee whose 65-page report is published in December 1986 (G4).

**A count worth noting against G3b.** The same item: "**Eight** other Shin Bet officials are
expected also to appeal for Presidential pardons." **Seven** were pardoned on 24 August (G3b). The
item says expected to appeal, not pardoned, so the two numbers do not contradict, but the
difference of one is recorded rather than smoothed over.

**The testimony conflicted, September 1986.** JTA bulletin **11 September 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/former-head-of-shin-bet-may-take-lie-detector-test-on-his-role-in-deaths-of-arab-bus-hijackers:
the former service head "may be given a **lie detector test** because his testimony in the case ...
**conflicted with the testimony** of Deputy Premier and Foreign Minister Yitzhak Shamir, Israel Radio
reported **Tuesday**", **computed here, 9 September 1986**. The conflict itself: "Shamir, who was
Prime Minister at the time of the incident, **testified last month that he knew nothing of the
cover-up**. Shalom maintained, however, that **whatever actions he took were with the full authority
and permission of the political echelons**." That is the same conflict G4 describes in December, and
it is dated to August-September here.

**And this sharpens the open question at G4, which was searched for and not closed. [C]** The same item says he resigned "under the cloud
of charges that he gave false testimony and suborned others to perjury during **two quasi-official
inquiries** into the case." So there were **two** such inquiries before the Harish committee, not
one. G4 records that the committee whose testimony "cast a shadow on Shamir's role" was "chaired by
Gen. (Res.) Meir Zorea" and gives it no date. The open question is therefore not simply whether that
was the 1984 commission (G3), but **which of the two quasi-official inquiries it was, and whether
the same chairman led both**. No item read here answers that, and this note does not guess.

**One more thing the same item records**, because it bears on what the pardons meant in practice:
"Shin Bet legal advisors who confessed to coaching witnesses in the cover-up **will remain in their
jobs** despite demands for their dismissal by Attorney General Yosef Harish." The decision was taken
at a meeting on the **Monday**, **computed here 8 September 1986**, of the Premier, the Foreign
Minister, the Justice Minister, the Attorney General "and the outgoing and newly appointed heads of
Shin Bet", and "was based on the opinion of a 'senior jurist' that the **pardons** granted the Shin
Bet men, including the legal advisors, **constituted total rehabilitation**."

### G4. The Shin Bet affair: the Harish committee, December 1986 [S]

JTA bulletin **30 December 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shamir-absolved-of-any-wrongdoing-in-1984-arab-bus-hijackers-killings.
This is inside the window and it changes the picture set out in G2.

The bulletin treats as established what the 1984 official version denied: it opens on "the 1984
killing of **two captured** Arab bus hijackers by Israeli agents and the subsequent cover-up of
what came to be known as the **Shin Bet affair**". So between 1984 and 1986 the account the army
gave at the time (G2) did not hold. **This note does not say when or how that changed**; what is
read of the inquiries is in G3, G3a and G6 to G8.

On the committee and its finding: "The special committee, a panel of lawyers headed by **Attorney
General Yosef Harish**, found that Shamir, who was Premier at the time of the incident, did not
order the killings and was not implicated in attempts to cover them up." The report was "the **65
page document** ... published at Shamir's request **Sunday night**", **computed here:** the
Sunday before a 30 December bulletin (dispatch 29 December, a Monday) is **28 December 1986**.

It "castigated the then Shin Bet chief, **Avraham Shalom**, who claimed he acted on orders from a
higher authority." An earlier draft of this note said that sentence ran on past the text read and
left the rest unquoted; it is complete above.

**The Zorea commission reappears here, and this is the one place the two inquiries meet [S].** The
same item: "It was Shalom's testimony before a government-appointed committee that cast a shadow on
Shamir's role, since the Prime Minister was the highest political figure and bore ultimate
responsibility for the Shin Bet. **The committee was chaired by Gen. (Res.) Meir Zorea.**"

**Not resolved here:** whether that is the same commission Arens appointed in April 1984 (G3) or a
later body under the same chairman. The item says only "a government-appointed committee ... chaired
by Gen. (Res.) Meir Zorea" and gives it no date. G3c narrows the question without closing it: a
September 1986 item establishes there were **two quasi-official inquiries** in which he testified,
so the question is which of the two this was. Nothing read so far settles it, so this note does
**not** merge them. **[C]**

**What was searched, so it is not repeated.** The JTA archive was searched on 24 October 2026 for a
second 1986 inquiry under the same chairman and for the name of the other quasi-official inquiry.
Nothing usable came back: the searches returned either unrelated items or a slug the archive no
longer serves. The question needs a source of a different kind, the inquiries' own terms of
reference, or a Knesset record, and none has been found.

The chairman's own verdict on the Harish report, from the same item: "Zorea was sharply critical of
the panel's report **Monday**. Calling the incident **one of the biggest scandals in Israel's
history**, he said on an Israel Radio interview that while the findings were legally in order, the
panel **failed to place any blame on the political establishment**", the sentence runs on past the
text read here and the rest is **not quoted**. **Computed here:** the Monday before a 30 December
1986 bulletin is **29 December 1986**, the day after the report was published.

The bulletin also records the reception, without this note endorsing either side: Likud spokesmen
"hailed the report and demanded that Laborites apologize to the Prime Minister for their 'blood
libel' that he was in any way involved", while "Critics of the report claimed it whitewashed the
political echelons which bore ultimate responsibility for the activities of Shin Bet".

### G5. A second Shin Bet matter, April 1987 [S]

The 17 April 1987 bulletin again. It is not on #162's list and is recorded only because it sits
inside the window, is bound up with the same service, and, as G7 shows, became one of the two
stated reasons for the Landau commission.

"The Shin Bet ... appears to be implicated in a new scandal involving the possible fabrication of
evidence that sent an Israel Defense Force officer to prison for treason six years ago." The
officer, a lieutenant, "was sentenced in 1980 to 18 years in prison and
reduced in rank to private" for "treason, espionage and passing military information to the
enemy"; the bulletin says he "was identified publicly for the first time". **He is a private
individual and is referred to here by role only**, though the page names him.

On the handling: "Tight censorship spawned rumor and speculation in the media" until an MK of the
Shinui Party "appealed to the Defense Minister and Minister of Justice to 'clear the air'", after
which "the cover of secrecy was lifted partially but the information which emerged in the media
Thursday was vague". Premier Yitzhak Shamir "told Israel Radio Thursday that the political
echelons were in no way involved or implicated", that those alleged to be connected "should be
investigated by the courts", and "There is no intention to cover anything up."

**The conviction is given three different ways across three items, and no version is chosen here.**
**[C]**

| Item | The conviction | The sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 17 April 1987 | "to prison for treason **six years ago**", and in the same item "**sentenced in 1980**" | "**18 years** in prison and reduced in rank to private" |
| 3 June 1987 | "the conviction by a military tribunal in **1981**" | "sentenced to **18 years**' imprisonment" |
| 2 November 1987 | released "after serving **seven years**" | "a **life sentence**" |

The 17 April item is **internally inconsistent**: "six years ago" from a 1987 dispatch points to
1981, while the same paragraph gives 1980. 18 years and a life sentence are not the same sentence.
**Computed here, and offered only as arithmetic:** seven years served before a release in May 1987
points to imprisonment from 1980, which is one of the two conviction years on offer; this does not
make either year right. The release itself is dated in G6.

### G6. The Landau commission appointed, June 1987 [S]

JTA bulletin **3 June 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/former-supreme-court-president-heads-judicial-probe-of-gss: "A
three-man judicial commission to investigate alleged misconduct by the General Security Services
(GSS) was appointed by **Supreme Court President Meir Shamgar Tuesday**. He acted at the formal
request of **Premier Yitzhak Shamir**. The panel is headed by **Justice Moshe Landau, a former
President of the Supreme Court**. Its other members are **State Comptroller Judge Yaacov Maltz**
and **Gen. (Res.) Yitzhak Hofi, a former head of Mossad**, Israel's external intelligence agency."

**Computed here:** the Tuesday before a 3 June 1987 bulletin is **2 June 1987**. 3 June 1987 was a
Wednesday, so the dispatch day and the appointment are consecutive days.

Who asked for it, and when: "**The Cabinet voted Sunday** to initiate a judicial inquiry into the
GSS, also known as Shin Bet, following charges that it fabricated evidence and extracted a
confession by illegal means to obtain the conviction by a military tribunal in 1981 of" the officer
of G5, "on charges of treason and espionage." **Computed here:** the Sunday before a 3 June 1987
bulletin is **31 May 1987**. So on this item's account the order is: Cabinet vote 31 May, the
Premier's formal request, appointment by the President of the Supreme Court 2 June.

The release that preceded it: the officer "was sentenced to 18 years' imprisonment. He **was
released on May 24** by order of the Supreme Court, which overturned his conviction and upheld his
charges against the GSS." The item gives no weekday for it; 24 May 1987 was a Sunday. Both this item and the 2 November one give his age and
community; neither is reproduced here, and an earlier draft of G5 carried the community before
this was made consistent.

**One trigger or two.** This item gives the inquiry **one** stated cause, the officer's case. The 2
November item (G7) gives it **two**, the second being Bus 300. Both are quoted where they appear and
neither is corrected against the other. **[C]**

### G7. The Landau report, late October and November 1987 [S]

Three items, and they disagree about the submission date.

**What it found.** JTA bulletin **2 November 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/report-finds-shin-bet-used-coercion-to-extract-confessions: "The Shin
Bet, Israel's internal security agency has, **since 1971**, used psychological and 'physical
pressures' to obtain confessions from suspected terrorists and **resorted to perjury to ensure
convictions**, according to the report of an investigating committee, made public, in part, on
Friday." JTA bulletin **12 November 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/three-shin-bet-agents-suspended-for-covering-up-arab-suspects-death,
puts the same finding as "systematically resorted to physical coercion to obtain confessions
**during the past 16 years** and committed perjury before courts and military tribunals in order to
conceal their methods and ensure convictions". **Computed here:** 16 years before 1987 is 1971, so
the two items agree on the period by different wording.

**The submission date is a conflict. [C]**

| Item | What it says |
|---|---|
| 2 November 1987 | "made public, in part, on Friday", **computed here**, the Friday before a 2 November (Monday) bulletin is **30 October 1987** |
| 5 and 6 November 1987 | "submitted to Premier Yitzhak Shamir **last Friday** and made public in part", the same **30 October 1987** |
| 12 November 1987 | "In a report submitted to Shamir **Oct. 28**", a Wednesday |

Submission and publication could be different days, which would reconcile them; **no item says so**,
and this note does not assume it.

**Why the commission was set up, per this item, and Bus 300 is one of the two reasons [S].** The 2
November item: "The commission was set up as a result of **two incidents** that created turmoil in
Israel and headlines around the world. **One was the April 1984 killing of two Arab bus hijackers in
the Gaza Strip after they were handed over to Shin Bet agents by the Israel Defense Force, which
captured them alive.** The second was the case of" the former officer of G5, "whom the Supreme Court
earlier this year ordered released from prison after serving seven years of a life sentence for
alleged spying and contact with terrorists."

That first sentence is the furthest any item read here goes on Bus 300: the men were **captured
alive** by the army and **handed over** to the service. It is a 1987 wire sentence about a 1984
event, not a finding quoted from any inquiry, and it is recorded as such, but it is flatly against
the 1984 official version in G2, and it names the handover, which no earlier item read does.

**The membership is given two different ways. [C]** The 3 June item has a **three-man** panel whose
third member is "Gen. (Res.) Yitzhak Hofi, a former head of Mossad". The 2 November item reads as
**four** people and reassigns him: the panel is "headed by former Supreme Court President Moshe
Landau, assisted by **a former head of Mossad, the external secret service, whose identity is
classified**, State Comptroller Yaacov Maltz and **Gen. Yitzhak Hofi, former commander of the
northern sector**." Neither version is adopted here.

**No prosecutions, and a secret part.** The 2 November item: the report "recommends **no criminal
action** against Shin Bet operatives who employed extra-legal methods and, in fact, **sanctions such
methods in some cases**. The extent to which they may be permitted is specified in a section of the
report submitted to Premier Yitzhak Shamir that **remains secret**." The 12 November item: "The
report, only parts of which were made public, recommended, however, that **no one be prosecuted**."
The 2 November item also expects "a flood of appeals to Israel's Supreme Court for new trials for
terrorists and others convicted on the basis of confessions".

**What the Cabinet did.** JTA bulletin **5 November 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/israeli-cabinet-expected-to-adopt-report-on-shin-bet, written in
advance: the Cabinet "is expected this Sunday to adopt the recommendations ... and appoint a
ministerial committee to oversee the activities of the internal security agency." The 12 November
item reports the outcome: "**On Sunday**, Israel's Cabinet **endorsed** the commission report and,
acceding to one of its key recommendations, **appointed an ad-hoc committee of four ministers** to
oversee the operations of the internal security service. Its members are **Shamir**, Foreign
Minister **Shimon Peres**, Defense Minister **Yitzhak Rabin** and Justice Minister **Avraham
Sharir**." **Computed here:** the Sunday between a 5 November and a 12 November 1987 bulletin is
**8 November 1987**.

**The reception, as the items give it and without this note taking a side.** JTA bulletin **6
November 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shin-bet-report-criticized-for-implied-support-of-torture: "The Knesset
has debated **10 motions this week**" on the report, which "has drawn the fire of leftist Knesset
members and others who believe oversight of the security service belongs to parliament." On the
substance of the criticism: "While the report **condemned perjury**, it allowed that 'physical
pressure,' **considered a euphemism for torture**, was sometimes necessary, but should be applied
under strict guidelines." The "considered a euphemism" is the wire's characterisation, not a
quotation from the report.

Three MKs are quoted, all office holders: **Shulamit Aloni** of the Citizens Rights Movement
"faulted the Landau commission for failing to examine the legal system within which Shin Bet
operated. The Shin Bet legal advisers who condoned lying in the courts have not lost their jobs, and
no mention was made of the political echelons, she said"; **Chaika Grossman** of Mapam "demanded the
creation of a parliamentary body to oversee the activities of Shin Bet's investigative department";
and **Amnon Rubinstein** of the Shinui party "proposed that confessions alone should no longer
suffice to convict."

The 5 November item adds that "**The findings of the Landau Commission are not in themselves

## The Jewish Underground, April 1984

**On the name.** "Jewish Underground" is the heading #162 uses, and it is kept here for that
reason. **Neither cited item uses it**: the 30 April bulletin speaks of "a well organized gang of
Jewish extremists based in the occupied territories". The later archive items use "Jewish
underground" in their own headlines, but those are not read yet, so the name in this heading is
#162's rather than a source's.

### H1. The bombs on the East Jerusalem buses [S]

JTA bulletin **30 April 1984**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/attempted-mass-sabotage-of-arab-owned-buses-shocks-israelis: "The
attempt was foiled **early Friday morning** when police sappers safely dismantled time bombs
planted in **six buses** only hours before they were to begin operating on a route from the center
of East Jerusalem to the Arab village of Kalandiya on the northern outskirts of the city."

**Computed here:** the Friday before a 30 April 1984 bulletin (a Monday, dispatch day 29 April) is
**27 April 1984**, which was a Friday.

On the arrests, the same item: "As of noon today, at least **30 suspects** had been rounded up and
remanded in custody for 15 days by district court judges all over the country. Under the law, none
could be immediately identified. But information filtering to the media indicated that most were
residents of the West Bank and Golan Heights and the rest of Israel proper." The figure is "as of
noon" on the dispatch day and is not a final count; later items in the archive give other numbers
(one is headed "25 suspected Jewish terrorists charged") and those are **not yet read**.

The charges, the trial and the sentences are in H2 and H3.

### H2. The trial and the sentences, 22 July 1985 [S]

JTA bulletin **23 July 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/three-jewish-underground-members-sentenced-to-life-imprisonment-twelve-other-members-sentenced-from.
**Computed here:** "today" on a 23 July bulletin puts the sentencing on **22 July 1985**.

**The bulletin names the convicted and I do not reproduce the names**, under the owner ruling: they
are private individuals. They are described by role below.

- "Three convicted members of a Jewish terrorist underground received mandatory life sentences for
  murder today." The three given life were **the underground's leader and two other members**. They
  "were found guilty of murder for their part in a machinegun and grenade attack on the Islamic
  College in Hebron in which **three Palestinian students were killed**." (The students are not
  named in the bulletin text read.)
- "Twelve others drew sentences ranging from **four months to seven years** imprisonment on a
  variety of lesser counts, all involving acts of violence against Arab residents of the West Bank
  and conspiracy to blow up Islamic shrines in East Jerusalem."
- The sentences were "pronounced by a three-judge panel in Jerusalem District Court" and "marked
  the end of the most controversial and politically explosive trial in Israel's history". The
  wire's characterisation is kept as the wire's.
- On the scale of the case: "There were originally **27 defendants** when the trial began over a
  year ago. **Ten** were sentenced in the course of the legal process as a result of
  plea-bargaining with the State Prosecutor. **Two**, who are Israel Defense Force officers, will
  be tried separately and are free on bail."
- The bulletin also records, without this note adopting it: the sentences "triggered new demands

**Arithmetic worth flagging rather than resolving:** 3 life + 12 lesser = 15 sentenced on the day,
against "originally 27 defendants", of whom 10 were sentenced earlier by plea bargain and 2 are to
be tried separately. 15 + 10 + 2 = 27, which is consistent. Recorded because the counts could
easily be read as overlapping.

Clemency and the releases inside this window are in **H3**.

### H3. The verdicts, and the clemency inside this window [S]

**The names of the convicted are not reproduced in this section either**, under the owner ruling.

**The verdicts came before the sentences, 10 July 1985.** JTA bulletin **11 July 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/15-jewish-underground-members-found-guilty-on-various-charges: "Guilty

**A date that does not line up with H2, and is left as a conflict. [C]** This item says "Sentences

**The attack's year is one item against one, and stays a [C].** This item dates the Hebron attack

**Two released, 13 May 1986.** JTA bulletin **14 May 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/two-more-members-of-the-jewish-underground-released: "Two more members

**No blanket amnesty, 17 September 1986, and this is where the two threads of this window meet.**
JTA bulletin **18 September 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/herzog-no-blanket-amnesty-to-convicted-jewish-underground-members:

to convicted members of a Jewish terrorist underground still in prison." **Computed here:** the
Wednesday before an 18 September 1986 bulletin, a Thursday, is **17 September 1986**. He "would
continue his policy of **considering each request for amnesty individually, on its merit**", and

The item gives the reason the question was being pressed, and it is the Shin Bet pardons of G3b:
"Herzog's remarks were prompted by the picketing of **Foreign Minister Yitzhak Shamir's** residence

"Herzog said he was **totally at peace with that decision** despite the controversy it aroused."

Two things worth noting beside other sections. The comparison being drawn in public, pardons
without charge for the service's men, continued imprisonment for the underground's, is the link
between G3b and this section, and it is the item's framing, not this note's. And the item has Shamir
holding the **foreign ministry** in September 1986, which agrees with the rotation in F1c and F2.

**Life commuted to 24 years, 27 March 1987.** JTA bulletin **30 March 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/herzog-rapped-for-reducing-sentences-of-3-members-of-jewish-underground:
"President Chaim Herzog came under fire from both the Israeli left and right **Sunday** after
**commuting the life sentences of three members of a Jewish underground to a maximum of 24 years** in
prison." The commutation itself was "the President's move **on Friday**". **Computed here:** on a 30

### I2. The stated cause, and the American reaction [S]

**This section existed on one bulletin until 24 October 2026**, and it was the only section of this
note resting on a single item. A second one, from inside the window, both confirms the date and
supplies what I1 entirely lacked: why the raid was said to have happened.

JTA bulletin **3 October 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/u-s-calls-for-end-of-violence-in-mideast-but-reiterates-view-that-israels-raid-on-plo-hq-was-justi.

**The date, independently.** The item describes "Israel's bombing of the Palestine Liberation
Organization headquarters in Tunisia **yesterday**". **Computed here:** on a 3 October bulletin the
dispatch day is 2 October, so "yesterday" is **1 October 1985**, the same day I1 reaches from "this
morning" in a 2 October bulletin, by a different item and a different phrase.

**The stated antecedent, which I1 does not give.** The State Department's deputy spokesman is quoted:
"While resort to violence is deplorable, it is useful to recall **the antecedents to this attack**,
which included **repeated attempts to infiltrate terrorists into Israel** and the **outrageous
murder of three Israeli civilians at Larnaca (Cyprus)**. ... Against this background, the air strike
is **understandable as an expression of self-defense**." The Larnaca killings are not dated in this

**The American position, and the tension inside it.** The administration "again today said that
Israel's bombing ... was an 'understandable' act of self-defense against terrorism", while the same
official statement "deplored 'the rising pattern of violence in the region, **including the latest

distress is especially acute, however, since **one act of violence touches off another** and a
pattern of escalation is established."

The President, "answering questions from reporters during a brief press conference, said any country
has the right to retaliate for terrorist attacks '**as long as you pick out the people
responsible**.'" Asked whether Israel had done so, he "replied, '**Well, I've always had a faith in
their intelligence abilities**.'" The Secretary of State, separately, said "**we need to be clear in
our opposition to the acts of violence from whatever quarter they come, and without respect to the
presumed rationale for them**."

The item also records that the spokesman denied a split: "there was **no difference** in the
Administration on this position as some observers suggested after the separate statements yesterday
by President Reagan and Secretary of State George Shultz." **This note does not adjudicate whether
the statements differ**; it records that the question was raised and denied. The page prints

## The prisoner exchange, 20 May 1985

**On the name.** #162 calls it "Jibril". The cited bulletin spells the man's name **both ways in
one item**: "pro-Syrian Ahmed **Jabril**" at first mention and "determined by **Jibril**" later.
Both spellings are the page's and neither is corrected here.

### J1. The exchange [S]

JTA bulletin **21 May 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/1100-palestinian-and-other-prisoners-exchanged-for-3-israelis: three
Israeli soldiers "held captive since 1982 by a Damascus-based Palestinian terrorist organization,
returned home **tonight** in the course of a lopsided, complex prisoner exchange during which
Israel simultaneously set free **1,100** Palestinians and others, among them some of the most
notorious terrorist mass killers in its prison population for as long as two decades." **That
figure is contested by two later items; see J4.**

**Computed here:** "tonight" on a 21 May bulletin's dispatch day puts the return on **20 May
1985**.

The bulletin names the three soldiers. **Under the owner ruling they are referred to here by role
only**: three Israeli soldiers, "captured in the early days of the war in Lebanon nearly three
years ago and held by the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine-General Command, headed by
pro-Syrian Ahmed Jabril." (The organisation's head is named because he is named in #162's own list

The wire's own characterisation of the terms is kept rather than softened: "lopsided", and "Many of

### J2. The precedent the item itself gives [S]

prisoners in the Ansar detention camp in south Lebanon for **six** Israeli soldiers". It adds why

That 1983 exchange falls inside this window and is **not** on #162's list. It is recorded here as
the source gives it, not proposed as a row.

### J3. Who negotiated it [S]

Same item: the terms "apparently were determined by Jibril in months of secret negotiations which

### J5. Kreisky's account of the negotiation [S]

JTA bulletin **23 May 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/kreisky-postpones-visit-to-israel-after-his-role-in-the-prisoner-exchange-is-disclosed.
The former Austrian Chancellor is an office holder and is named; the page also spells him

leader in Israel", and that he "would become the object of hatred and be held responsible for the
release of the terrorists". **Computed here:** the news conference was "yesterday" on a 23 May
bulletin, so **22 May 1985**, two days after the exchange.

His account of how it was done, which goes beyond anything in J3: he "had been asked **more than
two years ago** by families of Israeli soldiers captured in the Lebanon war to help gain their
release"; his special emissary "succeeded in mediating a first exchange of six Israeli prisoners
for more than 4,000 Palestinians"; the two of them "established **indirect contacts** between
Israeli authorities and" the head of the organisation holding the soldiers; and "it had been the
Libyan leader, Muammar Quadafi, who **convinced Jabril to meet Kreisky** during a visit to Damascus
**last October**", October 1984, computed here. Then: "In **December, 1984**, Kreisky wrote a
letter to Israeli Premier Shimon Peres and to Jabril presenting a compromise on which both sides
agreed."

On the limit of his role, in his own words: "**I only suggested the ratio of exchange** ... I have
nothing to do with the selection of those prisoners which the Israelis handed over." The spelling

**A small conflict with J3. [C]** J3's item calls the emissary "Dr. Herbert Amery, the **Austrian

### J6. What followed inside the window [S]

JTA bulletin **4 June 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/jewish-settlers-urge-government-to-deport-palestinian-terrorists-released-in-prisoner-exchange.
**Computed here:** the council "called yesterday", so **3 June 1985**.

**Where the released went:** "About 600 of the 1,150 freed Palestinians who had been serving time

**The Knesset upheld the deal.** Same item: "The lopsided deal created **grave misgivings, even

**The reaction in the territories**, as the item reports it and without this note endorsing any of

## The London Agreement, April 1987, a retrospective source [S], the primary still a [GAP]

An earlier draft of this section recorded that nothing contemporaneous was found in the JTA archive
and declined to use the later retrospectives at all. **The search result stands**, no item written

the time. **The refusal to read the retrospectives does not stand**: one has now been read, and it

**The source is a 1994 news feature looking back, not a 1987 report.** JTA bulletin **25 July
1994**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/behind-the-headlines-this-weeks-rabin-hussein-summit-will-cap-decades-of-secret-talks,
published seven years after the events and introducing its own list with "Media reports have made

Here are some of those details".

What it says, inside this note's window:

- "New contacts with Jordan led to a Peres-Hussein summit in London on **Oct. 5, 1985**, which led,
  in turn, to a **January 1986** tentative agreement on a temporary joint administration of the West

- "In **January 1987**, Peres traveled to Jordan with a sizable delegation. Between January and
  April, the two sides held several lower-level meetings."
- "These led to the so-called '**London Agreement**' between Peres and Hussein in **April 1987**,

  **Israel's provisional agreement to attend an international conference** on Middle East peace."

**What this is and is not.** It gives the agreement a month, two parties, and a substance, the

### What became of it, from a contemporaneous item inside the window [S]

The retrospective says the understanding "marked Israel's provisional agreement to attend an
international conference". **An item written at the time shows that proposal being killed**, and it
is contemporaneous even though it never uses the words "London Agreement".

JTA bulletin **14 May 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/double-setback-for-peres-his-mideast-peace-conference-plan-early-election-hopes-both-stalled:
"Foreign Minister Shimon Peres suffered a severe setback **Wednesday**. The **Inner Cabinet failed
to approve his proposals for an international conference for Middle East peace** while prospects
dimmed that the Knesset would vote to dissolve itself and open the way for early elections."
**Computed here:** on a 14 May 1987 bulletin, a Thursday, that Wednesday is **13 May 1987** and the
"inconclusive 4 1/2 hour debate Monday" the item also mentions is **11 May 1987**.

How it was blocked, which is a detail worth having exactly: "The Inner Cabinet, the government's top
policy-making body, comprised of **five Labor and five Likud Ministers**, did **not vote** on Peres'
plan Wednesday because the Foreign Minister, **anticipating a 5-5 deadlock, did not submit it**."
Then: "Premier Yitzhak Shamir stated that since the Peres proposal had not come up for a formal
vote, it **must be seen as having been rejected** and henceforth **no Minister may pursue it in
diplomatic contacts abroad**." The Foreign Minister "called that interpretation '**illegal**,'
maintaining that since there was no formal vote, the situation was unchanged", and said "**Let us
ask the nation to decide**" by disbanding the coalition. The Defence Minister supported him,
arguing "that the unity government was no longer committed to its basic policy guideline — the
pursuit of peace — and therefore must be dissolved."

**What this item does and does not establish.** It is a contemporaneous report that the
international-conference proposal was treated as rejected on 13 May 1987, five to five, without a
vote. It does **not** mention London, Hussein, or any April understanding. **The link between this
proposal and the London Agreement is made by the 1994 retrospective, not by this item**, and this
note does not assert it as the May item's content.

**A second, earlier source for that link, outside the window.** JTA bulletin **10 February 1988**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/israeli-government-split-on-u-s-peace-proposals, reports a 1988
American plan for "an international opening in Geneva" and adds: "Haaretz said that this was part of
an agreement reached at a **meeting Peres had with King Hussein of Jordan in London**". That is JTA
reporting Haaretz, so it is second-hand and it is 1988, outside this note's dates, but it shows the
London meeting being tied to the conference plan **six years before** the retrospective in the
section above, by a different route. Recorded as a pointer for the 1987-93 note. **Checked on 24
October 2026:** that note contains no mention of London, Hussein, an international conference or
the 1988 American plan, so this too is a lead rather than a duplicate.

**Still a [GAP]:** a published text of the understanding, a report written in April 1987 that names
it, a memoir, or a Knesset record of the debate that followed. The day in April is unknown here.
