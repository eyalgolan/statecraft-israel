# Israeli localities and Nahal outposts founded in Sinai and the Rafah approaches, 1978 to the April 1982 evacuation (research note, 2026-10-25)

Status: research only. Nothing here is in `content/`, `sim/`, `tools/` or `unity/`; the dated settlements file (#61)
stays paused until after the MVP. Refs #165 (1977-93 21), the settlements part of [the 1977-1993
design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-1977-1993-design.md) section 7.4. One topic of #165 only: the
localities and outposts founded in Sinai, including the Rafah approaches (Hebrew Pithat Rafiah), between 1 January 1978
and the hand-over of 25 April 1982. **The Golan (note `research_notes/Era 1977-93/golan-localities-1977-93.md`), the West Bank, the Jordan Valley,
the Gaza Strip proper (the Gaza 1978-93 note, `research_notes/Era 1977-93/gaza-localities-1978-93.md`), the localities inside the pre-1967 lines
and the lines are not in this note.** Which rows enter the game, and their names, wait for the owner's sensitivity list
(#150); this note makes no include or omit choice, no naming choice and no statement on the status of any place beyond
what a source says, quoted and tagged.

It follows the format of [`settlements-1949-51.md`](../GDD%20objectives/settlements-1949-51.md) and of the sibling
notes. Every page below was opened on **25 October 2026** (UTC) unless a row says otherwise. Not redone here: the

localities inside the pre-1967 lines ([`research_notes/Era 1977-93/localities-1977-93-pre67.md`](localities-1977-93-pre67.md)).

The question: which Israeli localities and Nahal outposts were founded in Sinai between 1 January 1978 and 25 April
1982, and for each: the founding date (and what it marks), coordinates from a named source, and the sources.

## Short answer

- **No source read gives a complete list for the window**, and none of the three primary or official lists that would
  (Statistical Abstract of Israel, Israel Government Year-Book, the Official Gazette) could be opened (section "What was
  not done"). The CBS 2024 localities file cannot supply it: the places were evacuated in 1982 (see the 1967-77 note,
  "How it was done"). Membership here is built from Hebrew Wikipedia's navigation template for the evacuated places and
  its category `סיני: התנחלויות` (both read 2026-10-25), checked against the English Wikipedia category and against the
  JTA Daily News Bulletin. Gvati's appendix (the list source of the 1967-77 note) stops at 1977 and has no row in the
  window.
- **Three new localities carry a founding event inside the window with a source I opened: Atzmona (20 March 1979),
  Hatzar Adar (December 1981) and Maoz HaYam (late 1981 or early 1982; end of December 1981 in Hebrew WP's Atzmona article).** A fourth, Pri Gan in Sinai, has no founding
  date from any source read ([GAP]); a fifth, Kadesh Barnea, is a 1967-77 row whose date is now in conflict (1977 against a
  March 1978 attempt). All of them are [S], [C] or [GAP]: the only primary document opened (the State Comptroller's
  annual report 30) gives a count and a regional body, not a founding.
- **Counts the sources give for the Rafah approaches in the window** (all as printed, none reconciled): 13 settlements
  (JTA, 16 Jan 1978, "Holit is the 13th settlement in the Rafah area"); "the doubling of the Rafah area settlements by
  1983 from 13 to 27" (a master plan of the head of the World Zionist Organization's Settlement Division, as the UN
  Special Committee report A/33/356, p. 17, para 51, prints it; it cites the Jerusalem Post of 27 Aug 1978; [S]);
  "the 15 Jewish settlements in the Rafah salient, and one town-in-the-making (Yamit)" (JTA, 5 Jan 1979); in the State
  Comptroller's audit (data of November 1979, printed p. 803, item 2.1) clinics in "thirteen settlements in the Rafah
  approaches" (my translation) and two more settlements that get medical help from neighbouring ones, so 13 + 2 = 15 (**derived**),
  which matches JTA's 15; and, on p. 802, 11 settlements in the cooperative "Eitam" plus three that did not join,
  14 in all without Yamit (**my reading**: 11 + 3, the report prints no total; its item 3 says Yamit is run by a
  "founding team"). The sources do not name which two
  localities lie between the 13 and the 15 (**derived**: 15 - 13 = 2); that is [GAP].
- **Coordinates:** 2 of the 4 section A rows have a point from one source (Wikidata, [S]); 2 have none ([GAP]). No
  coordinate comes from a map I opened.

## Conventions and tags

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]**: what a primary or official document I opened prints
  (here only the State Comptroller report), and what a data file prints. It says nothing about whether the printed value
  is right.
- **[S]** secondary: press (JTA is press, not a record), Wikipedia, Wikidata, or several sources that plainly copy one
  another. Where Wikipedia cites a newspaper (Davar, Maariv), I give the citation as Wikipedia gives it and **do not count that
  source as read**.
- **[C]** conflicting: sources disagree; every value is given and what each marks, as far as the source says.
- **[GAP]** not closed, with what was tried.
- **Derived** marks a number or weekday this note computed; the arithmetic is named.
- English quotes from JTA are copied from the archive page text, except where a row says the page image was read:
  there the printed words are quoted and any difference in the archive text is given in a bracket. Hebrew quotes are
  given without vowel signs; translations are mine. Each quoted string was checked by script against the page text I
  fetched.

## How it was done

- **The list.** Hebrew Wikipedia, template `נסיגת ישראל מסיני` (oldid 43174195), category `סיני: התנחלויות` (member list
  via the MediaWiki API) and category `חבל ימית`; for each year 1978 to 1982 the category `יישובים שהוקמו ב-<year>`,
  intersected with the Sinai category (1978: Pri'el; 1981: Hatzar Adar; 1982: Sufa (kibbutz), a successor inside the
  pre-1967 lines, outside this note; 1979 and 1980: none). English Wikipedia `Category:Former Israeli settlements in Sinai`.
- **The State Comptroller.** Annual Report 30 for 1979 and the accounts of the 1978 financial year, chapter "The military government
  in the held territories", section "Services to settlements", printed pages 801 to 804 (PDF of 4 pages):
  https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Documents/1950-2008/1980-30/1980-30-309-mimshal-zva.pdf (opened
  2026-10-25, 347,675 bytes), landing page https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Pages/Reports/5321-49.aspx.
  Pages 802 and 803 read as images as well as from the text layer (the layer scrambles the right-to-left order).
- **Wikipedia and Wikidata** as in the 1967-77 note: wikitext of the current revision at the fetch through the MediaWiki
  API, revisions in the last table. Wikidata coordinates and inception values come from `wbgetentities` (`P625`,
  `P571`), each with its Q-id and revision id in the last table. A Wikidata coordinate is a map point someone entered,
  not a survey.
- **GeoNames.** https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/EG.zip (downloaded 2026-10-25 11:51 UTC, 1,859,093 bytes,
  SHA-256 `03bcbd92b717ed4e96b33de9f57179a871ccc3efc6747956d1193134656fdd9f`, 35,027 rows). Searched for the Latin
  names of every row (yamit, atzmon, azmon, hatzar, hazar, adar, haruv, kadesh, maoz, neot, dikla, ofira, ophira, sadot,
  priel, pri gan, holit, sufa, talmei, talmai, nir avraham, zaharon, neviot). Only Yamit, Nahal Diqla and Sharm
  el-Sheikh matched a Sinai Israeli place, so GeoNames closes none of the rows below.
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin.** Queries through the archive's wp-json endpoint
  `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive?search=<term>&after=<from>T00:00:00&before=<to>T23:59:59&per_page=100&page=1`,
  page 1 only, run 2026-10-25. Every query with its date range, the total the endpoint reported, the number returned and
  the number of returned items whose text or title contains the query string (**derived**: my count by script) is in
  "Searches". The search is fuzzy, so I kept only items that contain the term; where the total exceeds 100, only the first
  100 returned were read. For every item cited I opened the bulletin PDF (`http://pdfs.jta.org/<year>/<date>_<no>.pdf`)
  and, where not marked "not read", read the dateline. **Dates rule:** the archive page shows the bulletin date; the event date is resolved from the
  dateline in the PDF and from "today", "yesterday" or a weekday read against it. Datelines marked **(image)** I read on a
  rendered page image; those marked **(OCR)** only in OCR text (RapidOCR, which drops spaces and mangles words) and not
  checked on the image; "not read" means I did not look for it.
- All requests used the User-Agent `statecraft-israel-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel)`.

## Row notes

### Section A: founded in the window

**A1. Atzmona [S].** Hebrew WP (oldid 43279026), infobox: founded 1979, evacuated May 1982 (its own value; see below);
text: the settlement "עלה על הקרקע ב-20 במרץ 1979, יום לפני הדיון בכנסת" (rose on the land on 20 March 1979, the day
before the Knesset debate) in protest, and was set up on a compound "שהוקם כשנה קודם לכן" (built about a year earlier), one of
the agricultural outposts of Sharon's ministry that the press called "ישובי הדמי" ("dummy settlements"). "בחול המועד פסח
1979, כארבעה שבועות לאחר הקמת היישוב" (during the intermediate days of Passover 1979, about four weeks after the founding) it moved to a
hill about 1.5 km north of Haruvit. It cites Maariv and Davar of 21 Mar 1979 and Davar of 28 Aug 1979 (not opened;
the National Library site answered HTTP 403, section "What was not done"). **JTA**, bulletin 21 Mar 1979, dateline
"JERUSALEM, March 20 (JTA)" (image), the article on the opening of the Knesset debate: "some 100 Gush Emunim members
demonstrated their defiance of the peace treaty by setting up camp near El Arish, some 50 miles inside Sinai, and
proclaiming a new Jewish settlement"; they claimed that the site, "Wadi el Arish", "is the Biblical 'river of Egypt'"; "The
squatters raised an Israeli flag and began erecting pre-fabricated huts" (quoted as the page prints them; the "i" of
"Wadi" is smudged on the page; the archive text reads "near. EI Arish" and "Wadf el Arish"). **Reading:** the JTA item never names Atzmona (it says "a new Jewish settlement"); that it is the same camp is a **derived** match on the day, the area (northern Sinai, near El Arish) and Hebrew WP's account, not a statement of the source. On that reading the day, 20 March 1979, agrees between Hebrew WP and the JTA dateline. They word the relation
to the debate differently (WP: the day before the debate; JTA: while the debate that opened that morning was in
progress); this note does not choose. JTA's first site (near El Arish, "some 50 miles inside Sinai") and WP's first
site (a compound near Haruvit, which Hebrew WP places on the coast near El Arish) are not reconciled here. Later JTA: bulletin 31 Mar 1982, "In the village of Atzmonah, established without government permissions after the
Camp David accords were signed, Orthodox squatters continued today to build a synagogue" (dateline not read);
bulletin 4 Mar 1982 spells it "Azmona". Evacuation: Hebrew WP says 19 April 1982; the evacuation days are in
`research_notes/Era 1977-93/lines-1977-93.md` section 1.4 and are not redone. Coordinates: Wikidata Q61125082 (revision 2228378755) 31.18165, 33.9405;
Haruvit's Wikidata point (Q7212421, revision 2521366182) is 31.17, 33.946, 1.4 km away (**derived**, haversine), which fits
Hebrew WP's "near Haruvit". The English Wikipedia article `Bnei Atzmon` gives a point for the successor in the Gaza Strip, not
for this place, and is not used; the successor is row 14 of the Gaza 1978-93 note, `research_notes/Era 1977-93/gaza-localities-1978-93.md`.

**A2. Hatzar Adar [S].** Hebrew WP (oldid 42805245): "הוקם בדצמבר 1981" by the Movement to Stop the Withdrawal from
Sinai, "כחצי שנה לפני נסיגת צה"ל" (about half a year before the IDF withdrawal), next to the Haruvit junction, 12 km from
Neot Sinai; "ב-24 בדצמבר 1981 התקיים במקום הכנסת ספר תורה" (on 24 December 1981 a Torah scroll was brought in there), citing Davar
25 Dec 1981 p. 2 (not opened). English WP (oldid 1378133723): "established in December 1981" and the same 24 December
item. **JTA** (existence and first removal): bulletin 4 Mar 1982, dateline "TEL AVIV, March 3 (JTA)" (image): "Unarmed
Israeli soldiers forcibly removed 60 resisting squatters from Hatzar Adar village in the Yamit area of northern Sinai
this morning"; bulletin 12 Mar 1982, dateline "TEL AVIV, March 11 (JTA)" (image): soldiers "forcibly removed kicking,
screaming militants from buildings they had occupied in Hatzar Adar village", the operation having begun the week before
and been suspended. The two JTA items fix existence and removal on 3 and 11 March 1982, not the founding. The year and the
month agree between the two Wikipedias (the English article appears to follow the Hebrew one; not established);
no source read gives the day of the founding other than the Torah-scroll day. The Hebrew Atzmona article
(oldid 43279026) narrows it: after Simchat Torah of October 1981 a group of 15 single people and one family came to
Atzmona, "ובחנוכה, עלה הגרעין והקים את היישוב חצר אדר" (and at Hanukkah the group went up and set up the settlement Hatzar
Adar), citing Davar of 25 Dec 1981 p. 2 (not opened). Hanukkah 1981 ran from 25 Kislev to 2 Tevet 5742, which is
21 to 28 December 1981 (**derived**: Hebrew-calendar conversion of those days by script), so on that one source the founding
lies in 21-28 Dec 1981, and it agrees with the 24 Dec Torah-scroll day. English WP spells the place "Hatzer Adar". Coordinates: Wikidata Q7212415 (revision
2272122381) 31.168, 33.953; 0.7 km from Haruvit's Wikidata point and 1.9 km from Atzmona's (**derived**, haversine).

**A3. Maoz HaYam [S].** Hebrew WP (oldid 43365334): infobox founded "סוף 1981 או תחילת 1982" (late 1981 or early 1982),
abandoned "מרץ 1982"; "במרץ 1982, שבועות ספורים לאחר הקמתו" (March 1982, a few weeks after its founding); a small group of returnees to religious observance, led by the group's leader,
first called the place "Magen David"; the activists of the Movement to Stop the Withdrawal gave it its name and sent
yeshiva students; about twenty settlers in a Maariv report of 3 Mar 1982 and a Davar article of 10 Mar 1982 (neither
opened). **A second Hebrew WP value:** the Atzmona article (oldid 43279026) says "בסוף דצמבר 1981, הוקם יישוב נוסף על חוף
הים מזרחית לעצמונה, מעוז הים שמו. 10 צעירים השתכנו במבנה ששיקמו, והתכוונו לעסוק בדיג" (at the end of December 1981
another settlement was set up on the seashore east of Atzmona, called Maoz HaYam; 10 young people settled in a building
they restored and meant to fish), citing Davar of 21 Jan 1982. So Hebrew WP has "late 1981 or early 1982" and "a few
weeks" before March 1982 in the Maoz HaYam article, and "end of December 1981" in the Atzmona article; this note does not
choose. The Maoz HaYam article also says Davar of 21 Jan 1982 reported that the coordinator of the Hatzar Adar outpost was
suspected of trying to take (the Hebrew says steal) six caravans from the settlement Pri Gan to the new outpost (Davar not opened); that is a
lead for A4 (a place called Pri Gan with caravans in January 1982), not a date. **JTA**, bulletin 5 Mar 1982, dateline "TEL AVIV, March 4 (JTA)" (image): "three families were evacuated in a
similar manner from Maoz Hayam and 12 from Nir Avraham", on the day 22 families were removed from Talmei Yosef at dawn.
The page prints "Maoz Hayam"; the archive text of the item reads "Maoz Hayom" (and "Yomit" for Yamit in the same item),
which is OCR. That is why the search "Maoz Hayam" (row 4 of "Searches") returned 1 item but 0 containing the term: the
archive text says Hayom, so the string "Maoz Hayam" is not in it; "Maoz Hayom" (row 19) returned the same item, 1
containing, and "Maoz Ha-Yam" returned 0. No founding date or day from any source read; the Hebrew WP values are
secondary. No coordinate (Wikidata Q140470160 has none): [GAP].

## What the sources say about the whole region in the window

- **Policy and "outposts", Jan to Apr 1978 (JTA, all [S]).** Bulletin 4 Jan 1978 (dateline OCR "JERUSALEM, Jan. 3"): the
  WZO settlement department "is going ahead with a project to establish new settlements in the Rafah salient of northern
  Sinai". Bulletin 6 Jan 1978: heavy equipment "levelling land for the establishment of eight new settlements in the
  Rafah salient". Bulletin 9 Jan 1978, dateline "JERUSALEM, Jan. 8 (JTA)" (image): "The Cabinet approved a compromise
  resolution today that called for strengthening existing settlements in the Rafah salient and other parts of northern
  Sinai but did not mention the establishment of additional settlements in the region." Bulletin 10 Jan 1978: Rivlin says the Cabinet "made it clear that no new settlements are presently under
  construction" and agreed to "strengthen existing settlements". Bulletin 3 Mar 1978 (B1): no new settlements in Sinai,
  "thickening" of existing ones. Bulletin 15 Mar 1978: "the government has decided to freeze new settlements in Sinai,
  including those planned and approved by the previous government". Bulletin 7 Apr 1978 (Knesset Finance Committee):
  "the 13 outposts" in the Rafah salient "were established several months ago"; Sharon "described them as centers of
  land reclamation. He said most consisted of a water tower surrounded by a fence." The "13 outposts" (Sharon, as JTA
  reports it) and Hebrew WP's "ישובי הדמי" are the same class of site on the reading of Hebrew WP's Atzmona article, which
  says Atzmona was set up on one of them; no source read names the 13 or gives their dates: [GAP]. They are not localities
  and are outside this note.
- **Regional body, 1978 (State Comptroller, [V, agent read 2026-10-25; a person's check pending]).** Annual Report 30,
  printed p. 802: "במחצית השנייה של 1978 הקימו המתיישבים בפיתחת רפיח ובצפון סיני אגודה שיתופית בשם "איתם", כמינהל
