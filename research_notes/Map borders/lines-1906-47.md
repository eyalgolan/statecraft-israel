## 1. The 1906 Sinai line (the Ottoman-Egyptian administrative separating line)

**Dates.**
- An agreement on the line was reached after the Sultan's Imperial Iradé of 11 September 1906 and "signed on 1 October
  1906" (Taba award, para. 44, p. 18).
- Demarcation: the commissioners left Rafah down the line towards Taba on 6 October 1906 (para. 53, p. 20). Owen
  reported arriving at Taba on 17 October 1906 after erecting ninety intervisible pillars (para. 54, p. 21;
  paraphrased because the scan's OCR garbles the bracket in the quote).
- Masonry pillars: according to Shoucair, "the first pillar was built on 31 December 1906 at Ras Taba and numbered 91"
  and "the last pillar, numbered 1, was built on 9 February 1907" (para. 58, p. 22).
- **[C] When it was signed.** Biger (p. 16) has an agreement signed in May 1906 that set an "administrative separating
  line" between the Sinai district and the Ottoman districts of Jerusalem and Hejaz (paraphrase), and then (p. 17) the
  boundary agreement finally signed in October 1906 (paraphrase). The Taba award (paras. 44-46, pp. 18-19) gives only 1
  October 1906 as the signature. This note prefers **1 October 1906**. Our reading is that Biger's "May" is the
  agreement in principle that ended the 1906 crisis, which the award describes as negotiations, not a signed text.
- Later status: in the House of Commons on 16 July 1925 the government said the line "was defined in 1906 by a boundary
  commission and has not since been modified" (Taba award, para. 72, pp. 24-25). In a letter of 25 June 1926 the High
  Commissioner told Egypt that "the Palestine and Egyptian frontier as defined in the year 1906 will be in no way
  affected by the delimitation of the frontiers of the mandated territory of Palestine" (para. 74, p. 25). A Foreign
  Office annex of 4 April 1932 said the line "does not appear at any stage to have been formally constituted an
  international frontier" (para. 76, p. 25). Biger (p. 28) adds that no formal act ever fixed it as Palestine's
  southern border, but Mandate maps marked it as an international boundary (paraphrase).
- Afterwards: Article II of the Egypt-Israel Treaty of Peace (26 March 1979) makes the permanent boundary "the
  recognized international boundary between Egypt and the former mandated territory of Palestine" (Avalon Project,
  https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/isregypt.asp). The game's period ends before that, so this only fixes the
  line's identity, not a date in scope.

**Instrument.** The Agreement signed at Rafah on 1 October 1906 (Ottoman and Egyptian commissioners). Article 1
begins: "The administrative separating line, as shown on map attached to this Agreement, begins at the point of Ras
Taba on the western shore of the Gulf of Akaba" and ends "to the Mediterranean Sea, passing over hill of ruins on the
sea-shore" (Taba award, para. 46, p. 19, quoting the contemporaneous English translation in Owen's General Report).
Between those points the line runs by straight lines through named summits and the astronomical stations A 3 to A 13
(same paragraph).
- Where published: the authentic text is Turkish. The English translation was made from an Arabic translation and
  "printed in a number of official sources" (para. 45, p. 18). The award uses Owen's General Report (para. 45, p. 19).
  Hertslet's map No. 44 (below) cites "[No. 373, page 1201.]", our reading: the treaty number and page in Hertslet's
  vol. III.
- The annexed map: Article 2 says the line was "indicated by a black broken line on duplicate maps . . . signed and
  exchanged simultaneously with the Agreement". Of those maps the award says "The fate of these duplicate maps annexed
  to the Agreement is not clear". The British copy is believed lost, and a photocopy said to come from Turkey's copy is
  contested (para. 49, pp. 19-20).

**Map source to digitise.**
- **Hertslet's map No. 44**, "MAP SHOWING TURCO-EGYPTIAN BOUNDARY OF 1st OCTOBER 1906", scale 1:500,000 (read on the
  map; legend "Boundary line as per Turco-Egyptian Agreement of 1st October 1906"), from Edward Hertslet, *The Map of
  Africa by Treaty*, 3rd ed., vol. III (HMSO, 1909). Library of Congress scan:
  https://www.loc.gov/resource/g8200m.gct00004/?sp=44. Commons copy: "File:No. 44 Map showing Turco-Egyptian boundary
  of 1st October.tif" (3436 x 6268 px; tagged {{PD-UKGov}}; page revision 1285730933). The sheet has a graticule
  (our reading of the preview), so it can be georeferenced on its own grid. Expected accuracy, our reading: about
  +-0.5-1 km at 1:500,000.
- **Better, if found:** the 1915 War Office map of Sinai, printed at 1:125,000 and 1:250,000 and published by the
  Survey of Egypt in 1926 at 1:250,000, "the only map for some time which plotted the position of individual boundary
  pillars along the line" (Taba award, paras. 63 and 75, pp. 23 and 25). Not found online ([GAP]). (24 Oct) Searched again
  and not found online. Read: the Albright Institute (Jerusalem) map-collection list,
  https://aiar.org/old-library/map-collection/ (opened 24 Oct), which lists "Sinai Peninsula 1:250,000", British War
  Office, 4 sheets, 46.5 x 64 cm, 1915, call number 35-15-1 to 35-15-4 [S] (a library holding, so a copy exists to
  request; it is not a scan). The Australian War Memorial and Library of Congress catalogue records for 1915 War Office
  Sinai sheets at 1:125,000 came up in a web search and were not opened. Whether the Albright sheets show the pillars
  is not stated in the list. The award (para. 60,
  p. 22) also refers to the 1906 Wade/Keeling survey map. Commons has "File:Egypt Ottoman border 1906.jpg" (578 x 1247
  px; source given as the *Report on the Delimitation of the Turco-Egyptian Boundary Between the Vilayet of the Hejaz and
  the Peninsula of Sinai* (June-September, 1906), dated 1907): too small to digitise.
- For the Mandate period, Survey of Palestine sheets show this frontier (a 1:250,000 village index sheet is on Commons,
  see section 5). The Mandate frontier already in `content/map` comes from D-B's own source, the Survey of Palestine
  "Administrative Divisions" map (1946, overprinted 1947; `docs/oss/DECISIONS.md`, D-B), from a 1:750,000 source, about +-1 km
  (`armistice-lines-1949.md`, Lebanon row).
- Licence: Hertslet is an HMSO publication of 1909. For a Crown work published commercially within 75 years of being
  made, Crown copyright runs "until the end of the period of 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which it was
  first so published" (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, s. 163(3)(b),
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/163; otherwise (3)(a) gives 125 years from the year it was made).
  Our reading: Hertslet (1909), Cmd. 1910 (1923) and Cmd. 5479 (1937) were published when made, so their terms ended by
  1959, 1973 and 1987; that they count as Crown works (made by officers of the Crown) is our reading for Hertslet's
  compilation, and the 1988 Act's transitional rules for older works were not checked. The Commons {{PD-UKGov}} tag
  agrees.
  The Taba award is a UN publication (RIAA). Only its quotations of the 1906 text are used.

**Names.**
- The instrument: "administrative separating line" (Taba award, para. 46). Hertslet: "Turco-Egyptian Boundary".
- Israeli perspective: Biger (p. 16) quotes the agreement's term "קו הפרדה מנהלי" and on p. 17 writes "קו ההפרדה
  המנהלי" between Rafah and Taba. He also calls it the 1906 line (p. 17), the oldest line in the country's borders in
  modern times (paraphrase).
- 1979 treaty term: "the recognized international boundary between Egypt and the former mandated territory of
  Palestine" (above).
- [GAP] no Egyptian or Arabic-language name was looked up.

## 2. The 1922 separation of Transjordan (the eastern line)

**Dates.**
- 14 September 1922: the Secretary-General submits to the Council the British memorandum on Article 25 (League of Nations
  note, Geneva, "September 14th. 1922", UNISPAL scan,
  https://www.un.org/unispal/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/C647-1921-VI_EN.pdf. The scan's OCR is poor; its heading reads
  "C.647" with "1921 VI", our reading, and the file name has the same).
- 16 September 1922: the memorandum was "presented by the British Government to the Council of the League on September
  16th, 1922", and "The memorandum was approved by the Council subject to the decision taken at its meeting in London on
  July 24th, 1922" about when the Palestine and Syrian mandates come into force (Note by the Secretary-General, Geneva,
  23 September 1922, as transcribed by the Economic Cooperation Foundation, https://content.ecf.org.il/files/M00302%20-%20TransjordanMemorandum.pdf) [T].
- 29 September 1923: the Mandate for Palestine "finally entered into force" (Taba award, para. 71, p. 24).
- **[C]** Biger (p. 38) says the High Commissioner's line was published in Hebrew in the government's Official Gazette in
  a special issue in September 1922, and that it gained international force when the League accepted the amendment to
  the Mandate in September 1923 (paraphrase). The League papers above give 16 September 1922 as the day the memorandum
  was presented to the Council and record its approval in the note of 23 September 1922; they do not state the day of
  approval. Our reading: the Council approved it at the session where it was presented, between 16 and 23 September
  1922, and it took effect when the Mandate came into force on 29 September 1923. For a `from` date this note prefers
  **16 September 1922** (the presentation day, the earliest the League papers support), with 29 September 1923 noted
  as the entry into force. [GAP] the Gazette issue of September 1922 was not found. (24 Oct) Searched again and not
  found. Read: Wikipedia's "Transjordan memorandum" and "Palestine Order in Council" pages (opened 24 Oct;
  [S]). They give the Order in Council of 1922 as published on 1 September 1922 in an Extraordinary issue of the Palestine
  Gazette, which is a different instrument from the memorandum, and say nothing of an issue carrying the memorandum. The
  Mandatory's report to the League (C. 452. I. M. 166. I. 1925. VI, UNISPAL PDF, https://www.un.org/unispal/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/C-452I-M-166I-1925-VI_EN.pdf, link from a web search; this box got HTTP 403, the reviewer opened it) was searched for
  "Gazette": its two hits are unrelated (PDF p. 15, a legal holiday "proclaimed in the Gazette"; p. 45, the Mining Ordinance draft of 15 December 1924, as reported by the reviewer). Not opened: the Israel State Archives' digital
  Palestine Gazette collection (reported in a news item, not found by this box) and the National Library of Israel.
- Ground marking: Biger (p. 38) says the line was not marked on the ground in the Arava during the Mandate, except a
  stretch of about 4 km north of the Gulf of Aqaba marked in 1946. He also says that in 1927 the High Commissioner Lord
  Plumer ruled that the line always follows the middle of the Jordan and moves with the river (paraphrase).

**Instrument.** The British memorandum asking the Council to resolve under Article 25 of the Mandate that certain
provisions do not apply to "the territory known as Trans-Jordan, which comprises all territory lying to the east of a
line drawn from a point two miles west of the town of Akaba on the Gulf of that name up the centre of the Wady Araba,
Dead Sea and River Jordan to its junction with the River Yarmuk; thence up the centre of that river to the Syrian
Frontier" (ECF transcription [T]). The UNISPAL scan of the 14 September note has the same wording as far as its OCR can
be read, including "two miles west of the town of Akaba", but ends in lower case ("Syrian fro n tie r" in the OCR).
Article 25 itself: "In the territories lying between the Jordan and the eastern boundary of Palestine as ultimately
determined, the Mandatory shall be entitled, with the consent of the Council of the League of Nations, to postpone or
withhold application of such provisions of this mandate as he may consider inapplicable to the existing local
conditions" (Avalon Project, The Palestine Mandate, https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/palmanda.asp).
- [GAP] the League symbol of the 23 September note was not on the transcription. A Command paper printing of the
  memorandum was reported in search results but not opened.

**Map source to digitise.** None is annexed: the line is defined in words (our reading of both copies, which have no
map reference). It follows river and wadi centres, so it is traced on Survey of Palestine sheets (1:250,000 for the
whole line, 1:100,000 where needed; D-B status). Two points need a ruling before drawing, our reading:
- the start "two miles west of the town of Akaba" (3.2 km), against the 1946 marking Biger shows (p. 38, fig. 17, a
  small sketch map credited on p. 2 to Moshe Brawer's book on Israel's borders; not digitisable at that size, our
  reading);
- "the centre of the Dead Sea", which the text gives no points for, so it needs a convention; Biger (p. 37) says the
  1922 talks agreed two points, one in the north and one in the south of the Dead Sea, joined by a line on a map
  (paraphrase). [GAP] that map was not found.
Expected accuracy, our reading: +-0.2-0.5 km on rivers at 1:250,000, worse in the Arava, where nothing was marked.

**Names.**
- The instrument: "the territory known as Trans-Jordan" (memorandum [T]). The ECF transcription's heading is
  "Territory known as Trans-Jordan".
- Israeli perspective: Biger (p. 37) calls it "הפרדת עבר הירדן מארץ ישראל". He says Zionist circles, mainly those
  close to the Revisionist movement, saw the British decision, and the Zionist acceptance of it, as a betrayal of the
  principle that eastern Transjordan is part of Eretz Israel (p. 37, paraphrase). He also uses "ארץ ישראל המזרחית" for
  how the Zionist movement saw the area east of the Jordan (p. 35). That is a view he reports, not a line name.
- [GAP] no Jordanian or Arabic-language name for the line was looked up.

## 3. The 1923 Mandate border (Paulet-Newcombe, the northern line)

**Dates.**
- 23 December 1920: the Franco-British Convention of Paris, whose Articles 1 and 2 the commission carried out (Treaty
  Series No. 13 (1923), p. 3, title of the enclosure). Its printing is given as "Misc. No. 4 (1921)"; Cmd. 1195 (cover
  of TS 13). [GAP] the 1920 text was not opened.
- Summer 1921: the line was marked on the ground with 71 boundary pillars (Biger, p. 33, paraphrase). Biger (p. 32)
  has the commission marking only Ras en Naqura to Tsemakh; the report itself runs to El Hammé (TS 13, p. 3). Our
  reading: the El Hammé stretch is the Yarmuk end beyond Tsemakh, not checked on a map; the report's extent is used.
- 3 February 1922: the commission "concluded their labours and drew up a final report at Beyrouth on the 3rd February,
  1922" (TS 13, p. 2). The report is signed by "S. F. NEWCOMBE, Lieutenant-Colonel, R.E." (p. 8) and "N. PAULET"
  (French text, p. 14).
- 7 March 1923: exchange of notes (British Embassy, Paris, March 7, 1923), with ratification so "that the present
  agreement may take effect from the 10th March next" (TS 13, p. 2).
- April 1924: the handover of the area north of the old line (the Galilee panhandle) was completed (Biger, p. 32,
  paraphrase).

**Instrument.** *Agreement between His Majesty's Government and the French Government respecting the Boundary Line
between Syria and Palestine from the Mediterranean to El Hammé*, Treaty Series No. 13 (1923), Cmd. 1910 ("with three
maps"). FCDO Treaties scan: https://treaties.fcdo.gov.uk/data/Library2/pdf/1923-TS0013.pdf (17 PDF pages; image only,
read as images). The report starts: "The frontier leaves the Mediterranean Sea at the point called Ras-el-Nakurah"
(p. 3). It runs from cairn to cairn and along roads and wadi thalwegs to El Hammé, where it "follows the upper edge of
the cliffs north of the railway to the bridge which stands 500 metres to the east of El Hammé Station" (p. 7). The
report covers the stretch from the Mediterranean to El Hammé only. It leaves Britain free "to reopen the question of
readjusting the frontier between Banias and Metallah" (p. 7).

**Map source to digitise.**
- The agreement: "Three maps, on a scale of 1 : 50,000, on which the line proposed by the commission has been traced,
  are annexed to this report" (TS 13, p. 2), and "the frontier, as delimited on the ground by the commission, is shown
  in red on the attached maps, which are signed by the members of the commission" (p. 8).
- **[C] Scale.** The maps printed with Cmd. 1910 are three sheets, "MAP SHOWING BOUNDARY BETWEEN SYRIA AND PALESTINE",
  sheets I-III (in the PDF in the order I, III, II on pp. 15-17), each marked "Scale 1 : 100,000"; sheet I also carries
  "Reproduced by the Survey of Egypt 21/677/1" (read on the images). They carry a degree graticule, boundary posts and the trig points of the plane-table survey, so they can
  be georeferenced on their own grid (our reading). The signed 1:50,000 originals were not found ([GAP]). This note
  prefers the **1:100,000 printed sheets** as the available source, noting that they are a reduction.
- Higher-resolution copies of the same three sheets, from the NLI's Eran Laor collection: "File:Map showing boundary
  between Syria and Palestine 01 (FL33133852 2604761).jpg" (9927 x 7111 px; page revision 1222113512; Commons gives
  publisher Survey of Egypt, 1923, scale 1:100,000, tag {{PD-EU-no author disclosure}}), with sheets 02 and 03 under
  the same name.
- Licence: an HMSO Command paper of 1923, so, on the reading in section 1, the Crown copyright term (CDPA s. 163(3)(b)) ended by 1973. The
  Survey of Egypt reproduction is a UK-government-era Egyptian printing; Commons tags it as an anonymous EU work. Only
  the agreed line's position, a fact of the agreement, is taken (D-B approach).
- Expected accuracy, our reading: +-0.1-0.2 km at 1:100,000; the report's cairn descriptions allow checks.
- Link to 1949: the Lebanon armistice line is this boundary (Art. V.1, in `armistice-lines-1949.md`), already in the
  sub-district layer at about +-1 km. Our reading: these sheets would sharpen it.

**Names.**
- The instrument: "Boundary Line between Syria and Palestine" (TS 13 title). The NLI catalogue, as shown on Commons, adds
  "Related subject: Paulet-Newcombe Agreement. הסכם ניוקומב פולה." (Commons page of sheet 01).
- Israeli perspective: Biger (p. 32) writes that the commission marked a line known as "קו ניוקומב-פולה". The caption of
  his figure 15 (p. 34) calls it part of the Newcombe-Paulet map ("חלק ממפת ניוקומב-פולה"). He calls the 1923 line the
  only international boundary of the country, the northern and north-eastern line (p. 34, paraphrase).
- Together, sections 1-3 make the Mandate frontier. Biger (p. 39) says the borders of Israel with Egypt and Lebanon are
  the Mandate borders (paraphrase).

## 4. The 1937 Peel proposal

**Dates.**
- 22 June 1937: the report is signed (Cmd. 5479, signature block "22nd June, 1937.", PDF p. 415 of the scan below).
- July 1937: presented to Parliament (cover: "Presented by the Secretary of State for the Colonies to Parliament by
  Command of His Majesty July, 1937"). (24 Oct) **[S]** (press; no official notice of the day found) published the evening of **7 July 1937** (London). Source: the Jewish Telegraphic Agency's Daily News
  Bulletin, vol. II no. 280, headed "Thursday, July 8, 1937" (PDF http://pdfs.jta.org/1937/1937-07-08_280.pdf, SHA-256
  prefix `f96a6c3f23e7e5c1`, opened 24 Oct), PDF page 3 (printed "-2-"), item "Hope tripartition will end irrepressible
  conflict": "LONDON, July 7. (JTA) -- The British Government tonight published its plan for settling the "irrepressible
  conflict" in Palestine by creating independent Jewish and Arab States there ..." (read on the scan; the same text is at
  https://www.jta.org/archive/hope-tripartition-will-end-irrepressible-conflict, which shows the bulletin date, 8 July, not
  the event date). It agrees with Wikipedia, "Peel Commission" (opened 24 Oct): "On 7 July 1937, the commission published
  a report" [S]. Hansard, 7 July 1937, c. 331 (https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1937/jul/07/royal-commissions-report)
  does not date the report: Attlee's "On what date will it be published?" concerns the Government's opinion on it, and the
  answer is "It will be published in the form of a White Paper". No official Command paper or Gazette notice giving the
  day was found.
- 30 November 1937: reissued by the League of Nations as C. 495. M. 336. 1937. VI. (same scan, PDF p. 3).
- 11 November 1938: the government sets partition aside. After the Partition (Woodhead) Commission, it concluded "that
  the political, administrative and financial difficulties involved in the proposal to create independent Arab and
  Jewish States inside Palestine are so great that this solution of the problem is impracticable" (Cmd. 5893, para. 4,
  UNISPAL, https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-197568/, publication date 11/11/1938). Our reading: the
  proposal's `until` is 11 November 1938.

**Instrument.** *Palestine Royal Commission Report*, Cmd. 5479 (HMSO, 1937), chapter XXII "A Plan of Partition". Scan:
https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1946/0368/194603681.23.pdf (440 PDF pages, a National Library of Scotland server; the
League reissue is bound in). Printed page = PDF page minus 18 in chapter XXII.
- The two states: "the one an Arab State, consisting of Trans-Jordan united with that part of Palestine which lies to
  the east and south of a frontier such as we suggest in Section 3 below; the other a Jewish State consisting of that
  part of Palestine which lies to the north and west of that frontier" (ch. XXII para. 6, p. 381).
- How precise: "It is not possible for us to draw a precise line: for that purpose we would recommend the appointment of

**Instrument.** The Land Transfers Regulations, 1940, made under Article 16D of the Palestine Orders in Council, 1922 to

## 6. The 1947 UN partition lines (Resolution 181 (II))

**Dates.**
- 29 November 1947: adopted, "Hundred and twenty-eighth plenary meeting, 29 November 1947." (A/RES/181(II), UN ODS,
  https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/RES/181(II)&l=en&t=pdf, PDF p. 2).
- The plan's own timetable: the states and the Jerusalem regime were to come into existence two months after the
  British evacuation, "but in any case not later than" 1 October 1948 (Part I, A.3, PDF p. 3; the date is OCR-damaged
  as "l October 1948"). The resolution takes note of the plan to evacuate "by 1 August 1948" (PDF p. 1).
- Not carried out: Biger (p. 50) says the partition resolution was not implemented. The 1948 war set new lines
  (paraphrase), which are D-A's armistice lines.

**Instrument.** General Assembly Resolution 181 (II), "Future government of Palestine", with the Plan of Partition with
Economic Union. Part II "Boundaries" describes the Arab State, the Jewish State and the City of Jerusalem in words,
mostly along village and sub-district boundaries. For example: "The area of the Arab State in Western Galilee is bounded
on the west by the Mediterranean and on the north by the frontier of the Lebanon from Ras en Naqura to a point north of
Saliha" (PDF p. 12; printed p. 142). Part III defines the City of Jerusalem (Annex B).
