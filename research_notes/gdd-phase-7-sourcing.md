## 1. Q-MIN-3: the Ministry of Immigration ends on 8 October 1951

**In content.** `content/offices.yaml` line 21: `immigration_minister`, "Minister of Immigration" (שר העלייה), from
1948-05-14, `until: 1951-10-08`. `content/people.yaml` gives Haim-Moshe Shapira every term of it; `content/rules.yaml`
(`budget_owners`, issue #108) moves the line to `prime_minister` from 1951-10-08 and says in its comment that this is an
unverified assumption.

**Holders.** One holder throughout: **Haim-Moshe Shapira**, from **14 May 1948** to **8 October 1951**. [V] Knesset
OData, ministers. The Knesset rows, by government:

| Government | Ministry ID | From | To |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provisional (0) | 775 | 14 May 1948 | 14 February 1949 |
| Provisional (0) | 775 | 14 February 1949 | 10 March 1949 |
| First (1) | 776 | 10 March 1949 | 1 November 1950 |
| Second (2) | 784 | 1 November 1950 | 20 August 1951 |
| Second (2), Second Knesset | 784 | 20 August 1951 | 8 October 1951 |

These match `people.yaml` row for row. he.wiki חיים משה שפירא gives the same span ("שר העלייה (1949-1951)", with the
Provisional Government from 1948) [V with the Knesset].

**What happened on 8 October 1951: the portfolio was not continued (neither merged by name nor given a successor
holder).**

- On Sunday 7 October 1951 the Prime Minister presented the Third Government to the Knesset with this list of members and
  portfolios. It contains no Immigration portfolio; Shapira is listed as "משה שפירא — פנים ודתות" (Interior and
  Religions). The list in full: Ben-Gurion (Prime Minister and Defence), Eshkol (Agriculture and Development), Burg
  (Health), Dinaburg (Education and Culture), Dov Yosef (Commerce and Industry, and Justice), Levin (Welfare), Meirson
  (Labour), Naftali (without portfolio), Pinkas (Transport), Kaplan (Finance), Sheetrit (Police), Shapira (Interior and
  Religions), Sharett (Foreign Affairs). [V] Knesset 2, session 12 (the list), with Knesset OData (Shapira's Third
  Government rows are Interior, ID 746, and Religions, ID 1275, both 8 October 1951 to 24 December 1952; no Immigration
  row exists for any government after the Second).
- The Knesset approved the government on Monday 8 October 1951 (session 13); the OData rows start that day. [V]
- Neither the presentation nor the two-day debate (sessions 12 and 13) mentions the end of the Ministry of Immigration:
  the words "משרד העלייה" and "שר העלייה" do not occur in either protocol (text search of both). **not found**: any
  government statement, in the Knesset record, explaining the change.
- he.wiki משרד העלייה והקליטה: the state's Ministry of Immigration ("המשרד לעלייה") worked from 1948 to 1951; it dealt
  with bringing immigrants to the country but not with their absorption, which stayed with other ministries and the
  Jewish Agency's absorption department; Shapira headed it. The next ministry in this field was the Ministry of
  Immigrant Absorption, founded in **1968**. [S] (Wikipedia; the paragraph has no footnote.)
- **[C], or a misleading summary.** en.wiki Ministry of Aliyah and Integration says the ministry "was known until 1951
  as the Ministry of Immigration" and was "later renamed" the Ministry of Immigrant Absorption. Read with he.wiki and
  the Knesset rows, there was no ministry in this field from 8 October 1951 to 1968; the 1968 ministry was a new body,
  not a rename. The Knesset rows support he.wiki.

**Where the functions went.** No source was found that records a transfer order in October 1951 (see "Not reached").
What the statutes show:

- The Law of Return, as passed on 20 Tammuz 5710 (**5 July 1950**), put the Minister of Immigration in charge of the
  law and of granting immigrant visas: section 5, "שר העליה ממונה על ביצוע חוק זה" ("the Minister of Immigration is
  charged with the implementation of this law"); section 2(b) gave the same minister the power to refuse a visa. It is
  signed by Shapira as "שר העליה". [V] Law of Return 1950 (Knesset scan of Sefer HaHukim).
- The Entry into Israel Law, 5712-1952, gives the visa, residence, frontier-control and removal powers to the
  **Minister of the Interior**. [S] Entry into Israel Law 1952 (UNODC translation; the day of passage was not checked).
- The Law of Return (Amendment), 5714-1954, passed on **23 August 1954**, replaced "שר העליה" with "שר הפנים" in
  sections 2 and 5 of the Law of Return. [V] Law of Return amendment 1954 (Knesset scan). So the statute itself still
  named the Minister of Immigration for almost three years after the office ended; how those powers were exercised in
  the gap (October 1951 to August 1954) is **not found** here.
- The Interior Minister from 8 October 1951 was the same person, Shapira. [V] Knesset OData.

**What the content should name as the successor office.** Recommendation for the owner, not a decision:

- **The statutory immigration functions** (visas, entry) passed, by the 1952 and 1954 laws, to the **Minister of the
  Interior** (`interior_minister`, already in `offices.yaml`), and the holder did not change. If Q-MIN-3 wants a sourced
  successor, `interior_minister` is the only one the records support; cite the Entry into Israel Law 1952 and the 1954
  amendment, and say that the October 1951 transfer instrument itself was not found.
- **Absorption** (housing, camps, employment) was never the ministry's job (he.wiki, above, [S]), so no office
  "succeeds" to it on 8 October 1951. If the game's absorption multiplier reads the Minister of Immigration, the honest
  options are: (a) pass it to `interior_minister` (continuity of the holder and of the immigration powers); or (b) make
  it neutral (1.0) from 8 October 1951, as the plan's draft answer to Q-MIN-3 proposes. **Owner pick.**
- `rules.yaml`'s `budget_owners` currently moves the Immigration budget line to `prime_minister` from 1951-10-08. No source
  found here supports the Prime Minister as successor: the Third Government's list gives him Defence only. The comment
  there already flags it as an assumption; this note found nothing for it and the statutes above point to Interior.

## 2. Q-NEW-1: King Abdullah's proposal of 17 February 1950

The #135 note (section 2a) already gives the terms, the 24 February answer and the deadlock from FRUS. This section adds
the Jordanian-side record of the terms, pins what is and is not known about the Israeli cabinet's decision, and gives
dates for a card window.

**What was proposed.** Two accounts, both in FRUS, both of the meeting at Shuneh on the night of Friday
**17 February 1950**:

- Israeli account (Shiloah to the US Ambassador, 19 February): a five-year renewable non-aggression pact keeping the
  armistice lines; free intercourse with a free zone at Haifa; study committees on specific issues as a possible basis
  for peace; mutual guarantees to the UN on access to and protection of the Holy Places. The King suggested some Israeli
  concession to Arab property owners, reserved his claim to an outlet to the sea, and was willing to provide access to
  Mount Scopus. Both sides had agreed earlier that night that peace was "now impossible". [S] FRUS 1950 V d364.
- Jordanian side (the British Minister, Kirkbride, to the US Legation, 20 February): nine terms. Demarcation lines to
  follow the armistice lines in general; a five-year non-aggression pact during which a joint committee would make minor
  adjustments; a free port zone for Jordan in Haifa with transit rights; the corridor claim reserved; trade opened;
  Israeli access to the Scopus institutions and the Western Wall "but no sovereignty over them"; Israel to restore
  certain Arab quarters of Jerusalem and pay cash compensation for property not restored; Arab property holders allowed
  to visit Israel to dispose of property; the Jerusalem Holy Places to be "demilitarized". The Jordanian cabinet had not
  yet been told. [S] FRUS 1950 V d365. With d364: **[V]** for the core (five-year non-aggression pact on the armistice
  lines, Haifa free zone, trade, Holy Places, Scopus access, property compensation).
- A scholarly summary, citing FRUS 1950 V and Israeli memoirs, gives the same core and says: "The Israeli cabinet
  approved these proposals as a proper base for further negotiations." [S] Nevo 1984, p. 40 (no date given for the
  cabinet decision).

**What the Israeli cabinet decided, and when.**

- Shiloah consulted the Prime Minister and the Foreign Minister after the meeting; his impression on 19 February was
  that the cabinet would accept the proposals "as basis talks". [S] FRUS 1950 V d364. (The printed telegram reads
  "February 1"; the meeting was on 17 February, so the day is garbled. Not resolved.)
- A follow-up meeting set for the evening of 20 February did not take place (Amman, telegram of 23 February, in an
  editorial footnote). [S] FRUS 1950 V d365.
- At the meeting of the night of Friday **24 February 1950**, Shiloah "announced Israel cabinet agreement in principle
  King's five year nonaggression proposal and delegation's authority to discuss details"; both sides initialled an
  agreed set of principles that night. [S] FRUS 1950 V d370; [V] with Nevo 1984, p. 40 ("a draft was initialed").
- **The day of the cabinet meeting itself is not found.** It lies between 19 and 24 February 1950. The Israeli record
  (*Documents on the Foreign Policy of Israel*, vol. 5, 1950, ed. Freundlich, 1988; the government's protocols at the
  Israel State Archives) was **not reached** (see below). The decision on record is "agreement in principle" to the
  five-year non-aggression proposal and authority to negotiate details.
- What followed, for the card's follow-up: deadlock over the drafts on 28 February (#135 note, FRUS d381 and d382);
  Jordan suspended the talks in March, and on **31 March 1950** its cabinet ratified the Arab League resolution to expel
  any member that made a separate peace with Israel. [S] Nevo 1984, pp. 45-46.

**Dates for a card window** (recommendation; the owner decides):

| Anchor | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Offer made | Friday 17 February 1950 (the 15 February 1950 turn) | FRUS d364, d365 |
| Israel's answer: agreement in principle, delegation authorised | announced Friday 24 February 1950 (same turn) | FRUS d370 |
| Drafts deadlock | 28 February 1950 | FRUS d381, d382 (#135 note) |
| Jordan's cabinet ratifies the League resolution | 31 March 1950 | Nevo 1984 |
| Union of the two banks | 24 April 1950 | FRUS d455 (#135 note) |

A window **from 17 February 1950** on the 15 February 1950 turn fits the record; the historical option is "agree in
principle and negotiate details" (24 February). If the window must be longer than one turn, the latest date that keeps
the choice real is before 31 March 1950, when Jordan's side closed. The plan's draft answer (window from 15 February
1950) starts two days before the offer; the offer's own day is 17 February.

## 3. Q-NEW-2: Israel's official response to the union of the two banks

The #135 note (section 2c) gives the Jordanian decision of Monday **24 April 1950** and the Israeli spokesman's
statement from JTA and FRUS. It found no cabinet or Knesset action. **There was a Knesset debate and vote**, which this
note adds.

- **The government's statement.** A government spokesman said, right after the annexation was published: it is a
  unilateral step that does not bind Israel; Israel is bound to Jordan by the armistice agreement and is determined to
  keep it strictly; that agreement contains no final political settlement, and none is possible without negotiation and
  a peace between the two sides; so the question of the status of the Arab areas west of the Jordan "נשארת לדידנו
  פתוחה" ("remains, as far as we are concerned, open"). A few days later, after the British announcement, the government
  repeated that it saw the status of those areas as open; it noted that Britain did not intend to set up military bases
  there in peacetime; and it said that applying the Anglo-Transjordan treaty to those areas "מעוררת תמהון" ("causes
  astonishment") and that Israel's reservation covered that too. [V] Knesset 1, session 135 (the Foreign Minister read
  both statements into the record), with JTA 25 Apr 1950 and FRUS 1950 V d456 (#135 note) for the first statement.
- **Date of the first statement.** The Foreign Minister told the Knesset the response was "ברורה למדי ומיידית, לאחר
  שנתפרסם דבר הסיפוח" (clear and immediate, after the annexation was published). With the #135 note's sources, it was
  made on 24 or 25 April 1950. **[C]** on the day (JTA's 25 April bulletin; FRUS d456 quotes Tel Aviv on 25 April citing
  "today's newspapers"). The British statement it answered was reported on 27-28 April (#135 note); the day of the
  second Israeli statement is **not found**.
- **The Knesset debate, Wednesday 3 May 1950** (16 Iyar 5710), First Knesset, session 135. Mapam, Herut, Maki and the
  General Zionists had each tabled motions on Israel's response "a few days" before; the government agreed to the debate
  (session 134, 2 May). The government did not open it with a statement: Mapam's opening speaker objected to this, and
  the Foreign Minister replied that the government's response had already been given and read the two statements. The
  Prime Minister and the Foreign Minister both spoke in the debate. [V] Knesset 1, sessions 134 and 135.
- **The motions and the vote** (all [V], Knesset 1, session 135; paraphrased):
  - Mapai (Kossoy): the Knesset takes note of the government's statements on the annexation, with the objections and
    reservations in them. **53 for. Adopted.**
  - Mapam (Rubin): the Knesset sees the annexation as harming the Jewish people's historic aim for the whole land, the
    right of the Arab population of that area to independence in economic union with Israel, and the terms of the
    armistice agreement (by extending the Anglo-Transjordan treaty west of the Jordan); Israel will not recognise it;
    the government is to complain to the Security Council; and is not to renew peace talks with Transjordan unless the
    annexation is cancelled. **16 for.**
  - Maki (Wilner): non-recognition and opposition, a complaint to the Security Council against Britain and Transjordan,
    and further points. **2 for.**
  - Herut (Meridor) declared that it would not take part in the votes and read a statement of its position; the Speaker
    recorded the abstention.

**What this means for Q-NEW-2.** The #135 note's blocker was that only one sourced Israeli response existed, so a card
would have one option. The Knesset record now gives a dated choice among sourced alternatives: (a) take note, keep the
question open, keep the armistice and the channel to the King (the government's line, adopted 53 to 16); (b) refuse
recognition, complain to the Security Council and freeze the talks until the annexation is cancelled (Mapam's motion,
defeated). Anchor: **3 May 1950** (the 15 April 1950 turn), or 24 April 1950 if the card is the government's immediate
statement. Whether that makes a card, a follow-up to Q-NEW-1, or a news line stays the owner's call. The record gives
options; no effect values are sourced.

**Sensitivity.** None of the four tags. The status of the West Bank is politically live; the sourced wording is the
documents' own ("unilateral", "not binding", "remains open"; the Knesset's "takes note ... with the objections and
reservations"). Party motions are attributed to the parties that tabled them.

## 4. Q-HFX-4: dated arrivals of named equipment, 1948-1949

The plan asks for "a sourced arrival of the named equipment on a dated day". Four candidates, in date order. Note:
`content/map/units_1948.yaml` already uses 20 May 1948 (Avia S-199) and 27 September 1948 (Spitfire) for 101 Squadron's
aircraft, sourced to Wikipedia; this section gives the better sources found for those days.

### 4a. The 65 mm mountain guns ("Napoleonchiks"): ship *Borea*, 13 to 15 May 1948

- **What and how many:** five French 65 mm mountain guns (model 1906), without sights, with about 50,000 shells; the ship
  *Borea* (renamed from *Santa Chiaro*) loaded them at Marseille. Only 5 of the 50 guns bought were loaded. [V] Ben-Tzur,
  Rex and Borea (quoting Arazi's report of 2 May 1948: "5 תותחים 65 מ"מ; 50 אלף פגזים") and he.wiki נפוליאונצ'יק
  ("5 נפוליאונצ'יקים ו-50 אלף פגזים", footnote to a Walla article, 20 October 2018).
- **When:** *Borea* reached the Tel Aviv anchorage on **13 May 1948**, was seized there by the Royal Navy, towed to Haifa
  and searched; its cover cargo held, and it was released with its cargo at **midnight 14/15 May 1948**, at the end of
  the Mandate. [S] Ben-Tzur, Rex and Borea (the date of arrival is given as 13 May in two places).
- **First use:** on 19 May the Chief of Operations and the Prime Minister argued over where to send four of the guns
  (Jerusalem or the Jordan Valley); they went to the Jordan Valley. On **20 May 1948** four guns reached the front at
  midday and opened fire at 13:20 on the Syrian force attacking the Deganias; it was the first Israeli artillery used in
  the war. [V] Ben-Tzur (quoting Bar-Zohar and Ben-Gurion's diary of 21 May: "4 התותחים ... העלו את המורל של
  יישובינו", "the 4 guns ... raised the morale of our settlements"); en.wiki Battles of the Kinarot Valley (footnotes
  to Morris, *1948* (2008), pp. 254-256; Wallach; Teveth; not opened).
- **[C] Number at Degania:** four (Ben-Gurion's diary, en.wiki) or two (Ben-Gurion's other note of the 19 May meeting:
  send 2 to Jerusalem). Ben-Tzur leaves it open.
- **[C] Ship and day:** a *Haaretz* article (2011), seen only as a search summary and not opened, says the first five
  Napoleonchiks reached Tel Aviv port on a Polish cargo ship on 6-7 May 1948. Not reconciled.
- **Fit:** the guns were in Israeli hands on the MVP's first day (15 May 1948), so an arrival card would sit before or on
  the first turn. Their first use (20 May) is inside the 15 May 1948 turn.

### 4b. The Avia S-199 fighters: Ekron, 20 May 1948; first mission 29 May 1948

- **What:** Avia S-199 fighters (the Czechoslovak Bf 109 variant), bought from Czechoslovakia; 25 in all. [S] he.wiki
  אוויה S-199 ("25 מטוסים"); 101 Squadron, S-199s (an initial order of ten, with options; the count of 25 is the
  Avia offer of December 1947).
- **When, day by day** (all from 101 Squadron, S-199s, [S], which follows Cull et al., *Spitfires over Israel* (1994),
  and Huertas (1998); not opened):
  - **20 May 1948:** the first disassembled S-199 lands at Ekron in a C-54, with five pilots trained in Czechoslovakia
    and Czech mechanics. [V] with he.wiki מבצע בלק ("בלק-5", footnote to Cohen and Lavi, *השמים אינם הגבול* (1990),
    pp. 50-52).
  - 21 May: a second, by C-46. 22 May: a third, by C-54. 23-24 May: a C-46 carrying a fourth crashes near Ekron in fog;
    the navigator is killed. [V] for 21 May and the crash with he.wiki מבצע בלק.
  - 28-29 May: the Air Force commander reports to the Prime Minister that the aircraft, bombs and guns are not yet ready.
  - **29 May 1948:** four S-199s, assembled at Ekron, fly their first mission, against the Egyptian column near Isdud
    (Ashdod); one of the four crashed on the way back. [V] with he.wiki אוויה S-199 ("ב-29 במאי 1948, כאשר ארבעה מטוסי אוויה S-199 תקפו
    את הטור המצרי באזור אסדוד").
- **[C] When four were ready:** 101 Squadron says "by the end of the day [29 May], four S-199s ... were ready"; a search
  summary of the same page read "by the end of May 21", which the page text does not say. The page text is used here.
- **Fit:** the clearest "assembled and in service" date for a grant is **29 May 1948** (four aircraft, 101 Squadron),
  inside the 15 May 1948 turn. `units_1948.yaml` already shows 101 Squadron from 20 May.

### 4c. The Hotchkiss H39 tanks: June 1948

- **What and how many:** ten French Hotchkiss H39 light tanks, bought in France and shipped from Marseille to Haifa.
  They formed a company of the 82nd Battalion, 8th Brigade, and fought in Operation Danny (Lydda airport, July 1948).
  [V] en.wiki Hotchkiss H35 ("Ten H39s were clandestinely sold to Israel ... shipped from Marseille to Haifa in 1948");
  Tank Encyclopedia, H39 (citing Eshel 1989, Ilan 1996).
- **[C] When:** "early June" 1948 (Tank Encyclopedia, H39) or **15 June 1948** (Lehi Museum blog, summarising Uria
  Aharon in *Shiryon*, April 2018). No primary source (IDF Archives, a ship's name) was reached. The ship is
  **not found**.
- **Condition:** the tanks arrived in poor technical condition; Tank Encyclopedia says only five of the ten could be made
  to run by the start of Operation Danny. [S]
- **Note for the wording:** 15 June 1948 is inside the first truce (11 June to 8 July 1948), when arms imports breached
  the truce terms (the #135 note, section 5, has the US record of that). A card on this arrival would sit with the
  existing `first_truce` event.
- **Fit:** weak as a dated grant until the day is pinned; the month (June 1948) is [V].

### 4d. The Spitfires from Czechoslovakia: Operation Velvetta, 27 September and 22-30 December 1948

- **Velvetta 1:** six Spitfires left Kunovice for Nikšić (Yugoslavia) on **24 September 1948**; one was damaged landing
  there. Five took off for Israel on **27 September 1948**; two put down on Rhodes with fuel-tank trouble; **three**
  landed at Ramat David that afternoon. [S] 101 Squadron, Spitfires. he.wiki מבצע ולווטה gives the 24 September
  departure and the landing accident [V for those]; it does not give the 27 September arrival or the count. The first
  sourced combat sortie by the Spitfires is 15 October 1948 (Operation Yoav; already in `units_1948.yaml`).
- **Velvetta 2:** twelve Spitfires flew from Czechoslovakia to Nikšić on **19 December 1948**. The first formation left
  for Israel on **22 December 1948**, the day Operation Horev began; the second on **26 December**; the last two on
  **30 December**. [V for the dates] he.wiki מבצע ולווטה and 101 Squadron, Spitfires.
- **[C] How many landed on 22 and 26 December:** he.wiki: of five on 22 December one turned back and the rest landed; all
  six on 26 December landed. 101 Squadron: on 22 December one landed at Hatzor after losing the others and one nearly
  collided at Ekron; on 26 December the leader turned back just after take-off. The counts differ by one or two.
- **Totals:** 18 Spitfires flew in during the war (6 in September and 12 in December, counted at departure); the rest
  came by sea after the war. [S] en.wiki Arms shipments from Czechoslovakia to Israel, as summarised in a search result
  (not opened). The IAF's own Spitfire page (iaf.org.il, "47 more by sea and air in the Velvetta operations") returned
  HTTP 403 and was **not reached**; its figure is from a search summary only.
- **Fit:** the best-sourced dated arrival in the set: **27 September 1948**, three Spitfires at Ramat David (the
  15 September 1948 turn). A December grant (22 December 1948, the 15 December turn) would coincide with Operation Horev.
