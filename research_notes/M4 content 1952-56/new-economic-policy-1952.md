# M4 content research: the New Economic Policy (13 to 20 February 1952)

Researched 2026-10-11. All URLs were checked on that date. Draft for issue #5: one note per event.

**Purpose.** The M4 plan lists the New Economic Policy (NEP) as an anchor event that feeds the economy calibration (#24 and the dated rules for 1952). This note gives the exact dates, what the government decided and who presented it, the options that were actually on the table, and the numbers a calibration would need.

**Conventions** (the same as `facts-1948.md`)
- **[V]** verified on the cited page, which was opened.
- **[S]** single source, or a search-result summary that was not opened directly.
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- No quotes are invented. Speeches are paraphrased; the few short phrases in quotation marks are translated from the cited text.
- **JTA dates.** A JTA bulletin is dated the day after the dispatches it carries. Each item has its own dateline, such as "JERUSALEM, Feb. 20", and "today" and "last night" refer to that dateline, not to the bulletin date. I checked this on the scanned originals of the bulletins of 21 Feb 1952, 10 Jun 1952, 12 Jun 1952 and 26 Mar 1953 (downloaded from pdfs.jta.org and read page by page). The web text of JTA's archive pages drops the datelines, so use the scans for dates.

**Source caveat.** The primary text opened is Ben-Gurion's Knesset speech of 13 Feb 1952 as printed in *Hazon VaDerekh* vol. 3 (Mapai, 1951/2) and reproduced by the Ben-Yehuda Project. It is the speech as published, not the Divrei HaKnesset record. The vote comes from a JTA bulletin. The Knesset record of the debate was not opened.

## 2. What was decided

The government presented a package in a Knesset speech by the Prime Minister. In the order of the published speech [V]:

1. **Budget.** Balance the budget, defence included, "whatever happens" (in the speech's words, paraphrased here), and aim for a surplus for development. No more Treasury bills or land bills. The development budget would rest on budget surplus, internal loans, and foreign grants and long-term loans. At least 1,000 civil servants would go, excluding teachers, doctors, nurses, police and inspectors. Collection of income tax would be tightened; the government would ask the Knesset for a law stripping tax evaders of the right to practise their profession if needed.
2. **Credit and enforcement.** Credit directed first to agriculture and industry; a strict campaign against the black market and currency smuggling.
3. **Three exchange rates for the pound** [V, and in JTA]:
   - $2.80 per pound (the speech also gives 375 prutot per dollar) for bread and other essential goods, the former official rate;
   - $1.40 per pound for donor funds, tourists, diplomatic missions and certain goods (the speech lists meat, fish, coffee, tea, medicine and fertiliser; JTA adds seeds);
   - $1.00 per pound for capital investors, who could now bring in their whole capital as machinery and raw materials with no foreign-currency allocation. Stock already in hand would not be repriced unless it was repurchased at the new rate.
4. **Exports.** An institute to promote exports, and an export bank taking foreign-currency deposits and lending foreign currency to exporters; raw materials for export without allocation.
5. **Pricing and wages.** End cost-plus pricing; prices and profits to follow efficiency; raw materials allocated by export and efficiency, not past entitlement. Wages tied to the cost-of-living index, with further rises linked to output. Joint production committees. A 47-hour week in government offices, local authorities and public bodies. Contractor or piece-rate work on public works.
6. **An Economic Council** of all economic groups, advisory only.

The Hebrew Wikipedia article (https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/המדיניות_הכלכלית_החדשה_(ישראל_1952), opened, no inline citations; its only reference is an external link to a 2010 *Quarterly for Economics* article by Yossi Goldstein) lists three items beyond the exchange rates. Each is now checked:

1. **Halt to issuing land bills and Treasury bills.** Confirmed [V]. Ben-Gurion's speech says the government would issue no more of either (section 2, point 1).
2. **A one-off compulsory loan on liquid money.** Real, but not part of the 13 Feb announcement. The article itself says it was not in the original announcement. It was a separate surprise order of June 1952, debated on 10 and 11 Jun and carried on 11 Jun (56 to 37, 4 abstaining; see the timeline). Giladi's 1998 survey of the Israeli economy counts it inside the NEP package (https://historynet.cet.ac.il/pages/item.asp?item=4124, opened), so sources differ on whether to call it part of the NEP, but not on the facts. Model it as a later event, not as a February option [V].
3. **A limit on immigration.** Not supported as an NEP measure [C]. The speech does not announce one and says immigration had fallen for "objective reasons". Giladi describes the steep fall in aliyah (about 50,000 immigrants in 1952 to 1954 in total) as a relief for the economy, not as a policy. What did exist was the Jewish Agency's selection policy (Seleqṣeya) for poor Moroccan and, less so, Tunisian Jews, adopted in mid 1951, before the NEP. It was rescinded in 1953 according to the Encyclopedia of Jews in the Islamic World as summarised by https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleqseya (opened), while the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration says Moroccan aliyah in 1954 and 1955 was spurred by the lifting of criteria (https://www.gov.il/en/pages/the-aliya-story?chapterIndex=9, opened), so the end date is [C]. The Hebrew Wikipedia version (criteria began in 1952 and were soon dropped because they nearly stopped aliyah) does not match either source. Do not use it. For #102 (immigration 1952 to 1956) the selection policy needs its own note.

**Who.** The Prime Minister presented it (Ben-Gurion's text and JTA). Finance Minister Kaplan then explained it to the press (JTA 15 Feb, snippet). The Hebrew Wikipedia article says Kaplan presented the parameters from the Knesset floor in February [S, conflicting in emphasis only].

## 3. Options and positions on the record

- **Rejected by the government in the speech** [V]: living on internal resources alone by requisition and confiscation, because tractors, machinery, wheat, fuel and raw materials had to be bought abroad; and relying only on private foreign capital, because the speech says no country was found where foreign capital flowed in at such volumes.
- **Chosen:** mobilising the Jewish people abroad and "international friends", plus the domestic package above [V].
- **Opposition** [S]: a Springer chapter (snippet only) says the opposition parties attacked the government's controls and advocated abolishing exchange and price controls and a floating rate. A Maki pamphlet (https://www.marxists.org/archive/vilenska/1952/new-economic-plan.pdf, snippet) opposes the policy from the left. The JTA report of 20 Feb gives only the vote counts and Ben-Gurion's reply.
- **Ben-Gurion's reply on 20 Feb** [V, JTA, paraphrased]: he denied that Israel was nearer to economic independence three years earlier, said the country was richer than at any time since statehood, and said capital does not follow penniless immigration.

Which parties moved or voted for the no-confidence motion is [GAP]; the JTA report does not say.

## 4. Numbers for calibration (#24, N11)

Figures quoted by Eshkol, then Finance Minister, in February 1962 in an essay comparing 1952 with the 1962 devaluation (https://benyehuda.org/read/41385, opened). They are his statements, not a statistical table:
- trade deficit: $333 million in 1951; $277 million in 1952; $223 million in 1953; $198 million in 1954;
- registered unemployment: 7.2 percent of the labour force (the year is implied to be 1952), 11.3 percent in 1953;
- consumer prices rose by more than 60 percent in 1952, and the cost-of-living supplement was raised, sometimes monthly;
- the 1952 policy kept several exchange rates; Eshkol's essay is about the later move to a single rate of 3 pounds per dollar in 1962, so do not use it for 1952 rates.

Hebrew Wikipedia gives the same 60 percent for prices in 1952, a rise of about 30 percent in exports and a fall of about 30 percent in the deficit [S]. The Central Bureau of Statistics series and the Bank of Israel are the sources #24 should use for the indicators; neither was opened here.

## 5. Proposed event shape (for #28, not drafted)

- `from: 1952-02-13`, `to: 1952-02-21` (`to` exclusive; the vote is 20 Feb). It lies inside the last turn of the `Hash1952` run (27 Nov 1951 to 27 Feb 1952), so it joins the 1952 golden batch.
- Anchor, office `prime_minister` (or `finance_minister`; the decision was the government's). No sensitivity tag.
- Options from the record: the package as presented (historical); keep the multiple-rate system with controls; abandon controls for a free rate (the opposition position, [S]). Do not invent a fourth.
- The effects of each belong to #24's calibration, not this note.
- The June 1952 compulsory loan is a separate candidate: window 8 to 12 Jun 1952 (`from: 1952-06-08`, `to: 1952-06-12`, exclusive), hard anchor with a Knesset confidence vote on 11 Jun. It falls after 27 Feb 1952, so it moves only `Hash1957`. The property-loan law followed on the night of 24 Mar 1953. Kaplan was Finance Minister until 25 June 1952 and Eshkol from that day (`content/people.yaml`, sourced to the Knesset plenum protocol of 25 June 1952), so the 8 Jun announcement was Kaplan's and the Nov 1952 bill Eshkol's.
