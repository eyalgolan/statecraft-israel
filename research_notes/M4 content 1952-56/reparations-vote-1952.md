# M4 content research: the Knesset vote on talks with Germany (7 to 9 January 1952)

Researched 2026-10-11. All URLs were checked on that date. Draft for issue #5: one note per event.

**Purpose.** The M4 plan lists the reparations vote as an anchor event (hard, `holocaust` tag). `content/events/reparations_claim.yaml` already covers Israel's claim of March 1951. This note covers the next step: the decision to open direct talks with West Germany, and what followed, with exact dates and the options that were actually debated.

**Conventions** (the same as `facts-1948.md`)
- **[V]** verified on the cited page, which was opened. Where two independent pages agree, both are given.
- **[S]** single source, or a search-result summary that was not opened directly. Treat it as "probably".
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- **JTA dates.** A JTA bulletin is dated the day after the dispatches it carries; each item has its own dateline, which the web text of jta.org drops. Event dates here use the dateline on the scan where it was read.
- No quotes are invented. Nothing below is quoted from a speaker.

**Source caveat.** Two Hebrew pages from the Sharett Archive were opened in full: the Knesset protocol of sitting 38 (7 Jan 1952) and the Foreign Ministry press release of 10 Sep 1952. Both are reproduced in *Polmos HaShilumim* (*The Reparations Controversy*, ed. Yaakov Sharett), which prints the Divrei HaKnesset text with editorial notes. Sittings 39 and 40 (8 and 9 Jan) were not opened, so the vote and its breakdown still come from a contemporaneous JTA bulletin and need the record of sitting 40 to reach "verified" under a strict reading. The JSTOR and De Gruyter copies of the same volume were not reachable.

## 2. The decision on 9 January 1952

**What was put to the Knesset.** A Mapai-sponsored resolution that the Knesset "has taken notice of the Government announcement and empowers the Foreign Affairs Committee to take adequate steps according to the circumstances". [V] JTA, 10 Jan 1952: https://www.jta.org/archive/knesset-votes-authorization-for-israel-negotiations-with-germany (also the original bulletin: http://pdfs.jta.org/1952/1952-01-10_007.pdf). In practical terms it authorised the government to open direct negotiations on claims of $1.5 billion. Hebrew Wikipedia says the resolution empowered the Foreign Affairs and Security Committee to approve the final text of any agreement: https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/המחאה_נגד_הסכם_השילומים [S, snippet].

**Result.** [C]
- 61 for, 50 against, five abstaining and four absent (JTA bulletin of 10 Jan 1952, scan page 1 read; the detail on the four absent is from the web text of the same item). The 120 seats add up.
- 61 for, 50 against, six abstaining (Hebrew Wikipedia, search snippet). The difference is in the abstentions only.
- Resolve from the Divrei HaKnesset record of sitting 40.

**Breakdown by party** [S] (JTA only; sums check out to 61, 50 and five)
- For: Mapai (46), Poale Mizrachi (6), Mizrachi (1), Progressives (3), and five Mapai-affiliated Arab deputies.
- Against: General Zionists (22), Mapam (13), Herut (8), Communists (5), Mizrachi's Rabbi Nurock, and Progressive Yizhar Harari.
- Abstaining: Agudist Rabbi I. M. Levin, two Poale Agudist deputies and two Poale Mizrachi deputies.

**Who decided, who spoke.**
- Prime Minister Ben-Gurion opened the debate on 7 Jan, then four speakers before the break: Elimelech Rimalt (General Zionists), Yaakov Hazan (Mapam), Yitzhak Raphael (Hapoel Hamizrachi) and Begin (Herut), with Pinhas Lavon (Mapai) after the evening resumption (protocol 38, opened). Foreign Minister Sharett was the government's main speaker before the vote (JTA). Mapai's Argov and Kossoy also spoke.
- Sitting 39 (8 Jan, protocol opened): Pinhas Rosen (Progressives, for himself and two colleagues) accepted the government's announcement; Zalman Shazar (Mapai); Shmuel Mikunis (Maki), who said his party would demand a roll-call vote; Mordechai Nurock (Mizrachi, speaking personally, with his party and coalition partners leaving him free to do so); after the break Yosef Sapir (General Zionists), Zalman Aran (Mapai), Yisrael Bar-Yehuda (Mapam), Transport Minister Pinkas, Michael Hazani (Hapoel Hamizrachi), Yizhar Harari (Progressives) and Labour Minister Golda Meir. Sharett and others intervened from the floor. Hazani and others note the debate fell on 10 Tevet, a day of remembrance for the Holocaust set by the Chief Rabbinate [V].
- Opponents named in JTA: Adolph Berman (Mapam, a former partisan in the Warsaw ghetto), Israel Rokach and Dr. M. Boger (General Zionists), Esther Wilenska (Communist), Rabbi Nurock (Mizrachi, a member of the coalition who opposed his own government).
- Begin addressed the Knesset in the same sitting. The record notes the chair suspended the sitting until Begin withdrew an insult to the Prime Minister, and that on 22 Jan 1952 the Knesset voted 56 to 47 to suspend Begin from its sittings until the Passover recess (editorial note 17 in the volume). The Knesset's own seven-hour debate on the events of 7 Jan is at Divrei HaKnesset vol. 10, pp. 1030-1055 [V, opened].

## 3. The options actually debated

1. **Open direct negotiations with West Germany.** The government's position, adopted. Arguments recorded by JTA: Sharett said private individuals, kibbutzim of both Mapai and Mapam, and whole factories already used German goods, and asked why the state should not; he asked where the line lay between claiming the property of the living and claiming heirless property. Ben-Gurion's opening speech in sitting 38 (protocol, opened) rested the claim on two premises, paraphrased here: the duty to recover as much heirless Jewish property as possible so that the murderers would not also be the heirs, and the cost to Israel of absorbing about half a million survivors. He gave $1.5 billion as the minimum for that absorption against an estimated $6 billion of Jewish property plundered. He said Bonn's chancellor had committed in writing, a few weeks earlier, to discuss the claim with Israel and Jewish representatives on the basis of the 12 March 1951 note [V].
2. **Reject any negotiation.** An opposition motion "flatly rejecting" talks with the Germans was defeated (JTA). In sitting 38, Rimalt (General Zionists) asked the Knesset to take the question off the agenda; Hazan (Mapam) said his party would vote against and would not treat a result as binding, and argued that talking to Bonn meant recognising it; Begin said there would be no negotiations [V, protocol 38].
3. **Hold a referendum.** Begin, in sitting 38, asked Ben-Gurion to put the question to the people before deciding, saying he did not mean a vote on the principle but a way out; he noted that parts of the coalition parties opposed the talks. Sharett later opposed holding one but said he was certain a majority would back the government if one were held [V, protocol 38 and JTA].
4. **Moral objection from within the coalition.** Rabbi Nurock accepted the claim for stolen property but opposed negotiating with Bonn; JTA reports Sharett answering him directly. Yitzhak Raphael (Hapoel Hamizrachi, a coalition party) backed the talks reluctantly, arguing that the cost of not absorbing immigrants outweighed the objection [V, protocol 38].

No fourth policy, such as refusing money but accepting restitution of property in kind, was found in the sources opened.

## 4. Context for the game, from the sources opened

- Ben-Gurion's economic argument is in the Wikipedia article (citing Haaretz, Sarid): Israel faced a deep economic crisis, high unemployment in the ma'abarot, and scarce foreign currency [S]. The game's 1951 currency crisis (`forex_crisis_1951`, `austerity_1949`) is the setting.
- Opposition came from the right (Herut, General Zionists) and the left (Mapam) [S, Wikipedia]. The vote itself in JTA's breakdown shows the same split, plus the Communists and some religious parties.
- The riots: the record's editorial notes say Begin addressed a rally in Zion Square (he put it at 15,000), that Knesset member Yohanan Bader burst into the chamber to say the police were using gas against demonstrators, and that stones thrown from outside broke the windows of the chamber. Begin said in the debate that he had told the crowd to surround the Knesset but not to disrupt it; Lavon called the day a calculated attempt to use physical terror against the elected house [V, protocol 38; editorial notes 5, 12 and 14]. The injury figures remain [S].
- A second wave of violence followed the agreement: Dov Shilansky was arrested in October 1952 with dynamite, and parcel bombs were sent to Adenauer and others, one of which killed a policeman [S, Wikipedia, citing Pedahzur and Perliger; Haaretz]. These bear on the `terror` tag and on how the event may be written. They are not part of this event's window.
