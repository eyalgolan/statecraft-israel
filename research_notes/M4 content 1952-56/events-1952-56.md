# M4 events research: candidate events for 1952 to 1956

Researched 14 October 2026. Draft for issue #5. Research only: nothing here changes `content/`, and no event is
drafted. Every URL was opened on 14 October 2026 unless it is marked as a snippet, a title only or not opened.

**Purpose.** A sourced list of candidate events for the M4 slice (1952 to 1956), for a game led by the Prime Minister.
For each event: the dates, a short factual summary, the sources, the sensitivity tag the event would need under
`docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md`, and a candidate decision with the historical choice marked. It feeds #28 (the 1952-56 events),
N6 (the decision scenes) and the D8 owner list in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-10-m4-plan.md`. The GDD v0.1 plan
(§7.2) pauses the 1952-56 content these notes feed; nothing here asks to unpause it.

**What is already covered elsewhere, and not repeated.** In `research_notes/M4 content 1952-56/`:
`reparations-vote-1952.md` (the January 1952 vote and the 10 September 1952 signing), `new-economic-policy-1952.md`
(February 1952) and `election-1955.md` (the June 1955 crisis, the 26 July 1955 election and the November 1955
government). `content/events/` has no event with a window in 1952 to 1956 (checked 14 October 2026); the latest is
`reparations_claim` (March 1951).

**Conventions** (the same as `facts-1948.md` and the other M4 notes)
- **[V]** verified on the cited page, which was opened.
- **[S]** single source, a snippet, a page not opened in full, or a day worked out from a JTA bulletin date.
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found. Anything unmarked and unsourced should be treated as unverified.
- **JTA dates.** The date on a JTA archive page is the bulletin date. "Today" in an item means its dispatch day,
  usually the day before. Where the scan's dateline was not read, a day taken from JTA alone is [S] or given as a range.
- **No quotes are invented.** Speeches are described, not quoted. The few short phrases in quotation marks are copied
  from the cited UN, FRUS or JTA text, and none is attributed to a speaker as words for game text.
- **Wikipedia** (English and Hebrew, read through the MediaWiki API) is used only as a pointer. A fact resting on it
  alone is [S].
- **Candidate decisions** are design proposals, not facts. Only the option marked "historical" is a statement of fact,
  and it carries a source. Options with no sourced backer are marked as roads not taken.
- **Sensitivity** uses the four tags in `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` §2 and `docs/WRITING_EVENTS.md`: `holocaust`,
  `terror`, `massacre`, `war-casualties`. An event with one of them cannot give a positive `treasury` or `fx_reserves`
  delta and needs the maintainer's sensitivity review. The tags below are proposals; the owner rules on them (AGENTS.md,
  "Leave to the maintainer").

**How the sources were reached**
- **FRUS** 1952-54 vol. IX part 1 and 1955-57 vols. XIV, XV and XVI, on history.state.gov (some read from the Office of
  the Historian's TEI files on GitHub, which are the source of that site). Citations give the volume and document number.
- **UN records** on UNISPAL (un.org/unispal, or Internet Archive copies of the old unispal.un.org pages): Security
  Council verbatim records and UNTSO reports. undocs.org and docs.un.org did not render, so resolution texts were read in
  transcriptions (Jewish Virtual Library, Avalon Project, unscr.com), marked as such.
- **Knesset OData** (`knesset.gov.il/Odata/ParliamentInfo.svc`): `KNS_IsraelLaw` (Official Gazette and start dates),
  `KNS_PersonToPosition` (minister and Prime Minister terms), and the plenum tables that lead to the Divrei HaKnesset
  files on fs.knesset.gov.il. Final votes for the education, Yad Vashem, National Insurance and Bank of Israel laws were
  read in those files.
- **JTA** archive (jta.org, found through its search API; a few scans on pdfs.jta.org).
- **CBS**, *Immigration to Israel 2002-2006* (Publication 1365), Tables 1 and 4.
- **Israel MFA** list of attacks before 1967, in a 2006 Internet Archive copy.
- **Not reached:** gov.il, archives.gov.il (Israel State Archives) and yadvashem.org returned 403; btl.gov.il 404;
  boi.org.il loaded with no text; the knesset.gov.il HTML pages sit behind a bot wall. No Cabinet minutes and no
  scholarship (Morris, Bar-On, Shlaim, Hacohen) were opened. The Israeli decision records for every raid in section B
  are therefore a [GAP].

Researched 14 October 2026; every URL below was opened on that date unless it is marked "snippet" or "not opened".

How the sources were read:
- FRUS was read from the Office of the Historian's TEI files (github.com/HistoryAtState/frus, the source of
  history.state.gov). Each citation gives the volume and document number, so the page is
  `https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/<volume>/<doc>`.
- JTA items were read on jta.org. A JTA bulletin is dated the day after the dispatch it carries; "today" in an item means

### A1. The Reparations Agreement signed in Luxembourg (10 September 1952)

- **Date(s).** Signed 10 September 1952 [V]; in force 27 March 1953 [V]. Both are already verified in

- **Summary.** Foreign Minister Sharett, Nahum Goldmann for the Claims Conference and Chancellor Adenauer signed the
  agreement and two protocols in Luxembourg. West Germany was to pay 3,000 million DM to Israel and 450 million DM to

- **Sources.** See the existing note, section 1 (timeline rows for 1–2 September 1952, 10 September 1952 and

- **Sensitivity.** `holocaust`.
- **Candidate decision.** Covered by the reparations-vote note; the signing itself is a follow-on (who signs, and in what
  form). The existing note records that the government decided the Foreign Minister would sign alongside Adenauer
  (historical), over the legal adviser's request that Sharett not sign in person.
- **Gaps.** UN Treaty Series No. 2137 not opened (see the existing note).

### A2. Weizmann's death, the Einstein offer and Ben-Zvi's election (9 November to 8 December 1952)

- **Date(s).**
  - Weizmann died 9 November 1952 [S: Wikipedia; JTA bulletin of 11 November 1952 reports the US press tributes on his
    death but its text does not give the day].
  - 16 November 1952: Ambassador Eban, "on instructions from the Prime Minister", asked Einstein whether he would accept
    the presidency if elected by the Knesset; Einstein said no. 17 November: Eban wrote asking him to reconsider.
    18 November: Einstein's written refusal was received by the embassy's minister, David Goitein [V, dates as given in
    the Israeli Embassy statement printed by JTA, bulletin of 20 November 1952].
  - Ben-Zvi elected by the Knesset on 8 December 1952 [S: JTA bulletin of 9 December 1952, "today"; dateline not read].
- **Summary.** After Weizmann's death the presidency fell vacant and the Knesset had to elect a successor. The Prime
  Minister had the Israeli ambassador in Washington ask Albert Einstein whether he would serve; Einstein declined in
  writing. The Knesset elected Yitzhak Ben-Zvi (Mapai) on the third secret ballot over Rabbi Mordechai Nurock, backed
  by the religious parties; Mapam switched to Ben-Zvi on that ballot.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Embassy Reveals Details of Offer of Israel Presidency to Einstein", bulletin of 20 November 1952 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/embassy-reveals-details-of-offer-of-israel-presidency-to-einstein . Supports the three
    dates and that Eban acted on the Prime Minister's instructions.
  - JTA, "Ben Gurion Denies He Asked Einstein to Become Israel President", bulletin of 18 November 1952 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/ben-gurion-denies-he-asked-einstein-to-become-israel-president . The Prime Minister's
    Office denied knowing of any cable to Einstein; the idea had first appeared in Maariv.
  - JTA, "Itzhak Ben Zvi Elected President of Israel; Inauguration Tomorrow", bulletin of 9 December 1952 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/itzhak-ben-zvi-elected-president-of-israel-inauguration-tomorrow . Ballot figures.
  - JTA, "American Press Lauds Dr. Weizmann…", bulletin of 11 November 1952 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/american-press-lauds-dr-weizmann-as-outstanding-world-figure
  - Pointer: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Weizmann (9 November 1952).
- **Sensitivity.** None.
- **Candidate decision.** Whom the government puts forward for the presidency: (a) approach a world figure from the
  Diaspora (Einstein) (historical: the approach was made, and refused); (b) back a senior Mapai figure in the Knesset vote
  (historical outcome: Ben-Zvi); (c) agree a cross-party candidate with the coalition's religious partners. The event can
  fire on Weizmann's death and resolve with the Knesset ballot.
- **Gaps and conflicts.**
  - [C] The Prime Minister's Office denied a cable (bulletin of 18 November) two days after Eban's approach was made on
    the Prime Minister's instructions (Embassy statement, bulletin of 20 November). Both can be true (no cable; an
    instruction to the ambassador). Do not write that the denial was false.
  - [C] JTA's third-ballot figures (Ben-Zvi 62, Nurock 40, Gruenbaum 5, blank 5) add up to 112, but JTA also says 110
    members took part. Resolve from Divrei HaKnesset for 8 December 1952 (not opened). Use only "elected on the third
    ballot" until then.
  - Weizmann's date of death needs an official source (Knesset or the President's Residence); not opened.

### A3. The bombing of the Soviet legation and the Soviet break (9 February to 21 July 1953)

- **Date(s).**
  - Bomb exploded in the legation garden in Tel Aviv on the night of 9 February 1953 [S: JTA bulletin of 11 February,
    "last night's bombing" in a dispatch of the 10th; the dateline was not read].
  - The USSR broke off relations in the early hours of 12 February 1953 [S: JTA bulletin of 13 February 1953, "early this
    morning"; [V] FRUS 1952–54 IX, doc 640, refers to "the break in relations last February"].
  - Relations resumed: announced 20 July 1953 in both capitals [S: JTA bulletin of 21 July 1953, "this evening"];
    [V] FRUS 1952–54 IX, doc 640 (Tel Aviv, 21 July 1953) discusses the resumption. Sharett's letter to Molotov
    opening the exchange was dated 6 July 1953 [S: JTA, quoting the published communiqué].
- **Summary.** A bomb exploded in the courtyard of the Soviet legation in Tel Aviv; five people were injured, among them
  four Soviet staff and a local child. The Prime Minister came back from leave, called the attack an outrage in the
  Knesset, and the Foreign Ministry offered compensation in a note the next morning; police arrested about 30 people.
  Moscow rejected the apology and broke off relations. Israeli police later linked members of an underground religious
  nationalist group to the bombing (the same group was linked to an attack on the Czechoslovak legation). Relations were
  restored in July 1953 after preliminary talks in April and May in an East European capital, through an exchange of
  letters between Sharett and Molotov. What Israel undertook in those letters was not read in full [GAP].
- **Sources.**
  - JTA, "Ben Gurion Calls Bombing of Soviet Legation 'Outrage'; 30 Arrested", bulletin of 11 February 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/ben-gurion-calls-bombing-of-soviet-legation-outrage-30-arrested
  - JTA, "Moscow Radio Tells Details of Soviet Break with Israel", bulletin of 13 February 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/moscow-radio-tells-details-of-soviet-break-with-israel
  - JTA, "Police Link Arrested Israeli with Bombing of Soviet Legation", 12 June 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/police-link-arrested-israeli-with-bombing-of-soviet-legation
  - JTA, "Moscow and Israel Announce Resumption of Diplomatic Relations", bulletin of 21 July 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/moscow-and-israel-announce-resumption-of-diplomatic-relations
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1, doc 640, chargé in Israel to the Department, 21 July 1953 (opened):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v09p1/d640 . The break left Israel with "a sharp sense of
    loss of maneuverability"; the embassy saw immigration from Eastern Europe as Israel's main interest in renewal.
  - Pointer: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Soviet_Union_relations
- **Sensitivity.** `terror` (a bombing by Jewish underground members on a diplomatic mission). The Slánský trial
  (November 1952) and the Doctors' Plot (January 1953) are the context and were not researched here; if the event text
  mentions them as antisemitic campaigns, it needs its own sources.
- **Candidate decision.** After the bombing: (a) apologise, offer compensation and pursue the bombers (historical);
  (b) express regret without compensation; (c) after the break, seek renewal through quiet talks, to keep a channel to
  the Jews of the Eastern bloc (historical, Sharett's letter of 6 July 1953), or (d) stay aligned with the West and leave
  relations broken. Options touching the bombers must not reward the attack.
- **Gaps.** JTA scans and datelines not read; the Knesset record of the Prime Minister's statement (10 February 1953)
  not opened; the text of the July 1953 letters not read in full.

### A4. The Foreign Ministry moves to Jerusalem (12 July 1953)

- **Date(s).**
  - Government announcement of the move: 4 May 1952 [V: quoted in the US aide-mémoire as "the announcement of the Israel
    Government of May 4", JTA, 24 July 1952].
  - US aide-mémoire of concern: July 1952 [S: JTA bulletins of 22 and 24 July 1952; exact delivery day not given].
  - 4 March 1953: the US ambassador asks Israel orally not to move the ministry and says the US embassy will stay in
    Tel Aviv [V: FRUS 1952–54 IX, doc 579].
  - Move: the Foreign Ministry's director general wrote to the embassies on 10 July that the move would take place on
    12 July 1953 [V: FRUS 1952–54 IX, doc 637]; JTA reports the Cabinet approving Sharett's decision the day of the move
    and staff reporting in Jerusalem the next morning [S: bulletin of 13 July 1953].
  - 28 July 1953: Dulles tells a press conference the US "regrets" the move and finds it "inopportune" [S: JTA bulletin of
    29 July 1953, "today"].
- **Summary.** The Foreign Ministry was the last major ministry still in Tel Aviv. The government announced the move in
  May 1952, held it for more than a year while the United States objected twice, and carried it out on 12 July 1953,
  keeping a liaison office in Tel Aviv for the embassies. The US embassy stayed in Tel Aviv, and Dulles publicly
  criticised the move; Arab governments protested to Washington.
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1, doc 579, ambassador in Israel, 4 March 1953 (opened):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v09p1/d579
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1, doc 637, chargé in Israel, 11 July 1953 (opened):
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v09p1/d637 . Gives the 10 July letter and the 12 July date.
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1, doc 9 (13 May 1953, Sharett's argument that the move did not bear on the Holy Places) and
    doc 647 (4 August 1953, Arab ambassadors' protest) (both found by search; doc 647 read only in part).
  - JTA, "Israel Foreign Ministry Starts Functioning Today in Jerusalem", bulletin of 13 July 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-foreign-ministry-starts-functioning-today-in-jerusalem
  - JTA, "U.S. Opposes Transfer…", 22 July 1952, and "State Dept. Releases Note…", 24 July 1952 (both opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/u-s-opposes-transfer-of-israels-foreign-ministry-to-jerusalem ;
    https://www.jta.org/archive/state-dept-releases-note-to-israel-on-foreign-ministry-transfer
  - JTA, "U.S. Considers Transfer of Israel's Foreign Ministry 'Inopportune'", 29 July 1953 (opened):
    https://www.jta.org/archive/u-s-considers-transfer-of-israels-foreign-ministry-inopportune
- **Sensitivity.** None.
- **Candidate decision.** (a) Move the ministry to Jerusalem now, against US requests (historical, 12 July 1953);
  (b) delay again until the US position softens; (c) move it but keep the main diplomatic work in Tel Aviv. Costs are
  US goodwill (`intl_standing`) against the claim to Jerusalem as capital.
- **Gaps.** The UK reaction was not found [GAP]. The exact day of the July 1952 aide-mémoire not found. Cabinet minutes
  not consulted.

### A5. The Jordan diversion canal at B'not Ya'akov (2 September to 28 October 1953)

- **Date(s).**
  - Work started in the demilitarised zone on 2 September 1953 [V: FRUS 1952–54 IX docs 743 and 770, quoting the
    UNSC drafts; UNSC resolution 100].
  - UNTSO chief of staff (General Vagn Bennike) asked Israel on 23 September 1953 to stop work in the zone until an
    agreement was reached [V: same FRUS docs].
  - The US deferred its first economic aid allocation of the fiscal year when the work began; Dulles confirmed it in
    public on 20 October 1953 [V: FRUS doc 698, press conference of 20 October 1953; doc 713].
  - Israel told the Security Council it would suspend the work while the Council examined the question; the Council
    noted this in resolution 100, adopted unanimously on 27 October 1953 [V: unscr.com record of S/RES/100(1953)].
  - The US released aid on 28 October 1953, "by the act of Israel yesterday" [V: FRUS doc 713, Dulles to Damascus,
    28 October 1953].
  - A Western draft resolution on the canal was vetoed by the USSR on 22 January 1954 (7 for, 2 against, 2 abstaining)
    [V: FRUS doc 770 source note].
- **Summary.** Israel began a canal in the central demilitarised zone with Syria to divert Jordan water for power and,
  later, for the national water system. Syria complained; the UN truce chief ordered a halt; Israel kept working. The
  United States held back its aid allocation to back the UN order. Israel undertook before the Security Council to suspend
  the work, and the US released the aid the next day. In 1954 the work had still not resumed, and the US told Israel it was
  estopped from resuming without UNTSO's consent (FRUS docs 843 and 880, July and August 1954).
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1 (opened), docs 661 (Israeli legal adviser's position, 10 September 1953), 673, 676, 694, 698,
    699, 705, 713, 743, 749, 770, 880: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v09p1/d713 (and so on).
  - UN Security Council resolution 100 (1953), record and abstract (opened): http://unscr.com/en/resolutions/100 ; UN
    symbol S/RES/100(1953). The UN's own document page was not opened.
- **Sensitivity.** None.
- **Candidate decision.** (a) Keep digging and accept the aid freeze; (b) suspend the work while the Security Council
  deliberates, to restore aid (historical, 27 October 1953); (c) move the intake out of the demilitarised zone (the
  later National Water Carrier intake at Eshed Kinrot; not researched here). Effects: `fx_reserves` and `intl_standing`
  against long-term water.
- **Gaps.** The Israeli Cabinet decision date and the Knesset debate were not found. The UN document of Israel's
  undertaking (S/PV.630s) not opened.

### A6. The Johnston mission and the Unified Plan (October 1953 to October 1955)

- **Date(s).**
  - Johnston's first mission reached the region in October 1953; he was in Amman on 22 October 1953 [V: FRUS 1952–54 IX,
    doc 704]. Eisenhower's letters introducing him were sent around 14 October [V: FRUS doc 693, footnote].
  - On 31 October 1953 Minister without Portfolio Lavon publicly rejected the TVA study on which the plan was based
    [V: FRUS doc 725, citing Tel Aviv telegram 557].
  - Johnston left the region in June 1954 after a second round [V: FRUS doc 938, 20 December 1954].
  - Further rounds in 1955; the Arab League did not approve the plan in October 1955 [S: Wikipedia; FRUS 1955–57 XIV,
    doc 341 (17 October 1955) shows the plan was before the Arab League that month]. The exact date of the Arab League
    decision is a [GAP].
  - Israel gave Johnston 1 March 1956 as the date after which it would feel free to proceed alone [S: a CIA paper of
    7 February 1956 quoted on Wikipedia; not opened].
- **Summary.** President Eisenhower sent Eric Johnston to negotiate a shared plan for the Jordan and Yarmuk waters among
  Israel, Jordan, Syria and Lebanon. The technical committees reached a plan, but the Arab League did not adopt it.
  Israel, which had suspended the B'not Ya'akov work partly in deference to the mission, later built its water carrier
  within the plan's allocations (Wikipedia pointer; not verified here).
- **Sources.**
  - FRUS 1952–54 IX part 1 (opened): docs 693, 704, 725, 829, 843, 938.
  - FRUS 1955–57 XIV (opened): docs 8, 114, 166, 177, 179, 196, 329, 333, 341:
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v14/d341
  - Pointer: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_Plan
- **Sensitivity.** None.
- **Candidate decision.** (a) Negotiate within the Johnston framework and hold the B'not Ya'akov work (historical);
  (b) reject the TVA allocations and press the Israeli plan publicly (the line Lavon took on 31 October 1953);
  (c) set a deadline after which Israel acts alone (historical, the 1 March 1956 date, [S]).
- **Gaps.** Israeli government decision dates; the allocation figures; the Arab League date.

### A7. Plan Alpha and the Anderson mission (26 August 1955 to 12 March 1956)

- **Date(s).**

  - Eisenhower signed letters introducing Robert B. Anderson to Nasser and Ben-Gurion on 9 January 1956 [V: FRUS 1955–57

### B2. Qibya (night of 14 to 15 October 1953)

- **Date(s):**
  - Night of 14 to 15 October 1953 [V], per the Security Council agenda, the Mixed Armistice Commission's resolution, and Resolution 101 as transcribed by the Jewish Virtual Library.
  - [C] The Avalon Project transcription of Resolution 101 reads "14-16 October". Treat it as a typo, since the UN agenda and the JVL text both have 14–15.
  - The attack began at about 21:30 on 14 October (Jordanian Foreign Minister, via FRUS) [S].
  - Security Council Resolution 101 was adopted on 24 November 1953 [V].
- **Summary:**
  - An Israeli regular-army force attacked the village of Qibya in Jordanian-held territory and blew up houses with the people inside. Smaller forces struck Shuqba and shelled Budrus [V, UN].
  - The Mixed Armistice Commission found that a force of about half a battalion of the Israeli regular army had blown up 41 houses and a school [V].
  - The acting chairman, Commander Hutchison, estimated 250 to 300 trained soldiers. UN observers found evidence that inhabitants had been forced to stay in their homes until the houses were blown up over them [V].
  - On the evening of 19 October Ben-Gurion said in a broadcast that army units had not carried out the raid. He placed it on border settlers provoked by years of attacks, and blamed the Arab governments [V for the content, as reported by JTA; the day is worked out from the bulletin, S].
  - The State Department publicly called the reports shocking on 18 October [V].
  - Resolution 101 expressed "the strongest censure" of the action. It called on Israel to prevent such actions, and on Jordan to keep preventing crossings of the line [V].
- **Deaths and damage [C]:**
  - 31 dead and 40 wounded: Radio Ramallah, as reported by JTA on 16 October.
  - 45 dead and 15 wounded, with 39 houses blown up: Jordan's Foreign Minister on 15 October (FRUS 689).
  - 42 dead and 15 wounded, with 41 houses and a school blown up: the Mixed Armistice Commission's resolution.
  - 27 bodies recovered by the time the acting chairman left, with villagers expecting up to 60: the UNTSO observer.
  - 53 dead and more than 40 dwellings destroyed: Bennike's report, S/PV.630, paragraph 42.
  - More than 69 dead, two-thirds of them women and children: en.wikipedia, citing scholarship [S].

### B4. Operation Susannah and the Lavon affair (July 1954 to February 1955)

- **Date(s):**
  - First action on 2 July 1954, at the Alexandria post office [S, he.wiki].
  - Incendiary devices in the US Information Agency libraries in Cairo and Alexandria on 14 July 1954 [V, Egyptian communique via JTA].
  - Arrests in July 1954: JTA's bulletin of 28 July reports the arrests announced that day.
  - Trial before Egypt's Supreme Military Tribunal from 11 December 1954 to 5 January 1955 [V, FRUS].
  - Verdict announced on 27 January 1955 [S, JTA bulletin of 28 January: "today"].
  - Executions on 31 January 1955 [S, JTA bulletin of 1 February: "early this morning". The day is standard; the bulletin was read].
  - Olshan–Dori inquiry set up on 29 December 1954, reported on 12 January 1955 [S, he.wiki].
  - Lavon tendered his resignation on 2 February 1955. It was announced on the evening of 17 February 1955 [V, FRUS 41 footnote and JTA].
  - Ben-Gurion became Minister of Defence on 21 February 1955 [C/S]. JTA's bulletin of 21 February says he "formally became" minister "today" after a Cabinet meeting; the bulletin of 23 February reports the Knesset confirming him. he.wiki wrongly says he was "re-elected Prime Minister" on 21 February. He became Prime Minister again only on 3 November 1955.
- **Summary:**
  - An Israeli military intelligence network of Egyptian Jews planted incendiary devices in Egyptian and Western public buildings. The aim was to damage British–Egyptian and American–Egyptian relations [S, scholarship not opened].
  - The members were arrested. Thirteen were charged [V, JTA]. The court sentenced:
    - Moshe Marzouk and Shmuel Azar to death;
    - two defendants to life;
    - two to 15 years and two to 7 years;
    - two were acquitted; two others had left Egypt; Max Bennett died in custody [V, JTA].
  - Marzouk and Azar were hanged despite American and British appeals for clemency [V].
  - Prime Minister Sharett did not know of the operation beforehand [S, he.wiki]. On 31 January he condemned the executions in the Knesset, which stood in silence [V, JTA]. The Knesset text was not read, so no quote is used.
  - The secret Olshan–Dori inquiry could not decide whether Lavon had given the order to the head of military intelligence, Gibli [S]. Lavon resigned. The government's announcement said his resignation followed Sharett's refusal of Lavon's proposals for changes in the defence set-up [V, JTA].

### D1. Decontrol and the end of rationing (December 1952 to February 1959)

- **Date(s)**
  - 19 or 20 November 1952: the government scraps rationing of most clothing and footwear; essential foods and a few clothing items stay rationed. [V] JTA bulletin of 21 November 1952 ("last night"; dateline not read, so the day is a range).
  - 16 to 17 December 1952: Mapai and the General Zionists sign a coalition agreement, ratified overnight by both central councils. Point 6: economic controls to be scrapped except on materials that cannot be produced in the country; point 7: steps to stabilise prices and the pound. [V] JTA bulletin of 17 December 1952.
  - 22 December 1952: Ben-Gurion presents the Fourth Government to the Knesset (Mapai, General Zionists, Progressives); its programme includes ending the cost-plus system. Peretz Bernstein (General Zionists) becomes Minister of Trade and Industry. [V] JTA bulletin of 23 December 1952 ("today"). [C] on the start day: Knesset OData gives 24 December 1952 as the start date of the Fourth Government's ministers. Use 24 December 1952 for office terms.
  - 18 August 1953: Bernstein tells the Knesset, in the debate on his ministry's budget, that all food rationing could end within six months. [V] JTA bulletin of 19 August 1953 ("last night"). It did not end then.
  - May 1955: Bernstein announces that locally produced meat will come off the ration; imported meat stays on it. [V] JTA bulletin of 20 May 1955 (the scan text is damaged in places).
  - 1956 to 1958: the list narrows to about fifteen goods, then eleven. [C] English Wikipedia ("Austerity in Israel", no citation on the line) puts fifteen goods in 1956 and eleven in 1958; Hebrew Wikipedia ("תקופת הצנע") puts fifteen after the Sinai campaign. Neither is sourced. [GAP] for an official list.
  - Early October 1957: a short egg rationing, begun before Rosh Hashanah, ends. [V] JTA bulletin of 11 October 1957.
  - 1 or 2 February 1959: rationing of all foods and consumer goods is abolished. [V] JTA bulletin of 2 February 1959 ("today"). Hebrew Wikipedia says the Minister of Trade and Industry, Pinhas Sapir, announced the formal end in 1959 [S].
- **Summary.** Rationing, set up in 1949, was taken apart in steps rather than ended at once. The big step came in November 1952, after the New Economic Policy and the June 1952 currency exchange had tightened the money supply; a government official told JTA that this had reduced the demand for goods. ### D2. The immigration trough and emigration (November 1951 to 1953)

- **Date(s)**
  - 18 or 19 November 1951: the Jewish Agency adopts selective immigration, except for "emergency immigration" of whole endangered communities. At least 80 percent of selected immigrants to be under 35; health certificates from boards under Israeli doctors; all but professionals, skilled workers and people with capital to agree to stay in farming settlements for two years; up to 20 percent may skip that if relatives in Israel can support them. [V] JTA bulletin of 19 November 1951 ("today").
  - 19 or 20 March 1952: the Agency says Jews from Eastern Europe are exempt from selection, and plans for 1,000 North African immigrants a month, many to receive hospital care and training first. [V] JTA bulletin of 20 March 1952.
  - 1951: 7,317 Jews and 330 non-Jews emigrated, per a CBS report. [V] JTA bulletin of 14 March 1952.
  - Late September 1953: David Horowitz says emigration now exceeds immigration. [V] JTA bulletin of 29 September 1953. JTA calls him governor of the Central Bank, but the Bank of Israel did not open until December 1954 (1 December 1954, C7); in 1953 he was Director-General of the Finance Ministry [S, to be checked].
  - Immigration by year (CBS, total immigrants): 1951 175,279; 1952 24,610; 1953 11,575; 1954 18,491; 1955 37,528; 1956 56,330. [V] CBS Publication 1365, Table 1. Jews only: 1952 24,369; 1953 11,326. [C] for 1953: the Jewish Agency reported 10,347 immigrants (JTA bulletin of 9 February 1954), of them almost 5,000 from Morocco, Tunisia and Libya. Use CBS.
- **Summary.** Mass immigration stopped almost at once at the end of 1951. Arrivals fell from about 175,000 in 1951 to about 24,600 in 1952 and 11,600 in 1953, the lowest year of the decade. Part of the fall was the Agency's selection rules of November 1951, which applied to communities not in danger (North Africa above all); part was that the large emergency movements (Iraq, Yemen, Romania) were over or blocked. The New Economic Policy note records that the 1952 speech gave no immigration limit and spoke of "objective reasons". In 1953 officials said emigration exceeded immigration.

  - JTA, 20 Mar 1952, Eastern Europe exempt: https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-will-not-restrict-immigration-of-east-european-jews (opened)
  - JTA, 14 Mar 1952, emigration in 1951: https://www.jta.org/archive/more-than-7000-jews-left-israel-for-other-countries-in-1951 (opened)
  - JTA, 29 Sep 1953, emigration exceeds immigration: https://www.jta.org/archive/emigration-from-israel-exceeds-immigration-official-reports (opened)
  - JTA, 9 Feb 1954, the Agency's 1953 total: https://www.jta.org/archive/10000-immigrants-arrived-in-israel-last-year-agency-reports (opened)
- **Sensitivity.** None. The selection rules screened poor and sick North African Jews in particular; describe them factually, as the Agency's rules, without judgement in either direction.
- **Candidate decision.** Late 1951 or early 1952, office `prime_minister` (the decision was the Jewish Agency's, with the government's agreement; frame it as the government's position). (a) Back selection for communities not in danger and keep the doors open for emergencies (historical); (b) keep open, unrestricted immigration at the 1949-51 pace despite the currency crisis; (c) a stricter cap. The cost of (b) is housing, food supply and treasury; the cost of (a) is morale among immigrant communities and standing with North African Jewry.
- **Gaps.** The annual CBS emigration series for 1952-56 (not found in the files opened); the Agency Executive's resolution text of November 1951; the Divrei HaKnesset debate on it.

### D3. North African immigration, "ship to village" and the Lachish region (August 1954 to July 1956)

- **Date(s)**
  - About 10 to 17 August 1954: 599 Jews from French Morocco arrive; within a week 264 are in their own houses. JTA calls this the Agency's new policy: immigrants go from the ship straight to permanent homes rather than to transit camps. [V] JTA bulletins of 16 and 18 August 1954.
  - 1954: Hebrew Wikipedia dates the "From the ship to the village" (מהאנייה לכפר) operation to 1954 [S, no citation on the line]. The Agency's report to the 24th Zionist Congress says mass immigration from North Africa was resumed after a hiatus from May 1951 to August 1954, and that in 1955, under the "ship to village" plan, 70 percent of immigrants went directly to prepared settlements in new and border development areas. [V] JTA bulletin of 20 April 1956.
  - About 30 December 1954: a selection office of the Agency and the Health Ministry in Casablanca finds nearly 4,000 people a month fit to emigrate; about 2,000 a month leave. [V] JTA bulletin of 31 December 1954.
  - About 25 January 1955: 28 families (163 people) from Morocco are taken from the ship to huts at Haruvit, the first new settlement in the Lachish development area. [V] JTA bulletin of 26 January 1955 ("yesterday").
  - About 16 February 1955: the Agency announces that 33 Jewish communities in Atlas Mountain villages will be moved to Israel. [V] JTA bulletin of 17 February 1955.
  - About 25 May 1955: Otzem, "the first settlement" in the Lachish region, opens for settlers from Atlas Mountain villages; plans for 21 settlements and an urban centre, Kiryat Gat, within the year at a cost of about $20 million. [V] JTA bulletin of 26 May 1955. [C] Haruvit in January 1955 is also called the first Lachish settlement (JTA, 26 January 1955); the two reports use "first" differently.
  - Late August 1955: at the Zionist Actions Committee, North African delegates warn of "illegal" emigration by Jews kept out by Israel's limits; Finance Minister Eshkol, also head of the Agency's settlement department, answers that it is a question of money (150 million pounds of initial cost per 100,000 immigrants), not a limit. [V] JTA bulletin of 29 August 1955. About 31 August 1955: Herut leads a demonstration of some 10,000 in Jerusalem for unrestricted North African immigration. [V] JTA bulletin of 1 September 1955.
  - End of 1955 to early 1956: Lachish has 18 villages and some 7,000 immigrants, most from North Africa, at a cost above $11 million; the plan is about 60,000 people. [V] JTA bulletin of 3 February 1956 (a report by the United Israel Appeal chairman, a fundraising source).
  - About 12 June 1956: Moroccan authorities (independent from March 1956) close Kadimah, the body running emigration to Israel, with about 2,000 people in its transit camp and 45,000 registered. [V] JTA bulletins of 13 and 14 June 1956.
  - About 23 July 1956: Ben-Gurion denies in the Knesset that the government and the Agency had discussed restricting North African immigration, and declines to discuss Morocco's halt in public session; 3,300 immigrants land in Haifa that day and are taken straight to settlements. [V] JTA bulletin of 24 July 1956.
  - Immigrants born in Africa, by year: 1953 4,995; 1954 12,343; 1955 32,646; 1956 45,138 (86 to 91 percent of all immigrants in 1954-56 were born in Asia or Africa). [V] CBS Publication 1365, Table 4.
- **Summary.** From August 1954 the Jewish Agency brought immigrants from Morocco and Tunisia in larger numbers and sent most of them directly from the port to new villages and towns, mainly in the Lachish region and the Negev, instead of to ma'abarot. Lachish was planned as a block of settlements with an urban centre at Kiryat Gat. The pace was set by money, not only by demand, and the size of the movement was argued over in the Agency and in the street. Morocco shut the emigration office in June 1956; the halt runs into the next era.

### D4. Ma'abarot into development towns (1952 to 1956)

- **Date(s)**
  - About 27 October 1952: the cabinet decides that no immigrant will spend the winter in a tent, adopting a Labour Ministry plan to move them before the end of December; a spokesman says a larger plan to wind up all ma'abarot within two years, moving about 50,000 families to permanent homes with a 50 million pound allocation, is under study. [V] JTA bulletin of 28 October 1952 ("today"). The next day, immigrants in ma'abarot ordered to move to new houses demonstrate and demand permanent houses where the camps stand; the Agency says it will cut off help to those who refuse. [V] JTA bulletin of 29 October 1952.
  - November 1952: Labour Minister Golda Myerson reports to the Zionist Actions Committee on winding up the ma'abarot; Eshkol proposes 60 new settlements a year for five years. [V] JTA bulletin of 24 November 1952.
  - Population of the ma'abarot. [C] across sources and definitions:
    - 213,000 at the start of the period (April 1951) and 70,000 at the end of 1955, per the Agency's report to the 24th Zionist Congress [V, JTA 20 April 1956].
    - 157,140 in January 1952 and 88,116 in May 1955, per Hebrew Wikipedia citing H. Darin-Drabkin, *Housing and Absorption in Israel* (Tel Aviv, 1955) [S, book not opened].
    - 129,000 residents in 1954 without street lighting, per Hebrew Wikipedia citing Rozin [S].
    - Over 220,000 in about 125 camps at the end of 1951, per English Wikipedia ("Development town") [S, citation not checked]. 15,300 left at the end of 1963, per Hebrew Wikipedia citing the Agency's absorption department (1964) [S].
  - Development towns founded in or near the slice [S, all from Wikipedia unless marked]:
    - Yeruham, 9 January 1951 (en.wiki); Sderot, 1951 as a transit camp, permanent housing 1954, local council 1956 (en.wiki); Kiryat Malakhi, 1951 as a ma'abara (en.wiki); Kiryat Shmona, settled from July 1949, named in June 1950 (en.wiki). These are before the slice.
    - Kiryat Gat: [C] en.wiki and he.wiki give 1954 as a ma'abara and 1955 as a development town (18 Moroccan families); JTA of 26 May 1955 has the urban centre "Kfar Gat" still to be built that year [V]; he.wiki has the cornerstone of the urban centre in 1956.
    - Ofakim, 19 April 1955, first residents from Morocco and Tunisia (he.wiki).
    - Dimona, 19 September 1955, 36 families from North Africa (he.wiki); en.wiki says "established in 1955". JTA of 28 June 1955 lists "Demona" as a planned destination for immigrants [V].
    - Ashdod: Finance Minister Eshkol approved the plan on 1 May 1956 and signed with the developers on 15 May; the first 22 families from Morocco arrived on 25 November 1956 (he.wiki). [C] en.wiki says "November 1956" without a day.
- **Summary.** By 1952 most ma'abara residents were from Asia and North Africa. The government and the Agency set out in late 1952 to empty the camps, first of tents and then of huts, and to turn many of them into permanent towns; residents resisted being moved away from the work and towns near the old camps. The number in ma'abarot fell by about half between 1952 and 1955, and many camps became development towns. From 1955 the new towns in the south (Ofakim, Dimona, Kiryat Gat, later Ashdod) were settled straight from the ship.

  - Pointers only: https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/מעברה ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_town ; the town articles on en.wiki and he.wiki (Kiryat Gat, Ofakim, Dimona, Ashdod, Sderot, Yeruham)

(a) Clear the tents before winter and plan to wind up the ma'abarot by building permanent homes in new towns, often away from the existing camps (historical); (b) build permanent homes where the camps stand, as the demonstrating residents asked, closer to existing jobs but not where the settlement map wants people; (c) leave the camps standing and spend on jobs first. ### D5. Water for the Negev: the Yarkon-Negev pipeline (1953 to July 1955)

- **Date(s)**

### D6. American aid and its suspension (1952 to 1954)
