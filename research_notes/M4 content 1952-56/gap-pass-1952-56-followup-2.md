## B3. The walkout and Nahalin (`events-1952-56.md:595-596`)

### Correction to the first-pass date of the walkout

- The first pass dates the walkout 22 March 1954 (heading of B3, the B3 Date(s) bullet, the Card A option (a) line). **After review of the printed record it is
  23 March 1954.** The Commission began the Israeli complaint on 22 March and the vote and walkout came on 23 March:
  - **[V, agent read 2026-10-29; a person's check pending]** JTA, Wednesday 24 March 1954, page 1, "Israel Walks Out of Armistice
    Commission; Protests Stand on Ambush", dateline "JERUSALEM, March 23. (JTA)" (http://pdfs.jta.org/1954/1954-03-24_057.pdf): "The Israeli
    delegation ... today walked out of an emergency meeting of the commission when the United Nations chairman ... abstained on a
    resolution". After the vote on the second paragraph "the senior Israeli delegate, Lt. Col. Arieh Shalev declared: 'The Israeli
    delegation is not in a position, under these circumstances, to continue its participation in the Israel-Jordan Mixed Armistice
    Commission.' The Israelis then walked out."
  - **[V, agent read 2026-10-29; a person's check pending]** UN document S/3252 (dated 25 June 1954; the report it carries was transmitted 19 June), page 14, section XIII, read on the page image
    (https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/603141/files/S_3252-EN.pdf): "Since 23 March, the Israel Government has severed all
    connexions with the Mixed Armistice Commission." The same report, section X, says "On 22 March, when the meeting was about to
    begin" the Israel Delegation gave the Chairman three names, and the continuation of the discussion is dated 23 March.
  - FRUS 780 (Jordan's Ambassador in Washington, 23 March, 6:30 p.m.) says the Israeli delegation "had walked out", consistent with
    23 March.
  - The first pass's "Commission examined the complaint on 22 March" stays true (it began that day).

### The Nahalin decision: narrowed

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-29; a person's check pending]** UN document S/3251 (25 June 1954), page 2, read on the page image
  (https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/603129/files/S_3251-EN.pdf): the written Jordanian complaint gives "Date and Time of Complaint:
  28-29/3/1954 at 24.00 hrs. J.T.", place "Nahhalin Village", "nine persons, eight men and one woman, were killed and fourteen others
  were injured". Paragraph 1 says the verbal complaint was made "On 29 March 1954, at 0700 hours" about an incident "seven hours

  condemned Israel for "an organized military attack March 29".
  - The Commission met in the early morning of 30 March without Israel and adopted a resolution condemning Israel (S/3251, section III,
    paragraphs 8 to 11). Israel had refused to receive the complaint and had declined to attend (paragraph 9).

  "Israel's Premier and Foreign Minister Moshe Sharett, told a press conference today that authoritative information on the Nahalin

## B7. Who approved the Kinneret raid (`events-1952-56.md:756`)

- **[S, one US embassy report on three unnamed sources; the document read on 2026-10-29]** FRUS 1955-57, vol. XIV, document 467,
  Telegram from the Embassy in Israel, Tel Aviv, 23 December 1955 (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v14/d467).
  The Chargé says it "represents mosaic of events leading up to and following Tiberias action" put together from "(A) source
  personally and officially close to Ben Gurion (B) source officially close to Sharett and (C) IDF general officer", and that "there
  appeared to be no inconsistencies". As printed:
  - "On Friday December 9 Ben Gurion met with Dayan and members of general staff in Tel Aviv to discuss steps to be taken to protect
    settlers during coming fishing season." A "limited" plan was "provisionally adopted on that date".
  - "On December 10 Chief of Staff Dayan went to Ben Gurion in Jerusalem and explained ... plan authorized day before which based on limited
    Syrian casualties and avoidance civilian casualties dangerous one and would probably result in heavy Israel losses. Ben Gurion then
    authorized shift of plans to one put in operation following evening."
  - "Ben Gurion however failed to consult any of his Mapai colleagues." Golda Myerson, the Acting Foreign Minister, had left for the
    US on the morning of 11 December.
  - "On December 14 Minister of Religion Shapira ... in his capacity as Chairman convened meeting of Cabinet Foreign Affairs
    Committee" and took "most critical line", "claiming Ben Gurion's decision unconstitutional because it had not been approved by
    his committee and stating he taking matter to Cabinet".
  - The raid's timing (night of 11 to 12 December) matches the note's date.
- Also read: FRUS 452 (Tel Aviv, 12 December 1955), where the Foreign Ministry's armistice adviser called the action "more of a
  preventive than punitive nature", and FRUS 456 (Jerusalem, 14 December), which reports General Burns saying Ben-Gurion was "well
  aware" of the attack when he saw Burns on 11 December. Both are [S] (embassy and consulate reports of conversations).
- **Reading for the note.** The only source read says Ben-Gurion approved it himself on 10 December, on Dayan's advice, with no
  consultation of the Mapai ministers; the source reports Shapira's complaint that it had not been approved by his Cabinet Foreign Affairs
  Committee (his reported complaint, not a finding about the decision). A Cabinet vote is not mentioned. Still
  **[GAP]**: the Israeli record (Cabinet minutes, the Defence Minister's own diary, or a scholarly account), none reachable here.
  Whether the Cabinet as a whole was consulted afterwards is not read.
