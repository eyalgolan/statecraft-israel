# Government policies by ministry, 1948 to 1956, and the voter groups of the period

Researched 2026-10-26. Research note for issue #25 (policies and voter groups, the research part only, opened by the owner's comment of 24 October 2026). Research only: no game code and nothing under `content/`. Facts only: no design values, no choice of which policies enter the game.

**Purpose.** Fix dated, sourced facts about what the governments of 1948 to 1956 did, grouped by ministry, plus the voter groups as the sources describe them. Where an existing note on main already covers a policy, this note links to it by path and does not re-source it. The new rows are the ones those notes do not cover.

**Reach of this pass.** The Knesset sites (`knesset.gov.il`, `fs.knesset.gov.il`, its OData service) and `gov.il` returned a block page to this machine, so no Divrei HaKnesset page, no Sefer HaChukim scan and no PMO Past Governments page was read. Most statute dates therefore rest on Hebrew Wikipedia infoboxes [S], and each is checked against a JTA bulletin where one could be found. Rows where the check found nothing say so, with the search recorded in section 10. Ministries with no row at all are listed in section 11 as [GAP].

## Conventions (the same as `reparations-vote-1952.md`)

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]** read on the cited page in this session and the page says it. Written out in full on every row; a bare V tag is never used.
- **[S]** single source, or a secondary or reprint source (Wikipedia, the Israel Democracy Institute, an NGO's archive article). Treat it as "probably".
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found, or the page could not be opened. A gap stays a gap: nothing is filled in by inference.
- **JTA dates.** A JTA archive page shows the bulletin date. The event date is the dateline on the item in the original bulletin PDF ("JERUSALEM, March 11"), resolved with "today" or "last night". Every JTA row here gives the dateline, read by eye on the scan.
- Quotes are as the page printed them. No quote is invented. Derived values show their arithmetic and are never given a V tag.
- "SH" is Sefer HaChukim (the statute book): number and page as the infobox of the Hebrew Wikipedia article gives them, matched against the Hebrew Wikisource header of the same law where I opened it. A matching pair is two readings of one printed reference, not two independent sources.

## 9. Voter groups of the period

Described only as the sources describe them. Tag [S] for the Israel Democracy Institute pages (IDI-1949, IDI-1951, IDI-1955). The vote tables below are copied from those pages; sums and ratios are mine and shown.

**1949 (Constituent Assembly, 25 January 1949).** Eligible voters 506,567, total votes 434,684, turnout 87.0 percent, threshold 0.83 percent [S IDI-1949]. IDI: "Of the 21 lists that ran in the elections, 12 entered the Knesset"; the main struggle was between two workers' parties, Mapai and Mapam, of which Mapai had a "pro-Western orientation" and Mapam was "pro-Soviet"; "Issues of religion and state, attitudes toward the Arab minority, and socio-economic issues also emerged in the election campaign". The religious bloc is "made up of four separate parties that had run on a single list". Seats: Mapai 46, Mapam 19, United Religious Front 16, Herut 14 (described as having "emerged from the Etzel (IZL) underground movement"), General Zionists 7, Progressive Party 5, Sephardim and Oriental Communities 4, Maki 4, Democratic List of Nazareth 2, Fighters List 1, WIZO 1, Yemenite Association 1. IDI describes the Democratic List of Nazareth as "an Arab minority party affiliated with Mapai". The coalition had 73 members from five parties.

**1951 (2nd Knesset, 30 July 1951).** Eligible voters 924,885, voter turnout 75.1 percent, threshold 1 percent [S IDI-1951]. Growth in the electorate since 1949: 924,885 / 506,567 = 1.826, that is 82.6 percent more eligible voters (derived). IDI: the elections "took place under the shadow of a serious economic crisis"; the State "absorbed approximately half a million new immigrants, nearly doubling the population that Israel had on the eve of its establishment", which "forced the government to adopt a policy of strict rationing and austerity"; "the religious bloc broke up into its four components parties, which ran in separate lists that won 15 seats in total"; the General Zionists became "the main opposition party with an impressive 20 seats". Seats: Mapai 45, General Zionists 20, Mapam 15, Hapoel Hamizrahi 8, Herut 8, Maki 5, Progressive Party 4, Democratic List for Israeli Arabs 3, Agudat Yisrael 3, Sephardim and Oriental Communities 2, Poalei Agudat Yisrael 2, Mizrahi 2, Progress and Work 1, Agriculture and Development 1. The 14 seat cells IDI prints as numbers sum to 119 (derived). The fifteenth row, Yemenite Association (7,965 votes), prints "7,965" in the seats cell, a repeat of its vote count and not a seat number; with the 1 seat that `content/legislatures.yaml` has for it (see election-1955.md), the total is 120 (derived), the size of the Knesset. The IDI 1951 page prints two "Total Votes" figures, 695,007 and 687,492, without saying which is the valid count; election-1955.md records a typo in IDI's Yemenite Association cell of the 1951 table.

**1955 (3rd Knesset, 26 July 1955).** Eligible voters 1,057,795, turnout 82.8 percent, threshold 1 percent [S IDI-1955]. The IDI 1955 page prints two figures, both under the label "Total Votes", 876,085 and 853,219, without saying which is the valid count (the same pattern as 1951; election-1955.md calls 876,085 "Total votes" and 853,219 "Valid votes", which is that note's reading, since the IDI page labels both "Total Votes"). Check, derived: the printed Mapai share of 32.2 percent (274,735 votes, see election-1955.md) equals 274,735 / 853,219 = 32.2 percent, while 274,735 / 876,085 = 31.4 percent; the same holds for Herut (107,190 / 853,219 = 12.6 percent against 12.2 percent), so IDI's share column rests on 853,219. IDI: of 18 lists, 12 won seats; Mapai 40 seats ("lost five seats"), Herut 15 ("overtook the General Zionists", 13); Mapam "split into its original two components", the more ideologically inclined branch keeping the name Mapam (9 seats) and the activist branch reverting to the name Ahdut HaAvoda-Poalei Zion (the table row "Labor Unity" has 10 seats); the National Religious Front 11, the Religious Torah Front 6, Maki 6, Progressive Party 5, Democratic List for Israeli Arabs 2, Progress and Work 2, Agriculture and Development 1. Growth in the electorate since 1951: 1,057,795 / 924,885 = 1.144, that is 14.4 percent more (derived). Further 1955 detail (first-time voters, the preliminary count, the coalition talks) is in election-1955.md and is not repeated.

What the three pages do not give: any survey of who voted for whom (by origin, religiosity, class or place). IDI describes parties and blocs, not voter groups by origin.
