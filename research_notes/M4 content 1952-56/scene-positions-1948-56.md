# Ministers' positions in six decision scenes, 1948 to 1956

Researched 2026-10-26 (every page below was opened by an agent on that day; a person's check is pending). Research note for
issue #106 (decision scenes, the research part only, opened by the owner's comment of 24 October 2026). Research only: no
game code and nothing under `content/`. The scene screen, the proposal and the scene content stay paused until after the MVP.

**Purpose.** For each candidate scene on the issue's list, say who argued what, when, and from which record. This note
adds only that. Dates, vote counts and the setting of each scene are already sourced in `reparations-vote-1952.md`,
`new-economic-policy-1952.md`, `road-to-suez-1956.md`, `events-1952-56.md` and `defence-1948-56.md` (sections 1.5 and 2; merged
on main), and are not repeated here.

**Hard rule from the issue.** No minister's position is named unless it was read in the cabinet or Knesset protocol, or in a
memoir read for that passage. Where no such record was reached, the row is [GAP] with what was tried (section 8).

## Conventions (the same as `reparations-vote-1952.md`)

- **[V]** read on the cited page in this session and the page says it. Every [V] here is written "agent read 2026-10-26; a
  person's check pending". On a memoir row the tag means only that the memoir says it; it is one participant's account. Memoir rows keep [V] because the owner's rule admits a memoir read for that passage; a memoir row on a meeting its author did not attend is [S].
- **[S]** a reported public statement (a JTA bulletin of what a minister said in public), kept apart from cabinet positions,
  or a single secondary source. Treat it as "probably".
- **[C]** sources conflict; every version is given. **[GAP]** not found, or the page could not be opened. Nothing is filled in by inference.
- Quotes are short and as the page gives them. Paraphrase otherwise. No quote, page, date or URL is invented.
- "Book p. N" is the printed page of the English edition named in section 1; the printed page numbers are in the text file
  the agent read.

## 10. What this feeds (design questions, not decided here)

For each scene, the rows above are the only sourced list of who argued what; whether a scene uses them is the owner's call.
The one scene with a full cabinet record, the reparations decision of 31 Dec 1951, also records the voting-discipline
argument (coalition members versus conscience), which is a different question from the moral one.
