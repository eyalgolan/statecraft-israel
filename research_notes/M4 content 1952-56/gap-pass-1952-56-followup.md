### A6. The Arab League's decision on the Johnston plan: 11 October 1955

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]** FRUS 1955-57, vol. XIV, document 336, Telegram from the Consulate
  General at Jerusalem to the Department of State, Jerusalem, 14 October 1955, "From Johnston", reporting the Cairo meetings
  of the four Foreign Ministers (Egypt, Syria, Jordan, Lebanon): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v14/d336
  - Its footnote gives the resolution adopted on 11 October: the Jordanian, Syrian, Lebanese and Egyptian representatives had
    studied the Arab plan for the Jordan Valley and decided "that the experts be asked to pursue the mission with which they
    have been entrusted until an agreement safeguarding Arab interests is reached."
  - Its next footnote says telegram 747 from Cairo, 15 October, transmitted a letter of the same date from Arab League
    Secretary General Hassouna to Johnston, enclosing the Political Committee's 11 October resolution "approving the decision
    taken by the four states concerned with the plan for the development of the Jordan Valley and its tributaries."
  - The telegram's own comment reads that the program "in fact has received technical acceptance", and the body says the
    Lebanese and Syrians had pressed for outright rejection and that Johnston's side worked out the proposal ("We worked out
    face-saving proposal for Lebanese wherein four Foreign Ministers would ask Arab League recognize technical validity JVP"); the four
    Foreign Ministers then "recommended further study and postponement decision". Johnston
    left for Jerusalem on 12 October (footnote).
- **Reading for the note.** The Arab League's formal step is dated 11 October 1955 (Political Committee resolution, passed on
  by letter on 15 October). It did not adopt the plan; it endorsed the four states' decision to keep the experts working.
  This is consistent with the note's "the Arab League did not adopt it", and it replaces the [GAP] on the date. The note's
  unopened claim about a 1 March 1956 Israeli deadline is not touched here.
- Also read in the same volume: document 330 (Johnston's telegram of 7 October, the eve of the Cairo meeting) and document 331 (the Cairo
  telegram of 8 October on his evening with Nasser). They give the build-up only and add no date for the decision.

### The start of the state school system (State Education Law, passed 12 August 1953)

- **[S]** An English translation of the law, as a PDF hosted by Adalah (page headed "No. 50", printed page 113), opened on
  2026-10-26: https://adalah.org/Public/files/Discriminatory-Laws-Database/English/24-State-Education-Law-1953.pdf
  - Section 3, printed: "From the school year 5714 onwards, State education shall be introduced in every official educational
    institution; in an official educational institution which in the school year 5713 belonged to the Mizrachi trend or the
    Agudat Israel trend or the religious section of the Labour trend, religious State education shall be introduced."
  - The page's footnote: passed by the Knesset on 1 Elul 5713 (12 August 1953), published in Sefer Ha-Chukkim No. 131 of
    9 Elul 5713 (20 August 1953), p. 137; the bill was published in Hatza'ot Chok No. 170 of 10 Tammuz 5713 (23 June 1953), p. 242.
    This matches the note's 12 and 20 August dates.
- **Reading for the note.** The law itself sets the start at the school year 5714 (the school year that began in autumn 1953),
  not at a calendar day. This turns "likely but not checked" into a statement about the law's text. It does not show
  that every school changed that autumn; no source on the day-by-day roll-out was read. The Hebrew original in Sefer
  HaChukim 131 p. 137 and the Knesset sitting record were not opened (knesset.gov.il returned a page without the text on this box;
  no Wayback copy of the sitting record exists: `archive.org/wayback/available` returned an empty result on 2026-10-26).
  So the row is closed on the translation only, tagged [S].

### Reparations talks: where and when they opened

- **[V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]** JTA Daily News Bulletin, vol. XIX no. 57, masthead "Friday, March 20, 1952" as printed (20 March 1952 was a Thursday; [C] masthead day against date; the dateline "THE HAGUE, March 20" does not lag the masthead as the other bulletins do, e.g. 24 March datelined March 23, so by the usual lag the bulletin is probably Friday 21 March, our reading),
  item datelined "THE HAGUE, March 20", headed "German-Israeli talks open today in strongly guarded Dutch castle"
  (http://pdfs.jta.org/1952/1952-03-20_057.pdf, page 1; the file was found by trying file numbers 050 to 062 for 20 to 26 March). Printed: "The opening session of the Israeli-German negotiations for
  reparations payments by West Germany will be held tomorrow in an old castle at Wassenaar, a suburb of The Hague, it was
  learned here today." "Tomorrow" from the dateline is 21 March. The headline says "open today", which the body contradicts. If the bulletin is really of Friday 21 March (see the masthead [C] above), the headline is simply that day's news; the postponement in the next bullet is another possible reason. No source read says which. The 21 March conclusion stands either way.
- **[V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]** JTA Daily News Bulletin, vol. XIX no. 58, Monday 24 March 1952,
  item datelined "THE HAGUE, March 23" (http://pdfs.jta.org/1952/1952-03-24_058.pdf, page 1): "The German-Jewish reparations
  talks which opened here Friday in historic Washington Castle in the suburb of Wassenaar will be resumed tomorrow and will last
  two weeks." Friday before 23 March 1952 (a Sunday) is 21 March. The bulletin prints the castle's name as "Washington
  Castle"; that name is not used elsewhere in this file. The same item says the German delegation made its opening statement
  "Friday morning" and that the Israeli and Conference delegations meet the Germans in turn, mornings and afternoons.
  Page 3 of the same bulletin (item datelined "THE HAGUE, March 23") says the Israelis were staying at Wassenaar and the Germans at
  a hotel in The Hague (OCR; not quoted).
- **[S]** Gideon Reuveni, "Who Was (Not) Invited to Wassenaar?", in De Vita and Goschler (eds), *Redefining Reparations:
  Wassenaar 1952 and the Global Politics of Repair* (Routledge, 2025), open-access PDF from the Utrecht University repository
  (https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/259862828/Redefining_Reparations_25_03_26_13_37_34.pdf): the talks "were supposed to
  commence on Thursday, March 20"; that morning the German side asked the Israeli embassy in The Hague to postpone to the next
  day because they needed more time for their opening statement (chapter 2, p. 55, citing a Keren to Sharett telegram of
  20 March 1952 and Küster's diary; the archive items were not opened). The introduction (De Vita and Goschler) gives
  "Friday March 21, 1952" at Kasteel Oud-Wassenaar.
- **Reading for the note.** Place: the Oud-Wassenaar castle hotel at Wassenaar, near The Hague. Day: Friday 21 March 1952,
  after a one-day postponement from the planned 20 March. The two readings agree. The "Mar 1952" row can carry an exact day.
- **Cross-check, not a source for the opening.** JTA, 2 April 1952, "First Stage of Hague Reparations Talks is Completed"
  (https://www.jta.org/archive/first-stage-of-hague-reparations-talks-is-completed-german-delegates-confer-at-bonn-on-definite-off,
  bulletin PDF http://pdfs.jta.org/1952/1952-04-02_065.pdf): the German delegation left "late last night" for Bonn, ending the
  first stage. Only the page text was read, not the PDF image.
