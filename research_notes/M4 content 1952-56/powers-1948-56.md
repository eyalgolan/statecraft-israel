# The four powers and Israel, 1948 to 1956: recognition, agreements and breaks

Researched 2026-10-24. Research note for issue #11 (foreign relations 1948-56, the research part only, opened by the owner's
comment of 24 October 2026). Research only: no game code and nothing under `content/`. The diplomacy design, the standing
system and the screen (#105) stay paused.

**Purpose.** Fix dated, sourced facts for Israel's relations with the United States, the United Kingdom, France and the USSR,
so that a later diplomacy design needs no new sourcing for the early years. This note does not repeat what is already on
main; it links to it and fills what those notes do not cover (see "Already on main" below).

## Conventions (the same as `reparations-vote-1952.md`)

- **[V]** read on the cited page in this session and the page says it. Where two independent pages agree, both are given.
  Every [V] here is "agent read 2026-10-24; a person's check pending".
- **[S]** single source, or a secondary or reprint source (for example the Jewish Virtual Library or an AP dispatch reprinted by
  COJS), or a search-result summary. Treat it as "probably".
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found, or the page could not be opened. A gap stays a gap: nothing is filled in by inference.
- Quotes are as the page gives them. No quote is invented. No Wikipedia page is used as a sole source.
- Dates of cables are the date on the document; the day of the event may differ.

Note on the "de facto" and "de jure" labels: they are the contemporary diplomatic terms used by the sources above. The AP
dispatch explains the difference in its own words (provisional government as de facto authority against recognition as the
legally constituted authority, normally followed by an exchange of representatives); the note uses them only as the sources do.

## 6. Conflicts kept (both values)

- **Soviet recognition:** announced 17 May 1948 (AP dispatch, Moscow dateline) and published in the Soviet press on 18 May (FRUS 286, the US chargé's cable of 18 May). They are consistent as an event and a publication, but secondary pages give either date as "the" date. Whether it was "de jure" is a secondary label; neither opened primary source uses it.
- **US Export-Import loan:** January 1949 (JTA, 1952) against spring 1949 (FRUS, 15 August 1949).
- **French de jure date:** 20 May 1949 against 11 May 1949 against the claim that it never happened formally.
- **US-Israel FCN treaty:** date in force 3 April 1954 (text) against 23 August 1951 (the metadata field).
- **Vermars:** 22 June 1956 (events A9) against 17-19 June and 23-24 June 1956 (secondary pages not opened).
- **Mystère consent, January 1956:** the earlier events note says the US consented; FRUS 43 records only Israel's counselor saying so, and FRUS 66 relays second-hand a French claim of oral approval through their Washington ambassador.
