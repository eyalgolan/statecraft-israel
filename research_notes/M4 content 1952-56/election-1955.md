# The Third Knesset election and the 1955 government crisis (June to November 1955)

Researched 2026-10-11. All URLs were checked on that date. Draft for issue #5: one note per event.

## Purpose

Fix the facts for the hard anchor event around the election of 26 July 1955 and the government crisis that ran from the fall of the Sixth Government in late June to Ben-Gurion's return as Prime Minister on 3 November 1955. This feeds issue #26 (governments and ministers) and issue #28 (the event card). Nothing here is drafted for the game.

Election day colour [V] JTA bulletin of 27 Jul (http://pdfs.jta.org/1955/1955-07-27_144.pdf): about 1,000,000 voters, 120 deputies, a quiet day, polls closed 11 pm, about 20% first-time voters, municipal elections held at the same time in 82 localities (19 cities, 63 towns and villages). IDI's reading of 1955 [V]: Mapai was split between a moderate group led by Sharett and the activists led by Ben-Gurion; Mapam split into Mapam (pro-Soviet) and Ahdut HaAvoda-Poalei Zion (activist), 19 seats together against the united Mapam's 15; Herut overtook the GZ; Mapai was the only force able to form a government. Mapai held 47 seats at the end of the Second Knesset according to he.wiki (Sixth Government page) and en.wiki [S]; the IDI pages do not give that figure.

## 4. Context: how the government fell

- Lavon resignation (Feb 1955) and the date of Ben-Gurion's return as Defence Minister: [GAP]. Not found in an opened source. The gov.il page for the Sixth Government lists Ben-Gurion as Defence Minister under Sharett [V] https://www.gov.il/he/Departments/General/gov6 .
- Trigger of the June crisis [V] JTA bulletin of 30 Jun: the Communists and Herut tabled separate no-confidence motions over the Kasztner case, after the Justice Minister ordered the attorney general to appeal to the Supreme Court against a District Court judge's findings. The GZ deputies abstained. Sharett held that they had broken collective responsibility. Pinhas Rosen (Progressive, Justice) proposed censuring the GZ ministers but keeping them; Sharett and the Mapai ministers refused, the GZ would not resign, and Sharett resigned. GZ leaders said a vote with the coalition would deepen the public impression that the government was tied to the Kasztner case, and that they had not been consulted on the appeal.
- Result: Sharett reformed the Cabinet without the four GZ ministers (P. Bernstein, Y. Sapir, Y. Serlin, I. Rokach) [V] gov.il Sixth Government page. IDI's 1951 page [V] notes that Sharett's second government was formed without the GZ after they abstained in a no-confidence vote.
- Caretaker: gov.il describes the Sixth Government as in effect a caretaker government that prepared the Third Knesset election [V]. The Sharett Cabinet resigned on 15 Aug and was again caretaker until 3 Nov [V JTA 17 Aug].
- The Kasztner case itself (verdict date, charges) is not sourced here. Content on it needs its own note and a sensitivity review.

## 5. Campaign issues and options

- The record in hand covers positions after the vote, not a campaign platform survey: [GAP] on party platforms. IDI [V] gives only the Sharett versus Ben-Gurion split inside Mapai.
- Positions of Sharett and Ben-Gurion on who should lead Mapai: [GAP]. Only IDI's one-line framing is read.
- JTA bulletin of 27 Jul [V]: in pre-count predictions, a coalition of Mapai, Progressives, Ahdut HaAvoda and the Mizrahi Labor groups was described as not precluded.
- JTA bulletin of 29 Jul [S]: Bernstein (GZ) blamed an unrestrained attack on his party; Herut's Arieh Ben-Eliezer called the gains a voters' revolt; Golda Meir blamed GZ and Ahdut HaAvoda attacks on the last Mapai-led government.
- Positions of the parties to the President on 16 Aug [V] JTA bulletin of 17 Aug: Mapai ready for the broadest coalition excluding only Herut and the Communists, conditional on collective responsibility (every coalition party supports what the government proposes). Mizrahi and HaPoel HaMizrahi willing to join a coalition of all but the Communists, asking guarantees on the Jewish way of life, freedom of schooling and settlement for religious Jews, and freedom of expression, and proposing that Mapai be asked to form the government. The Progressives also urged a broad coalition excluding only the Communists. The GZ and Agudah were seen later that day; other parties on 17 Aug.

## 6. Coalition formation

- Mandate: [S] 18 Aug, Ben-Zvi invites Ben-Gurion to form a cabinet on the "broadest" basis (JTA headline and snippet only).
- IDI [V]: talks lasted about three months. Ben-Gurion presented a government of Mapai, the National Religious Front (soon the NRP), Ahdut HaAvoda, Mapam and the Progressives, based on 75 MKs (80 with the Arab satellite parties). The JTA bulletin of 2 Nov [V] expected 80 votes.
- Options on the table in late October [V] JTA bulletin of 2 Nov: a four-party left coalition versus a five-party coalition that included HaPoel HaMizrahi. Its central committee first decided not to enter. Then Ben-Gurion and Shapira made "serious concessions" in a meeting held in the hotel room where Ben-Gurion was confined by illness, and President Ben-Zvi urged the most stable government possible. Sharett was returning from Geneva.
- Result: Ben-Gurion's 16-minister government won the vote on 3 Nov, 73 to 32 with 3 abstentions [V FRUS vol. XIV doc. 377 fn. 3], so 108 members took part, fewer than the 75 (IDI) to 80 (JTA) expected [C, expectation against result].
- [C] The inventory listed Ben-Gurion's return as "2 or 3 Nov". Resolved: presented 2 Nov, vote and swearing in 3 Nov (gov.il start date 3 Nov [V]).
- Second Ben-Gurion government of January 1958 [V IDI]; the 7th Government ended 7 Jan 1958 [V gov.il], with a note that the PM submitted his resignation on 31 Dec 1957.
