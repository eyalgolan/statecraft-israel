# M4 content research: annual economic series, 1952 to 1956

Researched 2026-10-22. All URLs were opened on that date. Research only, for step 1 of issue #24: no test, golden or `content/rules.yaml` change goes with it (step 2 stays paused).

**Purpose.** #24 step 1 asks for sourced annual series for 1952 to 1956: GNP and the national accounts, each year's fiscal stance, unemployment, and the official and black-market price indexes. This note gives each figure with its table and page, and gives both values where the sources disagree. It proposes no calibration targets; choosing anchors is step 2.

**Conventions** (the same as `new-economic-policy-1952.md`)
- **[V]** verified on the cited page, which was opened. Tables whose text extraction was jumbled were checked on a rendered image of the page.
- **[S]** single source, or a search-result summary that was not opened directly.
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- Nothing is estimated here. A figure "computed here" is plain arithmetic on the cited table and is marked as such.

**Source caveat.** No volume of the CBS *Statistical Abstract of Israel* for these years was found online (searched cbs.gov.il and the web; library catalogues hold print copies only). Where a figure comes from the Abstract, it is given as Patinkin or the Bank of Israel quotes it, with the Abstract page they cite; the Abstract page itself was not opened. Patinkin's 1950 to 1954 national accounts come from Lubell, *Israel's National Expenditure 1950-1954* (FP and CBS, Special Series No. 74, 1958), which was not opened either. Both annual reports call their own national accounts "provisional" (BoI 1955, p. 21, text after Table 2; BoI 1956, p. 29, opening of chapter II).

**Which series to use.** Patinkin's tables are the only opened source that covers all five years on one basis, and for 1955 and 1956 they take the later figures of the Bank of Israel *Annual Report 1957* (Patinkin, p. 45, sources to Table 12). The two annual reports printed earlier, provisional figures that differ (section 1.3). Suggestion for step 2: use Patinkin for a consistent series and the annual reports as the second value.

## 2. Fiscal stance

No opened source gives the government budget balance on one basis for all five years. Two measures were found.

### 2.1 General government saving, national-accounts basis (Patinkin, Table 37, line 3a, p. 96) [V]

| | 1952 | 1953 | 1954 | 1955 | 1956 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| General government saving, IL millions, current prices | -40.9 | -60.1 | -35.9 | -36 | -188 |

"General government" here covers the central government, local authorities, the Jewish Agency and the Jewish National Fund (p. 94). Saving is defined as government property and entrepreneurial income plus direct and indirect taxes, less consumption, subsidies and net current transfers (footnote b, p. 96). Patinkin writes that the general government sector dissaved in every year, most of all in the Sinai Campaign year 1956 (p. 95, paraphrased). Sources (p. 96): 1952 to 1954, Lubell, p. 76, Table 5-3; 1955 and 1956, Bank of Israel, *Annual Report 1957*, p. 11, Table II-1 and p. 18, Table II-6, and CBS, *Receipts and Expenditures of the Government: 1955/56 and 1956/57*.

Defence spending in the national accounts (line 2b of Table 12 above) went from 63.9 million IL in 1952 to 340 million in 1956, current prices [V].

### 2.2 State budget by financial year, April to March (BoI 1955, Table 141, p. 217) [V]

| IL millions | 1954-55 revenue | 1954-55 expenditure | 1955-56 revenue | 1955-56 expenditure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary Budget | 344.9 | 332.7 | 482.9 | 483.4 |
| Development Budget, debt redemption and special expenditure | 316.4 | 295.7 | 371.3 | 371.3 |
| Earmarked revenue and expenditure | 9.1 | 9.1 | 12.3 | 12.3 |
| Current surplus or deficit (as printed; for 1955-56 a deficit of 0.5 million in the estimates) | - | 33.0 | 0.5 | - |
| Total | 670.4 | 670.4 | 867.0 | 867.0 |

The 1954-55 columns are actuals; the 1955-56 columns are estimates and include the special Defence Fund Budget of 50 million IL (text, p. 217). The source is the Accountant General's financial reports. Read as printed: the 33.0 sits in the expenditure column of 1954-55, so revenue exceeded expenditure that year, and the 0.5 sits in the revenue column of 1955-56. In words: the 1955-56 estimates carry a deficit of 0.5 million IL; Table 142 (p. 218) prints it as "Surplus of Expenditure 0.5". The text on p. 219 says "the revenue surplus from the financial year 1954-55 was absorbed".

Figures in parentheses are, in Patinkin's words, "not too reliable" (list of symbols in the front matter, PDF p. 17). Before 1955 they are not survey figures. They extrapolate the labour force survey backwards with labour-exchange registrations, following Hovne's method (a ratio of 1.28 applied to the monthly registrations in *Statistics of Labor Exchanges* No. 8, 1958, p. 20); Patinkin says "this extrapolation is not too reliable" (sources to Table 7, p. 33).

### 3.2 Labour force surveys [V, C]

| Survey | Source | Unemployed | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 1954 | BoI 1955, Table 62 (p. 92), "corrected" | 47,800 | 8.0 percent |
| June 1954 | BoI 1956, Table VIII-4 (p. 129), "revised" | not given | gainfully employed 91.4 percent of the labour force |
| November 1955 | BoI 1955, Table 62 (p. 92) | 45,500 | 7.2 percent |
| November 1955 | BoI 1956, Table VIII-4 (p. 129) | not given | gainfully employed 92.8 percent |
| June 1956 | BoI 1956, Table VIII-4 (p. 129) | not given | gainfully employed 92.7 percent; civilian labour force 620,000 |

The two Bank of Israel reports disagree on June 1954: 8.0 percent unemployed against 91.4 percent employed (so 8.6 percent not employed). Both say they are corrected or revised figures. Table VIII-4 (p. 129) also prints the civilian labour force: 596,000 in June 1954, 631,000 in November 1955 and 620,000 in June 1956. Computed here: 8.6 percent of 596,000 is about 51,300 not employed, against the 47,800 unemployed in BoI 1955. Patinkin's annual figure for 1954 is 7.4 percent. The surveys give a single week each, and BoI 1956 warns they "do not permit the drawing of conclusions as to changes" between them (p. 130).

### 3.3 Registered unemployment

- BoI 1956: "In 1956, the level of registered unemployment was 8 per cent higher than in 1955", and in the last quarter of 1956 more than 23 percent higher than a year earlier (p. 135) [V]. The monthly daily averages for 1955 and 1956 are in Table VIII-8 (p. 136).
- Eshkol (1962), as cited in `new-economic-policy-1952.md` section 4: registered unemployment 7.2 percent of the labour force (year implied 1952) and 11.3 percent in 1953 [S here: from the sibling note, not re-opened]. These are higher than Patinkin's 6.3 and 8.9 for the same years [C]. They measure different things (registrations as against Patinkin's survey-equivalent extrapolation), so neither is wrong. Suggestion for step 2: use Patinkin's for a consistent series and say so.

### 4.2 Black-market prices of goods [GAP]

No black-market price index for goods was found: not in Patinkin, BoI 1955 or BoI 1956, and a web search found none. Patinkin says the official index does not include black-market prices ("black markets (whose prices are not reflected in the index) began to appear at an early date", p. 109), and that the rise of almost 60 percent from September 1951 to June 1952 "exaggerates the true one" because the September 1951 index "does not reflect the rise that had already taken place in the black-market prices" (p. 111). The only black-market price series found is the black-market rate for the dollar (4.3).
