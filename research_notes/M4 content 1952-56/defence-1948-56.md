# Command, procurement and manpower policy, 1948 to 1956

Researched 2026-10-24. Research note for issue #22 (defence depth, the research part only, opened by the owner's comment of
24 October 2026). Research only: no game code and nothing under `content/`. Which of the four parts enter the game is the
owner's call; this note decides nothing about that. The sim code and content for defence depth stay paused until after the MVP.

**Purpose.** Fix dated, sourced facts on three things: who commanded (Defence Minister, Chief of Staff, military intelligence,
the service branches), where arms came from and when they were blocked, and who had to serve, for how long and who was
exempt under the Defence Service Law and its amendments. Ratings such as competence are game values and are not in this
note. A stance on restraint or activism is given only where a named source states it.

  the "Page N" markers of the Internet Archive scan (https://archive.org/details/israels-border-wars-1949-1956-benny-morris),

## 3. Manpower policy, 1948-56

### 3.1 The law as passed: Defence Service Law, 5709-1949

Passed by the Knesset on 14 Elul 5709 (8 September 1949); in force from 8 Tishrei 5710 (1 October 1949); signed by Ben-Gurion as
Prime Minister and Minister of Defence and by Speaker Sprinzak; *Sefer HaHukim* 25, 15 Sep 1949, p. 271. [V] DSL-1949, s. 23 and
the closing lines. The bill was published on 16 Aug 1949 (footnote to s. 4).

| Topic | What the text says (section numbers of DSL-1949) |
|---|---|
| Who | A "permanent resident" in the military age band: men 18 to 49, women 18 to 34 (s. 1). Ages counted by the Hebrew calendar (s. 2, rewritten in 1950). No wording about religion or ethnicity appears in the text read |
| Registration and medical | A "*poked*" (a conscription officer appointed by the Defence Minister) may order registration and a medical board; 17-year-olds may be called to register (ss. 3-5) |
| Regular service (s. 6) | Men called at 18-26: 24 months; men called at 27-29: 18 months; women: 12 months (s. 6(b) and 6(c)). Call-up ages: men 18-29, women 18-26 (s. 6(a)). Transitional windows for those over 18 on the law's start (s. 6(d)) |
| What the first year is for | "The first twelve months" of a man's regular service, and a woman's twelve months, "shall be devoted mainly, after initial military training, to agricultural training" (s. 6(f)); the Defence Minister may route Air Force and Navy volunteers to those services instead. He must also make regulations that protect the integrity of settlement nuclei (*garinim*) (s. 6(g)) |
| Prior service counted | Time served from 1 Jan 1948 is deducted from the duty (s. 6(j)); a person released after a year or more of full IDF service before the law is not called (s. 6(i)(2)); the definition of countable service is "service the Minister declares" up to 30 May 1948 and "full service in the IDF" from 31 May 1948 (s. 6(k)) |
| Reserve | Men 18-39 and women 18-34: up to 31 consecutive days a year plus one day a month; men 40-49: up to 14 days plus a day a month; commanders (corporal and above) up to 7 more days (s. 7) |
| Special service | The Defence Minister may call any reservist to regular or reserve service by order, but must bring the order to the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee, and it lapses after 14 days unless the Committee or the Knesset confirms it (s. 8) |
| Exemptions | s. 11(a): the Minister may exempt a permanent resident who does not intend to settle in Israel. s. 11(b): exempt from all service are a woman who is a mother (the OCR reads "אם לילד") and a pregnant woman. s. 11(c): a married woman is exempt from regular service. s. 11(d): a woman who declares that conscience or religious conviction prevents her from serving is exempt, "in the manner set by regulations" |
| Deferral and release | s. 12: the Defence Minister may, for reasons of the size of the forces, "education, settlement, the national economy, family, or other similar reasons", release a person from regular service, shorten it, defer it at his request, or release a reservist. s. 15: such orders may be individual or for a class, and need not be published in the Official Gazette |
| Penalties | s. 16: up to 2 years or IL 600 for failing a duty; up to 5 years for evasion or self-injury |

Context on size: Morris p. 119: the IDF was "90,000-strong in December 1948", the bulk was demobilised, and a new system was set up
"based on a small standing army of some 30,000 conscripts and professionals, and a large pool of easily mobilized reserves". [V]
The 1948 force counts by date are on main (war-1948.md section 1).

|---|---|

### 3.3 Who was exempt or deferred

- **Women.** In the 1949 text, a mother, a pregnant woman and a married woman were out of regular service, and a woman could be
  exempt by declaring conscience or religion (s. 11, above); from March 1952 the declaration had to be proved [V]. The
  share of women exempt in these years: [GAP].
- **Yeshiva students.** The record, from the Knesset Research Center paper [V] KNS-YESH:
  - **9 Mar 1948**: Israel Galili, head of the national staff, writes to the brigades being formed: "A decision was made that
    yeshiva students, according to approved lists, are exempt from army service", valid for the Hebrew year 5708, to be
    reviewed at its end.
  - **1 Oct 1948**: Ben-Gurion, as Prime Minister and Defence Minister, reports to the Provisional State Council his
    decision not to draft yeshiva students; about **400** are covered, as his answer to Meir Grabovsky (Argov) in the "Knesset
    Defence Committee" shows (the paper's wording; the Knesset did not yet exist in October 1948, so a committee of the
    Provisional State Council is presumably meant). He says Agudat Israel told the Ministry there were 400 yeshiva students and
    confirms "Yes" that those 400 were released.
  - **9 Jan 1950**: his diary: yeshiva heads asked for a law; he told them "the law will not pass"; they suggested
    regulations by the government or the Defence Minister.
  - **9 Jan 1951**: his letter to the Ministry's director and the Chief of Staff: "On the basis of section 12 of the Defence
    Service Law, I released the yeshiva students from regular service", only for those actually studying and "as long as" they
    study; military police to arrest those found outside their yeshiva and not proved to have served.
  - **4 Mar 1954** (Knesset sitting 201): Ben-Gurion: the law allows release "for reasons of education, economy, family and
    health. On this basis yeshiva students were released".
  - **1954**: Lavon, as Defence Minister, issued an order to draft those who had studied four years; Chief Rabbi Herzog
    objected (9 Mar 1954); Sharett told Herzog it aimed at abuse, then set the order aside, and a government decision
    (no. 324, 15 Mar 1954) set up a ministerial committee. Ben-Gurion to Lavon, 16 Mar 1954: "never an agreement between me
    and any religious party" on the exemption; "I did it of my own free will ["התנדבות"]" (the source puts the Hebrew word in quote marks); the rise in numbers, if artificial or not,
    "requires, in my opinion, changing the system".
  - **1958**: Peres, with Ben-Gurion's approval, reaches a new arrangement with the yeshiva heads (annual deferral to 25, then
    29): outside the period but it marks the end of this phase.
  - HOOVER pp. 2-4 gives the same chain (p. 3: Ben-Gurion's condition of basic training for the 400 was "never implemented") and says (p. 4) the exempted number tripled "to more than twelve hundred between 1948 and 1958";
    IDI-1948 says Ben-Gurion complied with Agudat Israel's request for 400 and used section 12.
  - [C] on main (facts.md): "dated 1948 or 1949 depending on source". KNS-YESH settles the sequence: the first release was in
    March 1948 under the Haganah, the number 400 and the Defence Minister's authority date from Oct 1948, and the legal basis
    cited from 1951 is section 12 of the 1949 law. The "1951 letter" that facts.md takes from Jüdische Allgemeine is the 9 Jan
    1951 letter above (the same year; the identity is our reading, not the newspaper's).
- **Arab citizens.** The 1949 text has no ethnic wording (above). How most Arab citizens came not to be called: [GAP] (no
  primary source found; en.wiki "Exemption from military service in Israel" says the exemption "does not originate via Israeli
  statutory law" and rests on IDF personnel guidelines [S], citing the Law Library of Congress, which was not opened).
- **Druze.** Service became mandatory in **1956** by "an agreement between Druze leaders and the Israeli government" [S]
  GJIA (no statute named; it cites an MDPI article, https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/9/1/1, not opened). Another search summary names Ben-Gurion, and one
  gives 1957 for a related step; neither was opened, so the exact date, the instrument and the decider stay [GAP].
- **Circassians:** [GAP] (a search summary gives 1958; not opened).
- **Pacifists, immigrants, doctors:** immigrants and doctors are in the 1952 text above [V]; pacifists [GAP] for this period.
