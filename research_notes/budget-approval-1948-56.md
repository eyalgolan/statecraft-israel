# Budget approval, 1948-1956: evidence ledger

Checked 11 October 2026. Early research draft for #3, not a completed annual vote catalogue. No `budget_approval` or `open_questions` value is changed. Exact vote margins and the missing annual laws remain open below.

## What the sources establish

### Provisional State Council

The original Law and Administration Ordinance, No. 1 of 1948, chapter 2 section 5, says that the Provisional Government's budget is fixed by an ordinance of the Provisional State Council. Section 7 identifies that council as the legislative authority. This is direct legal evidence of a budget-approval role, even though `provisional_state_council.budget_approval` currently reads `false` in the game. Whether that boolean represents legislative authority or a particular game mechanic needs a separate maintainer decision; this note changes neither.

Primary source: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/law/0_lsr_312670.pdf (sections 5 and 7, Hebrew text). An English transcription is available at https://palquest.org/en/historictext/16010/israel-law-and-administration-ordinance . The latter is a mirror, not the official publication.

The Provisional State Council's sitting 23, 14 October 1948, has a contents entry for approval of the July-December 1948 budget and its ordinance (printed pages 7-43): https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/Plenum/0_ptm_249991.pdf . The contents list item B as the end of the debate on the six-month state budget and its approval (pages 7-42) and item C as approval of the budget ordinance (pages 42-43). On printed page 42 the chair, Yosef Sprinzak, puts the whole budget, as proposed by the Finance Committee, to a single vote: "נגד התקציב — 3, נמנע — 1, בעד התקציב — 28" (against 3, abstaining 1, for 28). Just before, Berl Repetur's amendment was rejected 2 for, 15 against, 10 abstaining. The council then moved on to the budget ordinance (July-December 1948) itself. Checked against the page image in review, 11 October 2026. Kaplan's contemporaneous speech, reproduced at https://benyehuda.org/read/34720 , describes the July-December 1948 budget as current revenues/expenditures plus a separate war budget. Do not conflate this six-month proposal with a later full fiscal-year law.

Knesset's own budget-history page says the first orderly budget proposal was for January-March 1949 under the Provisional State Council, followed by the first full-year proposal for 1949/50. It also notes frequent supplementary budgets in the first decade. This is an official retrospective, not the January-March ordinance itself: https://main.knesset.gov.il/About/Pages/Budget/BudgetHistory.aspx . Do not infer a vote date or tally from it, or turn "first orderly proposal" into "first budget ever": the 1948 primary record above is earlier.

A separate election appropriation demonstrates an actual council budget ordinance: the Constituent Assembly Elections Budget Ordinance, No. 46 of 1949, authorises 120,000 pounds and divides it in a schedule. It is dated 20 January 1949. It is an election-specific appropriation, not the general January-March budget: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/law/0_lsr_312736.PDF .

### First Knesset

The Transition Law, 1949, sections 1-2, calls the legislature the Knesset, the Constituent Assembly the First Knesset, and its legislation a law. Its publication footnote records adoption on 16 February 1949: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_208911.pdf .

Finance Minister Eliezer Kaplan's 1949/50 budget speech explicitly introduces the first whole-year budget and asks the Knesset to give the Finance Committee authority to approve the defence budget. This is a primary speech reproduced in his collected writings, not a roll-call or enacted-law text: https://benyehuda.org/read/34722 .

Official errata independently corroborate that the 1949/50 Budget Law was published in *Sefer HaHukim* 23 on 9 September 1949, citing corrections to pages 209, 215 and 216: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_ec_317740.pdf . The errata establish publication and the law's existence, not its full fiscal-date clause, passage date or vote tally. The full annual law remains blocked by source access. No corrected appropriation amount is imported into game data. Readable official text checked 11 October 2026; scan pixels were not available here.

The First Knesset's sitting 159 record shows the 1950/51 budget in second and third readings, with objections to ministry allocations voted on and rejected and committee-text sections adopted. The portion read includes Welfare, Labour and Immigration. The same PDF ends with the summaries of sittings 156-159. Sitting 159 (14 Tammuz 5710, 29 June 1950) records that the expenditure budget and then the 1950/51 Budget Law itself were adopted in third reading in the Finance Committee's wording. The summary gives no tally, so the final whole-budget margin is still open: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/Plenum/1_ptm_250044.pdf . (Summary page checked against the page image in review, 11 October 2026.) Rejected opposition amendments are not a failed government budget.

### The 1951/52 annual law

The 1951/52 Budget Law, section 1, defines 1 April 1951-31 March 1952 and includes amounts already authorised by April-September and October-December 1951 interim laws. Its footnote records adoption on 31 December 1951, under the Second Knesset. Section 4 gives the Finance Committee a defence-budget role on the government's proposal; section 5 distinguishes transfers needing committee approval from lower-level transfers. Source: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_595048.pdf . The annual law's existence and late adoption are verified from text; its passage margin is not.

### Second Knesset and the fiscal year

The 1953/54 Expenditure Budget Law section 1 explicitly defines 1 April 1953-31 March 1954. It incorporates expenditure already authorised by April-June and July-August interim budget laws. Its footnote records adoption on 18 August 1953. Sections 3-4 give the Finance Committee roles in defence allocations and specified transfers, rather than treating every budget change as a new full-plenum budget vote: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_560568.pdf .

The 1955/56 Budget Law section 1 explicitly defines 1 April 1955-31 March 1956. Section 4 gives defence-budget allocation to the Finance Committee on the government's proposal. Its footnote records adoption on 31 March 1955, the day before the fiscal year began: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_560767.pdf . Together with the 1951/52 law above, these laws prove April-March for three sampled years. The other annual laws must be read before treating the pattern as verified for every year in the slice.

Second Knesset sitting 218, 26 March 1953, records the Finance Committee chair, Israel Guri, presenting the 1952/53 supplementary budget (Budget Law (No. 2) for 1952/53, IL 4,465,000) in second/third readings, opposition amendments being rejected and the law with its schedule being adopted. The same sitting handles an April-June 1953 interim budget. This confirms supplementary/interim legislation was used; it does not prove a close final tally: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/Plenum/2_ptm_250434.pdf .

## Retrieval limits

The official PDF text was read through a readable fetch. Direct PDF downloads returned an HTML response in this environment, so original scanned-page pixels were not inspected. In review (11 October 2026) the PDFs downloaded directly; the scan images were checked for the 1948 tally (sitting 23, printed page 42), the 1950/51 sitting summary, the Transition Law's adoption footnote, Ordinance No. 46 and section 1 of the 1953/54 and 1955/56 laws. The Knesset budget-history page and the laws-as-enacted index below would not load from outside Israel, so they were not re-checked. OCR can corrupt digits, Hebrew years and margins. Before asserting an exact vote count, legal amount or adoption date as game data, inspect the source scan and corroborate it with another record.
