# War mode research: the 1948 war, 15 May 1948 to the 1949 armistices

Researched 2026-10-13. All URLs were checked on that date. Draft for issue #103 (1948 half). Feeds GDD Phase 5 (#385): the dated truce clock (D-C4), the Arab fronts (D-G1) and the prisoner return (impact plan section 4.3). Starting positions remain with #226 to #233.

## Purpose

Collect the dated, sourced facts that war mode needs for the 1948 half of the war: Israeli forces by period, fronts, main operations with objectives, truces, outside pressure and the four armistices. Arab forces are covered at order-of-battle level only (which army fought on which front and when it entered). Full Arab force modelling belongs to issues #226 to #233.

This note is research, not a design. It adds no event text and changes no content file.

## Conventions

- **[V]** verified on a page opened on 2026-10-13, with the URL.
- **[S]** single source, a Wikipedia-only fact (tier 3), a snippet, or a page opened on an earlier date (carried from `research_notes/M3 content 1950-51/facts-1948.md`, opened 2026-10-10).
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is listed.
- **[GAP]** not found.
- One value per fact is used in the tables. Conflicts are recorded in the text next to it.
- Scope, following D4(e) of the paused M4 plan: this note gives operations as date, units and military objective only, and leaves out locality-level detail. This is the note's research scope, not a game rule. Settlement control is GDD Phase 6 and has its own sensitivity review. Where an operation is mainly known for the fate of civilians, the entry says "sensitive: see docs/m3-release.md" and stops there. Locality-level detail from the Yoav and Hiram pages is left out on purpose.
- Casualty and strength figures are given only as a source gives them, with the version named. No quotation is reconstructed. Quoted wording below was read on the cited page.
- JTA bulletins are dated one day after dispatch. None is used in this note.
- Framing follows `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` section 1.

## 1. Israeli forces by period

**Before 15 May and the IDF order**
- The Haganah was the main force. It had nine brigades at independence, later twelve. [S] en.wiki 1948 war, section on Israeli forces.
- About 30,000 Palestinian Jews had served in British forces in the Second World War. Mahal volunteers served. [S]
- Arms at 15 May [C]. Karsh: about one weapon per three fighters before the Czech shipments. Collins and Lapierre: 20,000 rifles and Stens for 35,000 on paper by April 1948. Morris: about 60 percent of Haganah troops armed on 15 May. Khalidi: "plentiful". Present all four if a figure is ever shown.
- Czech arms came through Operation Balak. Spitfires came via Yugoslavia after the second truce. 32 Shermans were bought in Italy and arrived from November 1948 to January 1949. [S]

**15 May to the first truce (11 Jun)**
- IDF order signed 26 May 1948 (MoD/IDF Archives, opened 2026-10-10) or 28 May (JVL) [C]. Both agree on publication in Official Gazette No. 3 of 31 May. [S] carried from facts-1948.
- Irgun agreement to fold into the IDF: 1 June 1948 [S]. Jerusalem excluded.
- Brigades with a role read in the sources: Harel (Mount Zion, 17 to 18 May), Givati (Pleshet), Golani and Oded (Sea of Galilee and Jordan valley), Alexandroni (Geulim, Qaqun). The 7th Armoured was formed 16 May and the 8th Armoured 24 May, as in `content/map/units_1948.yaml`. [S]
- IAF: the First Fighter Squadron flew its first mission on 29 May. The first aerial victory was on 3 June. [V] JVL IAF page (source given: IDF).

**First truce (11 Jun to 8 or 9 Jul) and second truce (18 Jul to 15 Oct)**
- The IDF reorganised and trained, and materiel arrived, especially from Czechoslovakia. [S] JVL/IDF page opened 2026-10-10.
- Altalena, 19 to 23 Jun: the Irgun's independent units were dispersed after it. After the Bernadotte killing the remaining Jerusalem Irgun and Lehi units were broken up. [S]
- By July 1948 an air force, a navy and a tank battalion existed. [S]
- Three B-17s reached Israel on 15 July and struck targets on the way. [S] en.wiki 1948 war.

**Third phase (Oct 1948 to Mar 1949)**
- IDF at the end of 1948: 88,033 soldiers, of whom 60,000 combat (Morgan, via en.wiki). [C] The same page's strength table (Bregman 2002, p. 24, citing Ben-Gurion's diary) gives 88,033 on 7 Oct and 108,300 on 30 Dec 1948.
- IDF strength by date, same table [S]: initial 29,677; 4 Jun 40,825; 17 Jul 63,586; 7 Oct 88,033; 28 Oct 92,275; 2 Dec 106,900; 23 Dec 107,652; 30 Dec 108,300.
- An average of 10,300 immigrants arrived per month. [S]
- Oct 1948: Yoav used the Negev, Givati and Yiftach brigades, an 8th Armoured battalion and the largest artillery formation to date. [S]
- Dec 1948: Horev used five brigades: 8th Armoured, Negev, Golani, Harel, Alexandroni. [S]
- Golani moved to the southern front in December 1948 (`units_1948.yaml`, Wikipedia-sourced). [S]

## 2. Fronts and sectors

All entries [S] en.wiki 1948 war unless stated.

- **South (Negev, coast, Hebron hills).** Egypt's force was the largest. Its task force crossed on 15 May. Nirim and Kfar Darom were attacked 15 May. Beersheba was occupied and the Egyptians advanced to the Hebron hills by 19 May. Yad Mordechai was attacked 19 May and held about five days. The Egyptians reached Majdal on 24 May and stopped on 28 May at a destroyed bridge north of Isdud [S] en.wiki. The JVL IAF page says the Egyptian column halted at a bombed-out bridge near Ashdod, later called Gesher Ad Halom, and dates the first fighter mission against it to 29 May [V]. Negba was attacked 2 Jun and Nitzanim fell about 7 Jun (MoD exhibit index snippet, [S]). Israeli units: Givati, Negev, Yiftach.
- **Jerusalem.** The Legion was ordered into Jerusalem on 17 May and entered on 19 May. Harel attacked Mount Zion and Zion Gate on 17 to 18 May. Ramat Rachel was held by Israel after fighting on 24 to 26 May. The Jewish Quarter fell on 28 May per en.wiki (MFA says 29 May, [C] by one day; the MFA is [V] and is the value used). Israeli units: Harel (Mount Zion); Etzioni is the Jerusalem brigade in the table.
- **Latrun and the Jerusalem corridor.** The Legion held Latrun from 18 May. Israeli attacks ran from 24 May to 18 Jul. The Burma Road bypass opened 10 Jun. Israeli units: Yiftach in the 18 Jul attempt; others not read. [GAP]
- **Northern Samaria, Jenin and the Triangle.** An Iraqi brigade group crossed the Jordan on 15 May and attacked Gesher 15 to 16 May. Golani held Belvoir. Golani took the Gilboa area 28 to 30 May and attacked Jenin from 31 May to 4 Jun. Alexandroni held the Sharon side. The Iraqis later left the Kafr Qasim sector.
- **Sea of Galilee and Jordan valley.** The Syrians entered 15 May. Degania was attacked 20 to 21 May. Mishmar HaYarden was attacked 6 Jun and overrun 10 Jun, and the Oded Brigade then stopped the Syrians. Israeli units at first: Golani's 12th Battalion, a Yiftach company and local militia.
- **Galilee and the Lebanese border.** The Arab Liberation Army fought in the Galilee. Dekel (Jul) and Hiram (Oct) were directed against it. Lebanon fielded a token force (section 7). Israeli units: Carmeli, 7th Armoured, Golani, Oded.
- **Sinai edge and Eilat (Dec to Mar).** Horev carried the Negev Brigade into Sinai on 28 Dec 1948. Uvda reached Umm Rashrash and Eilat on 10 Mar 1949.

The Negev Brigade crossed into Sinai on the night of 28 Dec and reached the El-Arish airfield outskirts by 30 Dec. A Rafah attack began 3 Jan. Egypt said it would negotiate on 6 Jan. Israel accepted the cease-fire on 7 Jan.

Alternatives actually raised and not taken [S]: after Yoav, Allon asked to attack the Hebron hills and the Jericho valley, and Ben-Gurion refused, fearing British involvement. After Hiram, Ben-Gurion did not authorise a further advance into Lebanon. After the Horev halt, Allon's forces could have cut the Egyptians off from El-Arish (Avnery commentary, [S]).

## 5. Outside pressure

**UN and the Security Council** (resolution details [S] on Wikipedia resolution pages unless marked)
- Res 50, 29 May 1948 (S/801): four-week cease-fire. It barred bringing fighting men or war material into Palestine and the Arab states. This is the arms embargo. Chapter VII was raised in case of violation. Adopted in parts.
- Res 54, 15 Jul (S/902): Article 39 finding, cease-fire within three days, Jerusalem cease-fire. Vote 7 for, 1 against (Syria), 3 abstentions.
- Res 56, 19 Aug (S/983, in parts). Res 59, 19 or 20 Oct [C] (S/1045, Bernadotte and Serot investigation). Res 61, 4 Nov (S/1070, 9-1-1: withdraw to 14 Oct positions, neutral zones). Res 66, 29 Dec (S/1169, 8-0-3).
- Res 62, 16 Nov 1948 (S/1080): armistice. [V] cited in all four Avalon armistice preambles.
- Res 73, 11 Aug 1949 (S/1376 II): Bunche relieved, UNTSO continues. [S]
- UN admission, 11 May 1949. [V] MFA.

**Mediator**
- Bernadotte appointed 20 May 1948. First proposals 28 Jun [S]: Galilee to Israel, Negev to the Arabs, Jerusalem under UN authority. Israel rejected them but agreed to extend the truce, and the Arab states rejected both. Shertok's reply date: [GAP].
- Second plan completed 16 Sep [S]. Bernadotte killed 17 Sep, Bunche succeeds. Plan published 20 Sep. [V] MFA.
- Bunche chaired the Rhodes talks from 13 Jan 1949 [V] MFA. The Acting Mediator or his deputy was present at all four armistice signings. [V] Avalon.

**British ultimatum**
- Ben-Gurion told McDonald that Britain "threatens to take action against us under 1936 Treaty with Egypt and unless we obey the decisions of the SC". He added that Israeli forces which had crossed into Egypt "have already received orders to return to the Negev frontier". [V] FRUS 1949 v6 d341, McDonald to the Secretary of State, Tel Aviv, 1 Jan 1949, 9 a.m.
- The MFA says the Sinai fighting ended 7 Jan "following British ultimatum and US pressure". [V] MFA.
- Date and wording of the British note: [GAP].
- Britain blocked aviation fuel shipments in Jan 1949. [S] en.wiki. British reinforcements moved to Aqaba. [S] en.wiki Horev.

**RAF aircraft, 7 Jan 1949**
- The UK statement of 8 Jan 1949 says five British aircraft were shot down over Egyptian territory on 7 Jan. Four Spitfires on reconnaissance failed to return in the morning. A few hours later a further force, sent to find them, had one Tempest reported missing. The leader of the Spitfires reported being attacked 15 miles inside Egyptian territory. [V] Hansard, HC Deb 11 May 1949, cc1822 to 1824 https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1949/may/11/israel-attacked-british-aircraft
- A Royal Air Force Court of Inquiry reported that the Israeli fighters were Spitfires with British-type camouflage and red spinners like No. 208 Squadron's (Bevin, same Hansard page). Britain reserved its right to compensation and asked Bunche for a report. [V]
- JVL (source given: IDF): five RAF planes in two dogfights on 7 Jan during Horev. [V] https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Society_&_Culture/48iaf.html
- Date [C] resolved: en.wiki Horev says 6 Jan; Hansard and JVL both say 7 Jan. Value used: 7 Jan.
- Pilots [C]: two killed and two captured (en.wiki Horev, 1949 in Israel). RAF Jever 4 Sqn story snippet describes three 208 Squadron aircraft hit with two pilots baling out and one killed. [S] only.
- Israeli withdrawal wrecks: Ben-Gurion ordered the wrecks moved into Israeli territory. [S] en.wiki.

**US pressure**
- The State Department message of 30 Dec 1948 was read to Ben-Gurion by McDonald at Tiberias on the night of 31 Dec to 1 Jan 1949, for two hours, with Knox present. McDonald "returned from Tiberias 3:15 a.m."; the telegram is dated 1 Jan, 9 a.m. [V] FRUS d341.
- Withdrawal by 2 Jan 1949 per FRUS 1949 v6 d371 summary. [S]
- Ben-Gurion told McDonald that Israel was negotiating an armistice with Transjordan (next meeting 5 Jan) and that talks with Lebanon were progressing. [V] d341.
