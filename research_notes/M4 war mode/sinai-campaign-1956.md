# Sinai Campaign (Operation Kadesh), 1956: forces, fronts and operations for war mode

Researched 2026-10-11 and re-checked 2026-10-15. Draft for issue #103 (1956 half). This note is research, not a design. It adds no event text and changes no content file.

**Purpose.** Military data on the Sinai Campaign (29 October to early November 1956) for war mode (#21). Military side only: fronts, forces, operations, the ceasefire clock, outside pressure, withdrawal, casualties. It reuses the dates of `road-to-suez-1956.md` and does not repeat the political lead-up.

**Conventions.**
- [V] read on a page opened on 2026-10-15 and given by an official, primary or UN source (URL given).
- [S] single source, a secondary or tertiary page only (JVL, Wikipedia), an unopened snippet, or a page read on 2026-10-11 that could not be re-opened on 2026-10-15.
- [C] sources conflict; every version is given. [GAP] not found.
- A secondary source (JVL, Wikipedia) never makes a fact [V], even where it agrees with an official page. No figure, unit or name below was filled from memory.

**Source caveat.**
- gov.il "Selected Documents" (1947 to 1974, section IX) are Israeli government texts. They give Israeli claims, so they are [V] for what Israel said, not for the facts themselves.
- Wikipedia "Operation Kadesh" was fetched once. It is [S] throughout; its operational text cites Varble 2003 (Osprey), not opened here.
- The IDF Archive exhibition (MoD) exposes only Hebrew photo captions on its documents page. Those captions are read and used; the images were not.
- gov.il doc 15 (Ben-Gurion to senior officers) is titled 29 November 1956, shows "29.10.1956" in its metadata, and says "7 November 1949" in its text. Treated as an obvious typo for 1956.
- A hasbara.co.il page was opened and rejected: it names 395 paratroopers "under Ariel Sharon" and "eight days", which conflicts with the MoD page. Not used.
- The gov.il chronology (CHRON) returned HTTP 403 on 2026-10-15 and a Wayback copy did not load. Rows that rest only on it are tagged [S].
- Not reached: the US Army CSI study on Abu Ageila (access denied), idf.il mini-site, JVL war-map pages (images).

## 2. Fronts and sectors

- MoD names three axes: north (Gaza to El Qantara); centre (Qusayma, Abu Ageila, Ismailia); south (Qusayma, Mitla, Port Tawfiq). A further force went south toward Sharm el-Sheikh. [V] MOD
- Aims (28 Oct): threaten the Suez Canal from nearby objectives; take the Tiran Straits; upset and destroy the Egyptian presence in northern Sinai. [V] MOD
- gov.il calls the Mitla drop "a political rather than tactical or strategic objective"; the central and southern advances were also diversionary. [V] GOV1
- Ben-Gurion (7 Nov): forces had strict orders not to cross the Suez Canal or attack Egypt proper; the operation covered "the area from northern Sinai to the tip of the Red Sea". [V] GOV8
- Sectors for the game: (1) Gaza Strip and Rafah, (2) northern Sinai coast (El Arish), (3) central axis (Abu Ageila/Umm Katef), (4) Mitla and the Heitan defile, (5) Gulf of Aqaba and Sharm el-Sheikh/Tiran, (6) Israeli home front and other borders (Syria, where reserve battalions were mobilised [V] GOV8; Jordan, where Wikipedia says Israeli soldiers were stationed along the frontier [S] WIKI)

## 3. Israeli forces

- Whole divisions deployed for the first time; French aircraft, tanks and equipment bought in 1955 to 1956. [V] MOD
- Mobilisation: over 100,000 in under 72 hours; Air Force fully operational within 43 hours. [S] JVL (Bard 2008)
- Command: Ben-Gurion PM and Defence Minister; Dayan Chief of Staff; Peres MoD Director General. [V] MOD, MODDOCS
- Ben-Gurion: reserve battalions went to the Syrian border, a larger reserve force "especially of armour" to the southern border. [V] GOV8
- Armour was "queen of the battlefield"; the Air Force was still deficient. A reserve brigade on requisitioned civilian buses took Sharm. [V] GOV1
- Named formations (all [S] WIKI, Varble 2003):

| Formation | Commander as named | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Paratroop Brigade (202nd) | Col Ariel Sharon | link-up, Themed, Nakhl, Heitan |
| Battalion 890 (para) | Lt-Col Rafael Eitan | Mitla drop. [V] MOD for Bn 890 and Eitan |
| 9th Infantry Brigade | Col Abraham Yoffe | Ras al-Naqb, then Sharm el-Sheikh |
| 4th Infantry Brigade | Col Josef Harpaz | Qusayma, then Abu Ageila |
| 7th Armoured Brigade | not named in the text read; Maj Izhak Ben-Ari led a probe, Col Avraham Adan led the al-Dayyiqa force | Umm Katef, Abu Ageila, Ruafa |
| 10th Infantry Brigade (mostly reservists) | Col Shmuel Golinda, replaced by Col Israel Tal | Umm Katef assaults |
| 37th Armoured Brigade | not named | Umm Katef, Khan Yunis |
| 1st Infantry Brigade | Col Binyamin Gibli | Rafah |
| 27th Armoured Brigade (AMX-13) | Col Haim Bar-Lev | Rafah, El Arish |

- Equipment named [S] WIKI: DC-3 transports, P-51 Mustang, Meteor, Mystere IV, AMX-13 and Sherman tanks. Per-unit strength: [GAP].
- Navy: destroyers took Ibrahim el-Awal (31 Oct). Navy support to the Sharm advance is marked "citation needed" on Wikipedia, so [GAP].

## 4. Egyptian forces

- Ben-Gurion (7 Nov): about three divisions and a number of other units in Sinai; over 30,000 men; over two brigades arrived as reinforcements during the fighting; "hundreds" of Czech and British tanks; Vampire, Meteor and MiG jets; the navy also fought. An Israeli claim. [V] GOV8
- Ben-Gurion (8 Nov) names the 3rd Egyptian Division commander in Sinai as Maj-Gen Ahmed Salem, citing an order of 25 Feb 1956. [V] GOV7

## 5. Operations and objectives

- **Mitla.** Bn 890 under Eitan dropped on the evening of 29 Oct at the eastern approaches. [V] MOD. Wikipedia adds a navigation error of three miles, about 400 men, Mustangs cutting telephone lines, and the Sharon column linking up near Nakhl on 30 Oct. [S]. Objective: political, a pretext and a communications threat. [V] GOV1
- **Kuntilla and Ras al-Naqb.** Taken the first night. [V] GOV8. Wikipedia says Ras al-Naqb fell "as the paratroopers were dropped" and elsewhere "on 30 October". [C] on timing within 29/30 Oct.
- **Qusayma.** 45 minutes of resistance (Ben-Gurion). [V] GOV8. Night attack by pincers, 03:00 to sunrise. [S] WIKI. Starting point for Abu Ageila.
- **Abu Ageila and Umm Katef.** Objective: the central road hub. Egyptians held the "Hedgehog" ridges. 30 Oct armour probe at Umm Katef failed; 31 Oct dawn attack through the al-Dayyiqa gap took Abu Ageila; Ruafa fell that night at the cost of every tank involved; 10th Brigade attacks on Umm Katef failed on 31 Oct and 1 Nov; Egyptians withdrew on the evening of 1 Nov. [S] WIKI
- **Heitan (Mitla).** Afternoon of 31 Oct. 260 Egyptian and 38 Israeli killed. Sharon was criticised for acting without authorisation. [S] WIKI
- **Rafah.** Objective: cut Gaza off from Sinai and open the road to El Arish. Crossroads 12 taken after sappers cleared minefields; French cruiser Georges Leygues gave fire support; the garrison left on the morning of 1 Nov. [S] WIKI. Sensitive: the killings in Khan Yunis and Rafah during Kadesh; see `road-to-suez-1956.md` section 6 (D8 rule 4). Not described here.
- **El Arish.** 27th Armoured Brigade; Jeradi pass fought; the town fell on 2 Nov without a fight. [S] WIKI
- **Gaza City and Khan Yunis.** Military action: defences outside Gaza City attacked late 1 Nov, al-Muntar fort taken with 3,500 National Guard troops killed or captured (Wikipedia), no organised opposition by noon 2 Nov; 37th Armoured broke the Khan Yunis lines on 3 Nov; the Strip largely held by noon 3 Nov. [S] WIKI. Sensitive: the killings in Khan Yunis and Rafah during Kadesh; see `road-to-suez-1956.md` section 6 (D8 rule 4). Not described here.
- **Sharm el-Sheikh and Tiran.** 9th Brigade left Ras al-Naqb on 2 Nov, logistics and vehicle breakdown the main obstacle; paratroops sent to Tor to outflank; night attack near midnight 4 Nov repulsed after four hours; artillery and napalm on the morning of 5 Nov; surrender 09:30. Losses: 10 Israeli killed, about 100 Egyptian killed, 864 prisoners. [S] WIKI. Tiran: Egyptians had garrisoned the island. [V] GOV8
- **Egyptian withdrawal order.** Nasser ordered a fall back toward the canal; by the morning of 1 Nov the order was known at Rafah. [S] WIKI. Exact date and hour of the order: [GAP].

## 6. Air and naval actions

- Mustangs hit Egyptian positions from about 15:00 on 29 Oct; Egyptian MiG-15s escorted Vampires and Meteors; 7 to 9 Egyptian jets downed for one Israeli loss; 31 Oct strikes on the 1st Armoured Brigade; Egyptians withdrew aircraft south after the Anglo-French attack began. [S] WIKI
- 3 Nov: Israeli Mysteres attacked HMS Crane off the Gulf of Aqaba, mistaken for an Egyptian ship; three crew wounded. [S] WIKI
- Ben-Gurion: Egypt "brought in its air force" at the start and "fierce battles developed". [V] GOV8
- Naval: Ibrahim el-Awal bombarded Haifa on 31 Oct and surrendered to Israeli destroyers; it joined the Israeli Navy as INS Haifa. [S] WIKI; the 31 Oct surrender [V] MODDOCS
- Sorties, losses and aircraft numbers by type: [GAP].

## 7. Anglo-French timeline as it affected Israel

- 30 Oct ultimatum (hour [C], see Suez note). Israeli columns halted short of the canal under the plan. [S] JVL
- 30 Oct Security Council text vetoed by the UK and France; 31 Oct Anglo-French bombing of Egyptian airfields. [S] JVL
- 31 Oct to 1 Nov: Israeli and French aircraft attacked the 1st Armoured Brigade together; French warships supported Rafah. [S] WIKI
- 5 Nov landings near Port Said; 6 Nov ceasefire, effective midnight GMT. [S] JVL; [V] FRUS d525, note 2
- Effect: once Britain and France stopped, Israel stood alone against the UN, US and USSR.

## 8. Ceasefire and truce clock

- 2 Nov: Res 997 calls for a ceasefire and withdrawal. 3 Nov: Israel tells the Secretary-General it complied; "the cease-fire has in fact been maintained until this moment" (8 Nov). [V] GOV12
- 4 Nov: Meir asks five questions (Egyptian agreement, state of war, direct talks, boycott and blockade, fedayeen abroad); says Egyptian troops "are continuing to fire". [V] GOV5
- 7 Nov: Ben-Gurion declares the armistice with Egypt "dead", refuses any foreign force on territory Israel holds. 8 Nov: the cabinet agrees to accept the UN resolution and withdraw "after suitable arrangements had been made with the international Force" (GOV12); Ben-Gurion's reply to Eisenhower says "upon conclusion of satisfactory arrangements" with the UN for the Force (GOV9). [V] GOV8, GOV12, GOV9
- Effective Israeli ceasefire date: [C]. 3 Nov notification (GOV12); "ceased fire... for several days past no armed conflict" (8 Nov letter, GOV7); fighting over 5 Nov (GOV2); "two days after the fighting ceased" at the 7 Nov speech (GOV8 editor); "the fighting ended on 7 November" (GOV15); Sharm surrender 5 Nov 09:30 [S]. Hour of effect: [GAP].
- Suez note gives Israeli acceptance as 6 to 8 Nov [S]. This note finds 3 Nov (notice of compliance) and 8 Nov (cabinet acceptance of withdrawal); the Suez note should be read with both dates.

## 9. Outside pressure

- **US.** Eisenhower's 7 Nov letter: US "deep concern" if Israel would not withdraw; warns of condemnation as a violator; reached Israel 8 Nov after a transmission breakdown. Ben-Gurion's 8 Nov reply: "never planned to annex the Sinai desert". [V] GOV9. JVL adds a threat to end US aid and of sanctions and expulsion from the UN. [S] JVL
- **USSR.** Bulganin's notes of 5 Nov to Britain, France and Israel; the Israeli note says Israel's existence is in question and that the Soviet ambassador was recalled. Ben-Gurion replied 8 Nov. [V] GOV7. The US estimate of 6 Nov: threats "imprecise", Soviet attack on the UK or France "almost certainly" not coming. [V] FRUS d521. Bulganin also wrote to Eisenhower on 5 Nov (d505, title only). [S] FRUS index listing, page not opened
- **UN.** Res 997 to 1003 and the UNEF, advisory committee of seven states (Brazil, Canada, Ceylon, Colombia, India, Norway, Pakistan); Maj-Gen E. L. M. Burns as chief. Res 1002 (7 Nov) repeats the call to withdraw behind the 24 Feb 1949 armistice lines. [V] UN
- **Britain.** JVL (Bard) says Britain stopped because of the Soviet threats and a US decision to make a $1 billion IMF loan contingent on a ceasefire. [S] JVL. Not checked in FRUS: [GAP].
- **Israel's own line.** 29 Nov to senior officers: the "political imperative" is to "put off the end of the withdrawal as long as possible". [V] GOV15

## 10. Withdrawal timeline (Dec 1956 to Mar 1957)

21 Dec 1956 British and French withdrawal complete; 24 Dec Israeli withdrawal begins; 15 Jan El Arish; 22 Jan Israel back to the border but holding Sharm and Gaza; Feb US pressure and GA sanctions debate; 28 Feb US-Israel understanding; 1 Mar Meir announces withdrawal; 10 Mar IDF at the armistice lines; 11 Mar Egypt reappoints a military governor of Gaza. [S] CHRON (read 11 Oct, HTTP 403 on 15 Oct); same dates as the Suez note. UNEF arrival dates: [GAP].

## 11. Casualties and losses

- Israeli dead: about 150 (Ben-Gurion, 7 Nov) [V] GOV8; 231 (gov.il) [V] GOV1; JVL gives the same [S]. [C] 150 early figure against 231 final figure.
- Battle figures [S] WIKI: Heitan 38 Israeli and 260 Egyptian killed; Sharm 10 Israeli killed, about 100 Egyptian killed, 864 captured; Egyptian jets lost 7 to 9, Israeli 1.
- Civilian deaths in the Gaza Strip are not in these figures; see the D8 pointer in section 12.
- Egyptian total dead, wounded and captured; Israeli wounded; tank and aircraft losses: [GAP].

## 13. What war mode (#21) needs from this note

Available:
- Fronts: six sectors (section 2), three MoD axes, three stated aims.
- Units by side: nine named Israeli formations with commanders, seven Egyptian groupings (sections 3 and 4), [S] except Bn 890 and Eitan.
- Dated phases: night 29/30 Oct (southern border posts), 30 to 31 Oct (Abu Ageila, Mitla), 1 to 3 Nov (Rafah, El Arish, Gaza), 2 to 5 Nov (Sharm).
- Ceasefire clock: Anglo-French stop at midnight GMT 6/7 Nov; Israeli acceptance steps 3, 7 and 8 Nov.
- Outside-pressure events: Res 997 (2 Nov), 998 and 999 (4 Nov), 1000 (5 Nov), Soviet notes (5 Nov), Eisenhower letter (7 Nov), Res 1001 and 1002 (7 Nov), cabinet decision (8 Nov).
