## Question and result

Refs #1871. The issue proposes al-Malikiyya from 6 June 1948 as the anchor of the Lebanese army's map-only token (Dupuy p. 47;
Morris p. 260 still to be checked) and asks for a named frontier place for 15 May 1948, and whether and when al-Malikiyya fell
in Operation Hiram. The row under discussion is `lebanese_army` in `content/map/units_1948.yaml`.

**Short answer.**

- **6 June at al-Malikiyya: Dupuy p. 47 says it.** Dupuy has the combined Lebanese, Arab Liberation Army (ALA) and Syrian attack
  on 6 June, with the Lebanese capturing the town "for the second time" by evening. Morris p. 260 could not be opened [GAP]. Three
  JTA items name the village in June and none says Lebanese army: a Syrian attack on "the Jewish-held Arab village of Malakieh"
  (7 June bulletin, dateline 6 June), "the Arabs" opening "an offensive from Lebanon" that forced the Israelis out of it
  (8 June bulletin, dateline 7 June), and "Arab units" in a Syrian context (9 June bulletin, dateline 8 June): [C].
- **15 May: no source read here names a place the Lebanese army held on 15 May itself; the JTA names the al-Malikiyya area for
  16 May.** Dupuy p. 45 has the Lebanese taking al-Malikiyya after a dawn counter-attack on 15 May (this is the account the
  owner's ruling on #226 set aside in favour of the ALA at al-Malikiyya). The JTA bulletin of 17 May says only that the
  Lebanese "penetrated the Israel border for a distance of approximately one mile" on 15 May, with no place. The JTA bulletin
  of 18 May (dateline "TEL AVIV, May 17") has "200 Lebanese soldiers" killed "yesterday" in the "Malakia area", which puts
  Lebanese soldiers there on 16 May (derived from the dateline). So al-Malikiyya on 15 May rests on Dupuy alone, in conflict
  with the owner's ruling [C]; the only place-neutral fact for 15 May is the frontier near it.
- **Operation Hiram: al-Malikiyya fell to the Israeli 7th Brigade after Sasa and Saliha.** Dupuy p. 104 gives the order but no
  calendar day for the capture itself. The operation was "launched at dusk on October 28" (p. 101); Sasa was taken "by
  midnight" after dark on 29 October and Tarshiha on 30 October (p. 103); the 7th Brigade then takes Saliha and then Malkiya,
  and "Thus at about dawn on the 31st, Operation Hiram drew to a close" (p. 104). So, derived from Dupuy's order (pp. 103-104),
  not printed: the village fell on 30 or 31 October, by about dawn on 31 October. No JTA bulletin of 27 October to 8 November
  names it (search below).

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Who held al-Malikiyya on 15 May.** Dupuy p. 45: the Lebanese, after a counter-attack from across the border. The owner's ruling
   on #226 (ruling 2; dated 11 October 2026 in `research_notes/GDD phase 5/arab-forces-1948.md`, section 5) reads word for
   word (issue #226 on GitHub, read 28 October 2026): "al-Malikiyya, 15 May: the newer research applies (the ALA on 15 May; the Lebanese 3rd Battalion from 6 June). The
   card notes Lorch's account." The newer research is he.wiki and the official Lebanese account, which this box has not read.
   The JTA of 17 May has the Lebanese a mile inside the border with no place; the JTA of 18 May has 200 Lebanese soldiers
   killed in the "Malakia area" on 16 May. Nothing read here settles who held the village on 15 May.
2. **6 June or 7 June for the second fall, and who took it.** Dupuy p. 47: the Lebanese took it on the evening of 6 June. JTA
   7 June (dateline 6 June): Syrian units attacked "the Jewish-held Arab village of Malakieh", with no outcome and no day for
   the attack. JTA 8 June (dateline 7 June): "the Arabs today opened an offensive from Lebanon forcing Israeli forces out of the
   village of Malakieh". JTA 9 June (dateline 8 June): Jews "ousted" on 7 June, by Arab units in a Syrian context. All kept; the
   difference is one day and the attribution (Lebanese, Syrian, or "the Arabs" from Lebanon).
3. **Dupuy's Lebanese 15 May account against its own 28-29 May account.** Dupuy p. 46 has the Lebanese "motionless for a week"
   and Malkiya retaken by Yiftach on 28-29 May. The order of control below is derived from Dupuy pp. 45-47 and 104, not
   printed as one list: Israeli (Palmach, night of 14-15 May, p. 45), Lebanese (15 May), Israeli (28-29 May), Lebanese (6
   June), Israeli (Hiram). That is five captures, which lines up with en.wiki's "no fewer than five times" (citing Khalidi)
   if each capture is counted as a change of hands.

## What the issue's question needs, as facts

- **A frontier place on 15 May that no source read here contradicts:** the border at al-Malikiyya and Kadesh (Dupuy p. 45),
  or "about one mile inside the border" on the northern front with no place (JTA 17 May); the CIA's "Lebanese troops are on the
  frontier" is for the week ending 26 May (`research_notes/GDD phase 5/arab-forces-1948.md`, section 5). For 16 May, the JTA of
  18 May puts Lebanese soldiers in the "Malakia area". Dupuy's plan line (p. 43) names Nakura
  (an-Naqoura) to Nahariya as the coastal axis, as a plan, not a position.
- **For a map-only token from 6 June:** Dupuy p. 47 (second capture on 6 June), JTA 7 June (attack, dateline 6 June), JTA 8 June
  (forced out, 7 June) and JTA 9 June (7 June, Syrian context).
- **Hiram:** Israeli capture after Sasa and Saliha; derived from Dupuy's order (pp. 103-104), not printed: on 30 or 31 October,
  by about dawn on 31 October. The exact day is not read.
