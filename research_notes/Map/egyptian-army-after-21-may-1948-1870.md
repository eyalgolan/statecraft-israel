## Question and result

Refs #1870. The issue asks for sourced positions and dates for the Egyptian force drawn as `egyptian_expeditionary_force` in
`content/map/units_1948.yaml`: (1) the inland column that crossed at al-Auja and where it stood; (2) the force between 21 May
and 11 June 1948 and during the first truce; (3) the force after 8 July 1948.

**Short answer.**

- **The inland column: al-Auja, then Beersheba, then Bethlehem.** Dupuy has a smaller force (under 2,000 men, two regular
  battalions and Moslem Brotherhood volunteers) at al-Auja on 14 May and crossing there early on 15 May (pp. 55-56). Its advance
  guard reached Beersheba on 17 May and its main body on 20 May; an advance party met the Arab Legion at Bethlehem on 20 May and
  took over the city's control on 21 May (p. 58). Dupuy's account of the volunteers on the same days (p. 63) is kept beside it [C]. For 11 June the only source read that places the inland column is an Arab claim, AP 13 June (the Egyptian line "southeastward to Hebron and Beersheba", "one spearhead to Artuf and another to Bethlehem") [C].
- **21 May to 11 June, the coastal force.** Dupuy p. 58: Majdal reached on 21 May (by reading order: the sentence says "on that day" after the 21 May Bethlehem sentence), a small column sent east through Iraq Suwaydan and Faluja towards Beit Jibrin, Ashdod entered
  on 29 May, the Ashdod bridge about 3 km north (the page has the column reaching the bridge; "dug in" is this note's reading), Nitzanim taken on 7 June. The JTA reports the same Isdud
  area: an Israeli communique has a column "cut off and surrounded", and "Military experts here", in the next sentence, give it as "reputed to be 3,500 strong", against Dupuy's 2,500 [C].
- **The first truce.** One truce line is in a source read: the Arab joint headquarters' own line for the Egyptians, AP, Amman, 13 June
  1948, an Arab claim [C] already tabulated in `research_notes/GDD phase 5/arab-forces-1948.md` section 1 ("Positions on 11
  June"). Dupuy p. 80 describes the force "when the first truce expired" (four brigades: Gaza with the coastal strip, Majdal for the Ashdod area, Faluja for the Majdal to
  Hebron axis, Beersheba to Bethlehem for the volunteers).
- **After 8 July.** Dupuy p. 81: the Egyptian offensive opened at 6:00 a.m. on 8 July; Negba attacked 12 July and Berot Yitzhak
  after it; by 18 July Israeli forces held a position just south of Faluja and the Hatta and Karatya villages (pp. 82-83). The
  JTA of 25 July (PDF p. 2) has the Majdal to Faluja supply route cut. Later: early October layout (pp. 91-92), Beersheba lost on 21 October
  (p. 98), Ashdod evacuated 27 October and Majdal on 5 November (p. 99).

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Bethlehem on 20 and 21 May.** Dupuy p. 58 has the inland column's advance party meeting the Arab Legion at Bethlehem on
   20 May and taking over the city's control on 21 May; Dupuy p. 63 has "mostly Azziz's Volunteers" reaching Bethlehem "in
   force" on 20 May and occupying Ramat Rachel on 21 May. Both rest on the same book; the second names the volunteers, the first
   the column. The two are not contradicted by anything read, but who held the city on which day is not settled [C]. `research_notes/GDD phase 5/arab-forces-1948.md` section 1 adds O'Ballance p. 93 (not opened again here): Bethlehem handed over by the Legion on the morning of 22 May.
2. **The column at Isdud (Ashdod).** Dupuy p. 58: "about 2,500 men" under Naguib, with the column at the Ashdod bridge north of the town ("dug in" is this note's reading); the Israeli attack
   of 2-3 June failed. JTA 6 June (dateline 4 June, an Israeli communique): a column "cut off and surrounded"; "Military experts here" give it as "reputed to be
   3,500 strong"; JTA 9 June (dateline 8 June): the Egyptians were trying "to free" it and were reported to have reinforced it
   from the sea. Numbers and the "surrounded" claim differ; neither is a position on the first-truce day [C].
3. **Isdud on 1 June.** The Israeli delegation spokesman's statement (JTA 2 June, dateline 1 June) puts "the coastal plain from the Lebanese
   border down to Isdud--the Arab border city under the partition plan" under full Israeli control. Dupuy p. 58 has the
   Egyptians entering Ashdod on 29 May and reaching the bridge just north of it ("dug in" is this note's reading). The spokesman's sentence is an Israeli claim (the item says only that a spokesman at Lake Success was answering Cairo and Amman communiques; agent read 2026-10-28; a person's check pending), and the two accounts are kept side by side without a ruling [C].
4. **Where the force's forward post stood at the first truce.** The yaml's `egyptian_expeditionary_force` rows say Isdud forward,
   Majdal rear (`content/map/units_1948.yaml`, the rows for 11 June to 8 July). Dupuy p. 80 puts the 2nd Brigade (HQ Majdal)
   responsible for "the Ashdod area" at the truce expiry; JTA 11 July has an Egyptian armoured force "driving from Isdud" on
   8 July; FRUS doc. 486 (ORE 38-48 of 27 July, describing 18 July) has "Egyptian-occupied area near Isdud". These agree on Isdud and Majdal; none of them
   is dated 11 June to 7 July. For those four weeks the only dated front-line list is the Arab claim of AP 13 June (Gaza, Majdal, Isdud, then southeast to Faluja and Beit Jibrin, Hebron and Beersheba, with spearheads to Artuf and Bethlehem), which agrees with the yaml's Isdud forward and Majdal rear [C]; the list is one side's claim. JTA 11 June (10 June) and JTA 27 June (25 June) put Egyptian lines at the "Ishdud-Yibneh pocket" and "in the vicinity of Ishdud"; Dupuy pp. 71-72 puts the contested Majdal-Hebron road and Huleiqat in the truce.
5. **When Majdal was reached.** Dupuy p. 58, "on that day" after 21 May, so 21 May by reading order; the repo's yaml note gives
   21 to 24 May. JTA 28 May (dateline 27 May) has Majdal already "a major Egyptian invasion base". `research_notes/M4 war mode/war-1948.md` line 120 (en.wiki) has the Egyptians reaching Majdal on 24 May and stopping on 28 May at a destroyed bridge north of Isdud, against Dupuy p. 58 entering Ashdod on 29 May. No source read dates the
   arrival to a single day [C].
6. **The Faluja line after 18 July.** Dupuy p. 83: the Israelis were "blocking the east-west communications between Majdal and
   Hebron" at the second truce. JTA 25 July (dateline 23 July): "The Egyptian supply route from Majdal to Faluja ... has been
   cut by the Jews". Dupuy p. 82 adds that the Israeli attacks on Huleiqat, Kaukaba and Beit Affa failed. The two accounts look
   compatible: they differ in the stretch named (Majdal to Hebron against Majdal to Faluja) and the day (18 July against 23 July), so they are not tagged [C].

## What the issue's question needs, as facts

- **The inland column's places, with the days a source gives:** al-Auja 14-15 May (Dupuy pp. 55-56); Beersheba 17 May (advance
  guard) and 20 May (main body) (p. 58); Bethlehem 20-21 May (pp. 58 and 63); Hebron to Bethlehem with the volunteers after
  that (p. 80, when the first truce expired); Beersheba still Egyptian until 21 October (p. 98).
- **21 May to 11 June, the coastal force:** Majdal from 21 May (Dupuy p. 58), Ashdod 29 May (p. 58), the Ashdod bridge about 3 km
  north (p. 58; "dug in" is this note's reading), Nitzanim 7 June (p. 59), a branch east through Iraq Suwaydan and Faluja (p. 58).
- **After 8 July:** the offensive of 8 July (Dupuy p. 81; JTA 11 July, dateline 9 July), Negba 12 July (p. 81), the line Gaza to
  Majdal to Faluja to Beit Jibrin to Hebron (p. 80), and the lines later held (pp. 91-92, 98-99). The only first-truce line opened is the Arab joint headquarters' claim (AP, 13 June); no Israeli or UN dated line is in any source opened.
