## Question and result

#2399 asks for a source that names the rifle and light machine gun per Arab force and date (Arab Legion, Egyptian Army, Syrian
Army, Iraqi Army, Lebanese Army, Arab Liberation Army (ALA)), so that a Lee-Enfield or Bren model, or no weapon, can be decided.

**Result (11 November pass): one contemporary US estimate names the rifle and light machine gun for Egypt, Iraq and Syria,
and counts the Legion's Brens; Lebanon stays open.** CIA, ORE 48-48, "Probable Effects on Israel and the Arab States of a UN
Arms Embargo" (published 5 August 1948, information as of 1 July 1948), Appendix C, pp. 17-19, page images viewed. It is one
intelligence estimate, not a unit return; its counts are the agency's estimates and no second source opened confirms them.

- **Egypt:** "Lee-Enfield .303 rifles of World War I and II vintage", Sten and Thompson submachine guns, "at least 500 Bren
  light machine guns", Lewis light machine guns (p. 17).
- **Arab Legion:** "10,795 rifles and carbines and 463 Bren light machine guns" (p. 18). The rifle is not typed there, and
  the Sten is not named for the Legion on that page.
- **Iraq:** "The basic infantry weapon is the Lee-Enfield .303 rifle"; a British order largely filled by April 1948 included
  1,400 Stens and 250 Brens; some 15,000 French rifles from 1941 (p. 18).
- **Syria:** "French 1886/93 rifles" for the bulk of the infantry, some 1,300 Lee-Enfields and about 1,000 Czech Mausers, and
  French 7.5 mm "Chatellerault Model 24 (24-29)" light machine guns (p. 18). Separately, two British telegrams of 31 January
  and 3 February 1948 name "123 Bren guns awaiting delivery" to the Syrian government (FO 816/115 and 816/116, page images
  viewed); whether they arrived is not stated.
- **Lebanon:** "old French equipment in unknown quantity" (p. 19). No rifle or machine gun type [GAP].

The result below is the 3 November one, kept as written; where ORE 48-48 now answers a row, the 11 November rows in the
table say so.

**Result: one book names a weapon for one force; the rest is thin.**

- **Arab Legion: closed at the level of "British small arms".** O'Ballance (1956, p. 80, page image viewed) says the Legion "was
  equipped with small arms of British pattern, such as Bren guns, Stens, rifles, 3-inch mortars, Piats, and so on". It names Bren and
  Sten; "rifles" is not typed. The Lee-Enfield for the Legion rests on Wikipedia's article on the rifle, which cites Peter Young,
  *The Arab Legion* (Osprey, 1972), p. 24 (not opened: 403) [S].
- **Egyptian Army: British equipment in general (O'Ballance p. 77), and a collector page says the war was fought with ".303
  Enfield and 98k surplus Mausers".** One source names the Bren in the Egyptian sector: a JTA item datelined Tel Aviv, 17 May 1948, says Egyptian regulars were driven back at Nirim and "The Haganah also captured a number of Bren guns and other artillery weapons", without saying the guns came from the Egyptians (row below). No source opened types the Egyptian Army's own issue. The two sources do not conflict, but
  O'Ballance does not name a rifle, and the collector page is one enthusiast's, so the rifle type is [S].
- **Syrian Army, Lebanese Army, Iraqi Army: no source opened names a rifle or machine gun in 1948** [GAP]. O'Ballance gives the
  Iraqi army British supply under the January 1948 treaty and lists "aircraft, 25-pounder guns, A.A. guns, anti-tank guns and
  armoured cars" (p. 83), no small arms. Syria and Lebanon: no weapon named on p. 80 or 81.
- **ALA: an item printed in *The Nation* and O'Ballance.** "They are armed with rifles, Brens and other automatic weapons" (an item
  opening "According to a reliable source", dated 25 February 1948, printed in *The Nation*, 8 May 1948, under "British Record on
  Partition", hosted by cojs.org) and O'Ballance p. 84: "plentifully supplied with
  small arms and ammunition", the main source Syria, "where the French had left large stocks behind them", plus the Western Desert.
  Type of rifle: [GAP].
- **The Wikipedia infobox for the war lists, for the Arab side, "Lee-Enfield rifles, Bren Guns, Sten guns, MAS 36s"** with no
  per-force split and no source [S].

**Stays [GAP]:** the rifle and LMG type per force for Syria, Lebanon, Iraq; the Bren for Egypt as the army's own issue (Nirim item only); the printed Young p. 24; the
Dupuy and Hughes pages (not reachable from this box); Pollack, *Arabs at War*, and Malsagne on the Lebanese army (not opened).

**(11 Nov) Stays [GAP] after 11 November:** the rifle and LMG type for Lebanon; the Legion's rifle type and whether its Stens were
infantry issue; a second source for the ORE 48-48 rows (Egypt, Iraq, Syria); the printed Young p. 24; Hughes (not reachable
from this box); Pollack, *Arabs at War* (lending only), and Malsagne on the Lebanese army (not found online). Dupuy's chapter on 1947-1949
(printed pp. 41-104) was read on 11 November and types no rifle or LMG for any Arab army.

## Disagreements kept side by side

- **Egypt's rifle.** The collector page says ".303 Enfield and 98k surplus Mausers"; the Wikipedia infobox lists Lee-Enfield for the
  Arab side as a whole; O'Ballance says only "British". No source opened gives a count of either type [C] (Mauser or Enfield
  as the principal rifle is not decided by any source here).
- **Where a Mauser (the K98k) appears.** The collector page puts "98k surplus Mausers" with Egypt. `docs/units.md` lines 310-312
  say no source places a K98k with an Arab force; that line predates this page and rests on the sources it searched. Both are kept; a
  hobby page alone is not enough to change the [GAP] in `docs/units.md`.
  (11 Nov) On main at 445ce4945 those `docs/units.md` lines are 325-327.

- **The Sten with the Arab Legion.** O'Ballance p. 80 puts "Stens" with the Legion ("Bren guns, Stens, rifles"); `docs/units.md`
  line 310 on main says "No source found places a K98k, an MG34 or a Sten with the Arab Legion, Egypt, Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, the ALA or
  local irregulars" [C]. Both are kept; this note does not change `docs/units.md`.
  (11 Nov) On main at 445ce4945 that `docs/units.md` line is 325.

- **Egypt's rifle, 11 November.** ORE 48-48 (p. 17) types the Egyptian Army's rifle as the Lee-Enfield .303 and does not
  mention a Mauser; the collector page's "98k surplus Mausers" is kept beside it. ORE 48-48 does put "about 1,000 Mauser
  rifles purchased from Czechoslovakia" with Syria (p. 18); it does not call them K98k.
- **The Sten with Egypt and Iraq, 11 November.** ORE 48-48 names Sten submachine guns for Egypt (p. 17) and 1,400 for Iraq
  (p. 18); `docs/units.md` line 325 says no source found places a Sten with those armies [C]. This note does not change
  `docs/units.md`.
- **Nirim, 15-17 May 1948.** The JTA item says the Haganah "captured a number of Bren guns"; Dupuy (p. 56, n. 7) gives the
  Egyptian account that the armour was "only a few Bren gun carriers". The two do not describe the same thing (captured
  weapons against the attacking armour), so they are kept side by side and not tagged [C]; neither settles whether the
  captured "Bren guns" were guns or carriers.
