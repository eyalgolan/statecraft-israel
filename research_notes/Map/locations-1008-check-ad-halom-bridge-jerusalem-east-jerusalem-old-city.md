## Conventions

- **[V, agent read 28 Oct 2026; a person's check pending]**: the printed or served text was opened by an agent on that day,
  as a page image or map tile that the agent looked at, and says what the cell reports. For a book page it vouches that the page
  prints the sentence, not that the sentence is right.
- **[S]**: Wikipedia, Wikidata or the web text of a JTA item (a compilation or a transcription, never primary), read by
  revision id or item id. Wikilink brackets are removed from quoted wikitext, a hyphen stands for an en dash inside a quote, and
  square brackets inside a JTA quote mark letters lost at the left edge of the scan.
- **[C]**: sources conflict; both values are kept.
- **[GAP]**: not reached or not found; what was tried is written next to it.
- **Result** column: *match* (the source supports the row), *conflict* (the source says something different),
  *not covered* (the source is silent), *not a row field* (the row has no such field; the fact is recorded for the owner).
- Hex conversion is the repo's own: `tools/hexgrid.py` `settlement_hex(lon, lat, grid)` over `content/map/hexes.json`
  (hex circumradius 5 km), run on 28 Oct 2026. "Edge" is the distance from the point to the nearest hex edge. Run on the
  second slice's `shaar_hagai` and `latrun_fort` points the same script gave 4.75 km and 0.03 km, and 4.37 km and 0.54 km, the
  figures that note prints. The rows' stored `q`/`r` equal what it returns for their own `lon`/`lat`: ad_halom_bridge (-6, 23),
  jerusalem (0, 23), east_jerusalem_old_city (0, 23).
- Distances between coordinates are `pyproj` WGS84 geodesic metres, rounded.

What the sources show that the owner may want to know:
(1) **`ad_halom_bridge`**: the row's own two cited sources disagree on where the bridge is. Wikidata gives the row's point;
the en.wiki article (also cited by the row) prints 31°46'00"N 34°39'58"E, 1,739 m south (bearing about 190 degrees) of it, in the same hex. The
1940s map prints the name "Jisr Isdud" at the row's point, so the row stands. "Ad Halom" is a post-war name: he.wiki says the
Government Names Committee made it the official name of a planned settlement on 31 August 1952 and that the name then went to the
bridge; Dupuy, printed page 58, calls it "the Ashdod Bridge". The sources disagree on who blew it up in 1948 (Givati Brigade in
he.wiki and Dupuy, Golani Brigade in en.wiki) and on when it was built (a 13th-century Mamluk bridge in en.wiki, the end of the
19th century on older foundations in he.wiki, 1880 in Wikidata);
(2) **`jerusalem`**: the row's latitude and longitude (31.7819444, 35.2194444) are the figures en.wiki prints
(31°46'55"N 35°13'10"E, 0 m); Wikidata, the row's cited coordinate source, gives 31.7819, 35.2194 with a stated precision of
about 0.0106 degrees (about 1 km), 6 m from the row. he.wiki prints a point 1,503 m away. The names "West Jerusalem" and
"East Jerusalem" are, according to en.wiki and he.wiki, the names of the two halves after the 1949 armistice; a search of the JTA
web text of 14 May to 5 June 1948 finds neither (searches below; web text, not the bulletin PDFs, so
this does not show what the printed bulletins say), and the bulletins speak of "the new city";
(3) **`east_jerusalem_old_city`, starting control**: the row says `israeli` from 15 May 1948. What the sources say is narrower:
the Jewish Quarter, with between 1,500 and 1,600 Jews (JTA), was held and besieged. On 18 May (JTA of 19 May) the Jewish units
that had broken into the Old City during the night were forced to withdraw from it under heavy fire, while "the Jewish defenders
inside the [O]ld City were still holding out"; so the Jews did not leave the Old City, only the units that had entered it did.
Nothing the agent read says the rest of the Old City was Israeli-held on 15 May, and he.wiki says the Arab Legion took the Old
City in the war, without a date. The Knesset PDF the row cites says (read by a review agent): "On May 14, 1948, upon the
departure of the British from the country, the Israeli forces began to take over compounds held by the Mandatory government.",
"On May 18, the Arab Legion reached Jerusalem and entered the Old City." and "On May 28, the Jewish Quarter fell into the hands
of the Jordanians, its defenders were taken prisoner and its synagogues were demolished." The row's own YAML comment scopes the 28 May change to "the Jewish Quarter fell to the Jordanians on that date", which is what the sources say; the `starting_control` value is the wider claim. The 28 May change to Arab forces matches both JTA and
he.wiki. The row's control values are the owner's ruling (the YAML comment says "owner ruling, 18 October 2026", and the owner reconfirmed
it as "Keep Israeli-held" on 27 Oct 2026, see #1008). The conflict is recorded and the row stays as it is. This is reported with its pages, not changed;
(4) the Knesset PDF that the rows cite for both control claims (`https://m.knesset.gov.il/En/About/Documents/JerEnPDF1.pdf`)
is a 2-page PDF titled "Liberation of Jerusalem" that a review agent read on 28 Oct 2026 (a person's check pending); this box still gets an HTML page titled "Unavailable" from the same address. Its quotes are in the cells below. It prints 14 May for the start of the Israeli takeover of Mandate compounds, so no source read gives 15 May 1948 itself as a date of control for either Jerusalem row;
(5) `jerusalem` and `east_jerusalem_old_city` are in the same hex (0, 23), 1,512 m apart.
