# Artisan workshop on the 1948-51 map (#707, b21)

Status: **draft, needs a person to check**. This is research only. An AI agent found and read every source below on 22
October 2026, and a second agent reopened each URL and checked each quoted sentence. `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` section 2,
rule 6, still requires a person to check every claim against its source before use. This note does not change
`content/`, `unity/` or code, and it does not place `b21-artisan-workshop-v2`.

## Question and result

The question is whether a named workshop (a crafts workshop, a carpentry shop or a smithy) can be sourced to a place and
to a day or year when it was working between 15 May 1948 and 15 January 1952, so that `b21-artisan-workshop-v2` can
stand from that date. Netanya's diamond polishing is ruled out on #707 (it closed in February 1948).

**No source read here gives a workshop's opening day.** What the sources give:

- **Kibbutz Yiftah carpentry shop, Upper Galilee: working by 20 April 1950.** Hebrew Wikipedia says the kibbutz, founded
  on 18 August 1948, kept a carpentry shop in its early days, citing a *Davar* report of 20 April 1950.
- **Ramat Yishai carpentry shop, on the Lower Galilee and Jezreel Valley border: set up toward the end of 1951.** Hebrew Wikipedia, citing a *Davar*
  report of 24 October 1951.
- **Kibbutz Lavi carpentry shop, Lower Galilee: 1949, year only.** Hebrew Wikipedia says a small carpentry shop was set up
  when the kibbutz was founded in 1949. The factory's infobox gives 1950, which the article does not tie to the shop. Uncited.
- **New Bezalel school workshops, Jerusalem: working through the war and in 1950.** Hebrew Wikipedia says the school did
  not close in the 1948 war (its metal workshop was damaged) and returned to normal work, and captions a photograph of
  the weaving department in 1950.

The *Davar* reports were **not read**: the National Library returned HTTP 403 to this box on 22 October 2026, and the
Internet Archive's copy of the 20 April 1950 page holds only the viewer, not the article text.

## Findings

All findings have status **draft, needs a person to check**.

### 1. Kibbutz Yiftah carpentry shop: Upper Galilee, by 20 April 1950

Hebrew Wikipedia says Kibbutz Yiftah was founded on 18 August 1948 by demobilised Palmach fighters, and that in its early
days it farmed about 3,000 dunams of field crops and kept a carpentry shop, a chicken coop and a sheep pen. That
sentence is footnoted to Yemima Tchernovitz, "יפתח", *Davar*, 20 April 1950, p. 57 (and its continuation, p. 58). The
article does not say when the carpentry shop opened; the footnote dates a report that mentions it.

Exact quoted sentences:

> "היישוב הוקם ב-18 באוגוסט 1948, ומציין את ט"ו באב תש"ח כיום הקמתו."

> "בראשית דרכו עיבד הקיבוץ כ-3,000 דונם פלחה והחזיק נגרייה, לול ודיר כבשים."

Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, "יפתח (קיבוץ)"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%AA%D7%97_%28%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A5%29)
(revision 44022209, `checked: 2026-10-22`).

Lead, **not read**: [*Davar*, 20 April 1950, article 57](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/dav/1950/04/20/01/article/57)
(HTTP 403 on 22 October 2026).

Confidence: **moderate that the carpentry shop existed by April 1950** (a Wikipedia summary of a contemporary report not
read here); **none for an opening day.**

### 2. Ramat Yishai carpentry shop: Lower Galilee and Jezreel Valley border, toward the end of 1951

Hebrew Wikipedia says that toward the end of 1951 Ramat Yishai had grown to about 160 families and that a large bakery, a
carpentry shop and other workshops were set up there. The sentence is footnoted to I. Strinsky, "רמת ישי (ג'דה לשעבר)",
*Davar*, 24 October 1951, p. 28. Read with its footnote, the report suggests the carpentry shop stood by 24 October 1951, inside the
window, but neither the sentence nor the footnote gives an opening day.

Exact quoted sentence:

> "לקראת סוף שנת 1951 התרחב היישוב לכ-160 משפחות, והוקמו בו מאפייה גדולה, נגרייה ועוד מפעלים."

Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, "רמת ישי"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A8%D7%9E%D7%AA_%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%99)
(revision 43297141, `checked: 2026-10-22`).

Lead, **not read**: [*Davar*, 24 October 1951, article 28](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/dav/1951/10/24/01/article/28)
(HTTP 403 on 22 October 2026; the Internet Archive's availability lookup found no copy).

Confidence: **moderate for late 1951; low for any day.** "Toward the end of 1951" leaves only a few weeks before the
15 January 1952 limit, so this is the weakest date of the four for an early map.

### 3. Kibbutz Lavi carpentry shop: Lower Galilee, 1949

Hebrew Wikipedia's article on Lavi's synagogue-furniture factory says that when the kibbutz was founded in 1949 a small
carpentry shop was set up; its team put up the wooden huts the Jewish Agency had bought in Sweden, built the first dining
hall from the offcuts, and made tables and chairs that were sold to the Jewish Agency. The infobox gives the factory's
founding as 1950, while the lead sentence says the factory was set up in the 1960s; the source does not tie the 1950 to
the carpentry shop. None of these lines is cited.

Exact quoted sentences:

> "קיבוץ לביא הוקם בשנת 1949, ועל מנת לתת מענה לצרכים השונים הוקמה נגרייה קטנה."

> "בנוסף לשירותים שסיפק צוות הנגרייה לקיבוץ, עסק הצוות בייצור שולחנות וכיסאות."

Infobox field: `תאריך הקמה = 1950`.

Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, "לביא תעשיות רהיטים"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9C%D7%91%D7%99%D7%90_%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%A8%D7%94%D7%99%D7%98%D7%99%D7%9D)
(revision 41986653, `checked: 2026-10-22`). The kibbutz's own article gives the same founding year:

> "קיבוץ לביא הוקם בשנת 1949 על ידי צעירים עולים חברי בח"ד (ברית חלוצים דתיים) מאנגליה, רובם היו בין 10,000 הילדים היהודיים הפליטים שהובאו לבריטניה מגרמניה בקינדר-טרנספורטים של שנות השלושים."

Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, "לביא (קיבוץ)"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9C%D7%91%D7%99%D7%90_%28%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A5%29)
(revision 43555989, `checked: 2026-10-22`).

Confidence: **low to moderate for 1949** (uncited, and the infobox and the lead disagree on the factory's date);
**none for a day.**

### 4. New Bezalel school workshops: Jerusalem, 1948-50

Hebrew Wikipedia says most New Bezalel students were called up in the 1948 war, that the school did not close, that parts
of its building were damaged (mainly the metal workshop), and that despite the siege of Jerusalem it went back to normal
work as the war went on. It names Ludwig Yehuda Wolpert as head of the metal department from 1935 to 1956, and it
captions a photograph of Julia Keiner in the weaving department in 1950. This is a crafts school with workshops, not a
single artisan's shop, and the article gives no street address.

Exact quoted sentences:

> "בבית הספר, שלא סגר את שעריו, נותרו כ-20 תלמידות."

> "כמו כן נפגעו חלקים ממבנה בית הספר (בעיקר סדנת המתכת)."

> "למרות המצור על ירושלים, עם התקדמות המלחמה חזר בית הספר לפעילות רגילה."

> "מחלקה מרכזית נוספת הייתה מחלקת המתכת, שבראשה עמדו לודוויג יהודה וולפרט (1935-1956) ודוד היינץ גומבל (1936-1955)."

> "יוליה קיינר במחלקת האריגה, 1950. צילום: ורנר בראון."

Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, "בצלאל החדש"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%91%D7%A6%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%9C_%D7%94%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9)
(revision 43062331, `checked: 2026-10-22`).

Confidence: **moderate that the workshops worked in 1949-50; none for a day.** The place is Jerusalem; the building's
exact site is not given in this source.

## Recommendation

**None is sourced to an opening day.** This note does not place `b21-artisan-workshop-v2`.

The closest to what #707 asks for is **Kibbutz Yiftah's carpentry shop, by 20 April 1950**: a named place, a carpentry
shop as #707 lists, and a date taken from a dated contemporary report, though only through Wikipedia's footnote. Whether
a "working by" date from a report is enough for the model to stand, and whether a kibbutz carpentry shop fits the
artisan-workshop model, are owner calls.

Open questions for a person:

1. Open *Davar* of 20 April 1950 (article 57, continued as article 58) and check that it mentions Yiftah's carpentry shop.
   If it does, 20 April 1950 is a sourced "working by" day.
2. Open *Davar* of 24 October 1951 (article 28) for Ramat Yishai, and see whether it dates the carpentry shop's opening.
3. Lavi: find a cited source (the kibbutz archive, or *HaTzofe* of 4 March 1949, *"לביא" עלה לשדות הגליל*, listed under
   external links in the kibbutz's Hebrew Wikipedia article) for the year the carpentry shop was set up.
4. New Bezalel: find a source for the building's site in Jerusalem in 1948-51 before it can be placed on the map.
