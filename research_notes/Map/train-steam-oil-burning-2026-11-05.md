# The train's steam: Palestine Railways burned oil

Sources for the colour of the service train's exhaust (`TrainSteam`; owner, 4 November 2026, "Train steam"). The game draws dark grey-black smoke at the chimney, thinning to grey, with white steam puffs: the look of an oil-burning locomotive rather than a coal-burning one.

Tags follow the research convention (`.github/skills/statecraft-video-script/references/05-claims-quotes-and-sources.md`): [S] single or secondary source. The pages below were opened and the lines copied by an AI agent (Claude), not by a person, so none is [V].

## Palestine Railways converted from coal to oil, 1942-43

- English Wikipedia, "Palestine Railways", section on the Second World War (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Railways, page source checked 2026-11-05): "PR had fuelled its locomotives with Welsh coal [Hughes 1981, p. 41] but in June 1940 Italy declared war on the Allies and France surrendered to Germany and Italy, leaving the Mediterranean extremely dangerous for British merchant shipping. Early in 1942 PR belatedly began to convert its locomotives to burn oil, [Cotterell 1984, p. 77] but it did not complete the conversion programme until 1943. [Hughes 1981, p. 41]" [S]
- English Wikipedia, "Palestine Railways P class" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Railways_P_class, page source checked 2026-11-05):
  - Infobox, fuel type: "originally Welsh coal; converted to oil by 1943" [Hughes 1981, p. 41]. [S]
  - Infobox, fuel capacity: "1,193 imperial gallons" "(after conversion to oil fuel)" [Cotterell 1984, p. 130]. [S]
  - Body: "PR therefore began converting its locomotives to burn oil, but it did not complete the conversion programme until 1943." [Hughes 1981, p. 41] [S]
- English Wikipedia, "Palestine Railways H class" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Railways_H_class, page source checked 2026-11-05), infobox, fuel type: "originally Welsh coal; converted to oil fuel by 1943" [Hughes 1981, p. 41]. [S]

The books behind these lines are Hughes, *Middle East Railways* (1981), and Cotterell, *The Railways of Palestine and Israel* (1984). Neither was opened here.
