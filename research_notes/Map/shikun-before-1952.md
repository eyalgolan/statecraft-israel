# Shikun occupied before 1952 (#707)

Status: **draft, needs a person to check**. This is research only. Every source below was read by AI agents (a first
draft, then a second agent that reopened each source and checked each quoted sentence word for word on 18 October
2026). `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` section 2, rule 6, still requires a person to check every claim against its source
before use. This note does not change `content/`, `unity/` or code.

## Question and result

The question is whether a named *shikun* can be sourced to a place and to the day its first residents moved in between
15 May 1948 and 31 December 1951, so that `b11-shikun-housing-v2` can stand only from that sourced day.

**No source gives the day the first residents moved in.** What the sources do give:

- **Ramat HaHayal, Tel Aviv: 1950, year only.** An academic article says most families moved in during 1950.
- **Tzahala, Tel Aviv: 20 September 1951, as the day of the inauguration ceremony.** The same article dates the
  ceremony's invitation to 20 September 1951, and the neighbourhood's own history site says that ceremony was the
  inauguration and the handover of the houses to the first IDF soldiers and officers. Neither says that anyone moved in
  that day.
- **Hadar Yosef (Amidar), Tel Aviv: occupied by November 1951.** The same article says it was set up in 1949 and had
  about 6,000 residents in November 1951; it gives no first move-in date.
- **Shikun HaPoel HaMizrachi H, Bnei Brak: "mid-1951".** The only source that names the first residents' move is an
  uncited 2022 blog post.
- **Even Yehuda (built 1950) and Shikun Ata B, Kfar Ata (built 1949):** construction years only.

Whether a year-level date (Ramat HaHayal, 1950) or the Tzahala ceremony day is enough for the model to stand is an
owner decision (see the recommendation).

## Findings

All findings have status **draft, needs a person to check**.

### 1. Ramat HaHayal: Tel Aviv, 1950

Yaron Balslev's article in *Iyunim Bitkumat Israel* 23 (2013) says the Ministry of Defence set up Ramat HaHayal in 1950
as an estate for demobilized soldiers, that it had 830 families at the start of 1954, and that most families moved in
during 1950. The sentence is footnoted to a Ramat HaHayal memorandum of 16 March 1954 in the Tel Aviv municipal
archive. It proves occupation in the window, but it names neither the first residents nor a day.

Exact quoted sentence (p. 247, before footnote 74):

> “אכלוס השיכון היה מהיר, ומרבית המשפחות עברו אליו בשנת 1950.”

Source: [Yaron Balslev, “The First Decade of the Development of Tel Aviv's Neighbourhoods Beyond the Yarkon, 1947–1958,” *Iyunim Bitkumat Israel* 23 (2013), pp. 233–273, here p. 247](https://in.bgu.ac.il/bgi/iyunim/23/yaron-balslev.pdf)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`). On 18 October the university's server did not answer (connection timed out), so the PDF was
read from the Internet Archive's copy of that URL, captured on 7 February 2025
([archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20250207232934/https://in.bgu.ac.il/bgi/iyunim/23/yaron-balslev.pdf)). The
sentence was checked on the rendered page image, not only on the extracted text.

Confidence: **high that most families moved in during 1950; none for a first-resident day.**

### 2. Tzahala (“Shikun Tzva Keva near Hadar Yosef”): Tel Aviv, 20 September 1951 ceremony

Balslev (p. 248) says Tzahala was founded in 1951 for the IDF's career soldiers, and that Defence Minister David
Ben-Gurion and Chief of Staff Yigael Yadin took part in its inauguration. His footnote 82 cites an invitation to the
inauguration ceremony on 20 September 1951, held on the neighbourhood's website. The neighbourhood's history site says
the houses were occupied in 1951, that the inauguration ceremony was also the handover of the houses to the first
soldiers and officers, and captions a photograph of Ben-Gurion at the “occupancy ceremony” of September 1951.

So the sources date a ceremony at which the houses were handed over. They do not say that the first families moved in
that day, and the day comes from an invitation (the planned day), cited in a footnote; the invitation itself was not
found or read.

Exact quoted sentences:

> “הזמנה לטקס חנוכת שכונת צהלה ב־20.9.1951, אתר האינטרנט של שכונת צהלה”

(Balslev, p. 248, footnote 82; source as in finding 1.)

> “בשנת 1951 אוכלסו הבתים ובסמוך לכך נרשמה אגודה בשם: שיכון אנשי צבא בשירות קבע – צהלה – אגודה שיתופית בע"מ.”

> “טקס חנוכת השכונה ומסירת הבתים לחיילי וקציני צה"ל הראשונים, התקיים בנוכחות שר הביטחון וראש המטה הכללית דאז, מר דוד בן גוריון.”

> “דוד בן גוריון בטקס אכלוס השכונה ספטמבר 1951”

Source: [Tzahala neighbourhood site, “צהלה של פעם - תולדות” (Tzahala of old: history)](https://www.zahala.co.il/%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA-1.html)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`). This is a community history, not an official or academic publication, and it gives no
citations for these lines. Balslev cites the same site (as `www.zahala.org.il`) for the invitation.

Contemporary lead, **not read**: [*Herut*, 21 September 1951, an item the first draft linked to the inauguration](https://www.nli.org.il/he/newspapers/hrt/1951/09/21/01/article/110)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`). The first draft found it through the National Library's search, but on 18 October the page
returned HTTP 403, so neither its title nor its text was read, and it is not used as a source here.

Confidence: **moderate for a ceremony and handover on 20 September 1951; low that it was the first move-in day.**

### 3. Hadar Yosef (Amidar): Tel Aviv, occupied by November 1951

Balslev (p. 245) calls Hadar Yosef the first public housing estate built beyond the Yarkon. He says it was set up in
1949 on the Jewish Agency's initiative, its building was given to Amidar (so it was first called “Shikun Amidar”), and
it was occupied quickly, with about 6,000 residents in November 1951. He gives no first move-in date.

Exact quoted sentence (p. 245, footnote 68 is a residents' committee protest dated 13 November 1951):

> “הדר־יוסף אוכלסה במהירות, ובנובמבר 1951 מנתה כ־6,000 תושבים”

Source: Balslev, p. 245 (source as in finding 1).

Confidence: **high that it was occupied by November 1951; none for a first-resident day.**

### 4. Shikun HaPoel HaMizrachi H: Bnei Brak, mid-1951

This is the only source found that says when the *first* residents entered a named estate. It places the estate on the
north-eastern edge of Bnei Brak, says its cornerstone was laid on 27 November 1949, and dates the move only to
“mid-1951.” It is a blog post by Eli Alon in News1's bloggers' section (the archived page shows 25/04/2022), with no
citation for the statement, so it is not strong enough to turn “mid-1951” into a day.

Exact quoted sentence:

> “המשתכנים הראשונים, קרוב ל-500 משפחות, נכנסו לבתיהם באמצע 1951.”

Source: [Eli Alon, “סיפורו של שיכון ה' בבני ברק” (The story of Shikun H in Bnei Brak), News1](https://www.news1.co.il/Archive/0026-D-154119-00.html)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`). The live page returned HTTP 403 on 18 October; the sentence was read in the Internet Archive's
copy of 8 February 2026
([archived page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260208060217/https://www.news1.co.il/Archive/0026-D-154119-00.html)).

Confidence: **low to moderate for mid-1951; none for a day.**

### 5. The first immigrant shikun after the state's founding: Even Yehuda, 1950

The Beit Rishonim local museum, under the heading “שיכון עולים ראשון לאחר קום המדינה” (the first immigrant shikun after
the founding of the state), says 24 small semi-detached units were built in 1950 on today's Rehov HaVatikim, on JNF
land, by Nir Shitufi, a Histadrut company. It gives no move-in date. Construction in a year is not occupancy on a day.

Exact quoted sentence:

> “בשנת 1950 נבנו 24 יחידות דיור בבתים דו-משפחתיים קטנים של 30 מ”ר ברחוב הותיקים של היום.”

Source: [Beit Rishonim museum, Even Yehuda, “מדינה צעירה” (A young state)](https://rishonim-e-y.org.il/%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%94/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94-%D7%A6%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%94)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`).

Confidence: **high for construction in 1950; no sourced first-resident date.**

### 6. Shikun Ata B: Kfar Ata (today Kiryat Ata), 1949

The Beit Fischer municipal museum says Shikun Ata B's detached houses and four-flat shared buildings were built in 1949,
and that people of Kfar Ata and workers of the Ata factory bought homes there. It does not say when anyone moved in. The
same page gives July 1946 as the first group's entry into Shikun Ata A, which is outside the window and cannot be
carried over to Shikun Ata B.

Exact quoted sentence:

> “בשנת 1949 נבנו שיכוני אתא ב', בתים בודדים לצד בתים משותפים בני ארבע דירות.”

Source: [Beit Fischer municipal museum, “סיפורו של מקום” (Story of a place)](https://bet-fisher.co.il/history/story/?item=18296&parent=17610)
(`checked: 2026-10-18`).

Confidence: **high for construction in 1949; no sourced first-resident date.**

## Recommendation

**None is sourced to a first-resident day.** This note does not place `b11-shikun-housing-v2`.

Two options are close enough for the owner to decide on; neither is a first move-in day:

1. **Tzahala, 20 September 1951**: a dated inauguration ceremony at which, by the neighbourhood's history site, the
   houses were handed to the first soldiers and officers. Its weak points: the day comes from an invitation cited in a
   footnote, the handover is not shown to be the move-in, and Tzahala was an estate of private and two-family houses
   for career officers, so whether the b11 model fits it is a separate art call.
2. **Ramat HaHayal, 1950 (year only)**: the best-sourced occupation, from an academic article citing a municipal archive
   file, but with no month or day; the model would need a rule for a year-level date.

For a follow-up source check, open the *Herut* article of 21 September 1951 (blocked on 18 October) and the Tel Aviv
municipal archive files that Balslev cites (footnote 74 for Ramat HaHayal, footnote 68 for Hadar Yosef). Until a person
opens those sources, the dates above stay leads, not values.
