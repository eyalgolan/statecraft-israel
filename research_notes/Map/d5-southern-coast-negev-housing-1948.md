## 2. Which Mandate sub-district each place was in

**Village Statistics 1945** (Government of Palestine, figures as at 1 April 1945), scan
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/Village_Statistics_1945_full.pdf (fetched 2026-11-01, 52,198,759
bytes, SHA-256 `b265a1dab532486945f3840bb435bc9f2465188abd41173bf4af37de95ece51e`, the same file
`gdd03-region-allocation.md` cites). The pages were rendered and read by eye. [V] as a primary source for the listing.

- **Beersheba Sub-District**, printed page 33 (scan PDF page 34). The Jewish population column lists Beit Eshel 40,
  Gevulot 20, Ruhama 70 and Tel Tsofim 20, and the sub-district total of Jews is 150. Tel Tsofim is Revivim's first name
  (sibling note, Revivim; Encyclopaedia Judaica below). So Revivim, Ruhama, Gvulot and Beit Eshel were in Beersheba
  Sub-District. The four rows add up to the 150 in `gdd03-region-allocation.md` (40 + 20 + 70 + 20, **computed here**).
  Beersheba itself is listed with 5,360 Muslims, 200 Christians and no Jews, so the region's capital is not a candidate.
- **Gaza Sub-District**, printed pages 31 and 32 (scan PDF pages 32 and 33). Jewish rows include "Hûj and Dorot" 230,
  Negba 280, "'Irâq el Manshiya and Gat" 210, Beer Tuvya 690, Kefar Warburg 260, "Gan Yavne and Kefar Bitsaron" 610
  (430 + 180), Isdûd 290 and Sumsum 70. The Jews total 2,890, which matches the allocation note.
- **Nir Am is not named** in Village Statistics 1945. No Gaza or Beersheba row carries its name. Some Jewish
  population is listed under Arab territorial units (for example Beit Hânûn 50, Hamâma 60, Hirbiyâ 60, Sumsum 70), and
  the table does not say which settlements those rows cover. [GAP] for Nir Am's sub-district in this table.
- **The true polygons.** `content/map/subdistricts_1948.geojson` holds the 1947 Survey of Palestine lines. A
  point-in-polygon test **(computed here, shapely)** puts Nir Am's point in Gaza Sub-District, about 5 km from the
  Beersheba line. Dorot is in Gaza (about 1 km from the line), and Ruhama is in Beersheba (about 4.5 km). Revivim's
  original mitzpe is in Beersheba (about 35 km), as are Gvulot (about 13 km), Nirim at Dangur (about 5 km) and Mishmar
  HaNegev (about 11 km). Negba (about 8 km) and Yad Mordechai are in Gaza. The distances are rough: degree distances
  were scaled by 100 km.

**Our reading:** for Nir Am the sub-district rests on the Survey line (about 5 km inside Gaza) and on Dorot, which lies
further east at nearly the same latitude, being listed in Gaza. That is good, but it is not a printed listing.

## 3. Method

- Each 1948 point was looked up against the game's own hexes **(computed here)**. The steps:
  - The nearest land-hex centre in `content/map/hexes.json` (cosine-scaled longitude).
  - Its sub-district in `subdistrict_hexes_1948.yaml`.
  - Its opening control code in `content/map/control_hexes_1948_49.yaml` (period `opening`, 15 May to 11 June 1948).
- The lookup reproduces every hex the content already stores: Negba (-7, 25), Nir Am (-9, 27), Yad Mordechai (-9, 26)
  and Revivim (-11, 34). It also reproduces the stored hex of the Iraq Suwaydan police fort (-7, 25), from its ledger
  point 31.6480, 34.6818.
- CBS points come from the Central Bureau of Statistics localities file 2024,
  https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2019/ishuvim/bycode2024.xlsx (fetched 2026-11-01). The `קואורדינטות`
  value was read as ITM easting and northing and converted with `pyproj` (EPSG:2039 to EPSG:4326), as the sibling note
  does. These are the present localities.
- Presence in `content/` was checked with `git grep -i` over `content/` for each name in the tables (2026-11-01):
  Negba, Nir Am, Yad Mordechai, Revivim, Ruhama, Beit Eshel, Gvulot, Nirim, Mishmar HaNegev, Hatzerim, Shoval, Dorot,
  Kfar Darom.
- The Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) archive was searched through `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive`, and the
  item text was searched for the exact name. That text is the JTA's machine reading of the printed bulletin; the print
  PDFs were not opened.
- Haim Gvati, *100 Years of Settlement*, vol. 2, Ben-Yehuda Project edition, https://benyehuda.org/read/14684 (fetched
  2026-11-01, 1,348,249 bytes, the same size the sibling notes record). The full text was searched for each name.

### Nir Am, the recommended place

- **Founded January 1943. [C] on the day.** Gvati's appendix gives "ניר-עם דרום 12.1.43 האיחוד". The sibling note gives
  he.wiki's 21 January 1943 (15 Shevat 5703). Both are given. The difference does not matter for 15 May 1948.
- **The 15 May evidence, one source.** Palmach Heritage, "חטיבת הנגב (חטיבה 12)",
  https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=6190 (checked 2026-11-01): "למחרת הפלישה של הצבא המצרי ולאחר
  שקיבוץ ניר-עם ספג הפגזה ארטילרית, עברה מפקדת החטיבה לקיבוץ דורות." [S] The page gives no calendar date; "the day after
  the invasion" is 16 May 1948 **(our reading)**. It also does not say whether non-combatants stayed.
- **Context, not 15 May evidence.** Before the invasion Nir Am was the Negev Brigade's base:
  - Gvati: "על־יד קיבוץ ניר־עם הוקם מרכז אספקה לנגב ושם התמקמה מפקדת חטיבת הנגב של הפלמ״ח".
  - Palmach Heritage, "The Palmach and the Negev Settlements",
    https://www.palmach.org.il/en/history/database/?itemId=8465 (checked 2026-11-01): "The headquarters of the Negev
    Brigade and the 2nd Battalion were at Nir-Am".
  - Gvati, on the winter of 1948, names it among the older settlements with more members: "רק בישובים ה״ותיקים״ יותר:
    דורות, גת, ניר־עם, רוחמה, היה מספר חברים יותר גדול."
  - The headquarters left for Dorot after the shelling (itemId=6190 above), so the HQ is evidence for before the
    invasion, not for 15 May.
- **After.** JTA, 18 October 1948,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/israelis-warn-egyptians-against-use-of-gas-jews-capture-strategic-position-in-negev
  (checked 2026-11-01): the Israelis "reported the shelling of the Jewish settlements of Niram, Guaram and Mekoroth".
  [S] JTA web text. "Mekoroth" is the web text's spelling and may be a misreading of the print.
- **Site.** The content point is the CBS point of the present kibbutz. No source read here records a move. [GAP] for an
  explicit 1948 site.
- **The game's data.** Hex (-9, 27) is `I` in every period of the control layer. It is also the Negev Brigade's anchor
  hex (`content/map/unit_anchor_hexes.yaml`, `negev`, `q: -9, r: 27`).
- **Our reading:** Nir Am existed and was occupied in the winter of 1948 (Gvati) and the day after the invasion
  (Palmach), and was still a Jewish settlement under fire in October 1948 (JTA). That makes it very likely that it was
  inhabited and held on 15 May. But only one source speaks to those days, so the 15 May cell is [S] plus this reading,
  not [V].

### Why not the others

- **Negba** is the strongest alternative, and it is in `content/`.
  - Gvati: "את ההתקפה הראשונה על נגבה ערכו המצרים עוד ב־21.5".
  - JTA, 20 May 1948, https://www.jta.org/archive/haganah-breaks-through-encircled-old-city-of-jerusalem-captures-sarafand
    (checked 2026-11-01): "The southern Jewish settlement of Negba was raided by planes this morning". So Negba was
    there on 20 to 21 May [V], but no source read names 15 May.
  - Its hex (-7, 25) also holds the Iraq Suwaydan police fort. Gvati: "משטרת עיראק־סואידן שהצבא המצרי תפסה והתבצר בה"
    (no date).
  - The control layer has the hex `M` from 11 June and `E` from 18 July to the October period **(computed here)**.
- **Dorot** shares Nir Am's hex and was the Negev Brigade's headquarters the day after the invasion (itemId=6190), so
  it is an equal backup with no hex change. Nir Am is preferred only because it already has a content row.
- **Yad Mordechai.** Gvati: "ביום 19.5 החלה ההתקפה על יד־מרדכי". The members later left the place, per the full
  passage quoted below. Its hex is `E` from 11 June.
- **Kfar Darom.** Gvati: "ב־15.5 תקפו יחידות צבא מצרים את כפר־דרום", and "ב־8 ביולי הוחלט לפנות את הישוב. המגינים נטשו
  את המקום בלילה על נשקם ולמחרת נכבש הישוב על־ידי המצרים." Abandoned in July, and its point rests on he.wiki only.
- **Nitzanim.** Taken in the war (Gvati, passage below). Ruled out like Yad Mordechai.
- **The full Gvati passage** used above (it is not a complete list of lost settlements and is not used to show any
  place was held): "רק ישובים ספורים נכבשו על־ידי כוחות צבא גדולים, אחרי שהמגינים סבלו אבדות קשות ולא יכלו להמשיך
  עוד במלחמה. בכמה מהם עלה בידי החברים לנטוש את המקום בעוד מועד ולהסתנן דרך קווי האויב (עטרות, יד־מרדכי), ואילו
  באחרים (גוש עציון וניצנים) נלקחו הלוחמים ששרדו בשבי."

### The other Negev places

- **Beit Eshel** has a source two days after 15 May. Gvati: the Egyptians "פתחו ב־17.5 בהתקפה על בית־אשל, אולם נהדפו",
  and its members "החזיקו מעמד עד סוף המלחמה, ורק אחריה התיישבו בעמק במושב היוגב". [S] But it is in Beersheba's own hex
  (-9, 31), coded `-MMMMMcc` until 31 October 1948. It was abandoned after the war, which the sibling note flags for the owner's
  sensitivity review. It has no content row.
- **Ruhama** had the largest Jewish population in the sub-district in 1945 (70). The brigade headquarters moved there
  "לאחר זמן קצר" after Dorot (itemId=6190, undated). [S] No source read dates it on 15 May.
- **Mishmar HaNegev, Shoval and Hatzerim** are the only candidates whose hexes are `I` at the opening. If Amir's Q37
  requires friendly control of the housing hex, one of them would avoid the uncoloured-hex problem. No 15 May source
  was found for any of them, and none has a content row.

What follows for D5:

- D5's condition is not met until a person checks these sources.
- For the Negev, the condition is met by Nirim's sources. If the owner prefers Revivim to avoid a new place row, its
  own 15 May evidence is weaker and a person should accept that inference explicitly.
- For Southern Coast, a second source dated 14 to 16 May for Nir Am or Dorot would raise the row to [V].

What this note does not decide:

- Whether the housing place must stand in the field's hex. The field hexes are still empty (#1690).

  uncoloured at the opening.
- Population: these are design places (3 per project in the #1691 split), not a claim about how many people lived
  there.
