## Question and result

#1874 asks: what stood at Caesarea in 1948 (the village, the ruins, the harbour), which hex it falls on, a source for each, and
whether a railway halt served it. The hex-tile prototype is `unity/Assets/Scripts/Map/HexTiles/Prototypes/CaesareaRuins.cs`; the
railway prop is `Prototypes/RailwayHalt.cs`.

**What closed**

- **What stood.** An Arab village, called Qisariya in 1945, built inside the ruins of the ancient city on the coast. The 1945
  Village Statistics print 960 Arabs and 160 Jews (1,120 in all) and 31,786 dunums of land, of which 9,953 public and only 874
  Jewish-owned. The 1922 *Handbook of Palestine* prints that the ruins "are now inhabited by the Bosnians" and that the community
  numbered 331 souls. The same book says "Very little remains" of the ancient city (p. 104).
- **Hex.** The village point falls on hex `(1, 13)`, the same hex `content/map/settlement_hexes.yaml:21` gives Hadera. The 1940s map
  prints the railway at "Binyamina STA." about 5.7 km to the east, on hex `(2, 12)`. See "Hex".
- **Railway halt.** None was found at Caesarea. Three maps read as images (the 1922 *Handbook* railway map, the 1946 1:250,000
  sheet, the 1:20,000 sheet) draw no railway at the village. The nearest halt on the 1:20,000 sheet is Binyamina, about 5.7 km
  east. The only station called "Caesarea" that a source names opened on 1 July 2001 [S] (see the table).

**What did not close, and stays [GAP]:** the day the village was taken (both place the events in February 1948, Wikipedia the taking and Morris the demolition, and they differ on Rabin's role, [C]); what stood at the harbour in
1948 (the 1922 *Handbook* gives only a summer anchorage half a mile off the shore and a small fishing industry); a Mandate-era timetable or station list; any JTA bulletin on the village. The book and Israeli sources that
would settle them were not open to this box (see "What was tried and failed").

(Second pass, 29 Oct: of those four, none is closed. Narrowed: the railway question (the 1922 *Handbook* prints a station list
without Caesarea, and a fourth map is silent at the village), the harbour (the full 1942 sheet image draws the shore and a strip
projecting from the village, see the table), the tile edition (the tiles match the 1942 revision by eye, a colour comparison, not a printed statement) and the ruins' hex (the map
labels read all fall on `(1, 13)`). The day of the taking and the JTA 14 November item stay open. See the rows dated 2026-10-29.)

  0.0591 x 111.32 x cos(32.5 degrees) = 5.55 km east and 0.0121 x 110.57 = 1.34 km north, so sqrt(5.55^2 + 1.34^2) = 5.7 km.
- The village's hex is shared by Hadera, Sdot Yam and the ruins; the halt, if one is wanted from the 1940s map, would be on the
  next hex north-east. Whether to put a prop on a hex that holds no halt is the owner's call; this note gives the facts only.
- No source read gives a separate position for the harbour works or the ruins. The 1922 *Handbook* (p. 198) gives one position for
  Caesarea as a port, `(1, 13)`, with the anchorage half a mile off the shore, and has the Bosnian village inside the ruins
  (p. 105), so the village point, the port position and the ruins share a hex in these sources.
- Second pass, 29 Oct. The tools: `tools/hexgrid.py` needs numpy, which is not installed on this box, so the rows above dated 2026-10-29
  were computed with a pure-Python copy of its `lonlat_to_km` and `point_to_hex` arithmetic (same constants, `content/map/hexes.json`
  bounds and size 5 km), checked first on four rows of `content/map/settlement_hexes.yaml`: Haifa `(5, 8)`, Nazareth `(7, 10)`, Zikhron Ya'akov `(3, 11)`
  and Hadera `(1, 13)` all came out as the file has them. The copy lives outside the repo. The distance from each point to the nearest edge it shares with the next-nearest hex is
  (d2^2 - d1^2) / (2 x 8.66) km, with d1, d2 the distances to the two nearest hex centres and 8.66 km the distance between
  neighbouring centres (5 x sqrt 3). Results: village point 0.65 km (neighbour `(2, 12)`); 1946 ring 0.67; Handbook position 1.27
  (neighbour `(1, 12)`); "cient" label 1.12; triangulation point 1.24; Sdot Yam 1.61; 1:100,000 "Qisariya" label 1.07; the "Hippodrome" label 0.27 (neighbour
  `(2, 12)`). So every ruins label read falls on `(1, 13)`; only the label at the Hippodrome is within 0.3 km of the next hex, and a
  label's centre is not a feature's position.
