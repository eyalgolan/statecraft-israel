# Caesarea in 1948, for the hex-tile ruins and railway halt (#1874, research part)

## Question and result

#1874 asks: what stood at Caesarea in 1948 (the village, the ruins, the harbour), which hex it falls on, a source for each, and
whether a railway halt served it. The hex-tile prototype is `unity/Assets/Scripts/Map/HexTiles/Prototypes/CaesareaRuins.cs`; the
railway prop is `Prototypes/RailwayHalt.cs`.

**What closed**

- **What stood.** An Arab village, called Qisariya in 1945, built inside the ruins of the ancient city on the coast. The 1945
  Village Statistics print 960 Arabs and 160 Jews (1,120 in all) and 31,786 dunums of land, of which 9,953 public and only 874
  Jewish-owned. The 1922 *Handbook of Palestine* prints that the ruins "are now inhabited by the Bosnians" and that the community
  numbered 331 souls. The same book says "Very little remains" of the ancient city (p. 104).
- **Hex.** The village point falls on hex `(1, 13)`, the same hex `content/map/settlement_hexes.yaml:21` gives Hadera. The 1940s map
  prints the railway at "Binyamina STA." about 5.7 km to the east, on hex `(2, 12)`. See "Hex".
- **Railway halt.** None was found at Caesarea. Three maps read as images (the 1922 *Handbook* railway map, the 1946 1:250,000
  sheet, the 1:20,000 sheet) draw no railway at the village. The nearest halt on the 1:20,000 sheet is Binyamina, about 5.7 km
  east. The only station called "Caesarea" that a source names opened on 1 July 2001 [S] (see the table).

**What did not close, and stays [GAP]:** the day the village was taken (both place the events in February 1948, Wikipedia the taking and Morris the demolition, and they differ on Rabin's role, [C]); what stood at the harbour in
1948 (the 1922 *Handbook* gives only a summer anchorage half a mile off the shore and a small fishing industry); a Mandate-era timetable or station list; any JTA bulletin on the village. The book and Israeli sources that
would settle them were not open to this box (see "What was tried and failed").

## Disagreements

**1. Rabin's role in February 1948; both place the events that month.** Both are [S]: neither source's own page was opened by this box.

| Value | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| "In February 1948, the village was taken by a Palmach unit commanded by Yitzhak Rabin, following an earlier attack on a bus by Lehi militants." | en.wikipedia "Caesarea (modern town)", revision 1380466986, text of the History section | [C] |
| The footnote sentence before it is "And there was one authorised expulsion." One leading family left on 10 January; most villagers left after the 31 January Lehi ambush of a bus; the Haganah occupied the site because the land was PICA-owned; "Yitzhak Rabin, the Palmah's head of operations, opposed the destruction - but he was overruled. On 19-20 February, the Palmah's Fourth Battalion demolished the houses. The 20-odd inhabitants who were found at the site were forced to leave, and some of the troops looted the abandoned homes." | the same Wikipedia revision, footnote "Morris129130", which quotes Morris, *The Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem Revisited* (Cambridge University Press, 2004), pp. 129-130. The agent did not open Morris: the archive.org copies are lending-restricted (HTTP 401 and 403 on 28 October 2026) | [C] |

(Dashes in the Morris quotation are normalised to hyphens; the source has en dashes.) The accounts are not the same: the first names Rabin as the commander of the unit that took the village; the second has
him opposing the demolition and being overruled. Both place the events in February 1948 (Wikipedia: the taking; Morris: the demolition, 19-20 February) and they differ on Rabin's role. The game needs a day, and the owner chooses; no day of occupation is printed in either source.

**2. Number of mosques.** "Two mosques" (the 19th-century town, Marom via en.wikipedia) against "only one ... has survived"
(Petersen, 2001, describing a 1992 visit, same article). They describe different times, so this is a note and not a conflict; the repo row's
`landmark: mosque` (`content/map/localities_1948.yaml:5856`) matches either.

**3. Population.** Not a conflict: 1,120 (`content/map/localities_1948.yaml:5857`, from the 1945 Village Statistics) is the total of
960 Arabs and 160 Jews. The repo row `note:` records Wikidata's 960 as the other value, which is the Arab count alone.

- `content/map/settlement_hexes.yaml` has no row for Caesarea; the `caesarea` place lives only in `localities_1948.yaml`.
- Distance from the village point to the Binyamina station label: 0.0591 degrees of longitude east (34.9514 - 34.892349) and 0.0121 north (32.5144 - 32.502329), which is
  0.0591 x 111.32 x cos(32.5 degrees) = 5.55 km east and 0.0121 x 110.57 = 1.34 km north, so sqrt(5.55^2 + 1.34^2) = 5.7 km.
- The village's hex is shared by Hadera, Sdot Yam and the ruins; the halt, if one is wanted from the 1940s map, would be on the
  next hex north-east. Whether to put a prop on a hex that holds no halt is the owner's call; this note gives the facts only.
- No source read gives a separate position for the harbour works or the ruins. The 1922 *Handbook* (p. 198) gives one position for
  Caesarea as a port, `(1, 13)`, with the anchorage half a mile off the shore, and has the Bosnian village inside the ruins
  (p. 105), so the village point, the port position and the ruins share a hex in these sources.
