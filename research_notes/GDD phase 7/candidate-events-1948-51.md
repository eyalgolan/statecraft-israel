## 1. The Jericho Conference, 1 December 1948

**Dates.** The first Palestine Arab congress in Amman: 30 September or 1 October 1948 [C, below]. The second, at
Jericho: Wednesday **1 December 1948**. Anchor, if it were ever used: 1 December 1948 (the 15 November 1948 turn).

**What happened.**

- A "Second Palestine Arab conference" met at Jericho on 1 December 1948. Delegations included the mayors of Hebron,
  Bethlehem and Ramallah, the Arab Legion Military Governor General and the military governors of all districts; the
  audience was estimated at "several thousand". [V] FRUS 1948 V/2 d809; Shlaim 1990, p. 50 ("some 3,000 delegates",
  the same list of mayors and governors).
- Six resolutions were proposed and four adopted. The first asked that Arab Palestine be annexed to Transjordan at
  once and recognised Abdullah as King; the second and third thanked the Arab states; the fourth asked that the first
  be conveyed to the King at once. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d809 (the conference secretary's account, before the final text
  was drafted). Shlaim 1990, p. 50 agrees on the substance ("proclaimed the union of Palestine and Transjordan and
  acknowledged Abdullah as the King of the united country").
- A delegation took the resolution to the King at Shuneh; he replied that it must go to his government and that he must
  learn the views of the other Arab states. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d809.
- Egypt protested; Syria, Saudi Arabia and Yemen followed; the Arab League sent a note of protest to the Transjordanian
  Legation in Cairo on 14 December 1948. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d830 (telegram and editorial note).
- The US approved "the principle underlying Jericho resolutions" and thought incorporation of most of Arab Palestine in
  Transjordan the most satisfactory outcome. [V] FRUS 1949 VI d350 (instruction of 3 January 1949, with its footnote
  dating the resolutions to 1 December 1948).
- Shlaim calls Jericho "an important landmark on the road to annexation" and writes that the initiative came from the
  King. [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 50 (interpretation, cited here as his).
- **[C] The Amman congress.** Shlaim 1990, p. 44: the "First Palestinian Congress" met in Amman on **30 September 1948**,
  the same day as the Gaza council's declaration, denounced the Gaza government and declared Transjordan and Palestine a
  single territorial unit. FRUS 1948 V/2 d809: the Jericho preamble "referred to resolutions adopted at meeting of
  **October 1**". The Gaza declaration itself is dated 1 October 1948 (APG declarations, II), which also does not match
  Shlaim's "same day" as 30 September. The owner's spot-check in #135 reads FRUS's "October 1" as the Amman meeting;
  Shlaim supports that reading (a first congress in Amman before the "second" at Jericho), but not the day.
- **Not found:** the date on which the Transjordan parliament endorsed the Jericho resolutions. Secondary pages give
  13 December 1948; no primary or scholarly text for it was opened.

**Israel's decision point.** The Jericho resolutions were an inter-Arab matter. No Israeli cabinet decision on them was
found. What Israel was deciding in December 1948 was whether to keep talking to Transjordan or to resume fighting on the
central front:

- On 22 December 1948 the UN Truce Commission found Dayan saying that the Provisional Government of Israel was "ready
  negotiate armistice and peace either publicly or secretly", while implying it would resume hostilities if the
  stalemate continued; Dayan believed the King, "after building up Jericho conference", had stopped short of direct
  talks because of Arab reaction. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d844 (a US report of what Dayan said).
- Transjordan's Prime Minister told the US on 2 December that Transjordan and the Israelis could not yet meet "as
  governments" on political matters, and that the King kept his own channel through personal emissaries. [S] FRUS 1948
  V/2 d803.

**Fit.** **No card.** Use as dated context: one line in the text of an existing or new Jordan event. The December 1948
choice (talk to Abdullah or fight on) is already carried by `jerusalem_ceasefire` (28 November 1948), `rhodes_talks`
(January 1949) and `armistice_jordan` (3 April 1949).

**Sensitivity.** None of the four sensitive tags applies. `security` only if used with a front-line event.

**Overlaps.** `jerusalem_ceasefire`, `rhodes_talks`, `armistice_jordan`.

## 2. Jordan and Israel in 1950

### 2a. Abdullah's five-year non-aggression proposal, February to March 1950 (new candidate)

**Dates.** Proposal: night of Friday **17 February 1950**. Israeli cabinet agreement in principle announced and
"agreed set of principles" initialled: night of Friday **24 February 1950**. Drafts exchanged and deadlock: 28 February
and 3 March 1950. Anchor: **17 February 1950** (the 15 February 1950 turn), the day the offer was made; the window
could run to 24 February, when Israel's answer was given.

**What happened.**

- At an Israel-Transjordan meeting on the night of 17 February 1950, the Israeli representative (Reuven Shiloah), the
  Jordanian minister Samir al-Rifai and the King agreed that peace was "now impossible" because neither side could make
  the concessions. The King then proposed: a five-year renewable non-aggression pact keeping the armistice lines; free
  intercourse between the two countries with a free zone at Haifa; study committees on specific issues; and joint
  guarantees to the UN on the Holy Places. He also raised compensation for Arab property owners and reserved his claim
  to an outlet to the sea, and indicated willingness to provide access to Mount Scopus. [S] FRUS 1950 V d364 (Shiloah's
  account to the US Ambassador, 19 February 1950). [V] in substance by FRUS 1950 V d366 (Eban to the US delegation in
  Geneva, 21 February 1950: "Jordan now proposes five-year non-aggression pact as practical half-way step between
  armistice and peace").
- Shiloah consulted the Prime Minister and the Foreign Minister; his impression was that the cabinet would accept the
  proposals "as basis talks". [S] FRUS 1950 V d364. (The printed telegram says "February 1"; the meeting was on
  17 February, so the day is probably garbled in transmission. Not resolved.)
- At the meeting of 24 February, Shiloah announced "Israel cabinet agreement in principle" to the King's five-year
  non-aggression proposal and the delegation's authority to discuss details. After three hours both sides initialled an
  "agreed set of principles": it did not supersede the armistice; it kept the armistice lines and set up
  sub-committees on territorial issues (Jerusalem partition and an outlet to the sea named); trade and economic
  cooperation with free zones for Jordan in Haifa; Israel to start paying compensation to Arab property owners in
  Jerusalem; access to Mount Scopus and Bethlehem; Holy Places under joint guarantee to the UN with international
  supervision. [S] FRUS 1950 V d370 (Shiloah's account, 26 February 1950). Israel's delegation was Shiloah and Dayan;
  Jordan's the King, Samir al-Rifai and Fawzi al-Mulki.
- At the next meeting, on 28 February at Shuneh, the drafts diverged. The Israeli account: Jordan's draft removed the
  words "non-aggression pact", the five-year term and freedom of trade (for fear of the Arab League boycott), and stressed
  a corridor, an outlet to the sea and a free port at Haifa. [S] FRUS 1950 V d382 (Shiloah, 3 March 1950). The
  Jordanian side, as reported from Amman: the Israeli draft defined Israel's gains in detail and Jordan's concessions
  vaguely, and the Prime Minister was "green with anger". [S] FRUS 1950 V d381 (Kirkbride via the US Minister, 2 March
  1950). The two accounts blame each other's draft; both agree the 28 February meeting deadlocked. [C on the cause]
- Reports of the agreement leaked; the Israeli and Jordanian delegations at the UN issued formal denials, which UN
  circles did not take seriously; Egyptian delegates said such a pact would not legalise the annexation of Arab
  Palestine. [S] JTA 3 Mar 1950.
- The Syrian Prime Minister was reported (via Reuters, as relayed by Shiloah) to be considering closing Syria's frontier
  with Jordan if the five-year agreement took effect. [S] FRUS 1950 V d389.
- By 5 April 1950 the Arab League had adopted a resolution that any Arab state that negotiated a separate peace with
  Israel without the League's consent would be out of the League and subject to an economic boycott. Israel's
  ambassador told the Secretary of State that this would harm Israel's talks with Jordan. [S] FRUS 1950 V d434 (the
  ambassador's account of the resolution; the resolution's own text and date were not found). JTA 25 Apr 1950 mentions
  the same League threat of expulsion and sanctions [V in substance].
- After the union of 24 April, the King told the US Minister that resuming talks with Israel was premature; Shiloah had
  sent word hoping they would resume soon. [S] FRUS 1950 V d456. JTA 23 Jul 1951b (summarising a New York Times account)
  says the five-year formula was blocked by Abdullah's own political leaders and "nothing concrete emerged". [S]

**Israel's decision point.** This is the clearest decision in these files. The record shows the Israeli cabinet had the
King's offer of a five-year non-aggression pact instead of a peace treaty, and agreed to it in principle (FRUS 1950 V
d370). The options that were really on the table, from the record:

1. Accept the non-aggression formula as a step short of peace, with its costs: compensation to Arab property owners in
   Jerusalem, free zones for Jordan in Haifa, international supervision of the Holy Places, territorial questions left to
   committees (historical: FRUS 1950 V d370).
2. Hold out for a full peace treaty. This was Israel's stated preference before 17 February: both sides had just agreed
   that a peace was "now impossible" (FRUS 1950 V d364), and Israel's ambassador told the US that "the best way to peace"
   was direct negotiation with each Arab state (FRUS 1950 V d434). An option to refuse anything short of peace is
   therefore a real alternative, but no record was found of anyone in the Israeli cabinet arguing for it in February
   1950. Its wording would be a design assumption.
3. The later choice, on 28 February, between Israel's draft and Jordan's narrower one (no five-year term, no
   "non-aggression", no trade clause) is also on record (FRUS 1950 V d382), and could be a follow-up card.

Effects would fall mainly on International Support (the US and the Conciliation Commission welcomed the talks, JTA 3 Mar
1950; FRUS 1950 V d389) and, through trade, on money. The historical outcome is that nothing was signed.

**Fit.** **Yes, a decision card,** or a short chain of two. Window from 17 February 1950. Its options come from the
record. It also gives the annexation (2c) and the assassination (3) a reason to matter in the game.

**Sensitivity.** None of the four sensitive tags. It touches Jerusalem and the Holy Places; the wording should stay with
what the record says (access, supervision, compensation). It names real negotiators; event text should use offices.

**Overlaps and constraints.** `armistice_jordan` (its `sign` option is the prerequisite: a `requires:` link is natural).
The window falls inside both golden runs (`Hash1950` runs to 1950-02-27, `docs/determinism.md`), so adding it moves
both goldens.

### 2b. Jordanian nationality for West Bank residents, 20 December 1949

**Dates.** Additional Law No. 56 of 1949 to the Nationality Law, published in the Official Gazette no. 1004 of
**20 December 1949**. No card anchor proposed.

- The law came into force on its publication. Article 2: all inhabitants or residents of Jordan and of "the Western
  Part, which is ruled by the Jordan Government", who held Palestinian nationality, "are considered as Jordanians" with
  the rights and duties of Transjordanians. [V] UN Leg. Series, Jordan (text, section (c)); Frost 2022, fn. 35 (Official
  Gazette issue 1004, dated 20 December 1949).
- The 1954 Nationality Law later confirmed Jordanian nationality for those who acquired it under Law No. 56 of 1949, and
  for non-Jewish holders of Palestinian nationality before 15 May 1948 who were ordinarily resident in the Kingdom.
  [S] UN Leg. Series, Jordan (Art. 3 of the 1954 law).
- A passports law addendum (Law No. 50 of 1949) was published on 1 December 1949 (Official Gazette no. 1002). [S] Frost
  2022, fn. 34.

This settles the issue's open question ("Jordanian citizenship: the date of the grant is not found"): the grant was by
Law No. 56 of 1949, 20 December 1949, before the union of April 1950.

**Israel's decision point.** None found. **Fit: no card;** a dated line of context for 2c.

**Sensitivity.** None. The 1954 law's "not being Jewish" clause is a fact about the Jordanian law; if it is ever used, it
should be quoted exactly and attributed.

### 2c. Union of the two banks ("annexation of the West Bank"), 24 April 1950

**Dates.** Parliament's decision and the King's signature: Monday **24 April 1950** (in force from 5 p.m.). Israeli
government spokesman's statement: 24 April 1950 (JTA's dateline is the 25 April bulletin; the US Embassy reported it in
the papers of 25 April) [C on the day, below]. British recognition: announced in the Commons, JTA bulletin of 28 April 1950 [S, below]. Anchor, if a card: **24 April 1950** (the 15 April 1950 turn).

**What happened.**

- The Jordanian Parliament, meeting on Monday 24 April 1950 and representing both banks, approved "complete unity
  between the two banks of the Jordan, the eastern and western, and their amalgamation in one single state" under King
  Abdullah. The decision was unanimous in both houses and took effect from the King's signature at 5 p.m. that day.
  Article 2 said that Arab rights in Palestine would be protected and that the unity "shall in no way be connected with
  the final settlement of Palestine's just cause". [V] FRUS 1950 V d455 (the Jordanian Foreign Ministry's text); JTA 25
  Apr 1950 (unanimous approval, reported by Transjordan radio).
- The Arab League had warned that a member that did this would be expelled and face economic sanctions. [S] JTA 25 Apr
  1950. The King said publicly that he considered the League's resolution invalid. [S] JTA 25 Apr 1950.
- **Israel's response.** A spokesman for the Israeli government said the annexation was "a unilateral move and is not
  binding on Israel"; the armistice agreement did not settle the final political solution; no final settlement of the
  frontiers was possible without a peace treaty with Jordan; and the status of the Arab areas west of the Jordan
  "remains open as far as Israel is concerned". [V] JTA 25 Apr 1950; FRUS 1950 V d456, editorial note quoting Tel Aviv
  telegram 298 of 25 April 1950 (same points, and that the government had a "firm intention" to abide by the
  armistice). This is a spokesman's statement; no cabinet decision or Knesset debate on it was found (see "Not reached").
- Israel expected the King to seek more concessions to make up for the Arab League's threats. [S] FRUS 1950 V d456
  (Shiloah to the US, 24 April 1950, as summarised in the editorial note).
- The US favoured including central Palestine in Jordan "at the appropriate time" but found the unilateral act hard to
  approve publicly, and limited itself to an informal expression of opinion. [S] FRUS 1950 V d458.
- Britain gave de jure recognition to Israel and recognised the union at the same time, excluding the part of Jerusalem
  held by the Arab Legion; it treated the armistice lines as the boundary for the time being and said it would not place
  bases in the annexed area in peacetime. [S] JTA 28 Apr 1950 (the bulletin is dated 28 April and says "today"; Hansard was not opened, so the day of the
  Commons statement is not checked here).
- On 4 May 1950 the Foreign Minister told the US Ambassador that a private letter from the King showed him, after
  British recognition of the annexation, "excessively confident" and preparing new political and territorial demands
  on Israel. [S] FRUS 1950 V d464 (McDonald's report of what Sharett said).
- **[C] Day of the Israeli statement.** JTA's report sits in the 25 April 1950 bulletin and says "today"; FRUS d456
  (editorial note) has Tel Aviv reporting it on 25 April from "today's newspapers", which suggests the statement was made
  on 24 April. A card does not need the day.

**Israel's decision point.** What is on record is a public line (the annexation is unilateral, not binding, the question
stays open; Israel keeps to the armistice) and a diplomatic one (keep the channel to the King open; FRUS d456). No record
was found of a choice among options: no cabinet decision, no protest to the UN, no military posture. Writing options
other than the spokesman's line would mean inventing them, which is the blocker the issue already named.

**Fit.** **Maybe; better as a news item** on the 15 April 1950 turn, or as a consequence inside 2a ("if the
non-aggression talks stalled, the King annexes; Israel answers that it is not bound"). As a card it would have one
sourced option. Its effect would fall on International Support (the same week Britain recognised Israel de jure).

**Sensitivity.** None of the four tags. The status of the West Bank is a politically live subject; the house framing
(CONTENT_POLICY section 1) governs the words. The sourced, neutral wording is the documents' own: Jordan's "unity of the
two banks", Israel's "unilateral", "not binding", "remains open".

**Overlaps.** `armistice_jordan`; 2a; the map (the armistice lines note, `research_notes/Map borders/armistice-lines-1949.md`).
After the 1950 golden run (moves `Hash1952` only).

## 3. The assassination of King Abdullah, 20 July 1951

**Dates.** Friday **20 July 1951**, in Jerusalem. Anchor: 20 July 1951 (the 15 July 1951 turn). Israeli reactions:
21 to 24 July 1951.

**What happened.**

- The State Department's memorandum of 20 July 1951 is headed "Assassination of King Abdullah of Jordan". [V] FRUS 1951
  V d563; JTA 23 Jul 1951a and 24 Jul 1951 (reports of the killing and the funeral).
- Abdullah was killed a quarter of a mile from the Jewish quarters of Jerusalem, as the Foreign Minister pointed out.
  [S] JTA 23 Jul 1951a (as reported from Sharett's speech).
- US estimate on the day: the Arab Legion could keep internal control; Israel was unlikely to act militarily unless an
  Arab state moved into Jordan; there was "no evidence" that Israel was connected with the killing. A handwritten note
  on the memorandum adds that Abdullah had been the most cooperative of the Arab leaders toward Israel. [S] FRUS 1951 V
  d563 (an assessment, not a finding of fact).
- **Who was behind it.** The US memorandum calls the information "inconclusive" and says the killing "may have been
  inspired by the ex-Mufti" and was more likely a vendetta than an act of a state. [S] FRUS 1951 V d563. Israel's
  ambassador told the US the rumours of the ex-Mufti's involvement could not be discounted. [S] FRUS 1951 V d434. A
  Cairo broadcast reported more than 500 arrests in Jordan, including two relatives of the former Mufti. [S] JTA 24 Jul
  1951. None of these establishes responsibility. Event text should not assign it; the issue's caution stands.

**What Israel did (the record).**

- The Foreign Minister reported to the President, and senior officials met at the Foreign Ministry on the possible
  effects on Israel; the Foreign Minister might also report to the Knesset's Security and Foreign Affairs Committee.
  [S] JTA 24 Jul 1951.
- The Arab Legion commander in Jordanian-held Jerusalem told the Israeli authorities, unofficially, that the Legion had
  been reinforced in the Old City "for internal purposes". [S] JTA 24 Jul 1951.
- At a public rally (this was the campaign for the Knesset election of 30 July 1951), the Foreign Minister said the
  killing was a political assassination that showed danger for Israel and that Israel must increase its manpower and
  defensive strength. The Herut leader, at another rally, welcomed the end of a peace that would have given up Jerusalem
  and Hebron. [S] JTA 23 Jul 1951a. (The election date is `content/legislatures.yaml`, `elected: 1951-07-30`; not re-checked
  here.)
- On 24 July Israel's ambassador told the State Department that Israel had signed its armistice on the strength of
  Abdullah's integrity and a balance of forces, and that a merger of Jordan into "a great sprawling Iraq", with which
  Israel had no armistice, would change that; Israel's view was that Jordan's status should not change for now. [S] FRUS
  1951 V d434.

**Israel's decision point.** The record shows no military move and a diplomatic line: oppose any change in Jordan's
status (above all a union with Iraq) and raise defence readiness. The options really open are thin. A card could ask
whether to put forces on alert along the Jordan line (the Foreign Minister's call for more defensive strength is on
record; an alert order is not), or what line to take with Washington and London on Jordan's future (the "no change"
line is on record; alternatives are not).

**Fit.** **Maybe; better as a news item** that changes the board (talks with Jordan are over; Jordan is less stable). A
card is possible only with the diplomatic option above as the historical one and the others marked as design
assumptions.

**Sensitivity.** A political assassination. By the house precedent (`bernadotte_assassination` carries `terror`), it
would probably take **`terror`**; that is the maintainer's call. With a sensitive tag: no positive treasury or FX
effect in any option, and `sensitivity-reviewed` before a shareable build. Do not characterise the ex-Mufti or name a
motive in event text: the record is inconclusive, and his wartime record is a separate claim that needs its own source
and would bring the `holocaust` tag question with it.

**Overlaps.** The July 1951 Knesset election (in `content/legislatures.yaml`, not an event), and 2a (the talks it

## 4. The All-Palestine Government, September to October 1948 (and Gaza)

**Dates.** The sequence, with sources:

| Step | Date | Source and tag |
|---|---|---|
| Arab League Political Committee decides on a temporary civil administration for Palestine | 8 July 1948 | [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 39 |
| Political Committee meets in Alexandria and agrees on an Arab government for Palestine with its seat in Gaza | opened 6 September 1948, about ten days | [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 40 |
| Arab League's formal announcement of the decision | 20 September 1948 | [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 40 |
| AHC communique on the formation of the All-Palestine Government | 22 September 1948 | [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 41 (citing *al-Ahram*, 26 September 1948) |
| Meeting in Gaza at which the interim administration decided to consider itself the government | 23 September 1948 | [S] APG declarations, editorial note to III |
| Cable of the Premier to the UN Secretary-General (same text received in Washington, undated, on 2 October) | 28 September 1948 | [V] APG declarations, I; FRUS 1948 V/2 d639 |
| Palestinian National Council convenes in Gaza | 30 September 1948 | [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 42 |
| Declaration of independence, Gaza | 1 October 1948 (28 Dhu al-Qi'da 1367) | [V] APG declarations, II (the text's own date); Shlaim 1990, p. 43 (declaration issued by the council) [C, see below] |
| Saudi recognition | 13 October 1948 | [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d663, footnote |
| Recognition by Egypt, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq reported | by 14 October 1948 | [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d663 |
| Ministers moved to Cairo; none left in Gaza | by the end of October 1948 | [S] Shlaim 1990, pp. 48-49 |

- **[C] Dates differ by source for the founding.** The issue cites 22 September from Wikipedia; Shlaim has the League
  announcement on 20 September and the AHC communique on 22 September; the PASSIA editors note a Gaza meeting on 23
  September. These are steps, not rival dates for one step: announcement (20), communique (22), meeting in Gaza (23),
  council (30 September), declaration (1 October). The only clash is Shlaim's statement that the Amman congress met on
  30 September, "the very same day" as the Gaza declaration, which the declaration's text dates 1 October (see
  section 1).
- The government claimed all of Mandatory Palestine within its pre-15 May 1948 borders, with Jerusalem as capital.
  [V] APG declarations, II and III (Arts. 8, 14); FRUS 1948 V/2 d639 (the cable).
- Its Premier was Ahmad Hilmi Abdul Baqi; Jamal al-Husayni was foreign minister; the National Council elected the
  former Mufti, Hajj Amin al-Husayni, its president. [V] APG declarations, III (cabinet list); Shlaim 1990, pp. 41, 43.
- Britain did not recognise "this so-called government". The US told Arab capitals that it considered the government
  prejudicial to a settlement and "dominated by Mufti", and noted that by claiming all of Palestine it gave "Jewish
  revisionists" a pretext for similar claims. [V] FRUS 1948 V/2 d639 (editorial note) and d640; Shlaim 1990, p. 44.
- Transjordan refused to recognise it and did not let it operate where the Arab Legion held ground; on 3 October 1948
  the Arab Legion was ordered to bring all armed bodies in its area under its command or disband them, and the Holy War
  Army units were disarmed. [S] Shlaim 1990, pp. 43, 47-48.
- The US mission in Tel Aviv linked Egyptian army moves in the south to the formation of the Gaza government and
  thought Abdullah might stand by if Israel fought Egypt. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d663 (14 October 1948).
- Shlaim: Israel's offensive against Egypt from 15 October 1948 ended the government's presence in Gaza; destroying it
  "had not been the primary aim of the operation, but that was the effect". [S] Shlaim 1990, p. 48 (his assessment).

**Israel's decision point.** None found. No Israeli statement or cabinet decision about the Gaza government was found in
the sources read; the US circular (FRUS d640) treats Israel only as a party that might use the claim as a pretext. The
Israeli decision of those weeks was the offensive in the south, which is `operation_yoav` (15 October 1948).

**Fit.** **No card.** One dated line of context in the text of `operation_yoav` or of a Jordan event, if the owner wants
it ("Egypt-backed government proclaimed in Gaza, 1 October 1948"). The 15 September 1948 turn already holds
`bernadotte_assassination` and `latrun_proposal`.

**Sensitivity.** None of the four tags for the government itself. Naming the former Mufti brings a separate
characterisation question (his wartime record), which needs its own source and neutral wording and would raise the
`holocaust` tag. Recommendation: if the item is used, name the office ("the National Council's president") or leave him
out.

**Gaza administration (context only).** The sources read give no start date for an Egyptian administration of the Gaza
Strip. Shlaim (pp. 48-49) records that after mid-October 1948 Egypt treated the Gaza Strip as a military zone with "no
room for a government to operate". The armistice line there is defined in the Egypt agreement of 24 February 1949 (see
`research_notes/Map borders/armistice-lines-1949.md`). **No card;** map and actor context, as the issue proposed.

## 5. Czechoslovak arms, inside the MVP window (15 May 1948 to 1950)

**Dates.** The first contract (14 January 1948 in Wikipedia) and the first Balak flight and the *Nora* (31 March to
2 April 1948) fall before the MVP's first turn and are not re-checked here. Inside the window:

- **Fighter deliveries.** `content/map/units_1948.yaml` already has the first Avia S-199s arriving on 20 May 1948 and
  the first Spitfires flying in on 27 September 1948 (sourced there to Wikipedia). Not re-checked here; no primary source
  for either day was reached (see "Not reached").
- **The Žatec air base.** The State Department wrote in 1948 that, "in open defiance" of the Security Council's
  resolutions of 29 May and 15 July 1948, Czechoslovakia had allowed fighter aircraft, bombs and other war material to
  move "from an air base at Zatec to Tel Aviv", and that it was training volunteers for parachute and other landings in
  Israel. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d568 (undated; comments on NSC 27/1).
- **Arms as a truce issue.** The CIA reported on 5 August 1948 that Zionist agents had stockpiled light weapons and
  ammunition from Czechoslovakia and other Eastern European countries. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d512. The Secretary of State
  told the President on 16 August 1948 of "conclusive evidence of the organized transport of arms shipments to
  Palestine from France, Italy and Czechoslovakia". [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d531. On 14 December 1948 Israel's representative
  in Washington, answering British charges, admitted that Israel had received arms from Czechoslovakia and said Britain
  was still supplying the Arabs. [S] FRUS 1948 V/2 d832. Together: [V] that Czechoslovak arms reached Israel during the
  truces and that the US and Britain treated this as a breach of the truce terms.
- **Training in Czechoslovakia.** The Czech General Staff ran the help to Israel under the cover name "DI" ("Důvěrné
  Izrael"), with a Ministry of Defence plenipotentiary from August 1948. Israeli pilots and mechanics were retrained on
  the S-199 and later the Spitfire at Czech air units and schools; paratroopers and tank crews were also trained. An
  Israeli brigade began forming at the Libavá training area on Friday 20 August 1948; when the DI training centre there
  closed on 4 November 1948, 1,335 people had passed through it. [S] Srdce armády (Czech Ministry of Defence publication).
- **End.** From the end of 1948 the cooperation stagnated for political reasons and was ended at the turn of 1949-1950;
  officers involved were dismissed or demoted and many DI papers were shredded. [S] Srdce armády.

**Israel's decision point.** On record: Israel kept importing Czechoslovak arms during the UN truces and defended this
diplomatically (FRUS d531, d832). That is a real choice (keep importing and risk International Support, or keep to the
truce terms), but it belongs to the truce events that already exist, not to a new one. No Israeli cabinet decision on a
specific contract inside the window was found.

**Fit.** **No card.** As the owner proposed in #135: Czechoslovak arms are the Arms resource's supply in 1948 (GDD,
"Resources and national parameters"). If the owner wants the truce angle, it can be an option or a line in
`first_truce` / `second_truce`. The 20 May 1948 and 27 September 1948 deliveries are already in the units data.

**Sensitivity.** None of the four tags for supply as such. `security` if used in an event.

**Overlaps.** `first_truce`, `second_truce`, `idf_founding` (supply context), `content/map/units_1948.yaml` (aircraft).
