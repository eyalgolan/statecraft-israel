The question (owner, #9, 24 October): for the Compulsory Education Law (1949) and the Law of Return (1950), the date of each reading, the vote count, and how each party voted or what position it stated in the debate, from the Knesset record; and a primary source for the passing dates at [`facts.md`](facts.md) lines 439 and 444 (checked at commit `55dd6489`).

## Short answer

- **Passing dates, primary source found, both closed.** The Sefer HaChukim (the official statute book) prints the adoption date under each law. Compulsory Education Law: "נתקבל בכנסת ביום י"ח באלול תש"ט (12 בספטמבר 1949)", Sefer HaChukim 26, p. 294, issue of 24 Elul 5709 (18 September 1949). Law of Return: "נתקבל בכנסת ביום כ' בתמוז תש"י", Sefer HaChukim 51, p. 159, issue of 21 Tammuz 5710. Both bills' publication dates are printed in the same footnotes: Compulsory Education 11 July 1949, Law of Return 27 June 1950. Both match `facts.md` (12 Sep 1949; 5 Jul 1950), so the [S] in the first row and the unsourced date in the second row can be replaced by these. The "[C] another source says August 1949" in `facts.md` line 439 is not supported by the printed date; where it comes from was not found (below).
- **Law of Return, first reading: 3 July 1950 (sitting 160), read in the Divrei HaKnesset page images.** The Prime Minister introduced it; a vote (no count printed) put it into one debate with the Citizenship Law; the Chair then referred it to the Constitution, Law and Justice Committee, with Bader (Herut) objecting. Nine members spoke before the sitting closed at 20:15. Their positions are in the table below.
- **Law of Return, second and third readings: 5 July 1950 (sitting 162), read in the printed summary of sittings that closes volume 6 of Divrei HaKnesset.** The summary says the Constitution, Law and Justice Committee submitted the bill for second reading with 17 reservations, fourteen named speakers took part, two amendments were accepted, and "בקריאה שלישית נתקבל החוק" (in the third reading the law was adopted). **No vote count is printed there, and the debate pages of sitting 162 could not be opened, so the count is still [GAP].** The date is also [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending] from the statute-book footnote and [S] from a JTA bulletin. "Unanimous" in `facts.md` line 444 stays [S] (English Wikipedia, which cites the Jewish Virtual Library); it is not checked here against any count.
- **Compulsory Education Law: readings and vote count [GAP].** Only the publication of the bill (11 July 1949), the adoption (12 Sep 1949) and one press account of who voted against are sourced. The press account (JTA, dateline 13 September) says the Knesset adopted the bill "before the body adjourned" and that it "was opposed only by members of the General Zionist and Progressive Parties" [S]; 12 September is derived and agrees with the statute book. One member of Herut said in the Knesset on 3 July 1950 that he had voted for it [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].

**Arithmetic (derived, not verified).** 14 Tammuz 5709 to 18 Elul 5709: Tammuz has 29 days, so 15 days to the end of Tammuz, plus 30 days of Av, plus 18 days of Elul, is 63 days. 11 July 1949 plus 63 days is 12 September 1949 (20 days to 31 July, 31 to 31 August, 12 to 12 September). So the printed Hebrew and Gregorian dates agree with each other. 24 Elul is six days after 18 Elul, and 12 plus 6 is 18 September, the issue date printed in the footer.

**The "[C] August 1949" in `facts.md` line 439 and the 18 September date.** The statute book prints 12 September as the adoption date and 18 September as the issue date. Hebrew Wikipedia's article on the law (revision 43549056) lists 18 September 1949 in its box as "תאריך חקיקה" and says the law was enacted "בספטמבר 1949", so a Wikipedia reader can meet 18 September as the date; this is the issue date [S, Wikipedia as a pointer]. No source for "August 1949" was found in this pass; the `facts.md` row does not name it, so it could not be traced. The row can keep it as an unexplained conflict, or drop it; the primary gives September.

**The JTA item of 10 July against the record.** The statute book says the Law of Return was adopted on 20 Tammuz (5 July), and the summary shows its first reading on 3 July; the JTA item says a "first reading" of a "new citizenship bill" was approved on 10 July and attaches the name "Return to Zion Law" and a description of the Law of Return to it. The summary shows that on 10 July the Knesset ended the first-reading debate on the Citizenship Law and sent it on. So the JTA item most likely reports that event under a wrong label for the bill; that reading is mine, from the match of the date, and it is not the JTA's own. Both are kept: the date of the Law of Return's readings rests on the statute book and the record, not on this JTA item.

**Arithmetic (derived, not verified).** 12 Tammuz to 20 Tammuz is 8 days, and 27 June plus 8 days is 5 July. 18 Tammuz is the printed 3 July, so 20 Tammuz is 5 July, 12 Tammuz is 27 June (6 days before 3 July is 27 June, and 12 plus 6 is 18 Tammuz), and 21 Tammuz is 6 July.

### What the law says in the printed text (SH 51, p. 159)

Section 1: "כל יהודי זכאי לעלות ארצה." Section 2(b): an immigrant visa is given to every Jew who has said he wishes to settle in Israel "חוץ אם נוכח שר העליה שהמבקש" (1) "פועל נגד העם היהודי" or (2) "עלול לסכן בריאות הציבור או בטחון המדינה". The printed text has two exclusions. `facts.md` line 444 gives the same two, so that part agrees with the primary text [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
