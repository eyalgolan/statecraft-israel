Researched 26 October 2026. Research note for issue #95 (Yishuv 16, the research part only, opened by the owner's ruling of
22 October 2026 on #49). Research only: nothing here is drafted for the game, no file in `content/` is changed, and
there is no event YAML, option, effect, sensitivity tag or card text. The event files and decision scenes stay paused.
Every URL below was opened on 26 October 2026 unless it is marked as not opened. Every verified item was read by an agent on 2026-10-26; a person's check is pending.

**Purpose.** For each event that #95 lists, the dates, a short factual summary and the sources, with short quotes. It
feeds the 1918-1939 events of [the Yishuv design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md) §6.

was read in full. The riots (1920, 1921, 1929, 1936-39) are given as dates, the official casualty figures as the
sources print them (both values where they differ), and the official findings as the sources word them. No tag, no
judgment, no description beyond that. People are named by role unless they have a Wikipedia article.

  [S], never [V]. [V] is for a primary record opened by an agent.
- **[S]** single or secondary source only (Wikipedia read through the MediaWiki API with its oldid, a transcription site,
  or a page not opened in full). A lead, to be confirmed before it is encoded.
- **AJYB dates.** The Year Book's footnote (p. 170): "Except when followed by an asterisk, dates are only approximate."
  A day printed without an asterisk (for example 3 March, 25 and 26 April, the 29 April Commons answer and 1 May 1920) is
  therefore called approximate here; "MARCH 1*" and the other starred days are exact as printed.
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every value is given.
- **[GAP]** not found. What was tried is listed.
- Quotes are copied from the cited page. None is a speech written for game text. Where the PDF text layer misreads a
  letter (for example "jews", "Ere?"), the quote is taken from a copy that prints it correctly or the quote stops before it.
- This repo's notes use no en or em dashes, so where a source prints a dash the quote stops there or the range is written
  with "to".
- **JTA.** A JTA archive page shows the bulletin date. The event day comes from the dateline of the item in the original
  bulletin PDF. Where the bulletin was not read as a PDF, the day is [S].
- Neutral wording as the sources give it. "Casualties" are the official figures; where an official figure and a
  contemporary press figure differ, both are given.

## 1. Tel Hai (1 March 1920)

- **Date.** 1 March 1920 [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending] (AJYB-5681, p. 231, "MARCH 1*. Tel Hai (near
  Metulah)"). EJ *Tel Hai* gives the Hebrew date: "11 Adar 5680 (1920)" [S].
  The Hebrew-to-Gregorian conversion is not done here.
- **Summary.** An attack by Bedouin on the Jewish settlements of Tel Hai and Kfar Giladi in Upper Galilee (EJ *Tel Hai* [S] says the area "was marked for inclusion in the French Mandate territory of Syria"). AJYB-5681, p. 231: "Attack made on this Jewish Co-operative Colony of thirty-five
  men by two hundred Bedouins led by an Arab sheikh; four men, including Captain Trumpeldor, [...] and
  two girls killed; five wounded. Village burned and colonists withdraw" to Giladi. On 3 March (approximate, see Conventions) the Jews of Metulah and
  Giladi were warned and left for Ayelet ha-Shahar (same page). The item of 31 December 1919 on the page before (p. 230) reads "Jewish colony almost entirely destroyed in course of affray between Bedouins and French soldiers" (Metulah).
- **[C] number killed.** AJYB-5681: four men and two girls killed (six). EJ *Tel Hai*: "Joseph Trumpeldor and seven
  comrades fell in the defense of Tel" Hai (eight), the page printing the next letter as "?". Both stay; no preferred value.
  Trumpeldor's last words, as EJ prints them ("It is good to die for our country"), are a tradition quoted by EJ, not
  a contemporary record, and are not used here as a fact.
- **Sources.** AJYB-5681 pp. 230 to 231; EJ `tel-hai`; EJ `haganah` (which names the March 1920 assault as one cause of the
  Haganah, see event 4).
- **Gaps.** [GAP] a contemporary report of the day beyond the Year Book item (the JTA archive search of event 7 returned nothing for late 1920 and was not run for March 1920).

## 2. The Jerusalem riots, Nebi Musa (4 to 8 April 1920)

- **Date.** Began 4 April 1920 (Easter Sunday, the Nebi Musa pilgrimage); "Spasmodic anti-Jewish outbreaks occurred up till
  April 8, from which date the situation became normal" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending] (AJYB-5681, p. 176, reporting Mr. Churchill's answer in the Commons of 29 April 1920, an approximate date, see Conventions). SURV-I p. 17 dates it "April, 1920 (Easter Sunday)".
- **Summary.** Disturbances in Jerusalem during the pilgrimage; the native police were removed, British troops took
  control and martial law was declared (AJYB-5681, p. 176). A military court of inquiry was set up; its report was "Not
  published" (SURV-I p. 17, note). On 23 April a military court sentenced Vladimir Jabotinsky to fifteen years and nineteen
  others to three years for possessing firearms (AJYB-5681, pp. 233 to 234); on 1 May (approximate) the sentences were commuted (to one
  year, and to six months) (AJYB-5681 p. 234); the High Commissioner declared an amnesty on 12 July 1920 (AJYB-5682, p. 180); and the proceedings were quashed on 11 March 1921 (AJYB-5681 p. 234; AJYB-5682 p. 181). On 6 October 1920 the Treasury began paying "£20,000 for damages sustained by the Jews during the Passover riots of last year" (AJYB-5682, p. 180).
- **[C] casualties.** SURV-I p. 17: "Five Jews were killed and 211 injured" and "four Arabs were killed and 21 injured."
  PEEL p. 50 gives the same figures (five Jews and 211 wounded; four Arabs and 21 wounded). AJYB-5681 p. 176 (the Commons
  answer of 29 April 1920, an approximate date, from "abridged reports"): "Two hundred and fifty casualties occurred, of which nine-tenths
  were Jewish." The press figure of 5 April, "one hundred and eighty-eight casualties, majority slight" (AJYB-5681, p. 233,
  Reuters), is a third value. The official report's own table was not found.
- **Findings as the Survey words them** (SURV-I p. 17, summarising the unpublished military commission): the reasons were
  Arab disappointment at the non-fulfilment of promises of independence, the Arab belief that the Balfour Declaration
  implied a denial of self-determination, and the aggravation of these feelings by outside propaganda and by the activities
  of the Zionist Commission.
- **Sources.** SURV-I pp. 17; PEEL p. 50 (Chapter III); AJYB-5681 pp. 175 to 176, 233 to 234; AJYB-5682 pp. 180 to 181.
- **Gaps.** [GAP] the military commission's report (not published), the Palestine Gazette, the Hebrew press of April 1920.

## 3. San Remo (19 to 26 April 1920; the Mandate allotted 25 April 1920)

- **Date.** "25th April, 1920. The Mandate for Palestine was allotted to Great Britain by the Supreme Council of the Peace
  Conference at San Remo" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending] (SURV-I p. 17). PEEL, Chapter II para. 32 (p. 30): "on the 25th April, 1920,
  the Supreme Council at San Remo allotted the Mandate for Syria to France and the Mandates for Mesopotamia (Iraq) and
  Palestine to Great Britain, who was to be responsible for giving effect to the Balfour Declaration" (the scan prints
  "Traq" for Iraq) [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
- **Summary.** The Allied Supreme Council allotted the Palestine Mandate to Britain and tied it to the Balfour Declaration.
  The news reached the press on 25 April and was confirmed in the Commons on 26 April (both dates approximate, see Conventions; AJYB-5681, pp. 175 to 176: "Great Britain is
  to become the mandatory Power under the League of Nations"). The Chief Administrator announced it to the heads of the
  communities in Jerusalem on 29 April: "The Supreme Council has decided upon the mandate for Palestine ... The mandate has
  been offered to Great Britain" (AJYB-5681, p. 234; the scan's "ottered" is read as "offered"; the omission is marked).
- **The wider dates (19 to 26 April)** are the conference's span, not found in a source read: [GAP]. The Treaty of Sevres
  (10 August 1920, article 95) is dated in SURV-I (chapter II, introductory summary, "10th August, 1920").
- **Sources.** SURV-I p. 17; PEEL p. 30; AJYB-5681 pp. 175 to 176, 234.

## 4. The Haganah founded (June 1920; under the Histadrut in December 1920)

- **Date.** "The Haganah ... was founded in June 1920 by the Labor Zionist Party Ahdut ha-Avodah in response to Arab riots in
  April" [S] (a second encyclopedia entry on the same encyclopedia.com `haganah` page; the page text read does not name that
  entry's source). Same entry: "In December 1920 the Haganah was placed under the direct control of the newly created
  Histadrut" [S].
- **Summary.** EJ *Haganah* (the Judaica entry, [S]): the idea of a country-wide defence body began in the Ottoman
  period (Ha-Shomer's memorandum "at the end of 1912"); after the British conquest some, notably Jabotinsky, favoured the
  Jewish Legion as a garrison; and "The Arab assault on the Jewish settlements in Upper Galilee in March 1920 ..., the
  imminent danger to the settlements in Lower Galilee in the summer of 1920, and, above all, the failure of the self-defense
  activities openly organized by Jabotinsky during the Passover riots in Jerusalem in 1920" ended that position. The entry
  dates the organisation "from 1920 to 1948" and describes the 1920s Haganah as separate city and settlement branches
  led, after the ex-Ha-Shomer members left, by an ex-Legionnaire who was paid by the Histadrut executive.
- **Gaps.** [GAP] the exact day of the founding decision and a non-encyclopedia source for June 1920. The command structure is in `offices-1882-1948.md`, section 5.
- **Sources.** EJ `haganah` (opened 2026-10-26); AJYB-5681 p. 234 for the Self-Defence Corps petition of April 1920.

## 5. The civil administration (1 July 1920)

- **Date.** "1st July, 1920. The military regime was replaced by a civil administration of a type derived from the Colonial
  Empire. Sir Herbert Samuel assumed office as High Commissioner" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending] (SURV-I p. 17).
- **Summary.** SURV-I p. 17: a small Executive Council of officials and an Advisory Council "consisting of ten officials and
  ten nominated non-officials of whom four were Moslem Arabs, three Christian Arabs and three Jews." AJYB-5682 gives the
  contemporary dates: the High Commissioner arrived at Jaffa on 30 June 1920 (p. 179), read the King's message on
  7 July (p. 180), and the Advisory Council of ten (seven Christians and Muslims, three Jews) was appointed on
  21 September and first met on 6 October 1920 (p. 180). [C] the two descriptions of the Advisory Council differ: SURV-I has ten officials and ten nominated non-officials; AJYB-5682 has "advisory council of ten, seven of which are Christians and Muslims and three are Jews". Both kept; not resolved.
- **Immigration.** "26th August, 1920. The first Immigration Ordinance was enacted and a quota of 16,500 immigrant Jews fixed
  for the first year" (SURV-I p. 18). AJYB-5682 p. 181 records the regulations of 13 November 1920 ("Sixteen thousand five
  hundred of the first class and twelve to fifteen hundred of the second may be admitted during 1921").
- **Sources.** SURV-I pp. 17 to 18; AJYB-5682 pp. 179 to 181.

## 8. The Jaffa riots (1 to 7 May 1921)

- **Date.** "The disturbances ... began with a riot in Jaffa on Sunday, the 1st May, 1921, followed by serious acts of violence
  in the same town on the two succeeding days, and local attacks on Jewish agricultural colonies on the Wednesday, Thursday
  and Friday of that week" (HAYCRAFT, Part I, Introductory) [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]. SURV-I p. 18: "1st May, 1921".
- **Summary.** The Commission of Inquiry under the Chief Justice, whose reports are in Cmd. 1540, gave as the immediate cause (Appendix A, resume) "an unauthorised demonstration of Bolshevik Jews, followed by its clash with an authorised demonstration of the Jewish Labour Party" on 1 May in Jaffa, then raids on five Jewish agricultural colonies
  (Petah Tikvah on 5 May and Khedera on 6 May among them). The High Commissioner appointed the commission on 9 May
  (AJYB-5682, p. 182), and on 11 May obtained the Colonial Secretary's consent to stop immigration temporarily (same page;
  "12. Jaffa: Two steamers with Jewish immigrants ... not allowed to land their passengers", assured to last "a fortnight").
  Immigration regulations and the later reopening are not covered here.
- **[C] casualties.** HAYCRAFT, Appendix B (medical statistics): "a total of 95 killed", 48 Arabs and 47 Jews, and "219
  wounded, of whom 73 were Arabs and 146 Jews"; the Principal Medical Officer's estimate of all casualties dealt with by the
  medical authorities was 385 (95 killed and 290 wounded); "the Abu Kishk tribe of Bedouin removed the dead bodies about of 20 of their followers" [sic, as the Appendix B text layer prints it; the narrative section reads "removed the bodies of about 20 of their followers"] from near Petah Tikvah on 5 May. SURV-I p. 18 repeats 47 Jews killed and 146 wounded and 48 Arabs
  killed and 73 wounded. AJYB-5682, p. 182 (a contemporary press item, 2 May): "Forty Jews killed and two hundred wounded in
  course of riots." Different counts; both kept. For the first day alone HAYCRAFT's table (garbled in the text layer)
  gives separate Arab and Jewish totals; not used.
- **Findings as the sources word them.** SURV-I pp. 18 to 19 quotes the Commission: the fundamental cause "a feeling among
  the Arabs of discontent with, and hostility to, the Jews, due to political and economic causes, and connected with Jewish
  immigration, and with their conception of Zionist policy as derived from Jewish exponents". HAYCRAFT, Appendix A (resume):
  "The racial strife was begun by Arabs, and rapidly developed into a conflict of great violence between Arabs and Jews, in which the Arab
  majority, who were generally the aggressors, inflicted most of the casualties." Of the raids on the colonies: "In these raids there were few Jewish and many Arab casualties, chiefly on account of the intervention of the military."
- **Follow-up.** A Supreme Moslem Council was created by an Order in December 1921 (SURV-I p. 19).
- **Sources.** HAYCRAFT (Part I; Appendix A p. 59; Appendix B, printed pages 60 to 61); SURV-I pp. 18 to 19; AJYB-5682 p. 182.
- **Gaps.** [GAP] the Palestine Gazette notice of the commission, the day-by-day records of the raids.

## 19. The Arab revolt, first phase (15 April to 12 October 1936)

- **Dates.** SURV-I pp. 35 to 38 [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]:
  - 15 April 1936: three Jews killed in a hold-up on the Tulkarm to Nablus road (PEEL p. 96 says "the murder of two Jews"; the
    two counts are kept); the following night two Arabs killed near Petah Tikvah. In PEEL's order (Chapter IV, pp. 96 to 97): the
    funeral of one of the murdered Jews at Tel Aviv on 17 April "led to angry Jewish demonstrations. A series of assaults on Arabs
    in Tel Aviv began, and on the 19th April ... Arab mobs in Jaffa began attacking Jews and murdered three of them." The Survey
    gives the funeral date (17 April) and "serious rioting in Tel Aviv and Jaffa during which three Jews were murdered", with no
    separate day for the Jaffa killings; curfews and the Emergency Regulations for all Palestine followed.
  - 20 April: an Arab National Committee formed at Nablus; 21 April: the five party leaders called a general strike (PEEL: the strike
    began 22 April); 25 April: the Supreme Arab Committee, "subsequently known as the Arab Higher Committee", formed with the
    Mufti of Jerusalem as President. It resolved "to continue the general strike until the British Government changes its present
    policy in fundamental manner, the beginning of which is the stoppage of Jewish immigration".
  - 8 May: the National Committees resolved to refuse to pay taxes. 18 May: a labour schedule of 4,500 immigrants for six months was
    announced, and the Colonial Secretary said a Royal Commission would be appointed after order was restored.
  - 11 October 1936: the Arab Higher Committee announced it would call off the strike after appeals from the Saudi, Iraqi and
    Transjordan rulers; "Work was resumed on the following day" (12 October). PEEL, Chapter IV (p. 102): the disturbances "as an organized national movement had ceased. They had lasted six months."
- **Official casualties for the six months (SURV-I p. 38).** Killed and wounded: defence forces 21 and 104; police and
  Transjordan Frontier Force 16 and 102; Arabs 195 and 804; Jews 80 and 308; non-Arab Christians 2 and 19; total 314 and 1,337.
  "These are the numbers based on verified deaths and treatment in hospitals. It has been estimated that upward of 1,000 Arab
  rebels were killed, mostly in fighting with troops and police." [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]
- **Findings as the Survey words them** (SURV-I pp. 37 to 38, quoting the Royal Commission's causes): the underlying causes were
  "the desire of the Arabs for national independence and their hatred and fear of the establishment of the Jewish national
  home"; the Survey adds that, as in 1933 and "not in common with the disorders of 1920, 1921 and 1929", there was "the direction
  of its aim mainly at the Palestine Government rather than at the Jews".
- **Sources.** SURV-I pp. 34 to 38; PEEL pp. 96 to 97, 102.
