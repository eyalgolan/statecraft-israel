## 1. The White Paper (17 May 1939)

- **Date(s).** Published on 17 May 1939 [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]. The Commons debated it on
  22 and 23 May 1939 and approved it on 23 May [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
- **Summary (context only).** The British Government's policy statement (Command Paper No. 6019) after the London talks
  with Arab and Jewish delegations. Its main provisions on the Yishuv: an independent Palestine State within 10 years;
  Jewish immigration of some 75,000 over five years (10,000 a year and 25,000 refugees), then only with Arab consent;
  restrictions on land transfers. The Jewish Agency rejected it the same day (JTA-390518) and the Yishuv held demonstrations
  on 18 May (JTA-390519). Its immigration quota and land rules are the background of the Aliyah Bet events (items 5 and 6).
- **Sources.**
  - JTA-390518, page 1, dateline "LONDON, May 17. (JTA)": "announced this evening its decision to establish an
    independent Palestine state in which the Jews will be restricted to a one-third minority." The Agency's statement,
    "London, May 17", calls the policy "the White Paper of May 17" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
    The same bulletin, "JERUSALEM, May 17. (JTA)": "Palestine Jewry today declared unremitting war against the new
    British policy" [S] (read in the OCR text; the page image was not cropped).
  - JTA-390519 (bulletin of Friday 19 May 1939, No. 139), page 1, dateline "JERUSALEM, May 18. (JTA)", read on the page
    image: "More than 100 Jews were injured today in a pitched battle with police in the center of Jerusalem that
    featured nation-wide Jewish demonstrations and a general strike in protest against the British Government's new
    Palestine policy." [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
  - Avalon transcription of the White Paper [S]: "The objective of His Majesty's Government is the establishment within
    10 years of an independent Palestine State"; "this would allow of the admission, as from the beginning of April this
    year, of some 75,000 immigrants over the next five years"; "For each of the next five years a quota of 10,000
    Jewish immigrants will be allowed"; "25,000 refugees will be admitted as soon as the High Commissioner is satisfied
    that adequate provision for their maintenance is ensured". Arithmetic: 5 x 10,000 + 25,000 = 75,000 (derived).
    A scan of Cmd. 6019 was not opened.
  - HC Deb 23 May 1939 vol 347 cc2129-97 (Division No. 149): "Question put, 'That this House approves the policy of His Majesty's
    Government relating to Palestine as set out in Command Paper No. 6019.'"; "The House divided: Ayes, 268; Noes, 179."
    The debate was resumed from 22 May (cc1937-2056 of the same volume) [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
  - JTA-390524, dateline "LONDON, May 23. (JTA)": "The vote in the House of Commons was 268 to 179." [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].

## 2. The war and the Yishuv's stance (September 1939; decision scene)

- **Date(s).** The Jewish Agency president's letter to the Prime Minister is dated 29 August 1939 and the reply 2
  September 1939; both were made public on 5 September 1939 (JTA-390906). The registration of Jewish men and women for
  national service was announced in Jerusalem on 4 September 1939 (JTA-390905) [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's
  check pending]. The day Britain entered the war is not sourced in this note.
- **Summary.** Before and at the start of the war the Jewish Agency offered Jewish manpower and resources to the British
  Government while it kept its opposition to the White Paper. A registration for national service of all Jews aged 18 to
  50 was announced, to serve security needs and to help the British army in Palestine.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA-390906, page 1, dateline "LONDON, Sept. 5. (JTA)": the Agency "has placed 'Jewish manpower, technical ability
    and resources' at the disposal of the British Government and has received a reply from Prime Minister Neville
    Chamberlain stating that the 'public-spirited assurances are welcome and will be kept in mind.'" Page 2, the letter
    of 29 August: "the Jews stand by Great Britain and will fight on the side of the democracies" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
  - JTA-390905, page 1, dateline "JERUSALEM, Sept. 4. (JTA)": "Registration of all Jewish men and women between the
    ages of 18 and 50 for national service was announced today after a joint meeting of executives of the Jewish Agency
    and Jewish National Council." Page 2: "In this emergency, Palestine Jewry must maintain a triple front," the
    statement said, "for defence of the Jewish homeland, the security of the Jewish people and the victory of the
    British Empire." The item adds that "opposition to Britain's new Palestine policy was not directed against Britain
    itself." [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
- **Gaps and conflicts.**
  - [GAP] The phrase often attributed to David Ben-Gurion in September 1939, that the White Paper is to be fought as if
    there were no war, was not found in a primary text. (24 Oct rule, 26 Oct run) Searched and not found: JTA archive
    search `https://www.jta.org/wp-json/wp/v2/archive?search=Ben Gurion&after=1939-09-01T00:00:00&before=1940-06-30T23:59:59&per_page=100`
    run 26 Oct 2026: 26 items returned (`X-WP-Total` 26), 0 contain "no war", "as if there" or "fight the White Paper"
    in their text. Read: none of the 26 in full; the test was run on the archive text.
  - [GAP] The Yishuv's decision record (Jewish Agency Executive minutes of 1 to 4 September 1939) was not read.

## 3. Enlistment in the British forces (1939 to 1945)

- **Date(s).** Registration for service, 4 September 1939 (item 2). Figures read: 6,500 enrolled by 25 December 1940;
  over 10,000 in British Army units in the Middle East on 6 August 1942 [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]. The Palestine Regiment was announced in the Commons on 6 August 1942.
- **Summary.** Jews of Palestine enlisted in British units, in the Palestine police formations and in auxiliary
  services. In August 1942 the British Government announced a Palestine Regiment of separate Jewish and Arab infantry
  battalions, without strict numerical parity, and refused a separate Jewish army. The figures below are the ones read;
  a total for the whole war was not read.
- **Sources.**
  - JTA-401226, dateline "JERUSALEM, Dec. 25. (JTA)": "6,500 Jews are enrolled in the British forces in Palestine",
    "according to an announcement by Moshe Shertok, head of the Jewish Agency's political department, at a conference
    here" [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending].
  - HC Deb 6 August 1942 vol 382 cc1265-72, the Secretary of State for War: "there are over 10,000 Palestinian Jews
    serving in units in the British Army in the Middle East"; "There are also nearly 24,000 in various police
    formations" (the report does not say how many of these are Jews); "a Palestine Regiment of the British Army will be
    created at once, consisting of separate Jewish and Arab infantry battalions for general service in the Middle East";
    "There will be no insistence on strict numerical parity." He adds that "if you take the Air and other Forces, I
    daresay the number will come up to the 14,000 which, I think, was mentioned by" a Member [V, agent read 2026-10-26;
    a person's check pending].
  - JTA-420807, dateline "LONDON, Aug. 6. (JTA)": "Immediate formation of a Palestine Regiment composed of Jewish and
    Arab infantry battalions for general service in the Middle East was announced in the House of Commons today"
    [V, agent read 2026-10-26; a person's check pending]. The rest of the item (about 2,500 Jewish soldiers attached to
    the Palestine Buffs, a hoped-for additional 10,000) is [S] (archive page text).
  - The Biltmore declaration, point 7, asks "a Jewish military force fighting under its own flag" (item 7) [S].
- **Gaps and conflicts.**
  - [C] "over 10,000" (units of the British Army) against "14,000" (with the air and other forces, the Secretary's
    own estimate): different scopes, both given.
  - [GAP] A total number of Jewish volunteers for 1939 to 1945 was not read. The later steps, the Jewish Brigade Group
    (item 11) and the parachutists (item 10), are separate items.
  - (second pass, 27 Oct) Narrowed, not closed: no single total for 1939 to 1945 was found. The JTA bulletins below were
    read on the page images on 2026-10-27; each figure is what the bulletin prints, as the bulletin attributes it.
    - JTA-430328, page 2, dateline "JERUSALEM, March 26. (JTA)": "Dr. Bernard Joseph, legal adviser of the Jewish Agency,
      disclosed that 120 Jewish men in the armed forces are listed as killed, 300 are missing and about 1,300 have been
      taken captive. He said that 28,000 Jewish men and women are now serving in the armed forces and approximately
