Research draft, 2026-10-25. Refs #93. Sources were read by AI agents on that date;
a person's factual check is pending. This is facts-only research, not event YAML,
card text, gameplay values or a sensitivity decision.

## Scope and evidence convention

The [research ruling](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/93#issuecomment-6340381721)
opens dates, short factual summaries and sources; code, game content and decision
scenes remain paused. The [owner's scope/read-label confirmation](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/93#issuecomment-6397045275)
applies. Fifteen named anchors and five contexts have partial footholds, not complete
histories. The issue's “about 30” and the [Yishuv design, §§6 and 10.2](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/blob/a1e2237663646482f44701f799999c42bc4be3a8/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md)
are scope instructions, not historical evidence or an exhaustive event list.
How this material maps to events or cards is for the owner.

**Agent-read** means the stated extent was opened by an AI agent, not checked by a
person. **GAP** means unestablished in this draft. Planned action, actual action,
report date and later editorial account stay distinct. An editorial reading or
calendar conversion is labelled; it does not silently replace a printed value.

## 2. N05: Anglo-American Committee

[AAC-2](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v07/d548), Truman's message to Attlee of 3 October 1946, embeds text planned
for 4 October. The paragraph beginning “The unanimous report” says it `was made on April 20, 1946`.
That dates the report as attributed in this message; delivery of the proposed text is not established.

[AAC-4](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/angcov.asp), the report cover/transmittal, prints `April 20, 1946`;
[AAC-6](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/angch10.asp), chapter X's closing signature/date statement, reads
`Signed at Lausanne, Switzerland, on April 20, 1946.` These are document/signing
attributions in transcription, not a viewed signed original or release date.

[AAC-1](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v07/d450), circular telegram of 25 April 1946, opens with publication
`May 1 according to present plans`. Its numbered conclusion 2 summarizes immediate
authorization of 100,000 certificates. The 1 May date is a plan, not proof of release or admissions.

[AAC-3](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/92/statement-president-receiving-report-anglo-american-committee-inquiry), Truman's statement dated 30 April 1946, welcomes endorsement
of immediate admission of 100,000 Jews. The editorial NOTE says `The report, released with the President's statement`.
The statement/release account does not demonstrate admissions or implementation.

[AAC-5](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/angch01.asp), chapter I, Recommendation 2: `that 100,000 certificates be authorized immediately for the admission into Palestine`.
It recommends awards as far as possible in 1946 and immigration as conditions permit,
not evidence of authorization, awards or admissions in practice.

**GAP:** the full report, signed original, reception and remaining recommendations
were not established by these selected readings.

subject to Council reconsideration if they changed. The memorandum date is not each vote's date.

**GAP:** original minutes/resolutions and the editors' despatch remain unread.
See the offices cross-reference for the separate office-holder research.

## 5. N10: Resolution 181, selected readings

[UN-2](https://cdn.un.org/unyearbook/yun/pdf/1947-48/1947-48_268.pdf), printed page 247, left-column paragraph beginning “The report”,
records `33 to 13, with 10 abstentions`. Right-column section j dates the 128th plenary
meeting to `November 29, 1947` and reproduces a recommendation for adoption and
implementation of the plan. A recommendation is not an implementation finding.

[UN-3](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/res181.asp), Part I.A.3, envisages Arab and Jewish states and Jerusalem's
special regime after armed-forces evacuation, `not later than 1 October 1948`.
Part III.A provides that Jerusalem `shall be established as a corpus separatum`,
administered by the UN with the Trusteeship Council as administering authority.
These are proposed arrangements in a Yale transcription, not actual establishment.

**GAP:** the full plan/maps, territorial details, legal effect and actual
implementation remain unestablished here. The separate Agency position in §8
is not unconditional acceptance of the 29 November resolution.

## 9. N03 and N15: declaration, appointment and meeting are distinct

[DEC-1](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/israel.asp), Yale's declaration transcription, heads the text `Issued at Tel Aviv on May 14, 1948`.
The paragraph beginning “ACCORDINGLY WE” identifies People's Council declarants;
“WE DECLARE” assigns provisional Council/Government roles from the Mandate's termination.
This is wording and a stated transition, not proof of implementation.

[DEC-2](https://ecf.org.il/media_items/849), ECF's Knesset-credited reproduction, prints `14 במאי 1948`
in the attestation above signatures. That part of the image was read, not authenticated

Wikisource provenance; no new direct Israeli-archive reading is claimed.

This is an attributed encyclopaedia account, not an original appointment decision.
Assemblies §8 adds composition research and a 4 versus 5 May
sitting conflict; its JTA item was inaccessible here.

[PC-1](https://benyehuda.org/read/58770), Ben-Yehuda's digital speech edition, labels a first Council
meeting in Tel Aviv, Beit Ha-KKL; source edition: 1951. Its heading

**Calendar check, CAL-1:** the [documented converter](https://www.hebcal.com/home/219/hebrew-date-converter-rest-api)

**GAP:** original appointment records, meeting minutes, the 12 May vote and
implementation remain unverified here. The linked appointment research narrows the
former research gap; it does not authenticate originals. The issue/spec's

### N08: Exodus

[EX-1](https://www.kkl.org.il/education/today_in_history/exodus-18-7-1947.aspx), departure/clash/transfer passage, dates departure to 11 July
1947, the clash to `בבוקר 18 ביולי 1947`, and transfer at Haifa that night.
[EX-2](https://www.palmach.org.il/history/database/?itemId=5072), opening departure passage, identifies Sète and `11 ביולי 1947`.
These retrospectives do not make 18 July a return-to-Europe date.

[EX-3](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/exodus-1947), article's voyage account, says `On July 18 a struggle ensued`
and describes Haifa transfer to three transports, France and subsequent transport
to Hamburg/camps. It is an institutional retrospective, not a ship log.

[EX-6](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1947-07-30/debates/54a7899b-5193-452a-8e30-032d0ffe3e78/IllegalImmigrants), Commons question 41 and Creech Jones's answer of 30 July 1947,
reports France arrival at Port de Bouc on 29 July while disembarkation arrangements
were still discussed. Short excerpt: `29th July`. It does not date disembarkation
or arrival in Germany.

[EX-4](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1947v05/d793), telegram 3633 of 22 August 1947, opening paragraphs, reports
British intention to return the refugees to Germany and instructs a formal US approach:
`intends to return refugees from Exodus to Germany`. [EX-5](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1947v05/d795), telegram 3286 of 31 August, first two paragraphs,
reports a request to renew `voluntary landing` in France, conditioned on substantial
acceptance before approaching/entering a French port. These are plans/proposals,
not proof that an offer was accepted or landing occurred.

**GAP:** German landing dates, original ship records and disputed casualty
descriptions remain unestablished; no toll or UN causal claim is adopted.

**GAP:** announcement A/protocols unread; no general conscription law follows.
