## Purpose

Fix sourced terms for the offices in §4 of [the Yishuv design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md)
(Hovevei Zion, the Zionist Organization's presidents, the Jewish Agency Executive and its departments, the Vaad Leumi
and its departments, the Haganah command), with the Hebrew name and dates of each new person, so that
`offices.yaml` and `people.yaml` can be filled later without new research. It answers the design's *to verify*
marks in §4.1 and §4.2 (and the §2 mark on the founding of the Zionist Organization, which §4.1 starts from), and
marks vacancies as vacancies.

## Conventions

- [V] read this session in Encyclopaedia Judaica (2nd ed., on encyclopedia.com), in a Knesset biography (archived
  Knesset site), in Tidhar's *Encyclopedia of the Founders and Builders of Israel*, in a scholarly article, or in an
  archive catalogue entry (the Central Zionist Archives catalogue; it records the archive's description of a fonds,
  not a quoted document). These are the authoritative sources available here (see Sources).
- [S] secondary only: Hebrew or English Wikipedia, or Wikidata. Usable as a lead, to be confirmed before encoding.
  (24 Oct) Contemporary press is also [S], never [V]: the Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) daily bulletins, cited as
  "JTA, <date of the bulletin>". The event date is the date in the dateline of the item in the original bulletin
  PDF (the "See Original Daily Bulletin" link on the JTA page), not the bulletin date: the dateline is one day
  earlier in most items and up to two days earlier in the bulletins issued from London in 1931. Where the original
  was not opened, the row says the bulletin date is all that is known.
- [C] two sources disagree; every value is given, and the preferred one is named.
- [GAP] not found in any source read. A gap stays a gap: no holder or date is filled in by inference.
- [VACANT] the office existed (or the design's office runs) but no holder held it in that period.
- Periods are [from, to), as in `people.yaml`. Where a source gives only a year or a congress, that is what the
  table shows; the day is then a [GAP].
- Quotes from Hebrew sources (he.wiki, Tidhar, Weitz, the Hebrew Knesset pages) are this note's translation; the
  Hebrew is given beside them where the wording carries the fact.
- "EJ *Title*" means the Encyclopaedia Judaica article of that title at the encyclopedia.com URL listed in Sources.
  "he.wiki *title*" and "en.wiki *title*" mean the raw wikitext of that page, read on 22 October 2026.

Every [V] in this note was an agent's reading on 2026-10-22, unless the tag gives a later date; a person's check is pending for all of them.

(1 Nov) A Zionist Congress's own stenographic protocol, read on its page image, also counts as a source for the V tag; see §9.

the 1931 Congress, because no one else held the presidency before Sokolow's election; the 1930 resignation day
(27 Oct) [S] 20 October 1930 by the dateline, [C] with one later JTA item (26 October 1930, "Tuesday"), see the paragraph after this one. Weizmann's title in 1929-1931: at the Jewish Agency's first council (Zurich, August 1929) "Chaim Weizmann,
president" (EJ *Jewish Agency*), so he was President of both bodies, as the design says.

The day of Weizmann's 1930 resignation (27 Oct) [C]: JTA, 21 October 1930 (original bulletin p. 1 read, the item is
datelined "London, Oct. 20"): Weizmann "announces that he has resigned as president of the Jewish Agency and of the
World Zionist Organization" in "a letter to Lord Passfield, Colonial Minister, today", so 20 October 1930 [S]. JTA, 22
October 1930 (original p. 1, dateline "London, Oct. 21") says he "yesterday resigned", which agrees with 20 October; only
JTA, 26 October 1930 differs: it says he "resigned both offices Tuesday",
which is 21 October 1930. Preferred: 20 October, the dateline of the first report and of the letter's date as that
report gives it; the later wording is not settled against a primary source. The same bulletin chain has Lord
Melchett resigning as chairman of the Agency's council "today" in the item of 22 October 1930.

Sokolow's start as head of the Executive, 1920 or 1921 [C] (24 Oct): EJ *Zionist Congresses* has him elected at the
London Conference (1920); JTA, 15 July 1931, calls him "chairman of the Zionist Executive since 1920" [S]. en.wiki
*Nahum Sokolow* dates his chairmanship from the Twelfth Congress, 1921 (read through a page summary, so not quoted)
[S]. Preferred: 1920, which two sources give and one does not. The same JTA bulletin (dateline "BASLE, July 14") says
that the new Executive "will be elected late tonight", so on 14 July. The 17 July bulletin (dateline "BASLE, July 16")
says the Congress "concluded its lengthy deliberations at 9.30 P. M. last night", that is 15 July, which is the
date shown above. The JTA bulletin of 3 September 1935 opens "LUCERNE, Sept. 2 (JTA)" and says the Nineteenth
Congress "drew near its end today", that is 2 September, two days before the 4 September shown above. That one is
not settled.

## 4. The Vaad Leumi

### 4.1 Chairman (`vaad_leumi_chair`)

### 9.2 Election days from the protocols

- **Herzl, 1897 (§2, first row).** On 29 August 1897 the Congress elected its bureau "durch Acclamation", with "Präsident:
  Dr. Theodor Herzl" (Cong I p. 9) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. That is the presidency of the
  Congress. In the afternoon sitting of 31 August 1897 the Congress elected the five Vienna members of the Actions
  Committee, "Dr. Theodor Herzl, Dr. M. Schnirer, Dr. O. Kokesch, Dr. N. Birnbaum, Johann Meyer" in the commission's list,
  by acclamation ("Es wird beschlossen, die Wahl durch Acclamation vorzunehmen", p. 163): "Die fünf vorgeschlagenen Herren
  werden durch Acclamation gewählt" (Cong I pp. 163-164) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. In the
  pages read the Congress did not vote on a "president of the Zionist Organization"; a text search of the whole OCR for
  such an election found none (a search, not proof that none took place). So the §2 [GAP] "the day of the election within
  29 to 31 August" narrows to this: chairman of the Congress from 29 August, member of the Actions Committee from
  31 August; the day he became its head is still [GAP]. Dr. N. Birnbaum declined his seat, and "Es werden Stimmzettel für die
  Ersatzwahl vertheilt" for his replacement (p. 164); Herzl's election is not affected.
- **Wolffsohn, 1905 (§2, third row).** In the closing sitting, headed "2. AUGUST 1905", the Permanent Committee proposed
  the seven members of the narrower Actions Committee, "D. Wolffsohn (Köln), Prof. Warburg (Berlin), Dr. Kohan-Bernstein,
  J. Kann (im Haag), Ing. M. Ussischkin, Herrn Leopold J. Greenberg und Dr. Alex. Marmorek" (Cong VII p. 296). A motion to
  vote on the list en bloc was carried "mit 180 gegen 175 Stimmen" (p. 298), and the list was then adopted by a show of
  hands: "Die Liste, die Herr Dr. Hantke im Namen des Permanenzausschusses für die künftige Leitung vorgeschlagen hat, ist
  angenommen" (p. 299) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. The pages read do not name a chairman.
  Wolffsohn's start is therefore 2 August 1905 as the first-named member of the new leadership; the day he became its
  chairman (EJ's title) is [GAP].
- **Wolffsohn, 1907 (§2, third row).** The protocol supports placing EJ *Wolffsohn*'s vote at the Eighth Congress, which the row
  above calls "our inference": at the Eighth Congress, seventh day, Wednesday 21 August 1907, afternoon sitting, the
  Permanent Committee proposed "in das Engere A. C. zu wählen: als Präsidenten David Wolffsohn"; on a separate vote "Die
  Wahl ist mit 132 gegen 59 Stimmen angenommen!" (Cong VIII p. 394, read on the page image at 300 dpi)
  [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. A motion to vote by ballot papers had just been rejected (same
  page). [C] on the count: 132 in the protocol, "135 votes to 59" in EJ *Wolffsohn*. Preferred: 132, the protocol read on
  its image.
- **Warburg, 1911 (§2, fourth row).** On the sixth day, Tuesday 15 August 1911, "Dr. Weizmann", reporting for the
  Permanent Committee, proposed for the narrower Actions Committee "Prof. Dr. Otto Warburg ..., Dr. Arthur Hantke ..., Dr.
  Schmarja Lewin ... und Nahum Sokolow", and the list was adopted en bloc by a show of hands; Warburg then spoke "Im Namen
  der eben gewählten Mitglieder des E. A. C." (Cong X pp. 341-342) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending].
  No separate vote on a president is in those pages. The Congress closed on "15. August 1911, 10 Uhr 45 Min. abends" (p.
  348) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. Warburg's start as a member of the new leadership is
  15 August 1911; the title "president" stays EJ's.
- **Weizmann and the Executive, 1935 (§2, sixth row; §3.1).** The twenty-third sitting is headed "Dienstag, 3. September
  1935, abends", began at 22:30 (Cong XIX p. 640) and ended at "24 Uhr 30 Min. nachts" (p. 659)
  [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]; 24:30 is 00:30 on 4 September (our reading of the clock time). In
  it the Permanent Committee proposed "eine Exekutive zu wählen, die sich aus sieben Mitgliedern unter dem Vorsitz von Dr.
  Chaim Weizmann zusammensetzt", with "David Ben-Gurion" first in the list of members (p. 643). "Dr. Chaim Weizmann ist
  mit allen gegen sechs Stimmen zum Präsidenten der Zionistischen Organisation gewählt" (p. 646), and the other members of
  the Executive were elected en bloc "mit allen gegen 5 Stimmen bei 37 Stimmenthaltungen" (p. 648)
  [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. At the first sitting of the Agency's Council, "Mittwoch, 4.
  September 1935, vormittags", its chairman said that "Gemäß der Verfassung der Jewish Agency ist der Präsident der
  Zionistischen Organisation auch Präsident der Jewish Agency" and that the Congress "der gestern geschlossen wurde" had
  elected Weizmann (p. 723) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending].
  - [C] on Weizmann's election day: 3 September 1935 in the protocol (the sitting of that evening), 2 September 1935 in
    the row above (JTA, 3 September 1935, taken from the bulletin's other datelines). Preferred: 3 September, the
    protocol. This also bears on the row above's remark that the 2 September dateline "is not settled" against the
    Congress's end on 4 September: the closing sitting ran from the evening of 3 September past midnight.
  - On `jae_chair` (§3.1): in the pages read (pp. 640 to 648) the Congress elected Ben-Gurion a member of an Executive
    "unter dem Vorsitz von Dr. Chaim Weizmann" and named no chairman of the Executive in Jerusalem. That agrees with
    §3.1's reading that Ben-Gurion was first named chairman when the portfolios were divided in Jerusalem on 11 November
    1935 [S]. It narrows the §3.1 [GAP] (whether he held the chair from the Congress) to this: the Congress itself did not
    name him chairman in these pages.
- **Weizmann's end, and Ben-Gurion's 1946 Executive (§2, last two rows; §3.1).** JTA-461225, dateline "BASLE, Dec. 24":
  "The 22nd World Zionist Congress closed here today without electing a new president or executive", "at 5 p.m.", and
  "authorized the newly-elected Actions Committee to elect an executive" [S]. JTA-461230, dateline "BASLE, Dec. 29": "The
  Zionist Actions Committee early today elected a new executive of the World Zionist Organization, after defeating a
  proposal by the Palestine Labor Party that Dr. Weizmann be named president. No president was elected. The vote ... came
  shortly before 4 a.m."; "David Ben Gurion was named chairman of the executive" [S]. On the reading that Weizmann's term ended when the
  Congress closed, the [VACANT] ZO presidency in §2 runs from 24 December 1946 (an inference; the Actions Committee again
  elected no president on 29 December), and Ben-Gurion's chairmanship was renewed by the Actions
  Committee, not the Congress, on 29 December 1946 [S]. Both days agree with 
  `research_notes/Era 1882-1948/events-1945-48.md` §3.

### 9.3 How the Zionist Organization decided

The design's §5.2 makes the Zionist Congresses hard events in the Ottoman chapter. What the protocols read say about how
the Organization decided between and at Congresses:

- The statute of 1897: "1. Das Hauptorgan der Zionisten ist der Congress." "3. Der Congress wählt durch Abgabe von
  Stimmzetteln ein zionistisches Actionscomite zur Ausführung der Beschlüsse, Führung der Angelegenheiten und Bestimmung
  des nächsten Congressortes." The draft's §4 seated the Actions Committee in Vienna with 15 members, five living in
  Vienna and elected by the Congress, the others "vom Congress, aber nach Nomination der landsmannschaftlichen zionistischen
  Gruppen, gewählt" (Cong I pp. 151-152); §1 was adopted in its original form (p. 154), §3 "ohne Debatte", and on Farbstein's motion the Committee was
  enlarged to 23 (p. 160) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. The final wording of §4 as adopted was not
  read [GAP].
- In practice the Congress voted on lists that its Permanent Committee (Permanenzausschuss) put to it: en bloc by a show
  of hands in 1905 (after a 180 to 175 procedural vote) and 1911, name by name in 1907 after a motion for ballot papers
  failed (§9.2) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. The ballot papers of the 1897 statute's §3 were not
  used in these three elections. In 1897 the five Vienna members were elected by acclamation (Cong I p. 163), and ballot
  papers were handed out only for the replacement of Dr. Birnbaum (p. 164).
- The 1905 standing orders, §2: "Der Obmann des Aktionskomitees oder im Falle seiner Verhinderung der Obmannstellvertreter
  übernimmt für die Frist bis zur vollzogenen Wahl des Präsidenten den einstweiligen Vorsitz und eröffnet den Kongress"
  (Cong VII, standing orders printed in the volume) [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending].
- Who chose the Actions Committee's own chairman: in 1935 the Permanent Committee proposed "von der bisher geübten
  Tradition abzugehen und die Wahl des Vorsitzenden des Aktionskomitees nicht dem neuen Aktionskomitee zu überlassen,
  sondern selbst vorzunehmen", and the Congress elected Ussishkin unanimously (Cong XIX pp. 643, 648)
  [V, agent read 2026-11-01; a person's check pending]. Read with "bisher geübten Tradition", this suggests
  (an inference, not a statement of the source) that before 1935 the new (large) Actions Committee chose its own chairman;
  it is a different post from the president of the narrower Actions Committee that the Congress elected in 1907.
- Between Congresses the Actions Committee could fill the Executive itself: in December 1946 the Congress left the
  election of the Executive to it (JTA-461225, JTA-461230, §9.2) [S].
