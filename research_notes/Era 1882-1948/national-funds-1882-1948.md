# National funds, the Haganah budget and the currency, 1882-1948

Researched 22 October 2026. Every URL below was opened on that date. Research note for issue #91 (Yishuv 12, the
research part only, opened by the owner's ruling of 22 October 2026 on #49). Research only: nothing here is drafted
for the game, and no file in `content/` is changed. The era rule-file values and `currency.yaml` (#66) stay paused.

## Purpose

Fix sourced values for §5.3 and §9 of [the Yishuv design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md):
the Jewish Agency and Keren Hayesod budgets, the Jewish National Fund (JNF) and its land spending, Kofer HaYishuv and
the emergency taxes, the Haganah budget (the design's anchor: £400,000 in 1946 and £3.3 million by October 1947,
"Perlmutter, cited in the Haganah article"), and the currency dates the design marks *to verify* (the Ottoman lira,
the Egyptian pound before 1927, the Palestine pound from 1927, the Israeli pound, and the par at the boundary).

Issue #91 asked first for "a table of figures with sources, in a comment here". No such table was ever posted: on 22
October 2026 the issue had only the owner's two status comments. This note is that table.

## Conventions

- [V] read this session in a primary source or a scholarly work, on the page image or in the text layer of an open
  scan (all listed under Sources). Tables were read from the page image, and their sums were redone.
- [FTS] read only as a snippet in the Internet Archive full-text search, inside a lending-only scan that could not be
  opened. The words are the search index's OCR, and the page was not seen. Usable as a lead, not as a value.
- [S] secondary only (Wikipedia). The exact page and revision are named.
- [C] two sources disagree; every value is given, and the preferred one is named.
- [GAP] not found in any source read. No value is filled in by inference.
- **Derived** marks a number this note computed. The arithmetic is shown next to it.
- **My reading** marks an interpretation of a printed figure or phrase that the source does not state itself.
- "Page" is the printed page number; "PDF p." or "scan n" is the position in the file when it differs.
- Quotes are copied from the cited page. Where a printed dash between years was turned into a hyphen (repository
  policy), the quote says "dash adapted".
- Raised decimal points (66·7) are written as full stops, and thousands separators that the scan prints as full
  stops (£7.356.000) as commas.
- £E is the Egyptian pound, £P (also printed LP or £) the Palestine pound. No amount below is converted between them.

## 1. Jewish Agency and Keren Hayesod

### 1a. Yearly series

year (October to September), and in some years what Keren Hayesod collected. Kaplan gives a series of "National Funds

- AR 1927 p. 97: "The Jewish Agency spent in Palestine from October, 1926, to September, 1927, £E.590,000 on economic
  and social services". The items (agriculture 158,000, education 84,000, public health 39,000, labour and immigration
  216,000, trade and industry 43,000, national organisation 6,000, Mizrachi 10,000, various 34,000) sum to 590,000.
  The cumulative column sums to 3,989,000. The OCR text layer reads trade and industry as 48,000; the image reads
  43,000, which is the value that makes the total.
- AR 1928 p. 117: "The Jewish Agency spent in Palestine from October 1927 to September 1928 the sum of £P.715,000".
  The eleven items sum to 715,000, including land purchase and betterment 156,000, the Hadassah Medical Organisation
  118,000 and WIZO 20,000 (the OCR reads education as 69,000; the image reads 59,000, which makes the total).
- AR 1929 p. 169: "The Jewish Agency spent in Palestine from October, 1927, to September, 1929, the sum of
  £P.670,000". The ten items sum to 670,000, including land purchase and betterment 199,000. My reading: "1927" is a
  misprint for 1928, because the 1928 report already covers October 1927 to September 1928 with £P 715,000. The page
  does not say so.
- AR 1930 p. 32: "During the year October, 1929-September, 1930, the Agency spent the sum of £P.764,000 on economic and

  expenditure of the Zionist institutions amounted in the year 5693 to £P.454,000": Keren Hayesod 168,000, Keren

- AR 1934 pp. 32-33: "The collections of the Keren Hayesod, which is the principal financial instrument of the Jewish
  Agency, amounted to £P.242,805." Spending in 5694: ten items summing to 166,600. Receipts in 1934: Keren Hayesod

  been spent by the end of 1934.

- Kaplan p. 114 prints "the total income of the Keren Hayesod and Jewish Agency from all sources during these five
  years amounted to £19,950,000". My reading: "five" is a misprint for the 25 years of the paragraph, because 14,532,000
  + 5,419,000 = 19,951,000 (derived) and the same sentence says £9,023,000 of it came "up to the end of 1939".
- Kaplan pp. 113-114 also prints (dash adapted): "American donations to the Keren Hayesod until 1939 (i.e. the

  October, 1939 to September. 1945, of which £2,340,000 was received in 5705. The amount received from America during
  the Jewish year 5705 totalled £1,561,000, or 66.7 per cent. of the total income from contributions." My reading:
  7,356,000 and 7,176,000 are all contributions, not American ones, because they add up to the 14,532,000 total
  (derived: 7,356,000 + 7,176,000 = 14,532,000), and £2,340,000 is the 5705 total, because 1,561,000 / 2,340,000 =
  66.7 per cent (derived).
- [C] 25-year spending: £19,884,000 (Kaplan, Keren Hayesod and Jewish Agency, "exclusive of the expenditure on
  organisation and propaganda of the Keren Hayesod Head Office") against £P 19,977,000 (Survey II, Keren Hayesod,
  1921-1945). The scopes are not the same; neither is preferred. The Survey's split: agricultural settlement 5,892,000,
  urban development 1,364,000, education 2,269,000, immigration 3,604,000, public works 2,823,000.
- Kaplan's spending table (p. 114) has two printed totals that do not add. The immigration and settlement total for
  the whole period is printed 12,586,000, but its four items (3,590,000 + 5,917,000 + 2,005,000 + 1,374,000) and its two
  period columns (5,367,000 + 7,519,000) both give 12,886,000 (derived), which also matches its 64.8 per cent. The

  With 427,000 the Public Services total (6,998,000) adds up and the 2.1 per cent fits; with 447,000 it would be
  7,018,000 (derived).

- Berlin p. 5 reprints General Allenby's Notice No. 1 of 23 November 1917 (from Raphael Dabbah, *Currency Notes of the
  Palestine Currency Board*, 2005, p. 46): "during the occupation by the Egyptian Expeditionary Force of territories
  formally under Turkish rule Egyptian coinage and Egyptian bank notes shall be accepted as legal tender."
- Berlin pp. 6-7 reprints Notice No. 10, signed at Jaffa on 18 January 1918 by Lieutenant Colonel A.C. Parker, Acting
  Administrator of the Occupied Enemy Territory: "Turkish notes are not to be accepted for the purpose of receipts or
  payments, and are now declared not to be legal tender in the Occupied Enemy Territory." My reading: listed Ottoman
  coins stayed in limited legal use until the 1921 notice below. When the Ottoman lira stopped being legal tender
  outright is not stated: [GAP].
- Berlin p. 11 reprints Herbert Samuel's Public Notice No. 36 of 2 January 1921: "from the 22nd day of January, 1921,
  the following only shall be legal tender in Palestine: Egyptian gold, notes, silver, and nickel coins; the British
  gold sovereign, at the rate of 97½ Egyptian Piastres."
- [C] Berlin p. 67 says the Board's notes date "From their original issue of September 1, 1927", while p. 37 gives
  1 November 1927 for putting the currency into circulation. My reading: 1 September 1927 is the date printed on the
  notes, not the day they became legal tender; prefer 1 November 1927 for the changeover.
- Berlin p. 37: the introduction date was moved from 1 October 1927 (a Saturday) to 1 November 1927. Berlin p. 38: "On
  February 9, 1928, Lord Plumer, declared in a proclamation that Egyptian banknotes, gold, silver, and nickel coins,
  which had been legal tender in Palestine, would be demonetized on March 31, 1928." The sovereign "was to be withdrawn
  by February 29, 1928".
- Survey II p. 535: "the substitution of the present Palestine currency for the Egyptian currency which was legal
  tender in Palestine up to the 31st March, 1928. (The rate of conversion was one Palestine pound for £E.0.975)."
- [C] The end date for Egyptian currency: en.wiki Palestine pound (revision 1379420491) says "The Egyptian pound (at the
  fixed rate of £P1 = £E0.975) and the British gold sovereign remained legal tender until 1 March 1928", citing the
  *Palestine Gazette* Nos. 205 and 206, which could not be opened. Prefer 31 March 1928: the Survey and Berlin agree,
  and the article's 1 March may be the sovereign's date (Berlin p. 38 gives 29 February 1928 for it; my reading).
- BoI APB: "It was therefore decided to print "Palestine Pound" on the notes, the currency of the mandate." and "On
  August 17 the government passed a law declaring the notes legal tender, and they were put into circulation on the
  following day."
- Berlin p. 62 gives the end of legal tender as "Israel September 15, 1948 (coins and currency notes)", after Sylvia
  Haffner, *The History of Modern Israel's Money* (1967), p. 128; p. 67 says Board notes "were legal tender in Israel
  until September 15, 1948".
- BoI BL: "The name of the currency was the "Israeli Pound". The new money was introduced in June 1952, along with a 10
  percent compulsory loan levied on cash holdings and current accounts." Its notes carry "Issue date June 9, 1952".

**The par the design asks about.** In 1948 the Anglo-Palestine Bank notes were printed in "Palestine Pound" (BoI APB),
[V]; my reading is that the unit therefore did not change at the boundary. For the 1952 change to the Israeli pound,
no source opened states an exchange rate; the BoI page names only the 10 per cent compulsory loan. A primary statement
that it was at par is [GAP].
