## Where the figures agree and where they conflict

- **Agree on the order of magnitude.** Rows 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 give 6,373 or "more than 6,000"; row 3's soldiers-only 4,795 fits "more than 4,000 soldiers" (row 4) and "some 4,000" (row 5). The MFA's "almost 1%" (row 1) matches the Knesset's "almost 1%" (row 4).
- **[C] the end of the count.** The MFA (row 1) and the Ministry of Defense (row 3) count to 20 July 1949, the day the Syrian armistice was signed (`research_notes/GDD objectives/objective-cards.md`, section 1). The Yesodot quote in the first-pass note says the war "ended on 17 July 1949" (row 2, not re-read). The Knesset sheet's opening paragraph says the war began on the eve of the state's founding and lasted until January 1949 (row 4); it gives no start or end for the casualty figure.
- **[C] the start of the count.** Rows 2, 3 and the first-pass note start on 29 or 30 November 1947; row 1 says "from pre-state days" without a day. All of them include the months before 15 May 1948, so none is a count for the MVP window (15 May 1948 onward). No source I read splits the total at 15 May 1948.
- **[C] soldiers and civilians.** Soldiers: "more than 4,000" (row 4), "some 4,000" (row 5), exactly 4,795 (row 3). Civilians: "more than 2,000" (row 5), about 2,000 (row 12, not re-read), 2,373 or 2,400 (rows 8 and 10, secondary), 931 on the narrower insurance definition (row 12). The subtraction 6,373 - 4,795 = 1,578 is a derived value, not a source's figure, and it does not match any published civilian figure; it is not for a card. The sources do not say the 6,373 and the 4,795 were counted by the same body on the same rules.
- **[C] the IDF English page's "more than 10%" (row 5)** does not fit "almost 1%" (rows 1 and 4). The same page's own parts, about 4,000 soldiers and more than 2,000 civilians, add to about 6,000 or more, which is about 1% of a Jewish population near 600,000 (the 600,000 is the first-pass note's figure; I did not read a population source here), not 10%. Treat the 10% as a slip on that page; it is not usable.
- **Lower figures.** Row 7 (6,074, 1991) and row 9 (Morris, 5,700 to 5,800) are below 6,373. Neither says what it includes. They show the figure is not settled to the unit among historians, which is why a card should say "more than 6,000", not a precise number, unless the owner wants 6,373.
- **Words.** MFA says "killed in action", Yesodot "fallen", the Knesset "הרוגים" (killed or dead), the Ministry of Defense "fell". The owner's ruling uses "killed".

## Check of the owner's proposed wording

"More than 6,000 Israelis were killed in the war, about one in a hundred of the Jewish population; more than 4,000 of them were soldiers."

- "More than 6,000 ... killed": supported by row 4 ("more than 6,000") and rows 1 and 6 (6,373). [V, agent read 2026-10-31; a person's check pending] for rows 1 and 4.
- "About one in a hundred of the Jewish population": the MFA's "almost 1% of the yishuv" (row 1) and the Knesset's "almost 1% of the whole population" (row 4, which says the whole population, not the Jewish population; the MFA says the yishuv). Both read 2026-10-31.
- "More than 4,000 of them were soldiers": supported by row 4. The Ministry of Defense's 4,795 (row 3) is also "more than 4,000" and is the more exact figure, so the sentence is true but understates it; "nearly 5,000" would match row 3.
- The sentence's three parts come from three bodies (Knesset, MFA, Ministry of Defense) and none of the pages I read gives all three together except the Knesset sheet, which supplies the same three claims in round figures. That is the closest single source for the wording.
- The count behind the sentence begins before the MVP window, as above. The card says "in the war", which fits a count from 29 or 30 November 1947.

## Recommended figure

**6,373 Israeli dead, 30 November 1947 (or "pre-state days") to 20 July 1949** is the most defensible single figure: it is the MFA's own number (row 1, read this session) and is repeated by the IDF journal (row 2, first pass only), and by the Jewish Virtual Library (row 6) and Sandler (row 8) as secondary sources. For card text, "more than 6,000" is supported word for word by the Knesset sheet (row 4, read this session via an archive copy) and is true under every row that gives a total except Morris's 5,700 to 5,800 (row 9) and the unread row 11 lead (5,677); 6,074 (row 7) is still more than 6,000. The wording rests on the official 6,373.

There is **no single defensible soldier/civilian split**: soldiers are "more than 4,000" or 4,795 depending on the source, civilians run from 931 to about 2,400 depending on the definition, and no source gives all of them on one rule. The card's sentence should keep to "more than 4,000 soldiers" (true under rows 3, 4 and 5) and give no civilian number.

Whether the 6,373 includes civilians is stated only by secondary sources: the MFA says "killed in action" and JVL lists it under "Losses" without saying, while Sandler (row 8) and the English Wikipedia casualties paragraph of revision 1379281169 ("Israel lost 6,373 people, about 1% of its population, in the war. About 4,000 were soldiers and the rest were civilians.") read it as soldiers plus civilians. The MFA's words and its mention of new immigrants and foreign volunteers leave the point open, so a person should settle it against the Yesodot article or the Yizkor database before the card says "people" or "Israelis" of the whole 6,373.

This is a finding, not a decision: the owner picks the wording and the value.
