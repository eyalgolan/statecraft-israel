# Rules of the GDD v0.1 MVP

The rules of the 1948–1951 MVP in [docs/GDD.md](../GDD.md), as Phase 1 built them behind the `systems.loop` switch
([the approved plan](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-gdd-v0.1-impact-and-plan.md), §6). Since the cutover (Phase 2 PR 1)
the real game runs them. Balance values are the owner's.

**15 October 2026 (owner, Q-CUT-1: "Labelled design values").** From the cutover, every balance number is a labelled
design value in `content/`, tagged on its line and listed by `DesignValuesTests` (R-LOOP-005). This replaces controller
ruling 2 of 12 October 2026, under which placeholders lived only in the dev and test overlay, never in `content/`.

## Turns

R-TURN-001: The turn length on each security rung (1 to 5) is read from the rules key `turn_lengths` of the era the turn starts in. Without the key it is quarter, quarter, quarter, month, week, the legacy table. The real game sets all five to month (D-B1), so turns from 15 May 1948 end on the 15th of each month.

R-TURN-002: An event that asks for a decision opens its window on the 15th on or before the historical decision, so the card reaches the player on the turn before it (#567 §4, owner, 15 October 2026). The exceptions are listed in the tests: `first_truce` (+5 days) and `rhodes_talks` (+2 days) arrive late by design, `israel_bonds` opens on its historical date (10 May 1951): the owner chose "Keep it on 10 May and record this as an exception to rule R-TURN-002" (4 November 2026, #2551); `hula_drainage` opens on 7 March 1951, the day of General Riley's memo asking that the works stop (they began about 14 February), so it comes on 15 March: the owner chose "Record as exception (Recommended)" (6 November 2026, #2551, #2833); and the five war cards PR 1a rewrites as decisions during or after the fact.

R-TURN-003: In the historical run, no more than 6 months pass from one card to the next (or from the last card to the game's end), and no more than 2 when the country is at full war (security level 5) on any turn of the stretch (owner, 15 October 2026, Q-CUT-8: "6 months, 2 in wartime"; the M3 gap ruling restated in months).

R-TURN-004: A move (`Move`) and a unit order (`UnitOrder`) other than Send home take effect at once and are final (the owner, 23 October 2026: "No undo, like Civ"): each is committed to the turn in progress, in the order given, and the game shows the state after them (`Simulation.Preview`). Every other order waits for End turn, may be changed (and withdrawn where the panel offers it) until then, and costs and runs as before. End turn runs the route step (R-MOVE-009), then the committed orders in the order given, then the waiting orders in the order queued, except that dollar conversions queued after the first Arms purchase run just before it, in their queue order (R-MONEY-004, R-ARMS-002), as one batch (`Simulation.Step`), so a waiting order never changes the result of a committed one (spec 2026-10-23 section 3).

Then changed (4 Nov 2026): the owner chose the option labelled "Reword the rule", whose text read "Change R-TURN-004 to "may be changed (and withdrawn where the panel offers it)"; no UI work." (maintainer reading, not part of the owner's option: in today's UI some waiting orders cannot be changed once given. An event choice and a capture or occupation choice, once made, stand until End turn (their card no longer shows: `EventCardPresenter.cs:226-230`, `CaptureDialogPresenter.cs:110-149`); Reinforce, StopWork, CancelProject and Send home cannot be changed once queued (their control hides or is disabled: `InfoPanelPresenter.cs:387-396`, `:862`; `GddHexPanel.cs:257-272`, `:341-343`).)

## The loop switch

R-LOOP-001: `systems.loop` in eras.yaml is `legacy` (the default) or `gdd`, and every era has the same value. Its ruleset id, which a save records, is `legacy` or `gdd-0.1`. Optional `systems.ruleset` selects the save identity explicitly: `legacy` requires the legacy loop, while `gdd-0.1` and `gdd-0.3` require the GDD loop. Absent means the original loop-based inference. Every era must resolve to the same identity. Unknown, empty or incompatible selectors are refused at load; `gdd-0.3` also requires a non-null `gdd03-balance.yaml` catalogue. The shared `Content.UsesAttention` predicate describes this identity. Shipping eras still select GDD 0.1 implicitly (#1687, part 3). Since #1688 part 1 the sim reads the predicate: gdd-0.3 has no Attention (R-GOV-002).

R-LOOP-002: The GDD state (cities, units, the next unit id, completed objectives, the score) is written into the state hash only when it is set. With the legacy loop the hash text is unchanged, byte for byte.

R-LOOP-004: The GDD game ends on the rules key `mvp.end`, 15 January 1952 in the real content (owner, 15 October 2026, D-E5: "End on 15 Jan 1952"), after 44 monthly turns. The end is a boundary the last turn reaches, like the last era's end; the game is then over (`mvp_ended`) and raises no new event, except the card of an objective that turn recorded, or of an earlier one whose card has not fired yet (R-OBJ-003). It is the same in every era, after the start and before the last era's end (so the game ends as `mvp_ended`, not `era_ended`). The eras after it stay in the repository and load (G2).

R-LOOP-005: Every balance number of the GDD loop (all of `cities.yaml` and `unit_types.yaml`, the rules' `mvp:` block, and the GDD orders' `attention_cost`) carries the tag `# DESIGN VALUE: owner-set balance, not a historical figure` and its decision row on its own line, and `DesignValuesTests` lists every one of them: a number missing from the list, or a listed one without the tag, fails. For the separately identified gdd-0.3 ruleset, the design catalogue is `content/gdd03-balance.yaml`, transcribed from #1668 `docs/gdd/balance.md` at 605277df: one key per source key (including project and biome rows), with Agreed, Default or Deferred basis and provenance; mixed rows label their parts separately. `DesignValuesTests` checks this inventory and the basis distinctions. Deferred `start.army` remains symbol `A`: the loader refuses any numeric guess or unapproved relabelling, pending #1717 and validation of the full 65-unit partition. The catalogue does not switch either existing loop or supply production defaults; legacy/gdd-0.1 keep the preceding tagged-value contract. The rows #2115 lists, which the owner ruled on after the transcription (30 October and 1 November 2026), hold the ruling's value and their note names the ruling and its date; of them, the Open-mode start rows (D2), the transit charge and port-queue penalty (post-MVP) and the declined unpaid-upkeep deterioration are Default, not Agreed. Other post-MVP rows that the inactive fixtures read as Agreed keep that label for now (#2115). See [the catalogue mapping](../gdd03-catalogue.md) for derived/mixed bases and the decision summaries of the six owner rulings (the rulings as posted are linked there). Under gdd-0.3, Attention is absent: no pool, price or cost-map requirement; the older tagged Attention contract remains only legacy/gdd-0.1 (#1688, part 1). For gdd-0.3, current owner: [loop-switch](../gdd/loop-switch.md).

The ID R-LOOP-003 (the Phase 1 overlay, `MvpSlice.Overlay`) is retired with the overlay at the cutover and is not reused.

Synthetic #1716 fixtures provide an internal immutable monthly snapshot for future gdd-0.3 work (R-LOOP-001/002; #1668, loop-switch.md:17-39 at 605277df). There is no shipping caller, resolver integration, persistence or activation. Consumers must revalidate eligibility; R-LOOP-004 is unchanged. The month's work and course snapshot is taken after that turn's orders (Amir's 28 October answers 4 and 5, [owner-relayed](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1668#issuecomment-6465662174): "Training: occupies people from the turn training starts until it finishes"; "Job changes: take effect immediately. assigning the same job is a no op"). A job change made this turn is in this month's snapshot, a re-assignment to the same job leaves the state and its hash unchanged, and a course started this turn shows its pupil on the course and off work in the same snapshot (R-CITY-001/002, R-TRAIN-005, R-TRAIN-008). When a course end or a cancel releases people was answered on 1 November 2026 ([#1668 Q43(b)/(c)](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1668#issuecomment-6553884547)). A cancel releases them at once: this month's snapshot shows the teacher and pupil without a job, a job given that turn is in it, and either may start another course that month (R-TRAIN-007, R-TRAIN-008). Graduation releases them in the turn the last course turn counts: neither can start a course that month, and since the graduation month's snapshot still shows the course, work starts from the next month's snapshot (R-TRAIN-006, R-TRAIN-008).

## Settlement control

R-CTRL-001: Only a settlement in `settlement_targets.yaml` may be captured. Each target is an `arab_town` row of `map/settlements_1948.yaml` (Q-TGT-1 (a), owner ruling of 18 October 2026), stands on a land hex of `map/hexes.json` that is its own hex in `map/settlement_hexes.yaml` (one named exception: Ramla's target is (-3, 24), the neighbour nearest Ramla's point, because at 4.4 km Ramla shares its hex (-4, 24) with Rehovot and a unit there would capture it; owner on #1796, 26 October 2026, "Contested + move target"; `ContentLoader.TargetOnANeighbour`; a retreat or a Raid's withdrawal never ends on that hex, and a depopulated Ramla closes that hex, not Rehovot's), names a sourced operation (each source an https URL with a `checked` date) and carries a review status; any other row is a load error naming file and field. The shipped list holds the six rows the owner approved on 19 October 2026 and filled on 21 October 2026 (#793: jaffa, acre, nazareth, lod, ramla, beersheba; Gaza excluded), which the owner sensitivity-reviewed on #793. #799 adds each row's residents outcome (R-CTRL-016) and returns every row to `status: draft` until the owner reviews those fields, so the release gate holds them (D-F5).

R-CTRL-002: Settlement control is an ordinal `SortedDictionary<string, SettlementControl>`. It is written to the state hash as `ctrl{...}` only when non-empty, in ordinal id order; each record field is written only when it differs from its default (R-LOOP-002). An ownerless, non-depopulated record is the lasting marker that a captured place returned to enemy control; it retains the capture date and whether the residents fled (owner answer, 4 November 2026, #2542: "Keep a 'lost' marker (Recommended)"). This representation and the rules below are shared by `gdd-0.1` and `gdd-0.3`; the legacy loop remains unchanged. On the map, a settlement-control record overlays only that settlement's displayed hex on the dated historical control layer: an Israeli owner uses the Israeli colour and an ownerless record the enemy colour; fronts, zones and armistice lines remain dated display data. The selected-hex panel shows the live holder first and the settlement state detail second (coordinator decision on #2542); with no live record, both map and panel keep the dated path unchanged.

R-CTRL-003: Residents and combatants are distinct fields. Residents are a state, inhabited, fled or depopulated, not a count (Q-CAP-2 (b)). Fled is the record's `Fled`, set at capture from the target row's `residents: fled` and never cleared (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799); flight is not Deport: it adds nothing to `DisplacedByPlayer` and costs nothing. No rule reads residents as combatants, prisoners, refugee units, labour or a resource; annexation is the one place a later owner-approved rule may say otherwise.

R-CTRL-004: The capture check runs after the route step and each order, and still in resolver step 3 after enemy combat, in ordinal settlement id (Q-CAP-1 (a), Q-CAP-3 (a), owner rulings of 18 October 2026). A target that starts under enemy control, one listed in `mvp.enemy_held_localities` (owner, 21 October 2026, #796), is captured at once when Israeli military units are on its hex and no enemy military unit is; only enemy military units are combatants (Q-CAP-3 (a)), and a halutz holds nothing (#796). Passing through a target does not capture it; only a move's or go-to's stopping hex counts (owner, 23 October 2026, OQ-2: "Instant"). Its record names `israel` as owner and has the state `awaiting_choice`; the preview leaves `Since` unset, and Step stamps the turn's end before the resolver, preserving the date through a same-turn answer. The answer stays deferred until End turn (R-TURN-004). A capture remains when the unit leaves; the player's units stay on the hex. A captured target still awaiting the choice that only enemy military units are on is under enemy control again, and its record becomes the ownerless marker of R-CTRL-002. A target not enemy-held at the start (Jaffa, owner ruling of 18 October 2026 on #793, "Only in a what-if") is never captured. The check is an integer rule on unit positions and draws nothing from the RNG. Enemy captures of the player's localities (Q-TGT-2 (c), the plan's R-CTRL-006) are not built yet: with the real starting forces a Syrian unit stands on Degania Alef's hex with no Israeli unit, so the rule would record a capture at the first turn's end (#796). Every ownerless, non-depopulated record is no record for the capture check, so the place can be captured again; the new record keeps its `fled` value and takes the new turn's date. `Stamp` dates every new settlement record, including the stayed lost marker, as it dates Deport's (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799).

R-CTRL-005: Capture writes the settlement state only. It changes no resident field except that a target whose row says `residents: fled` is recorded with `fled=1` and its capture line says its residents fled (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799), no unit, no resource, parameter, city or score, and counts towards no objective (constraint 6 of the Phase 6 plan; impact plan section 4.3).

R-CTRL-007: `ResolveCapture(settlement, outcome)` answers a capture. It is one command kind for every outcome, with one `attention_cost.resolve_capture` (Q-TECH-3, owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 1), set at 0 as a design value proposed on #797 (D-A6: unit orders are free). It is valid only for a settlement whose record is awaiting the choice, including the turn of its instant capture until it is answered, and only for an outcome that is built. The answer remains deferred and runs at End turn (owner, 23 October 2026; R-TURN-004). Otherwise it is refused with a reason, the state unchanged and the Attention refunded: the place is not awaiting a decision, the outcome is not known, or Deport is not in play: with `mvp.deport_in_play` off, as in the real content until the owner has reviewed Deport's cards and dialog (Phase 6 PRs 9 and 10), it is refused as "not built yet" (R-CTRL-018). The legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention.

R-CTRL-008: `SettlementControl.CaptureOptions(content, state, settlement)` lists the outcomes in one fixed order, Raid and withdraw, Occupy, Deport, so Deport is never first (impact plan section 4.1 item 1). An option carries no default or selected flag. Each option has either the reason it is refused or a preview. A refusal carries a stable id from a fixed list and the values its line needs beyond the place itself (for Attention, what the option needs and what is left), so the dialog shows a reviewed line in the player's language and never reads the English reason the command returns (Q-TECH-5, #802). A preview is the change resolving it makes at once to Attention, Money (the Treasury and the debt), Food, Arms, Social cohesion, International Support and the score, computed by resolving it on a copy of the state, so the preview is the outcome (spec section 2.6). A place not awaiting the choice offers no option. A capture the player does not answer waits, and the units stay (Q-HIST-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). On the turn that reaches the game's end, where resolver step 10 sits and before objectives and score, every capture still awaiting the choice counts as Raid, in ordinal id order (owner, 18 October 2026 on #797, "Counts as Raid"): the units leave as R-CTRL-010 says, nothing changes for the residents and the score gains nothing. A unit with nowhere to go stays while the others leave, and the ownerless marker remains all the same. No unanswered choice ever resolves as Deport.

R-CTRL-009: `HistoricalAI` answers every capture awaiting the choice with Raid, in ordinal id order, leaving out a Raid the sim would refuse (a unit with nowhere to go), which then waits, and the reference player sends `HistoricalAI`'s orders, so it does too (Q-REF-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). Neither ever issues Deport, and nothing delegates a capture: `EventSystem.Delegate` answers events only (impact plan section 4.1 item 1).

R-CTRL-010: Raid and withdraw (Q-RAID-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). The player's units on the place's hex, soldiers and halutzim alike, leave in ascending id, each to the first neighbouring hex in the fixed neighbour order (R-MOVE-002) that is land, holds no enemy unit, is not an enemy-held locality (R-MOVE-005) and has room under the stack limit (R-MOVE-003). Leaving costs no movement points, and the units keep their orders, as after a `Move` that ends on another hex: a fortify is lost at the turn's order step (R-ORDER-002); both are defaults proposed on #797. The record becomes ownerless, preserving `Since` and `Fled`, so the place is under enemy control again at once and its residents are unchanged. Raid changes nothing else: no resource, parameter, city, score or objective, and no RNG draw. If a unit has no such hex, Raid is refused with a reason and the state is unchanged, so the choice waits. For a fled place the line says "Its residents fled when it was captured" instead of "Its residents are unchanged" (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799).

R-CTRL-011: Occupy (Q-OCC-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3): `ResolveCapture(settlement, occupy)` keeps the place under the player's control. It needs an Israeli military unit on the place's hex, the one unit that stays (a halutz holds nothing, #796), and is refused with a reason otherwise, the state unchanged. It moves no unit. It changes the record's state to `occupied` and starts its timer (R-CTRL-012), and nothing else: no resident field (the place stays inhabited, R-CTRL-003), unit, resource, parameter, city, score or objective, so its preview (R-CTRL-008) is all zeros. Each turn's step 10, while the place is occupied or awaiting `EndOccupation`, the Treasury pays `mvp.occupation_upkeep_per_turn` for it, borrowing what it lacks (R-MONEY-003); the Occupy turn pays too. The upkeep is a design value accepted by the owner on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6338594839): IL 0.005m, one month of `income_per_job_per_year` for the one population unit that stays, derived as `held_soldier_cost_per_turn` is. It comes on top of the garrison formation's own `military_upkeep_per_turn` (IL 0.05m, R-UNIT-010), so holding a place costs IL 0.055m a turn in all (owner ruling, 22 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6341877796). Money is shown to one decimal of IL millions, so when the Treasury has to borrow for it the Why line shows the borrowed amount, at most IL 0.005m, as "IL 0.0m". For a fled place Occupy's line ends "Its residents fled when it was captured", and Occupy and the timer keep `fled=1` on every record (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799). The owner's rulings of 27 October 2026 on #1712 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1712#issuecomment-6461246243) keep this flow for capture, "Existing Raid/Occupy", and the occupation "3 turns, keep upkeep" (R-CTRL-012); the owner's ruling of 1 November 2026, "30 Oct wins for the MVP (Recommended)", leaves Occupy as written here for the MVP. It holds unchanged under gdd-0.3: a capture, Occupy, a withdrawal and a retake end every turn as under gdd-0.1, the Attention pool aside (`OccupyTests.WithdrawingOnTurnTwoAndRetakingIsTheSameUnderGdd03`).

R-CTRL-012: The occupation timer (Q-OCC-3 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 4: one X for every place). Occupy sets the record's `Turns` to `mvp.occupation_turns`, X, a design value: 3 monthly turns (owner, 27 October 2026, #1712, "3 turns, keep upkeep", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1712#issuecomment-6461246243; confirmed by the owner on 1 November 2026, "3 turns (27 Oct)"), replacing the 6 monthly turns the owner accepted on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6338594839). Resolver step 10, after step 9's arrivals and before objectives and score (step 11), takes each occupied place in ordinal id order and counts one turn off, the Occupy turn included; at 0 the place's state is `awaiting_end`, where it awaits `EndOccupation` (Phase 6 PR 7) and the count stops. `Turns` is written to the hash as `turns=` only when non-zero (R-CTRL-002). The step draws nothing from the RNG.

R-CTRL-013: Withdrawal before the timer ends (Q-OCC-2 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3: allowed, and it becomes a Raid). An occupation lasts while an Israeli military unit is on the place's hex. When step 10 finds none on an occupied or `awaiting_end` place (the player moved them out, or they were lost), the occupation ends as a Raid does: the record becomes ownerless, preserving `Since` and `Fled`, so the place is under enemy control again at once, its residents unchanged, and no upkeep is paid that turn. Nothing else changes, and nothing is refunded. A halutz left on the hex keeps nothing. For a fled place the line says the residents fled when it was captured (OWNER QUESTION 2 (b), owner, 24 October 2026, #799). The owner's rulings of 27 October 2026 on #1712 ("Existing Raid/Occupy", "3 turns, keep upkeep"; https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1712#issuecomment-6461246243) and of 1 November 2026 ("30 Oct wins for the MVP (Recommended)") leave it as written: a withdrawal on the occupation's second turn returns the town and clears its timer, and a retake is a new capture, whose Occupy starts a fresh timer. It holds unchanged under gdd-0.3 (`OccupyTests.WithdrawingOnTurnTwoAndRetakingIsTheSameUnderGdd03`). An operation card's order never takes the last Israeli unit off an occupied place (R-ENEMY-005; owner, 2 November 2026, #2087: "Keep a garrison in Nazareth (Recommended)"). That rule covers only the cards' orders: the player's own orders (a move, a go-to, Send home) and combat (a unit's loss, its surrender, or its retreat after an enemy attack, R-COMBAT-003) can still leave an occupied place with no Israeli unit, and step 10 then ends the occupation as above.

R-CTRL-014: `EndOccupation(settlement, outcome)` answers the end of an occupation (#799). It is one deferred command kind (not instant, R-TURN-004) with one `attention_cost.end_occupation`, set at 0 as a design value proposed on #799 (D-A6: unit orders are free). It is valid only for a place in `awaiting_end` with an Israeli military unit on its hex, as Occupy needs (R-CTRL-011), and an outcome of `annex` or `withdraw`; the unit is checked for both outcomes. Otherwise it is refused with a reason, the state unchanged and the Attention refunded. It draws nothing from the RNG. Saved as `endocc <settlement> <outcome>`; scenario kind `end_occupation`. The owner's rulings of 27 October 2026 on #1712 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1712#issuecomment-6461246243) set the occupation it ends at "3 turns, keep upkeep" (R-CTRL-012), and the owner's ruling of 1 November 2026, "30 Oct wins for the MVP (Recommended)", keeps its outcomes to Annex and Withdraw for the MVP (R-CTRL-015, R-CTRL-016). Each option's preview equals its result (`EndOccupationTests.TheEndPreviewIsTheOutcome`), and annexing a stayed town adds one city and no unit (`EndOccupationTests.AnnexingAStayedRealTownAddsOneCityAndNoUnit`). It holds unchanged under gdd-0.3: Occupy, the three turns, Annex and the unit's leaving end every turn as under gdd-0.1, the Attention pool aside (`EndOccupationTests.OccupyThenAnnexIsTheSameUnderGdd03`).

R-CTRL-015: No puppet regime (Q-PUP-1 (c), owner, 18 October 2026, #783). `SettlementControl.EndOptions(content, state, settlement)` offers Annex and Withdraw, in that order (the ruling's words, "annex or withdraw"), with no default or selected flag, each with its refusal or its preview as `CaptureOptions` (R-CTRL-008); it is empty when the place is not awaiting the end. Withdraw is R-CTRL-010's withdrawal, the same code path.

R-CTRL-016: Annexation and the residents (Q-ANNEX-1, owner, 18 October 2026, #783; OWNER QUESTIONS 1 (a) and 3 (a), owner, 24 October 2026, #799). The record becomes `owner=israel,state=annexed`, keeping its capture date and `fled`, with no timer. A fled place adds nothing else: no city, people, housing, jobs, Food, Money, Arms, indicator change, score or objective progress ("Territory only, no gain"). A stayed place adds one city by the starting-city rule: its row's `annexation.people` (a sourced count the owner approves) in population units (R-CITY-007), housing equal to them, job slots equal to the employed (people times `annexation.employed_share`, the national share 0.439, to the nearest unit), the condition `mvp.housing_start_condition`, and no refugees, recruits or craftsmen, and the same approved people join `GameState.Population` (R-CITY-008; owner, 4 November 2026, #2608, the option labelled "Yes, add them (Recommended)"), in a group of their own, `annexed`, unless an initialized gdd-0.3 ledger keeps the record (the separate group and that exception are this change's design, not the owner's ruling); refused, the state unchanged, if the id is already a city. Each target row says `residents: fled` or `stayed` with at least one source (`residents_sources`); only a stayed row has an `annexation` block, whose `people` must be a sourced whole number; any other row is a load error naming file and field. The town stays Israeli for good: step 10 ignores an annexed record, so the upkeep stops and no unit has to stay, and an annexed target is no enemy-held locality for movement (R-MOVE-005, R-ENEMY-001). A city that exists only in state has no training facility.

R-CTRL-017: A depopulated place (Deport) is never offered or given `EndOccupation`, so it is never annexed (Q-ANNEX-2 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783). The owner's rulings of 27 October 2026 on #1712 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1712#issuecomment-6461246243) and of 1 November 2026 ("30 Oct wins for the MVP (Recommended)") leave it as written. It holds unchanged under gdd-0.3 (`EndOccupationTests.ADepopulatedPlaceIsNeverAnnexed`).

R-CTRL-018: Deport (impact plan section 4.1; owner ruling 1, "honest cost, no reward"; Q-DEP-1 (a), Q-DEP-5 (c) and Q-DEP-7 (c), which includes (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 "Accept all 16 drafts"): `ResolveCapture(settlement, deport)`, only while `mvp.deport_in_play` is on (R-CTRL-007). The player's units on the place's hex leave exactly as in Raid (R-CTRL-010), and Deport is refused the same way, the state unchanged, when one has nowhere to go: it gives no military advantage over Raid and pays no occupation upkeep. Social cohesion (`morale`) falls at once by `mvp.deport_cohesion_cost` and International Support (`intl_standing`) by `mvp.deport_support_cost`, through the permanent event shift. The Treasury pays `mvp.deport_money_cost` at once, borrowing what it lacks (R-MONEY-003), so Deport never pays on Money (owner decision, 22 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1327#issuecomment-6337946480): a design value set at or above the most Occupy can pay in upkeep over the whole game plus the dearest city project, IL 6.25m. The place is added to `DisplacedByPlayer`. From then on, every turn's resolver step 10, after the occupation timers, International Support falls by `mvp.deport_support_drag_per_turn` for each place in `DisplacedByPlayer`, the Deport turn included, for the rest of the game; and International Support's ceiling is 1 less `mvp.deport_support_ceiling_drop` for each such place (never below 0), which every write to it respects. The four indicator values (0.15, 0.15, 0.005 and 0.25, the drag and the ceiling drop per place) and the IL 6.25m were proposed on #800 and #1327 and accepted by the owner on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1327#issuecomment-6350226175); they stay design values the balance pass (#813) may revisit. Nothing draws from the RNG. R-CITY-003: Arrivals from the CBS series (`aliyah.yaml`) become refugees in the cities. Let `P(t)` be the people the series brings from `city_arrivals_from` to the date `t` (0 before it), summed over the origin groups in ordinal order. `city_arrivals_from` is 8 November 1948, the census day the cities' start values count: the census already holds the people who arrived from 15 May, so the cities take none before it (Q-ARRV-3, owner, 15 October 2026). The cities' arrived units by `t` are `U(t) = floor(P(t) / people_per_unit)`, and a turn from date `d` to date `e` adds `U(e) - U(d)` refugees over all cities. `people_per_unit` and `city_arrivals_from` are the same in every era (the loader refuses an era that changes either), so over any run of turns the refugees added are `U(end) - U(start)`: no unit is lost or counted twice. The units are split in proportion to the cities' census `people` (Q-ARRV-1): the first `U` units are handed out one at a time, unit `n` to the city with the largest remainder `n * people / total people - units it holds`, a tie to the lower city id (ordinal), and a turn gives each city its share of `U(e)` minus its share of `U(d)`. Building the split up one unit at a time keeps it cumulative: a fresh largest-remainder split of each total could take a unit back from a city as the total grows (the Alabama paradox; on the twelve cities it first happens at 7 units). The loader refuses a GDD game with no city. With an initialized Historical ledger (the dark start, R-POP-002; #1725 PR 7) each arrived unit is one refugee record instead of a count (GDD Q11: arrivals "take new ids as refugees in a city"), registered with source key `cbs/<city id>` and, as its ordinal, the unit's place in that city's cumulative share from `city_arrivals_from`, so the same units get the same keys whichever turn brings them and a replay of a turn adds nobody twice (R-POP-001). The record is located in its city, in the region that lists it, with reception 0 and no assignment, profession or tier. The split and the city messages are as above; the aggregate city fields are not written, and `PopulationSystem` adds no second count of these people (R-CITY-008). A synthetic or Open ledger is still refused.

## Corridors

Corridor work is step 5 too, after the sites (Phase 4 PR 2, #786; owner, 17 October 2026, #782: the plan's Q-COR-1 to Q-COR-7 and Q-RAIL-1). `content/corridors.yaml` holds each corridor's kind (`road` or `rail`), names, map route and segments; a segment's hexes are derived from the route's traced line by `tools/corridor_hexes.py`. The Money and turns per segment are labelled design values in `rules.yaml` `mvp:` (Q-COR-6). On screen a segment is its corridor's name and its number ("Burma Road, segment 2"), never a waypoint's name (Q-SENS-1). The MVP's corridors are the Burma Road (four segments), the 1949 Haifa–Tel Aviv rail link (one) and the Tel Aviv–Netanya road (four, on straight draft lines between its five sourced places; owner, 18 October 2026, #786, Q-COR-3 (b)). A segment point in a hex the grid counts as sea (the coast) counts in its nearest land neighbour, as a shore town does.

R-COR-001: `PioneerWork(unit, build_road | build_rail, corridor:N)` starts segment N (from 1) of a corridor whose kind the action builds (`build_road` a road, `build_rail` a railway), when the segment is neither being built nor built, with a halutz unit that has no other project (D-D6). Segments may be started in any order, one unit per segment, so several units can build one corridor at once (Q-COR-4); the unit works from where it stands (Q-COR-5). The Treasury pays the kind's `road_segment_cost` or `rail_segment_cost` at once and borrows the shortfall (R-MONEY-003). It is refused, with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, for a busy unit, an unknown corridor or segment number, a kind the action does not build, or a segment being built or built; the legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention. Its Attention cost is 0 (D-A6).

R-COR-002: Each turn, after the sites, in ordinal corridor id order and then segment order, every segment being built gains `progress x m` of a turn of work (R-MIN-002), where progress is 1 or `veteran_progress` with a veteran (R-VET-002) and m is Transportation's multiplier, counting the turn of the order. Whole turns apply at once; the fraction is carried on the segment and hashed as 16 hexadecimal digits of its IEEE bit pattern only while non-zero, as in R-SITE-003. A segment whose progress reaches its kind's `road_segment_turns` or `rail_segment_turns` is built, on the turn's end date, and its unit is idle again. Corridors with a segment being built or built are written into the state hash only when there are some.

R-COR-003: A corridor is complete when every one of its segments is built, in any order. `StopWork(unit)` on a unit building a segment ends it as R-SITE-002 does for a site: the segment is unbuilt and free again, the unit is idle, and the progress and the Money paid are lost (D-A3). A built corridor needs no upkeep and yields nothing in the MVP (Q-COR-7).

## Movement

Phase 5 PR 5 (#744). The numbers are design values this PR proposed (Q-MOVE-1, Q-MOVE-3; owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations") and the owner accepted them on 19 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1073#issuecomment-6259021508).

R-MOVE-001: Each unit type that moves has an integer `movement_points` a turn (`unit_types.yaml`; required, at least 1, on a military type; refused on a population type), and each land biome of `map/hexes.json` an integer cost to enter (`rules.yaml` `mvp.move_cost`, at least 1; the loader refuses a land biome with no cost and a cost for a water biome, so lake and sea are impassable). A path's cost is the sum of the costs of the hexes it enters. A unit's Moves in one turn share its points: a second Move has what the first left, and the next turn has them all again. The points left are the type's points less those the unit spent this turn (`Movement.PointsLeft`). A unit needs the full cost of the next hex, with one exception: a unit that has not moved this turn may enter one neighbouring land hex whatever it costs, and that move spends all its points (the owner, 23 October 2026, OQ-3: "No, strict like Civ VI", "with full points it can always move one hex"). A type with no movement points (the halutz types) cannot move. Real values: recruit 3 (owner, 19 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1130#issuecomment-6269103829), veteran soldier 3, artillery 2, armoured car 5; coastal plain, valley and lowland 1, hills and desert 2, mountains 3. Under exact identity `gdd-0.3` (#1708), `Movement.Allowance` reads `gdd03-balance.yaml` `military.move_infantry` (2) or `military.move_armoured` (3), by type class for either army. The six land rows' `movement` pairs are infantry/armour: coastal plain, lowland and valley 1/1; hills and desert 2/2; mountains 2/3. These are existing Default catalogue values sourced to #1668 balance.md at 605277df. Reachability, path, route ETA, immediate execution, enemy movement, points-left, needs-orders and the UI denominator share this contract, including the full-points exception. Artillery keeps main's value, its type's `movement_points` (2), and pays the infantry column of those rows (the owner, 1 November 2026, Q12 on #1668: https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1668#issuecomment-6553884547); its fire spends the points it has left like any attack (R-COMBAT-001, R-COMBAT-007). No shared content bytes or older identity allowances change.

R-MOVE-002: `Move(unit, q, r)` takes the cheapest path, at command time (spec §2.3). Hexes leave the search in (cost, q, r) ordinal order, neighbours are tried in `tools/hexgrid.py`'s `NEIGHBOURS` order, and a hex reached again at the same cost keeps the hex it was first entered from, so of two equal paths the one through the hex first in that order wins, and the path is the same every run. `Movement.Reachable(c, s, unit, spent)` and `Movement.Path(c, s, unit, q, r, spent)` search with the points left; without `spent`, with all of them. `Movement.Reachable` lists the hexes a Move can end on this turn with their costs, and `Movement.Path` the hexes a path enters; both come from the same search Move uses (spec §2.6). `Movement.Route(c, s, unit, q, r, spent)` is the cheapest path with no points limit, each hex numbered with the turn it is reached on: turn 1 spends the points left, later turns all of them, a step that does not fit what is left of a turn starts the next, and a step costing more than the full points is a turn of its own (R-MOVE-001). Under `gdd-0.3`, the same ordering and search consume R-MOVE-001's role costs and effective allowance; player actions still take effect at command time (owner ruling on #1708, 26 October 2026).

R-MOVE-003: A hex holds at most `mvp.stack_limit` units of one side (Q-MOVE-3; real value 3). The limit counts the moving unit's side only and applies where a move ends; a path may pass through a full hex. A hex never holds both sides' units: entering an enemy unit's hex is an attack (Phase 5 PR 8), so Move refuses it. Under `gdd-0.3`, the limit is the existing Default catalogue key `military.stack_limit` (3), including combat retreats.

R-MOVE-004: There is no zone of control (Q-MOVE-4): a unit passes next to an enemy unit at the terrain's cost, but no path enters or passes through an enemy unit's hex. Units may enter any land hex on the board; the 1948 control layer stays display-only. There is no transport (Q-MOVE-5). The same water, enemy-hex and no-zone-of-control restrictions apply under `gdd-0.3`; water catalogue rows must remain `impassable`.

R-MOVE-005: A player unit cannot enter the hex of an enemy-held locality that is not a curated target (`settlement_targets.yaml`, R-CTRL-001), and no path passes through one: in Phase 6 only Gaza stays closed (Q-CIV-1, owner, 15 October 2026: "No entry until Phase 6"; Q-TGT-1 (a)). The hex of an enemy-held target is open, so a player unit may move onto it, or through it, and capture it in play (R-CTRL-004; the owner's ruling on #793, 21 October 2026, that the targets "can be captured in play", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/793#issuecomment-6322289565; #1304). Raid's withdrawal (R-CTRL-010) and combat retreat (R-COMBAT-003) still never end on any enemy-held locality, target or not, and the enemy's mirror (R-ENEMY-001) is unchanged. The localities are the settlements listed in `rules.yaml` `mvp.enemy_held_localities` (`settlements_1948.yaml` ids; the loader refuses an unknown one). The real list is the owner-accepted DRAFT (19 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1073#issuecomment-6259083180): acre, nazareth, lod, ramla, gaza, beersheba. It is unchecked: no person has yet checked each place's control on 15 May 1948; research task #1096 will check it. An annexed target (R-CTRL-016) is no enemy-held locality: the player's withdrawal and retreat may end on it, and enemy units may not enter it, as any Israeli town (#799). Under `gdd-0.3` these guards remain; a `kind: fort` location grants neither locality capture eligibility nor peaceful entry onto hostile combatants (R-COMBAT-010).

R-MOVE-006: `Move` is refused with the state unchanged and a reason when the unit does not exist, is not the player's (a unit of a side other than Israel), cannot move or is already on the hex, when the hex is water or off the board, holds an enemy unit, is an enemy-held locality closed by R-MOVE-005 or is full of the unit's side, or when no path reaches it within the points the unit has left this turn. It costs no Attention (D-A6). A Move that goes through clears a fortify order (`fortify`, or `fortifying` the turn it is given) and the hex it names at once, so a unit that leaves its hex and comes back in the same turn is not fortified (Q-HOLD-1, "lost on move"); hold moves with the unit; a refused Move changes nothing. Under `gdd-0.3` the same immediate/refusal/order-clearing contract uses the effective allowance and role terrain costs in R-MOVE-001. Moving never removes a permanent fort (R-COMBAT-010).

R-MOVE-007: Roads and rail are cheaper (D-G2; Phase 5 PR 6, #745). A step into a hex from a hex of the same built corridor segment (`corridors.yaml`, R-COR-002) costs the kind's `mvp.road_move_cost` or `mvp.rail_move_cost` (at least 1; the loader requires both in the GDD loop) when that is lower than the terrain's cost; otherwise it costs the terrain's. Only the corridors Phase 4 builds count, not the 1948 roads of `map/routes_1948.yaml` (Q-MOVE-2, owner, 15 October 2026, #375: option (a)). A segment not started or still being built costs the terrain, and a step from one segment onto another costs the terrain unless both hexes lie on one built segment. Real values: road 1, rail 1, design values Phase 5 PR 6 proposes; the owner may override them. A cost of 1 saves nothing on the coastal plain, valley or lowland, which already cost 1. Under `gdd-0.3`, the corridor costs come from Default catalogue keys `military.move_road` and `military.move_rail` (1 each); the same built-segment, both-endpoint and minimum-with-biome conditions apply.

R-MOVE-008: `UnitOrder(unit, go_to, q, r)` gives a standing go-to order and moves the unit at once as far as its points left reach along `Movement.Route`. It is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown or enemy unit, one that cannot move or is already there, water or off the board, a hex holding an enemy unit, a closed locality (R-MOVE-005), or a hex no route reaches. Otherwise it replaces the unit's order, including fortify. On arrival the order is cleared. A route may pass through a full friendly hex; at each turn's stop the unit uses the last hex with room. If the destination is full on arrival, the route stops and the order is cleared. Under `gdd-0.3`, R-MOVE-001 supplies allowance and role costs; no second allowance is applied at End turn.

R-MOVE-009: At the start of each player turn, before that turn's committed and waiting orders, every player's unit with a go-to order advances in ascending unit id along a freshly computed route. It spends movement points normally, including the full-points exception (R-MOVE-001). If no route reaches its target, or the target is full on arrival, it stops, clears the order and needs orders. An adjacent enemy does not stop it (Q-MOVE-4: no zone of control). If no hex it can reach this turn has room, it waits where it is and keeps the order. Under `gdd-0.3`, only an outstanding route continues at the turn boundary; ordinary moves and immediate attacks are not repeated, and the enemy follows the player's actions (owner ruling on #1708). Allowance and ETA follow R-MOVE-001.

R-MOVE-010: A unit needs orders when it is one of the player's military units, its type has movement points, it has neither moved nor attacked this turn, and it has no order (`fortify`, `fortifying`, `hold` and `go_to` are orders) (`Movement.NeedsOrders`, ascending id). End turn waits until no unit needs orders, apart from those the player skips this turn (the owner, 23 October 2026: "Block like Civ"; spec 2026-10-23 section 7). Skipping is not an order and changes nothing in the game.

## Standing orders

Phase 5 PR 7 (#746). Q-HOLD-1, "(a) 1 turn, free, lost on move", and Q-HOLD-2, "(a) Own hex, no interception" (owner, 15 October 2026, #375). The two percents are design values this PR proposes from avlco's option 1 on #746; the owner may override them.

R-ORDER-001: `UnitOrder(unit, order)` gives one of the player's military units the standing order `fortify` or `hold`, replacing the one it had. A new order replaces a go-to too. The unit keeps its order, turn after turn, until the player gives another; moving clears fortify (Q-HOLD-1): in the resolver after combat (step 2), in id order, a unit no longer on the hex its fortify names (`OrderTarget`, "q,r") loses the order. The order is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown order or unit, a unit with no side (a halutz), a unit of another side, a hold for artillery (R-COMBAT-007), or the order the unit already has (fortify on the hex it fortifies). It costs `attention_cost.unit_order` (0, D-A6) and is refused in the legacy loop.

R-ORDER-002: Fortify takes one turn to set and costs no Arms or Money (Q-HOLD-1). Given, it reads `fortifying` and names the unit's hex; in the same resolver step it becomes `fortify`, so the combat of the turn it is given sees no bonus. A fortified unit on its hex defends at `mvp.fortify_defence_percent` of its strength (`Orders.DefencePercent`; real value 125), and from the moment it leaves the hex at 100. Under `gdd-0.3`, the catalogue's Agreed `military.fortify_turns` must remain 1; settled fortify on its named hex adds the Default `military.fortify_bonus` (25) to the other defence bonuses, once (R-COMBAT-002). `fortifying` adds nothing.

R-ORDER-003: Hold acts on the unit's own hex only and does not intercept or stop any enemy move, past or next to it (Q-HOLD-2). It names no hex and stays when the unit moves. A holding unit defends at `mvp.hold_defence_percent` of its strength (real value 100: hold keeps the position and adds nothing); a unit with no order defends at 100. Under `gdd-0.3`, Hold adds no defence bonus; it does not multiply terrain or permanent forts (R-COMBAT-002).

## Combat

Phase 5 PR 8 (#747). Q-COMBAT-1 to Q-COMBAT-4 are the plan's recommendations (owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations"); Q-CIV-2 is the owner's "Enemy units only, no effect" and Q-CIV-4 his "2+3". The numbers (`rules.yaml` `mvp.arms_per_attack`, `mvp.combat_terrain_percent`, `mvp.combat_table`), the loss order, the retreat order and the attack's standing are agent defaults asked on #747 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/747#issuecomment-6297295556) and accepted by the owner on 20 October 2026, all six, as design values for the balance pass #813 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/747#issuecomment-6299184133). The truce refusal (Q-WAR-3) is PR 11, prisoners PR 12, artillery PR 9 and the enemy's orders PR 10.

R-COMBAT-001: `UnitOrder(unit, attack, q, r)` makes one of the player's military units attack the hex (q, r) at once, alone, when the order runs (the owner, 23 October 2026: "Instant, like Civ" and OQ-1: "One unit per attack, Civ"). It replaces the unit's order (a fortify is lost), spends the rest of its movement points for the turn and leaves no attack order. The hex must be next to the unit (artillery: within its range, R-COMBAT-007) and hold an enemy military unit; enemy units on a hex with a locality and its residents may be attacked, and the residents are never a target (Q-CIV-2). The order is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown unit, a halutz, a unit of another side, no hex, a hex not next to it (artillery: beyond its range), no enemy unit, no movement points left or a stock below the Arms this attack needs. Fortify and hold name no hex: one that names a hex is refused. The order costs `attention_cost.unit_order` (0, D-A6). A save writes it as `order <unit> attack <q> <r>`; a scenario takes `q` and `r`. Under `gdd-0.3`, attacks and artillery fire use the same instant timing without an Arms affordability check or deduction; all other validity checks remain.

R-COMBAT-002: Resolver step 2 resolves the enemy's attacks in ascending attacker id: the lowest-id unit with an attack not yet in an engagement leads one, with every enemy unit attacking the same hex. A player's attack is fought when its order runs (R-COMBAT-001), as one engagement with that unit alone and one draw. Each unit's strength is first multiplied by its type's `combat_percent` (R-COMBAT-007, R-COMBAT-008). The attackers' strengths are summed; each defender's strength is multiplied by its order's defence percent (`Orders.DefencePercent`: fortify 125, R-ORDER-002) and by `mvp.combat_terrain_percent` for its hex's biome (real values: hills 125, mountains 150, every other land biome 100). The ratio is attack over defence in whole percent, rounded down, in integers only; the row of `mvp.combat_table` is the last whose `min_ratio_percent` it reaches (real rows: below 1:2, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, 3:1). Each engagement draws exactly one RNG value, a face of the row (six in the real table), whatever its outcome; a step 2 with no engagement draws nothing (docs/determinism.md, "Combat draws"). `Combat.Preview(c, s, unit, q, r)` gives the ratio and row the UI shows (Q-UI-1; spec §2.6), counting that unit alone for a player's attack. Under exact identity `gdd-0.3`, each defender instead counts once at `100 + biome defence_percent + permanent fort bonus + applicable settled Fortify bonus` percent, with bonuses read from the existing catalogue (#1668 military-combat.md:27, additive). Hold and unsettled/mismatched Fortify add nothing. Mountain + fort + settled Fortify is 225% of base defence (50 + 50 + 25); preview and resolution share this integer calculation. The older multiplicative formula remains unchanged. Under exact `gdd-0.3`, after the draw and the losses, retreats and surrenders, the engagement's survivors gain combat experience (R-UNIT-011), drawing nothing more.

R-COMBAT-003: The face gives the population units each side loses and whether the defenders retreat. Losses fall one unit at a time on the side's strongest unit in the engagement (a tie on the lower id; the attackers' losses spare their artillery, R-COMBAT-007); a unit at 0 is destroyed and leaves the state, and its id is never reused (`NextId` does not move). When the face says retreat, each defender left, in id order, moves one hex to the first neighbour in the fixed order (R-MOVE-002) that is land, holds no unit of the other side, has room for its side under `mvp.stack_limit`, and is not a locality closed to its side: for a player's unit an enemy-held locality (R-MOVE-005), for an enemy unit a player's locality (R-ENEMY-001, #2932). A defender with no such hex surrenders and leaves the state: the only way a unit is captured (impact plan §4.3 item 1; its people are counted as prisoners or as the player's soldiers held, R-POW-001, R-POW-005). The attackers never advance into the hex (Q-COMBAT-3). A player's unit's first loss stores the strength it had as its `Full`; only replacements (R-TRAIN-004) restore lost strength.

R-COMBAT-004: Each of the player's units pays `mvp.arms_per_attack` (real value 1; artillery pays its `arms_per_fire` instead, R-COMBAT-007) from the Arms stock when the attack is given; an attack the stock cannot pay for is refused. Enemy units pay nothing (Q-ENEMY-4: no supply). This price rule remains for `gdd-0.1`; under `gdd-0.3`, player attacks and artillery fire neither read nor spend these shot prices (#1706; owner ruling, 25 October 2026, on #1668). Arms stock, purchases, equipment, replacements and training refunds remain governed by their existing rules.

R-COMBAT-005: Combat changes only combatant units, the Arms and, through a surrender, the counts of prisoners and of the player's soldiers held (R-POW-001, R-POW-005), and, under `gdd-0.3`, the quiet-turn count of each front in the engagement (R-WAR-005). No city, locality, resident, building, job or other resource, and no score, changes because of a combat outcome (Q-CIV-2; impact plan §4.3: kills and enemy losses never score). Halutzim are never attackers or targets. Lost strength comes back only through replacements (R-TRAIN-004), which are training, not a combat outcome. Under `gdd-0.3`, attacks and artillery fire leave the Arms stock unchanged; the other combat effects in this rule remain.

R-COMBAT-006: Each engagement's Why line names the hex, the units on each side and the ratio, and states "the attackers lost N people and the defenders lost M people", the strength each side lost times `people_per_unit`, in the same words whichever side attacks (Q-CIV-4; impact plan §4.2). A destroyed unit gets "Unit N was destroyed", a retreat the hex it went to, a surrender the people it surrendered with. No running count of losses is kept; the war's Israeli total is PR 13's.

Phase 5 PR 9, part 1 (#748): artillery's combat role and the armoured car. Q-ARTY-1 and Q-ARTY-2 are the plan's recommendations (owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations"). The numbers in `unit_types.yaml` are agent defaults proposed on #748 and accepted by the owner as design values on 21 October 2026, together with three of R-COMBAT-007's mechanics that the Q-ARTY-1 text does not settle: the attackers' losses sparing the guns, the hold refused as "never holds ground", and the 200% counting on defence too (owner, 21 October 2026, #748, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/748#issuecomment-6317339304). R-COMBAT-007: Artillery (a type of class `artillery`, which needs a `range` and an `arms_per_fire`; real values 2 and 2) attacks alone from range. Its `UnitOrder(unit, attack, q, r)` names an enemy hex at a hex distance of 1 to its `range`; a farther hex is refused with "That hex is beyond the unit's range". Under `gdd-0.3`, artillery still fires alone at once from the existing range and keeps the same outcomes, but has no Arms affordability check or deduction; `arms_per_fire` is not required or read in that identity. Range and combat factors retain their existing validation. The remaining combat redesign is held.

R-COMBAT-008: A military type may carry a `combat_percent` (at least 1; none is 100): its units' strength counts at that percent in the ratio, attacking and defending, in integers (R-COMBAT-002). ## Food

The national Food pool (D-C2, "National pools", decided 15 October 2026), built on the maintainer's food rule of 16 October 2026 (#382, option (b): "food_supply adds a stock term: it rises and falls with months of cover (new design values `k_food`, `cover_months`), rationing and events still act on top, and the unmet share lowers it further").

R-FOOD-001: Food is a national stock of whole units (`GameState.Food`); one unit is one population unit's food for one month, and there is no storage cap (Q-POOL-2, decided 15 October 2026). A new game starts the stock at `cover_months` of the start's need, the need being the national head count in population units (`people_per_unit`), rounded half away from zero; with an initialized `gdd-0.3` ledger (the dark start, R-POP-002) the need is the ledger's, one ration for each living own population id (#2470). `k_food` and `cover_months` are required in the GDD loop (`rules.yaml` `mvp:`, labelled design values); a missing or out-of-range value is a load error. `cover_months` runs from 1 to 120 (`Pools.MaxCoverMonths`), so its multiply cannot wrap the stock (#2804); the stock itself still has no cap.

R-FOOD-002: In step 7 the nation eats the turn's need from the stock as far as it goes: the stock stops at 0, and the rest is the unmet share, kept in the state with the need (Q-POOL-5, decided 15 October 2026). Then a monthly import brings the stock back to `cover_months` of the need, unless foreign currency is exhausted (the `fx_crisis` situation at the turn's start), when nothing is imported (Q-POOL-4, decided 15 October 2026). Imports are already paid for in foreign currency per head (`imports_per_capita_per_year`), so they are not charged again. In the GDD loop the crisis cuts the imports, not `food_supply` directly: `fx_crisis_food_per_year` acts in the legacy loop only.

For R-FOOD-001 and R-FOOD-002 under gdd-0.3 with an initialized population ledger (#2470), the need is the month's: one ration for each living own population id in the month's frozen opening snapshot (the #1716 synthetic month context, `OpeningPeople`), whatever its status or assignment (idle, at work, teaching, on a course, travelling, serving, in reception or held by the enemy), each id once and in any storage order, so a ledger change before step 7 (a combat loss, an arrival) does not move that month's need. The live ledger is reconciled first, so an invalid one moves no Food. The settlement after it is the one above: the stock stops at 0, `FoodNeed` and `FoodUnmet` record that need, and the import refills to `cover_months` of it unless the foreign-currency crisis is on. Counting changes no id, status or assignment. Legacy and gdd-0.1 keep the aggregate head count. Counting a captured own soldier is the owner's decision (5 November 2026, the recommended option, [recorded on #2678](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/2678#issuecomment-6652800885) as "Captured own-population records do eat the national ration"), the reading of `docs/gdd/balance.md` `population.food_per_turn` ("Food per own population unit, every status"); the ledger's head count (`TotalPeople`) already includes them. No turn reaches this yet: only synthetic fixtures create a month context, and without one an initialized ledger is refused (R-POP-002).

R-FOOD-003: `food_supply` stays the 0 to 1 level the UI, the situations and the events read (Q-POOL-3, decided 15 October 2026), with a stock term inside its effect sum: `k_food * (stock - full) / full - unmet / need`, where `full` is `cover_months` of the last need. The term is exactly 0 at a full stock, `-k_food` when the stock is empty, and the unmet share lowers it further; rationing, the other effects and event shifts act on top as before, and the level's clamp to 0..1 bounds the result. The GDD loop requires the `food_supply` indicator. The term runs on a game with an initialized Historical ledger (the dark start, R-POP-002; #1704, #1725 PR 7) as in the aggregate game: it reads only the stock, the last need and the unmet share, never a record, and the need is already the ledger's (R-FOOD-001), so no ledger record changes; a synthetic or Open ledger is still refused (R-POP-002).

## Arms

The national Arms pool (D-G6, decided 15 October 2026: "From events (sourced arms deals) and Money. Training and attacks consume it"). The four numbers are the owner's (18 October 2026, #781: "Approve as drafted"); each is a labelled design value in `rules.yaml`, and the balance pass (#813) may tune them. An event can move the Arms stock (R-EFX-001, R-EFX-002), and the Arms an attack spends is Phase 5 (Q-COMBAT-4).

R-ARMS-001: Arms is a national stock of whole units (`GameState.Arms`, hashed as `arms=` only when not 0); one Arms is the equipment package for one recruit group of `people_per_unit` (1,000) people. A new game starts the stock at `arms_start` (12). It never goes below 0. Under gdd-0.3 an arms workshop's completed cycle adds 1 (R-ARMS-006). `arms_start`, `arms_price` and `arms_per_recruit_group` are required in the GDD loop (`rules.yaml` `mvp:`); a missing or negative value is a load error.

R-ARMS-002: `BuyArms(count)` adds `count` units to the stock at once, with no delivery delay, and the Treasury pays `count * arms_price` (IL 1m an Arms), borrowing what it lacks (R-MONEY-003). It is refused, state and Attention unchanged, for fewer than one unit, a stock that would pass a whole number's range, or a purchase whose borrowing would take the debt past `debt_ceiling` (#2198; owner, 2 November 2026: "Refuse buys over ceiling (Recommended)", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/2198#issuecomment-6564721486), with the reason "Buying that many Arms would take the debt past its ceiling"; the legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention. The debt after is R-MONEY-003's: the debt plus what the Treasury lacks (`Debt + max(0, cost - Treasury)`). A debt exactly at the ceiling is allowed, and a purchase the Treasury pays in full borrows nothing and is never refused at it, even with the debt already past it. This overrides Q22 for Arms purchases; an arms workshop's cycle also waits rather than borrow past it (R-ARMS-006), and every other cost still borrows past the ceiling (R-MONEY-003). The Defence screen shows the same reason before End turn when End turn would refuse the amount for the ceiling with the turn's orders as they stand: it runs them from the turn's start on a copy, as End turn runs them, with this purchase in place of a queued one or after them, so the dollar conversions and any other spending queued before the purchase count (#2206). The turn's dollar conversions (R-MONEY-004) run before its Arms purchases, whatever the queue order: a conversion queued after the turn's first `BuyArms` runs just before it, with the other such conversions in their queue order, and every other order keeps its queue place (#2206). Its Attention cost is `attention_cost.buy_arms` (1), whatever the count. It runs on a game with an initialized Historical ledger (the dark start, R-POP-002; #1725 PR 7) as in the aggregate game, at the same `arms_price`: a bought Arms joins the national stock and is not an issued item on any record, so no ledger record changes; a synthetic or Open ledger is still refused (R-POP-002).

R-ARMS-003: Recruit training (R-TRAIN-001) takes `arms_per_recruit_group` (1) Arms for each group when it starts, after its other checks and before it pays. A stock short of that refuses the order with a reason naming the need and the stock, state and Attention unchanged (Q-UNIT-3). A gun or armoured car crew takes its type's `training_arms` a group instead, by the same rule (R-UNIT-008). Pioneer training takes no Arms. The cohort keeps the Arms it took, and the state hash writes them with it; cancelling the cohort (R-TRAIN-002) returns them to the pool, up to the most the stock can hold, although the Money stays paid (owner, 18 October 2026, #1047: "Refund on cancel"). Replacement groups (R-TRAIN-004) take their course's Arms by the same rule (owner, 23 October 2026, #1510: "Replacements cost Arms").

R-ARMS-004: Under gdd-0.3 only, the arms workshop (`projects.yaml` `arms_workshop`, #1701) is a city project kind: `StartProject` builds it as R-PROJ-001 to R-PROJ-003 build any kind, for IL 15m and 3 turns (owner, 2 November 2026, #1701 OQ2: "IL 15m, 3 turns (Recommended)"; the gdd-0.3 catalogue's `arms_workshop` cost 15 and work 3). Built, it gives one workshop worker slot (`worker_slots: 1`, the catalogue's `arms_workshop.parts.worker_slots`) and adds no housing, condition or job slots: a workshop gives no Industry job (`docs/gdd/cities.md`, "Arms workshops"). Its row carries `rulesets: [gdd-0.3]`, and the loader leaves it out of every other ruleset's kinds, so a gdd-0.1 game neither lists nor starts it (`StartProject` refuses it as an unknown kind) and its state does not change; the row still moves the content hash. The loader refuses an unknown ruleset id in `rulesets`, `worker_slots` other than 0 or 1, a kind with a worker slot that has capacity, condition or job slots or is not limited to exactly gdd-0.3, and, under gdd-0.3, an `arms_workshop` whose worker slots differ from the catalogue's. It pays no upkeep: R-MONEY-002 has no rate for it (the catalogue's 0.50 is a Default). Its Hebrew name סדנת נשק remains a draft until a native speaker, or an AI-assisted contributor who discloses it, has read it (`name_he_review: agent-checked`, #564; owner, 2 November 2026, #1701 OQ8; owner, 4 November 2026: "AI-assisted can clear it").

R-ARMS-005: Under gdd-0.3, `AssignWorkers(city, "workshop", n)` is a standing order that staffs the city's first `n` built arms workshops, in ascending project id, and leaves the rest unstaffed (27M-13, owner-approved 1 November 2026: "Any free civilian. A newly staffed workshop takes one of the city's civilians and craftsmen who hold no job; the worker stays one of them, with no job, profession or qualification change, is not employed (R-CITY-001) and earns no job income (R-CITY-002). On the aggregate cities every worker is priced at the ordinary or novice tier until the population ledger's tiers are live (owner, 2 November 2026, #1701 OQ6: "Accept all four (Recommended)"; R-POP-004). It is refused, state unchanged, for an unknown city, a negative count, more than the city's built workshops ("Town A has 1 arms workshop"), or more newly staffed workshops than the city's people without a job ("... civilians and craftsmen without a job"); under gdd-0.1 the `workshop` role is refused as "There is no such role", and the legacy loop refuses the order before charging Attention ("That order is not available in this game", R-CITY-001). ## Defeat

The game can be lost (D-B5, decided: "Money and Food exhausted for N turns, or a war loss. Values for the owner. Coalition collapse is off in the MVP"; Phase 7 PR 5, #8). This part covers the economic defeat (R-END-001, R-END-002), which remains in gdd-0.3, and the gdd-0.1 war loss (R-END-003). The defeat texts are decided (Q-DEF-4 (b), owner, 18 October 2026, #784): a short factual line per cause - "Money and Food ran out" and "The enemy holds a city" - plus one sourced history note beside each, which goes on the end screen with #812.

R-END-001: Money is exhausted when the debt is at or above `debt_ceiling` (Q-DEF-1, owner, 17 October 2026, #784: a debt ceiling; below it borrowing goes on as R-MONEY-003 allows). Food is exhausted when the national stock is 0 (R-FOOD-002; Q-POOL-5, "The stock stops at 0"). ## Alternate timelines

The alternate-timeline mechanism ([the plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-17-alternate-timelines.md); the owner's answers, 17 October 2026, #945).

R-ALT-001: An event's `requires` is a list, and the event is eligible only when every entry holds. An entry `{ event, option }` holds when that event was resolved (chosen or delegated) with that option. A list names each event at most once.

R-ALT-002: An entry `{ event, diverged: true }` holds when that event was resolved with any option except its historical one. An event with no historical option cannot diverge, and the loader refuses `diverged` on it.

R-ALT-003: A non-historical option may carry `alternative: { basis, by, date, sources }`. `basis` is `proposed` or `debated` (a body or office, in words, is recorded putting it forward on that date; at least one https source with a checked date) or `design` (nobody is recorded proposing it: a design assumption, which names nobody and no date; sources optional). The historical option carries none.

R-ALT-004: Every non-historical option of an event that has a historical option carries an `alternative` record (R-ALT-003).

R-ALT-005: An event of `kind: consequence` is soft, has no historical option, has at least one requirement naming a road not taken (`diverged: true`, a non-historical option, or an option of another consequence card), carries an `alternative` record on every option, and lies at most 2 cards deep from the record (`EventRules.MaxConsequenceDepth`, a labelled design value, Q-ALT-5). It is never an objective's card, and only a consequence card may require one.

R-ALT-006: A consequence card may follow only an option whose `alternative.basis` is `proposed` or `debated`; with `diverged: true`, every non-historical option of that event must be (the Ferguson test, Q-ALT-3).

R-ALT-007: A consequence card carries every sensitive tag (case aside) of each event it requires, so the no-gain rule (R-EFX-002, R-EFX-005) covers it.

R-ALT-008: Consequence cards take a turn's soft slots before the other soft events, then by `from` (an objective's card after the others of its `from`, R-OBJ-007), `order` (default 0, R-EVT-006) and id (Q-ALT-14). An early `front_quiet` armistice (R-WAR-008, R-EVT-002) comes before them. With no consequence card eligible the order is unchanged.

R-ALT-009: An option of a consequence card may `rejoin` an event whose road the card follows (and which has a historical option). Once it is chosen, a requirement on that event's historical option holds, so the record's later cards fire again if their windows are still open. The event's own chosen option is not rewritten, and `diverged: true` on it still holds.

R-ALT-010: An event of `kind: reconvergence` has a historical option and may carry `variants`. The first variant whose every `when` entry holds supplies the card's title (if it gives one) and text; options and effects never vary. Each variant follows a road not taken, carries sources, has its placeholders checked over the event's window, and names only roads whose windows close on or before the event's `from` (for a road's historical option, also every consequence card that can `rejoin` it, R-ALT-009). R-MEDIT-003: Schema validity is not semantic validation. R-MEDIT-004: A visual or label offset (#1721 slice B; owner, 1 November 2026, Q58 and Q59) is one `offsets` row per location id and kind (`visual` or `label`): the axial hex (q, r) plus whole metres east and north of that hex's centre. The point is kept as written: no offset, location or sourced geometry is moved to clear the warning. Offsets serialize ordered by id and kind, so save and reload give the same bytes.
