# Rules of the GDD v0.1 MVP

The rules of the 1948–1951 MVP in [docs/GDD.md](../GDD.md), as Phase 1 built them behind the `systems.loop` switch
([the approved plan](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-gdd-v0.1-impact-and-plan.md), §6). Since the cutover (Phase 2 PR 1)
the real game runs them. Balance values are the owner's.

**15 October 2026 (owner, Q-CUT-1: "Labelled design values").** From the cutover, every balance number is a labelled
design value in `content/`, tagged on its line and listed by `DesignValuesTests` (R-LOOP-005). This replaces controller
ruling 2 of 12 October 2026, under which placeholders lived only in the dev and test overlay, never in `content/`.

## Turns

R-TURN-001: The turn length on each security rung (1 to 5) is read from the rules key `turn_lengths` of the era the turn starts in. Without the key it is quarter, quarter, quarter, month, week, the legacy table. The real game sets all five to month (D-B1), so turns from 15 May 1948 end on the 15th of each month.

R-TURN-002: An event that asks for a decision opens its window on the 15th on or before the historical decision, so the card reaches the player on the turn before it (#567 §4, owner, 15 October 2026). The exceptions are listed in the tests: `first_truce` (+5 days) and `rhodes_talks` (+2 days) arrive late by design, and the five war cards PR 1a rewrites as decisions during or after the fact.

R-TURN-003: In the historical run, no more than 6 months pass from one card to the next (or from the last card to the game's end), and no more than 2 when the country is at full war (security level 5) on any turn of the stretch (owner, 15 October 2026, Q-CUT-8: "6 months, 2 in wartime"; the M3 gap ruling restated in months).

R-TURN-004: A move (`Move`) and a unit order (`UnitOrder`) other than Send home take effect at once and are final (the owner, 23 October 2026: "No undo, like Civ"): each is committed to the turn in progress, in the order given, and the game shows the state after them (`Simulation.Preview`). Every other order waits for End turn, may be changed or withdrawn until then, and costs and runs as before. End turn runs the route step (R-MOVE-009), then the committed orders in the order given, then the waiting orders in the order queued, as one batch (`Simulation.Step`), so a waiting order never changes the result of a committed one (spec 2026-10-23 section 3).

## The loop switch

R-LOOP-001: `systems.loop` in eras.yaml is `legacy` (the default) or `gdd`, and every era has the same value. Its ruleset id, which a save records, is `legacy` or `gdd-0.1`.

R-LOOP-002: The GDD state (cities, units, the next unit id, completed objectives, the score) is written into the state hash only when it is set. With the legacy loop the hash text is unchanged, byte for byte.

R-LOOP-004: The GDD game ends on the rules key `mvp.end`, 15 January 1952 in the real content (owner, 15 October 2026, D-E5: "End on 15 Jan 1952"), after 44 monthly turns. The end is a boundary the last turn reaches, like the last era's end; the game is then over (`mvp_ended`) and raises no new event. It is the same in every era, after the start and before the last era's end (so the game ends as `mvp_ended`, not `era_ended`). The eras after it stay in the repository and load (G2).

R-LOOP-005: Every balance number of the GDD loop (all of `cities.yaml` and `unit_types.yaml`, the rules' `mvp:` block, and the GDD orders' `attention_cost`) carries the tag `# DESIGN VALUE: owner-set balance, not a historical figure` and its decision row on its own line, and `DesignValuesTests` lists every one of them: a number missing from the list, or a listed one without the tag, fails.

The ID R-LOOP-003 (the Phase 1 overlay, `MvpSlice.Overlay`) is retired with the overlay at the cutover and is not reused.

## Settlement control

R-CTRL-001: Only a settlement in `settlement_targets.yaml` may be captured. Each target is an `arab_town` row of `map/settlements_1948.yaml` (Q-TGT-1 (a), owner ruling of 18 October 2026), stands on a land hex of `map/hexes.json` that is its own hex in `map/settlement_hexes.yaml`, names a sourced operation (each source an https URL with a `checked` date) and carries a review status; any other row is a load error naming file and field. The shipped list holds the six rows the owner approved on 19 October 2026 and filled on 21 October 2026 (#793: jaffa, acre, nazareth, lod, ramla, beersheba; Gaza excluded), each `status: draft` until a person checks its sources (D-F5).

R-CTRL-002: Settlement control is an ordinal `SortedDictionary<string, SettlementControl>`. It is written to the state hash as `ctrl{...}` only when non-empty, in ordinal id order; each record field is written only when it differs from its default (R-LOOP-002).

R-CTRL-003: Residents and combatants are distinct fields. Residents are a state, inhabited or depopulated, not a count (Q-CAP-2 (b)). No rule reads residents as combatants, prisoners, refugee units, labour or a resource; annexation is the one place a later owner-approved rule may say otherwise.

R-CTRL-004: The capture check runs after the route step and each order, and still in resolver step 3 after enemy combat, in ordinal settlement id (Q-CAP-1 (a), Q-CAP-3 (a), owner rulings of 18 October 2026). A target that starts under enemy control, one listed in `mvp.enemy_held_localities` (owner, 21 October 2026, #796), is captured at once when Israeli military units are on its hex and no enemy military unit is; only enemy military units are combatants (Q-CAP-3 (a)), and a halutz holds nothing (#796). Passing through a target does not capture it; only a move's or go-to's stopping hex counts (owner, 23 October 2026, OQ-2: "Instant"). Its record names `israel` as owner and has the state `awaiting_choice`; the preview leaves `Since` unset, and Step stamps the turn's end before the resolver, preserving the date through a same-turn answer. The answer stays deferred until End turn (R-TURN-004). A capture remains when the unit leaves; the player's units stay on the hex. A captured target still awaiting the choice that only enemy military units are on is under enemy control again, and its record is removed (#796). A target not enemy-held at the start (Jaffa, owner ruling of 18 October 2026 on #793, "Only in a what-if") is never captured. The check is an integer rule on unit positions and draws nothing from the RNG. Enemy captures of the player's localities (Q-TGT-2 (c), the plan's R-CTRL-006) are not built yet: with the real starting forces a Syrian unit stands on Degania Alef's hex with no Israeli unit, so the rule would record a capture at the first turn's end (#796).

R-CTRL-005: Capture writes the settlement state only. It changes no resident field (the place stays inhabited), no unit, no resource, parameter, city or score, and counts towards no objective (constraint 6 of the Phase 6 plan; impact plan section 4.3).

R-CTRL-007: `ResolveCapture(settlement, outcome)` answers a capture. It is one command kind for every outcome, with one `attention_cost.resolve_capture` (Q-TECH-3, owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 1), set at 0 as a design value proposed on #797 (D-A6: unit orders are free). It is valid only for a settlement whose record is awaiting the choice, including the turn of its instant capture until it is answered, and only for an outcome that is built. The answer remains deferred and runs at End turn (owner, 23 October 2026; R-TURN-004). Otherwise it is refused with a reason, the state unchanged and the Attention refunded: the place is not awaiting a decision, the outcome is not known, or Deport is not in play: with `mvp.deport_in_play` off, as in the real content until the owner has reviewed Deport's cards and dialog (Phase 6 PRs 9 and 10), it is refused as "not built yet" (R-CTRL-018). The legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention.

R-CTRL-008: `SettlementControl.CaptureOptions(content, state, settlement)` lists the outcomes in one fixed order, Raid and withdraw, Occupy, Deport, so Deport is never first (impact plan section 4.1 item 1). An option carries no default or selected flag. Each option has either the reason it is refused or a preview: the change resolving it makes at once to Attention, Money (the Treasury and the debt), Food, Arms, Social cohesion, International Support and the score, computed by resolving it on a copy of the state, so the preview is the outcome (spec section 2.6). A place not awaiting the choice offers no option. A capture the player does not answer waits, and the units stay (Q-HIST-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). On the turn that reaches the game's end, where resolver step 10 sits and before objectives and score, every capture still awaiting the choice counts as Raid, in ordinal id order (owner, 18 October 2026 on #797, "Counts as Raid"): the units leave as R-CTRL-010 says, nothing changes for the residents and the score gains nothing. A unit with nowhere to go stays while the others leave, and the record goes all the same. No unanswered choice ever resolves as Deport.

R-CTRL-009: `HistoricalAI` answers every capture awaiting the choice with Raid, in ordinal id order, leaving out a Raid the sim would refuse (a unit with nowhere to go), which then waits, and the reference player sends `HistoricalAI`'s orders, so it does too (Q-REF-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). Neither ever issues Deport, and nothing delegates a capture: `EventSystem.Delegate` answers events only (impact plan section 4.1 item 1).

R-CTRL-010: Raid and withdraw (Q-RAID-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3). The player's units on the place's hex, soldiers and halutzim alike, leave in ascending id, each to the first neighbouring hex in the fixed neighbour order (R-MOVE-002) that is land, holds no enemy unit, is not an enemy-held locality (R-MOVE-005) and has room under the stack limit (R-MOVE-003). Leaving costs no movement points, and the units keep their orders, as after a `Move` that ends on another hex: a fortify is lost at the turn's order step (R-ORDER-002); both are defaults proposed on #797. The record is removed, so the place is under enemy control again at once, and its residents are unchanged. Raid changes nothing else: no resource, parameter, city, score or objective, and no RNG draw. If a unit has no such hex, Raid is refused with a reason and the state is unchanged, so the choice waits.

R-CTRL-011: Occupy (Q-OCC-1 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3): `ResolveCapture(settlement, occupy)` keeps the place under the player's control. It needs an Israeli military unit on the place's hex, the one unit that stays (a halutz holds nothing, #796), and is refused with a reason otherwise, the state unchanged. It moves no unit. It changes the record's state to `occupied` and starts its timer (R-CTRL-012), and nothing else: no resident field (the place stays inhabited, R-CTRL-003), unit, resource, parameter, city, score or objective, so its preview (R-CTRL-008) is all zeros. Each turn's step 10, while the place is occupied or awaiting `EndOccupation`, the Treasury pays `mvp.occupation_upkeep_per_turn` for it, borrowing what it lacks (R-MONEY-003); the Occupy turn pays too. The upkeep is a design value accepted by the owner on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6338594839): IL 0.005m, one month of `income_per_job_per_year` for the one population unit that stays, derived as `held_soldier_cost_per_turn` is. It comes on top of the garrison formation's own `military_upkeep_per_turn` (IL 0.05m, R-UNIT-010), so holding a place costs IL 0.055m a turn in all (owner ruling, 22 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6341877796). Money is shown to one decimal of IL millions, so when the Treasury has to borrow for it the Why line shows the borrowed amount, at most IL 0.005m, as "IL 0.0m".

R-CTRL-012: The occupation timer (Q-OCC-3 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 4: one X for every place). Occupy sets the record's `Turns` to `mvp.occupation_turns`, X, a design value accepted by the owner on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1324#issuecomment-6338594839): 6 monthly turns. Resolver step 10, after step 9's arrivals and before objectives and score (step 11), takes each occupied place in ordinal id order and counts one turn off, the Occupy turn included; at 0 the place's state is `awaiting_end`, where it awaits `EndOccupation` (Phase 6 PR 7) and the count stops. `Turns` is written to the hash as `turns=` only when non-zero (R-CTRL-002). The step draws nothing from the RNG.

R-CTRL-013: Withdrawal before the timer ends (Q-OCC-2 (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 batch 3: allowed, and it becomes a Raid). An occupation lasts while an Israeli military unit is on the place's hex. When step 10 finds none on an occupied or `awaiting_end` place (the player moved them out, or they were lost), the occupation ends as a Raid does: the record is removed, so the place is under enemy control again at once, its residents unchanged, and no upkeep is paid that turn. Nothing else changes, and nothing is refunded. A halutz left on the hex keeps nothing.

R-CTRL-018: Deport (impact plan section 4.1; owner ruling 1, "honest cost, no reward"; Q-DEP-1 (a), Q-DEP-5 (c) and Q-DEP-7 (c), which includes (a), owner, 18 October 2026, #783 "Accept all 16 drafts"): `ResolveCapture(settlement, deport)`, only while `mvp.deport_in_play` is on (R-CTRL-007). The player's units on the place's hex leave exactly as in Raid (R-CTRL-010), and Deport is refused the same way, the state unchanged, when one has nowhere to go: it gives no military advantage over Raid and pays no occupation upkeep. Social cohesion (`morale`) falls at once by `mvp.deport_cohesion_cost` and International Support (`intl_standing`) by `mvp.deport_support_cost`, through the permanent event shift. The Treasury pays `mvp.deport_money_cost` at once, borrowing what it lacks (R-MONEY-003), so Deport never pays on Money (owner decision, 22 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1327#issuecomment-6337946480): a design value set at or above the most Occupy can pay in upkeep over the whole game plus the dearest city project, IL 6.25m. The place is added to `DisplacedByPlayer`. From then on, every turn's resolver step 10, after the occupation timers, International Support falls by `mvp.deport_support_drag_per_turn` for each place in `DisplacedByPlayer`, the Deport turn included, for the rest of the game; and International Support's ceiling is 1 less `mvp.deport_support_ceiling_drop` for each such place (never below 0), which every write to it respects. The four indicator values (0.15, 0.15, 0.005 and 0.25, the drag and the ceiling drop per place) and the IL 6.25m were proposed on #800 and #1327 and accepted by the owner on 22 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1327#issuecomment-6350226175); they stay design values the balance pass (#813) may revisit. Nothing draws from the RNG.

R-CTRL-019: The residents (Q-DEP-2 (a), every resident the state holds; Q-DEP-3 (a), a count only, with no destination on the board): a Deport adds 1 to `DisplacedByPlayer`, a count of places, not people (Q-CAP-2 (b)), written to the hash as `displaced=` only when non-zero. They are outside the player's control: never refugee units, civilians, cohorts, units, prisoners, labour, housing occupants or score.

R-CTRL-020: A depopulated place (impact plan section 4.1 item 4; Q-DEP-7 (c); Q-SENS-2 (c)). Deport leaves the place's record with its capture date and `depopulated=1` only: no owner, state or timer. It stays on `mvp.enemy_held_localities`, so the player's units cannot enter it (R-MOVE-005) and it is never captured again, as a raided place is for the enemy. It adds no housing, jobs, Food, Money, Arms or score, and counts towards no objective. With no timer, resolver step 10 neither counts it down nor charges upkeep for it. No city project may be started on its hex (refused with the reason kind `depopulated`), and no corridor segment through it (this stops new starts; one already under way there is not cancelled); no designated site can be on a 1948 settlement's hex at all, and no event effect can name a place.

R-CTRL-021: Buildings (D-F3; Q-DEP-4 (a)): deportation and razing are separate rules, and there is no razing option in the MVP. The record's buildings stay at their default, and a capture offers only Raid, Occupy and Deport.

R-CTRL-022: `DeportNeverDominates` (impact plan section 4.1 item 5; Q-TECH-4 as drafted; Q-DEP-7 (b); Q-DEP-5 (c), "must hold with a margin"). From any state where Deport is offered, the state is cloned for Raid, Occupy and Deport, each clone resolves its outcome and is stepped by the reference policy to the game's end. At the turn's end and at the end, Deport is no better than Raid or Occupy on any measure, with no exception (owner decision, 22 October 2026, #1327): Money, foreign currency, Food, Arms, Attention, coalition stability, any indicator, the score, the objectives and the day each was met, the player's units and their strength, housing, jobs, people, built sites and the war loss, and it is below both on Social cohesion and International Support by a margin. Where a city shares the place's hex (as Tel Aviv shares Jaffa's), the city cannot build there after a Deport, so it may spend less; `mvp.deport_money_cost` covers that too, and Money is compared there as everywhere. Until a capture is reachable in the real content (#1304) the fixture's capture states carry the test.

## Saves

R-SAVE-001: A save names the ruleset it was played under (`ruleset legacy` or `ruleset gdd-0.1`, save schema 2). A game refuses a save made under another ruleset. A schema 1 save has no ruleset line and is legacy, so the GDD game refuses it as made under earlier rules. The refusal carries a code (`older_rules`) the UI translates, and the sim's reason does not itself ask for a new game: the player reads that once (N4).

R-SAVE-002: A save that names a ruleset this build does not know, or a newer save schema, was made by a newer version of the game and is refused with the code `newer_version` (N2).

R-SAVE-003: Save schema 3 adds the open turn: after the last `turn`, a line `open` and the turn in progress's committed orders (R-TURN-004), one a line (a waiting order there is refused: the save was edited); the `end` hash is still the state's after the last finished turn. Loading replays the turns, then commits the open orders again; one refused on the same content means the save is damaged (`SaveException`), and on other content the rest are dropped with the `replay_differs` warning. A schema 2 save has no open turn and loads as before (but see R-SAVE-004).

R-SAVE-004: A schema 2 save of the GDD game with a player attack (`order <unit> attack <q> <r>`) is refused with the code `older_rules` (R-SAVE-001): its attacks were standing orders, so it would replay differently (the owner, 23 October 2026: "Refuse with a clear message"). So is a schema 2 GDD save whose replay under the current rules does not end on its saved hash (instant capture, the full-points step). Other schema 2 saves load.

## Cities

Each GDD turn resolves in the order of the approved plan's section 2.3 (decision D-A6): production, then housing and Social cohesion, then arrivals.

R-CITY-001: `AssignWorkers(city, role, n)` sets how many of the city's people work in `role` (only `job` exists: a craftsman fills an ordinary job slot, R-CRAFT-002). It is refused, with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, when the city or role is unknown, `n` is negative, or `n` is above the city's jobs or its civilians and craftsmen. An accepted order turns the city's job default off (R-CITY-009). The legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention.

R-CITY-002: Each turn every city's employed units add `employed * income_per_job_per_year * the turn's share of a year` to the Treasury (an employed craftsman earns more, R-CRAFT-002).

R-CITY-003: Arrivals from the CBS series (`aliyah.yaml`) become refugees in the cities. Let `P(t)` be the people the series brings from `city_arrivals_from` to the date `t` (0 before it), summed over the origin groups in ordinal order. `city_arrivals_from` is 8 November 1948, the census day the cities' start values count: the census already holds the people who arrived from 15 May, so the cities take none before it (Q-ARRV-3, owner, 15 October 2026). The cities' arrived units by `t` are `U(t) = floor(P(t) / people_per_unit)`, and a turn from date `d` to date `e` adds `U(e) - U(d)` refugees over all cities. `people_per_unit` and `city_arrivals_from` are the same in every era (the loader refuses an era that changes either), so over any run of turns the refugees added are `U(end) - U(start)`: no unit is lost or counted twice. The units are split in proportion to the cities' census `people` (Q-ARRV-1): the first `U` units are handed out one at a time, unit `n` to the city with the largest remainder `n * people / total people - units it holds`, a tie to the lower city id (ordinal), and a turn gives each city its share of `U(e)` minus its share of `U(d)`. Building the split up one unit at a time keeps it cumulative: a fresh largest-remainder split of each total could take a unit back from a city as the total grows (the Alabama paradox; on the twelve cities it first happens at 7 units). The loader refuses a GDD game with no city.

R-CITY-008: `GameState.Population` is the one record of the nation's people: it takes every arrival from 15 May 1948 and is the count the CBS anchors check. The cities are the part of it placed in towns, so their people (civilians, craftsmen, refugees, recruits and the groups in training, times `people_per_unit`) never exceed it (Q-ARRV-2, owner, 15 October 2026). No person is counted twice.

R-CITY-004: Each turn, on the housing step, a city's refugees become civilians without a job (R-CITY-009's default may then give them jobs), `floor(pool x m)` of them at the Immigration minister's multiplier since the ministers (R-MIN-002; all of them before); the rest wait as refugees for the next turns. There is no hard cap (D-D1, Q-HOUSE-1, owner, 15 October 2026): the people beyond the city's housing stay in the city as its overflow (R-HOUSE-002), and residents are housed automatically (Q-HOUSE-4).

R-CITY-009: The job default (Q-JOBS-1, owner, 15 October 2026). On each housing step, after the refugees become civilians (R-CITY-004), each city whose default is on sets its employed to `min(job slots, civilians + craftsmen)`, in ascending city id, at no Attention cost. Every city's default is on at the start; an accepted `AssignWorkers` order fixes that city's number and turns its default off for the rest of the game. Each change is a `city` cause on the Why screen ("Town: N groups in work"). A city's record writes `jobs=manual` into the state hash only when its default is off.

R-CITY-005: The cities' employed share (employed over civilians and craftsmen, summed over all cities; 1 with none) is an effect-web source of `morale`, Social cohesion (`work_lifts_cohesion`, source id `employed_share`), read after the turn's housing. The legacy loop has no cities, so there it reads 0 and adds nothing. Housing reaches Social cohesion through the existing `housing` link (`homes_lift_morale`) until Phase 3 makes it the cities' aggregate (Q-NAT-2, decided 15 October 2026). Phase 1's separate cohesion value and its two weights are gone (D-C1, Reading 2).

R-CITY-006: The cities are the `city` and `town` rows of `map/settlements_1948.yaml`, in that file's order, and their start values are `cities.yaml`'s. Each of a city's values (`people`, `employed_share`, `housing_capacity`, `job_slots`) is required and has a status: `sourced` (read from the source named), `derived` (computed from sourced values), `wikipedia-only` (an owner-accepted Wikipedia figure) or `design` (a labelled design value, R-LOOP-005). A `sourced`, `derived` or `wikipedia-only` value has at least one source, each with a url and a checked date, and the day (YYYY-MM-DD) or month (YYYY-MM) it describes; the loader refuses a value without them, naming the file and field (GDD Phase 3 plan, review focus 3).

R-CITY-007: A city starts with `civilians` = `people / people_per_unit` to the nearest unit (a half rounds up), and at least 1 when `people` is above 0 (Q-START-2, owner, 15 October 2026); `employed` = `people * employed_share / people_per_unit` to the nearest unit; `housing_capacity` and `job_slots` as written, in units. The loader refuses a city whose start employed is above its job slots. The arithmetic is decimal, so it is exact on every runtime.

R-HOUSE-001: Each city has a housing condition from 0 to 1, an average over the city (D-D2, Q-HOUSE-2, owner, 15 October 2026). Every city starts at `mvp.housing_start_condition` (Q-START-3: one design value for all the cities, 0.35, `model.yaml`'s `housing` base). The condition is per city: one city's counts never change another's. It is in the state hash (R-LOOP-002).

R-HOUSE-002: A city's overflow is `max(0, housed - housing_capacity)`, one capacity point housing one population unit (D-D1, Q-HOUSE-1, owner, 15 October 2026). On the housing step, after R-CITY-004 and before Social cohesion (D-A6), a city with overflow loses `overflow_condition_loss * overflow / housed` of its condition, never below 0; a city without overflow keeps its condition. `overflow_condition_loss` is 0.15, a design value. The loader requires both values, from 0 to 1. Overflow reaches Social cohesion through R-HOUSE-004 (a lower condition lowers the base of the national `housing` indicator) and the existing `homes_lift_morale` link (k 0.15) from `housing` into `morale`, which is Social cohesion (R-NAT-001); there is no direct overflow term (Q-HOUSE-1, decided by the owner 15 October 2026, plan:165 of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-15-gdd-phase-3.md`: cohesion follows through the existing `housing` to `morale` link, "so it needs no number of its own"; the cohesion half was built after #728, merged as #857, owner 17 October 2026).

R-HOUSE-003: The people who take a city's housing (`housed`) are its civilians, its craftsmen, its recruits and its groups in training, a halutz group included until it becomes a movable unit. Halutz units do not (Q-HOUSE-5, owner, 15 October 2026). Refugees wait for housing and are not in it.

R-HOUSE-004: In the GDD loop the base of the national `housing` indicator is the people-weighted mean of the cities' housing condition, each city weighed by its housed people (R-HOUSE-003): `sum(condition * housed) / sum(housed)`. Policy effects, map actions and events still act on top, as on any indicator. Decided (owner, 17 Oct 2026, option (a) of Q-PR3-A, relayed on [#855 at 06:33Z](https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/855#issuecomment-6230731177)): the national housing base is the people-weighted mean of city condition, with policy, map actions and events still applied on top. When no city houses anybody the mean is undefined, and the base is `model.yaml`'s. Capacity reaches the level through overflow and condition (R-HOUSE-002).

## City projects

A city project is built in a city (GDD "Cities and construction"; Phase 3 PR 4, #735). The kinds are in `content/projects.yaml`, every number a design value (Q-PROJ-1, Q-PROJ-5, owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations", with agent-proposed values the owner may override): permanent housing (`housing`), the transit camp (`transit_camp`, D-D3, Q-HOUSE-3) and `industry`. They replace the legacy map actions, which the GDD loop refuses (R-PROJ-004).

R-PROJ-001: `StartProject(kind, city, q, r)` starts a city project of a `projects.yaml` kind for a city on hex (q, r). The Treasury pays the kind's cost at once and borrows what it lacks (Q-PROJ-1, Q-MONEY-3, "Allow borrowing"); the project takes the next id and the kind's turns, the turn of the order the first (D-A3). It needs no workers (Q-PROJ-3). It is refused, state and Attention unchanged, for an unknown kind or city; a locked kind that no event has unlocked (R-EFX-008); a transit camp without the `maabarot` policy at 0.5 or more (Q-HOUSE-3: the policy carries the camp's sourced date, and the map actions' 1 January 1950 window does not apply); a hex that is not land, is more than `map_action_max_distance` from the city's settlement hex, or holds a map-action placement or another city project, built or not (Q-PROJ-2); and once `map_actions_per_turn` projects were started this turn, counted nationally (Q-PROJ-4; the GDD loop builds no map action, R-PROJ-004). The legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention. Its Attention cost is `attention_cost.start_project` (1, Q-PROJ-5).

R-PROJ-002: `CancelProject(id)` removes a city project that is being built. The Money paid is not returned, and there are no people to return (D-A3, Q-PROJ-3). An unknown or built project is refused with the state unchanged. Its Attention cost is `attention_cost.cancel_project` (0.5, Q-PROJ-5).

R-PROJ-003: On step 5 of the turn (D-A6), after the pioneer work, every city project being built has one turn of work less to go, in ascending id - `1 x m` of one at the system's office multiplier since the ministers (R-MIN-002), the fraction carried on the record as on R-SITE-003. One that reaches 0 is built on the turn's end date and adds its kind's `capacity` to the city's housing capacity, its `condition` to the city's housing condition (kept from 0 to 1, R-HOUSE-001) and its `job_slots` to the city's job slots, once: a built project keeps its record, with its hex, and is never advanced again. `GameState.CityProjects` is hashed as `cproj[...]` only when it has a project (R-LOOP-002).

R-PROJ-004: In the GDD loop a map action (`map_action`, the legacy transit camp or housing placement) is refused with the reason "Map actions are not part of this game: start a city project instead" and its Attention refunded, whatever its action or hex (plan PR 4, spec §1.4: "Placements become completed projects"). So no placement is built and none counts against R-PROJ-001's turn limit. The info panel shows no legacy Build button in the GDD loop. The legacy loop keeps the map actions until that code is deleted (D-E1). The pioneer orders from the map (`pioneer_work`, R-SITE-001) are not map actions.

R-PROJ-005: A specialised project is allowed only in a city that has its resource (D-D8: "A winery requires areas for grapes"; Phase 3 PR 6, #737). A `projects.yaml` kind may name a `requires:` resource; a `cities.yaml` city lists its `resources:`, each with an id and sources (a url and a checked date each), and may say what the sources leave open (`to_verify`). `StartProject` of such a kind in a city without the resource is refused like the other R-PROJ-001 refusals, before the hex checks; a city founded in play has no resources. The loader refuses a resource without sources and a kind whose resource no city has. Otherwise the kind is an ordinary city project (R-PROJ-003). The one such kind is the winery, which needs vineyards, in Rishon LeZion with its vineyards flagged `to_verify` (owner, 20 October 2026, on #737, as GDD v0.2 says; this replaces the 18 October default of Zikhron Ya'akov, which has no winery).

## Training

Training is step 4 of the turn in the approved order (§2.3, D-A6): it runs before production. One military camp and one Hakhshara exist in Tel Aviv at the start, a labelled design assumption (D-D5); pioneers build more at camp sites (R-CAMP-001).

R-TRAIN-001: `Train(city, track, n)` takes `n` of the city's civilians without a job into a cohort that trains as `track` (a unit type with a `facility`: `recruit`, `artillery` and `armoured_car` in a military camp (R-UNIT-008), `halutz` in a Hakhshara, `craftsman` at industry, R-CRAFT-001) for the type's `training_turns`, counting the turn it was ordered in. The Treasury pays `n * training_cost` at once. When the Treasury holds less, the shortfall is borrowed, as for map actions: it is added to the debt and the Treasury stops at 0 (owner decision "Allow borrowing", 15 October 2026). The trainees leave the civilians at once; the cohort takes the next id, which units share. It is refused, with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, when the city or track is unknown, `n` is below 1, the city lacks the facility, or it has fewer civilians without a job. A city has a facility when `cities.yaml` gives it one at the start or a camp site of that kind built for it is in the state (R-CAMP-001). The legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention. `Reinforce` (R-TRAIN-004) starts a cohort by the same rules, aimed at a unit. A queued `ReassignCraftsmen` or `ReassignPioneers` (R-QUAL-004, R-QUAL-005) takes its civilians without a job as a queued `Train` does (counted in full, so the orders are accepted in any order), in `Training.Ordered` and `Training.Available` (#1548; owner, 24 October 2026: "Yes, count them", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6378360813).

R-TRAIN-002: `CancelTraining(cohort)` returns the cohort's people to its city as civilians without a job and refunds none of the Money paid (D-A3); a recruit cohort's Arms go back to the pool (R-ARMS-003). A cohort that does not exist, or has finished, is refused with the state unchanged. Replacements (R-TRAIN-004) cancel the same way.

R-TRAIN-003: Each turn, in ascending id, every cohort has one turn less to go. A cohort that reaches 0 ends: a recruit cohort becomes one military unit with its count as strength (R-UNIT-003), and so does a gun or armoured car cohort, of its own type (R-UNIT-008), craftsmen join the city's count (R-CRAFT-001), and halutzim become one movable unit per group. Units appear at the city's settlement hex, taking the next ids in turn. New recruit completions never fill `City.Recruits`; its old canonical field is retained, and saves still replay their command log. Cohorts are written into the state hash only when there are some. A cohort aimed at a unit ends as R-TRAIN-004 says instead. Reassignment of a qualified civilian is a second way into the role, with no cohort (R-QUAL-004, R-QUAL-005).

R-TRAIN-004: `Reinforce(unit, city, n)` (#1510; owner, 23 October 2026: "Replacements cost Arms", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1510#issuecomment-6372356139, and "Default (Recommended)") refills a damaged formation of the player's with trained replacements. The unit must be a military unit of the player's side standing on `city`'s settlement hex. Its course is the trained type of the unit's side and class (a type with a `facility`): the recruit's basic training for a recruit or a veteran soldier, the guns' and the armoured cars' own for artillery and armoured cars (R-UNIT-008); a type no course trains (any enemy type) takes none. The cohort then follows R-TRAIN-001: the city's facility, `n` of its civilians without a job, `n * training_cost` with borrowing (R-MONEY-003), and the Arms of R-ARMS-003 for that course; it carries the unit's id, written into the state hash (`|unit=<id>`) only when set. The cap is the strength the unit had before its first loss, `Unit.Full`: combat stores it on a player's unit at its first loss (R-COMBAT-003) and clears it when replacements bring the unit back to it, and it is written into the state hash (`|full=<n>`) only when set, so a unit that has lost nobody, and every enemy unit, hashes as before. The order is refused, the state and Attention unchanged, for an unknown unit ("There is no such unit"), a unit with no side or an enemy unit (R-ORDER-001's wording), an unknown city, a unit off the city's hex ("Unit N is not in <city>"), a type no course trains ("Unit N cannot take replacements"), a unit with nothing to refill ("Unit N has no losses to replace") and `n` past the cap less the unit's strength and the replacements already training for it ("Unit N can take K more groups"), then for R-TRAIN-001's and R-ARMS-003's reasons. A queued `Reinforce` takes its city's civilians as a queued `Train` does (R-CITY-001). In step 4, before a turn is counted, replacements whose unit has left the state (destroyed, captured, sent home) or stands off the city's hex are cancelled as R-TRAIN-002 cancels a cohort: people and Arms back, the Money spent, with a Why line. A cohort that finishes adds its count to the unit's strength and makes no unit and takes no id; the unit keeps its type, service, order and home (a veteran stays a veteran, R-UNIT-004). The people join the unit's home city's housing (R-HOUSE-003). `attention_cost.reinforce` is 1, Train's cost, a design value (owner, 24 October 2026: "1, like Train (Recommended)", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1510#issuecomment-6375591436). The save line is `reinforce <unit> <city> <n>` and the scenario form `{ kind: reinforce, unit, city, count }`.

### Return to civilian life and reassignment (#1511)

Owner ruling "Two-way, keep training" and its three answers (23 October 2026, #1511): https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6372356482 and https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6372642736. Craftsmen, halutzim and veteran pioneers can go back to civilian life and be put back into the trained role, in either direction, without repeating R-TRAIN-001.

R-QUAL-001: Each city keeps its qualified civilians (`City.Qualified`): qualified craftsmen, qualified halutz groups (each with the turns of service it had, ascending) and qualified veteran pioneers. They are part of the city's civilians, not in addition to them, so a qualified civilian is an ordinary civilian for jobs (R-CITY-001, R-CITY-009), housing (R-HOUSE-003), income (R-CITY-002, the ordinary rate, not R-CRAFT-002's) and training (R-TRAIN-001), and keeps the qualification for reassignment (owner ruling 2). The sum of the three never exceeds the city's civilians.

R-QUAL-002: `ReturnCraftsmen(city, n)` turns `n` of the city's craftsmen into civilians and qualified craftsmen, at once and with no Money. Employed is unchanged. It is refused, with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, when the city is unknown, `n` is below 1 or `n` is above the city's craftsmen.

R-QUAL-003: `UnitOrder(unit, return_civilian)` (the unit card's "Return", an order with no hex) takes an idle halutz or veteran pioneer unit off the map, at once and with no Money. It joins the nearest city (hex distance to the settlement hex, ties by ordinal city id, R-UNIT-005's rule) as one civilian and one qualified halutz group with the unit's service, or one qualified veteran pioneer. A halutz's partial service toward veteran is kept (owner ruling 3). It is refused, state unchanged, for an unknown unit, a unit that is not a halutz or veteran ("Only a pioneer unit takes that order") and a unit at work on a site or corridor segment ("That unit is at work; stop the work first").

R-QUAL-004: `ReassignCraftsmen(city, n)` turns `n` of the city's qualified craftsmen, who have no job, into craftsmen again, at once, with no Money and no training. It is refused, state unchanged, when the city is unknown, `n` is below 1, `n` is above its qualified craftsmen or `n` is above its civilians without work (R-TRAIN-001's Idle; owner, 24 October 2026: "Accept all as built", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6375597377: the check counts all the city's idle civilians). Queued, it counts in full against the city's civilians without work as a queued `Train` does (R-TRAIN-001), so the orders are accepted in any order, and it counts against `Cities.MaxWorkers` (R-CITY-001) for the turn it is queued: its craftsmen stay in the city, but if the job order runs first they are already in work.

R-QUAL-005: `ReassignPioneers(city, track, n)` (`track` is `halutz` or `veteran_halutz`) turns `n` of the city's qualified halutz groups or veteran pioneers, who have no job, into `n` units at the city's settlement hex, as R-TRAIN-003 places them, taking the next ids in turn: a halutz keeps its service and a veteran comes back a veteran with `veteran_halutz_service`, and the halutz groups with the most service come back first (owner, 24 October 2026: "Accept all as built", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6375597377). Refusals as R-QUAL-004's, and "There is no such qualification" for another track. Queued, it counts against the city's civilians without work as a queued `Train` does (R-TRAIN-001) and against `Cities.MaxWorkers` (R-CITY-001), since its groups leave the city as units.

R-QUAL-006: A Train order (R-TRAIN-001) takes the city's unqualified civilians first. A qualified civilian it has to take loses its qualification, in the order craftsmen, halutz groups with the least service, veterans (owner, 24 October 2026: "Accept all as built", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/1511#issuecomment-6375597377).

R-QUAL-007: Attention is that of the matching existing order (owner ruling 1): `return_craftsmen` and `reassign_craftsmen` cost what `assign_workers` does (0.5), and `reassign_pioneers` and the `return_civilian` unit order cost what a unit order does (0), all design values in `rules.yaml`. The qualified counts are written into the state hash only when not 0 (`,qcraftsmen=N,qhalutz=S:S,qveterans=N` after the other city fields), so a game without them hashes as before. The saves write `retcraft CITY N`, `regcraft CITY N`, `regpioneer CITY TRACK N` and `order UNIT return_civilian`; scenarios use `return_craftsmen`, `reassign_craftsmen`, `reassign_pioneers` and `unit_order` with `order: return_civilian`.

R-SITE-004: Every designated site has one or more kinds from a fixed list: `yishuv`, `field`, `swamp`, `military_camp` and `hakhshara` (Phase 4, Q-FIELD-1, Q-SWAMP-1, Q-CAMP-3). A pioneer action is accepted only at a site of its kind; any other is refused with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded. A yishuv site has a founding date inside the MVP's years and no other kind has one. A camp site (`military_camp` or `hakhshara`) names a city of `cities.yaml`, the city it trains for (Q-CAMP-1), and no other kind names one. The loader refuses any other row, naming the file and field.

R-SITE-005: At most one `yishuv` site sits in a hex, so a hex shows at most one new yishuv. A site of another kind may share a hex with a yishuv site, as a yishuv's field site does (Q-SITE-3).

R-FIELD-001: `PioneerWork(unit, plow_fields, site)` plows a field at a site whose kinds include `field`, by R-SITE-001 to R-SITE-003's rules, with `plow_fields_turns` and `plow_fields_cost` (labelled design values, Q-FIELD-3, decided 17 October 2026). A field site in a yishuv site's hex is ready only once that yishuv is built; before that the order is refused with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded (Q-FIELD-1, decided 17 October 2026). Built, the field site is plowed. A field site is not also a yishuv site, so building a yishuv cannot plow it; the loader refuses a row that gives it both kinds.

R-FIELD-002: Each turn in step 6, after the cities' production and in ordinal site id order, every plowed field adds `field_food_per_turn` Food units to the national stock (R-FOOD-001), with no workers (Q-FIELD-2, decided 17 October 2026). A field being plowed adds nothing. The Food arrives before the nation eats in step 7, so in a turn with imports (R-FOOD-002) the import tops the stock up by that much less. `field_food_per_turn` is 1, a labelled design value (Q-FIELD-3: "1 Food a turn"), and required in the GDD loop.

R-SWAMP-001: `PioneerWork(unit, dry_swamp, site)` drains a swamp at a site whose kinds include `swamp`, by R-SITE-001 to R-SITE-003's rules, with `dry_swamp_turns` and `dry_swamp_cost` (12 turns and IL 4m, labelled design values, required in the GDD loop; Q-SWAMP-1, decided 17 October 2026). Built, the swamp site is drained, and the field site in its hex becomes ready: before that a `plow_fields` order there is refused with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, as at an unbuilt yishuv (R-FIELD-001). Drained, the swamp does nothing else. A field site is not also a swamp site, since draining would plow it; the loader refuses a row that gives it both kinds. The MVP's one swamp site is the Hula (Q-SWAMP-1; Q-SWAMP-3, "Allow as planned", owner, 17 October 2026, #789).

R-SWAMP-002: An event with `site: <id>` names a swamp site (the loader checks it in the GDD loop) and, in the GDD loop, can fire only while that site is being worked: it has a record that is not built. With no works, or once the swamp is drained, it waits, and it can still fire later in its window (Q-SWAMP-2, decided 17 October 2026). The legacy loop reads no sites and ignores the key, so the legacy goldens hold. `hula_drainage` carries `site: hula`.

## Military units

R-UNIT-006: A new Historical game places one military unit per row of `map/units_1948.yaml` that is not `hidden`, has `service: army` (the squadron and the ships stay presentation-only tokens, Q-UNIT-5), has a `strength` whose `as_of` is the start day, and has an anchor window [from, to) covering the start. Every IDF brigade is a `veteran_soldier`; an Arab force takes its side's one military type, a labelled design value of class infantry with 3 movement points. ## Corridors

Corridor work is step 5 too, after the sites (Phase 4 PR 2, #786; owner, 17 October 2026, #782: the plan's Q-COR-1 to Q-COR-7 and Q-RAIL-1). `content/corridors.yaml` holds each corridor's kind (`road` or `rail`), names, map route and segments; a segment's hexes are derived from the route's traced line by `tools/corridor_hexes.py`. The Money and turns per segment are labelled design values in `rules.yaml` `mvp:` (Q-COR-6). On screen a segment is its corridor's name and its number ("Burma Road, segment 2"), never a waypoint's name (Q-SENS-1). The MVP's corridors are the Burma Road (four segments), the 1949 Haifa–Tel Aviv rail link (one) and the Tel Aviv–Netanya road (four, on straight draft lines between its five sourced places; owner, 18 October 2026, #786, Q-COR-3 (b)). A segment point in a hex the grid counts as sea (the coast) counts in its nearest land neighbour, as a shore town does.

R-COR-001: `PioneerWork(unit, build_road | build_rail, corridor:N)` starts segment N (from 1) of a corridor whose kind the action builds (`build_road` a road, `build_rail` a railway), when the segment is neither being built nor built, with a halutz unit that has no other project (D-D6). Segments may be started in any order, one unit per segment, so several units can build one corridor at once (Q-COR-4); the unit works from where it stands (Q-COR-5). The Treasury pays the kind's `road_segment_cost` or `rail_segment_cost` at once and borrows the shortfall (R-MONEY-003). It is refused, with the state unchanged and the Attention refunded, for a busy unit, an unknown corridor or segment number, a kind the action does not build, or a segment being built or built; the legacy loop refuses it before charging Attention. Its Attention cost is 0 (D-A6).

R-COR-002: Each turn, after the sites, in ordinal corridor id order and then segment order, every segment being built gains `progress x m` of a turn of work (R-MIN-002), where progress is 1 or `veteran_progress` with a veteran (R-VET-002) and m is Transportation's multiplier, counting the turn of the order. Whole turns apply at once; the fraction is carried on the segment and hashed as 16 hexadecimal digits of its IEEE bit pattern only while non-zero, as in R-SITE-003. A segment whose progress reaches its kind's `road_segment_turns` or `rail_segment_turns` is built, on the turn's end date, and its unit is idle again. Corridors with a segment being built or built are written into the state hash only when there are some.

R-COR-003: A corridor is complete when every one of its segments is built, in any order. `StopWork(unit)` on a unit building a segment ends it as R-SITE-002 does for a site: the segment is unbuilt and free again, the unit is idle, and the progress and the Money paid are lost (D-A3). A built corridor needs no upkeep and yields nothing in the MVP (Q-COR-7).

## Movement

Phase 5 PR 5 (#744). The numbers are design values this PR proposed (Q-MOVE-1, Q-MOVE-3; owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations") and the owner accepted them on 19 October 2026 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1073#issuecomment-6259021508).

R-MOVE-001: Each unit type that moves has an integer `movement_points` a turn (`unit_types.yaml`; required, at least 1, on a military type; refused on a population type), and each land biome of `map/hexes.json` an integer cost to enter (`rules.yaml` `mvp.move_cost`, at least 1; the loader refuses a land biome with no cost and a cost for a water biome, so lake and sea are impassable). A path's cost is the sum of the costs of the hexes it enters. A unit's Moves in one turn share its points: a second Move has what the first left, and the next turn has them all again. The points left are the type's points less those the unit spent this turn (`Movement.PointsLeft`). A unit needs the full cost of the next hex, with one exception: a unit that has not moved this turn may enter one neighbouring land hex whatever it costs, and that move spends all its points (the owner, 23 October 2026, OQ-3: "No, strict like Civ VI", "with full points it can always move one hex"). A type with no movement points (the halutz types) cannot move. Real values: recruit 3 (owner, 19 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1130#issuecomment-6269103829), veteran soldier 3, artillery 2, armoured car 5; coastal plain, valley and lowland 1, hills and desert 2, mountains 3.

R-MOVE-002: `Move(unit, q, r)` takes the cheapest path, at command time (spec §2.3). Hexes leave the search in (cost, q, r) ordinal order, neighbours are tried in `tools/hexgrid.py`'s `NEIGHBOURS` order, and a hex reached again at the same cost keeps the hex it was first entered from, so of two equal paths the one through the hex first in that order wins, and the path is the same every run. `Movement.Reachable(c, s, unit, spent)` and `Movement.Path(c, s, unit, q, r, spent)` search with the points left; without `spent`, with all of them. `Movement.Reachable` lists the hexes a Move can end on this turn with their costs, and `Movement.Path` the hexes a path enters; both come from the same search Move uses (spec §2.6). `Movement.Route(c, s, unit, q, r, spent)` is the cheapest path with no points limit, each hex numbered with the turn it is reached on: turn 1 spends the points left, later turns all of them, a step that does not fit what is left of a turn starts the next, and a step costing more than the full points is a turn of its own (R-MOVE-001).

R-MOVE-003: A hex holds at most `mvp.stack_limit` units of one side (Q-MOVE-3; real value 3). The limit counts the moving unit's side only and applies where a move ends; a path may pass through a full hex. A hex never holds both sides' units: entering an enemy unit's hex is an attack (Phase 5 PR 8), so Move refuses it.

R-MOVE-004: There is no zone of control (Q-MOVE-4): a unit passes next to an enemy unit at the terrain's cost, but no path enters or passes through an enemy unit's hex. Units may enter any land hex on the board; the 1948 control layer stays display-only. There is no transport (Q-MOVE-5).

R-MOVE-005: A player unit cannot enter the hex of an enemy-held locality that is not a curated target (`settlement_targets.yaml`, R-CTRL-001), and no path passes through one: in Phase 6 only Gaza stays closed (Q-CIV-1, owner, 15 October 2026: "No entry until Phase 6"; Q-TGT-1 (a)). The hex of an enemy-held target is open, so a player unit may move onto it, or through it, and capture it in play (R-CTRL-004; the owner's ruling on #793, 21 October 2026, that the targets "can be captured in play", https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/793#issuecomment-6322289565; #1304). Raid's withdrawal (R-CTRL-010) and combat retreat (R-COMBAT-003) still never end on any enemy-held locality, target or not, and the enemy's mirror (R-ENEMY-001) is unchanged. The localities are the settlements listed in `rules.yaml` `mvp.enemy_held_localities` (`settlements_1948.yaml` ids; the loader refuses an unknown one). The real list is the owner-accepted DRAFT (19 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1073#issuecomment-6259083180): acre, nazareth, lod, ramla, gaza, beersheba. It is unchecked: no person has yet checked each place's control on 15 May 1948; research task #1096 will check it.

R-MOVE-006: `Move` is refused with the state unchanged and a reason when the unit does not exist, is not the player's (a unit of a side other than Israel), cannot move or is already on the hex, when the hex is water or off the board, holds an enemy unit, is an enemy-held locality closed by R-MOVE-005 or is full of the unit's side, or when no path reaches it within the points the unit has left this turn. It costs no Attention (D-A6). A Move that goes through clears a fortify order (`fortify`, or `fortifying` the turn it is given) and the hex it names at once, so a unit that leaves its hex and comes back in the same turn is not fortified (Q-HOLD-1, "lost on move"); hold moves with the unit; a refused Move changes nothing.

R-MOVE-007: Roads and rail are cheaper (D-G2; Phase 5 PR 6, #745). A step into a hex from a hex of the same built corridor segment (`corridors.yaml`, R-COR-002) costs the kind's `mvp.road_move_cost` or `mvp.rail_move_cost` (at least 1; the loader requires both in the GDD loop) when that is lower than the terrain's cost; otherwise it costs the terrain's. Only the corridors Phase 4 builds count, not the 1948 roads of `map/routes_1948.yaml` (Q-MOVE-2, owner, 15 October 2026, #375: option (a)). A segment not started or still being built costs the terrain, and a step from one segment onto another costs the terrain unless both hexes lie on one built segment. Real values: road 1, rail 1, design values Phase 5 PR 6 proposes; the owner may override them. A cost of 1 saves nothing on the coastal plain, valley or lowland, which already cost 1.

R-MOVE-008: `UnitOrder(unit, go_to, q, r)` gives a standing go-to order and moves the unit at once as far as its points left reach along `Movement.Route`. It is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown or enemy unit, one that cannot move or is already there, water or off the board, a hex holding an enemy unit, a closed locality (R-MOVE-005), or a hex no route reaches. Otherwise it replaces the unit's order, including fortify. On arrival the order is cleared. A route may pass through a full friendly hex; at each turn's stop the unit uses the last hex with room. If the destination is full on arrival, the route stops and the order is cleared.

R-MOVE-009: At the start of each player turn, before that turn's committed and waiting orders, every player's unit with a go-to order advances in ascending unit id along a freshly computed route. It spends movement points normally, including the full-points exception (R-MOVE-001). If no route reaches its target, or the target is full on arrival, it stops, clears the order and needs orders. An adjacent enemy does not stop it (Q-MOVE-4: no zone of control). If no hex it can reach this turn has room, it waits where it is and keeps the order.

R-MOVE-010: A unit needs orders when it is one of the player's military units, its type has movement points, it has neither moved nor attacked this turn, and it has no order (`fortify`, `fortifying`, `hold` and `go_to` are orders) (`Movement.NeedsOrders`, ascending id). End turn waits until no unit needs orders, apart from those the player skips this turn (the owner, 23 October 2026: "Block like Civ"; spec 2026-10-23 section 7). Skipping is not an order and changes nothing in the game.

## Standing orders

Phase 5 PR 7 (#746). Q-HOLD-1, "(a) 1 turn, free, lost on move", and Q-HOLD-2, "(a) Own hex, no interception" (owner, 15 October 2026, #375). The two percents are design values this PR proposes from avlco's option 1 on #746; the owner may override them.

R-ORDER-001: `UnitOrder(unit, order)` gives one of the player's military units the standing order `fortify` or `hold`, replacing the one it had. A new order replaces a go-to too. The unit keeps its order, turn after turn, until the player gives another; moving clears fortify (Q-HOLD-1): in the resolver after combat (step 2), in id order, a unit no longer on the hex its fortify names (`OrderTarget`, "q,r") loses the order. The order is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown order or unit, a unit with no side (a halutz), a unit of another side, a hold for artillery (R-COMBAT-007), or the order the unit already has (fortify on the hex it fortifies). It costs `attention_cost.unit_order` (0, D-A6) and is refused in the legacy loop.

R-ORDER-002: Fortify takes one turn to set and costs no Arms or Money (Q-HOLD-1). Given, it reads `fortifying` and names the unit's hex; in the same resolver step it becomes `fortify`, so the combat of the turn it is given sees no bonus. A fortified unit on its hex defends at `mvp.fortify_defence_percent` of its strength (`Orders.DefencePercent`; real value 125), and from the moment it leaves the hex at 100.

R-ORDER-003: Hold acts on the unit's own hex only and does not intercept or stop any enemy move, past or next to it (Q-HOLD-2). It names no hex and stays when the unit moves. A holding unit defends at `mvp.hold_defence_percent` of its strength (real value 100: hold keeps the position and adds nothing); a unit with no order defends at 100.

## Combat

Phase 5 PR 8 (#747). Q-COMBAT-1 to Q-COMBAT-4 are the plan's recommendations (owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations"); Q-CIV-2 is the owner's "Enemy units only, no effect" and Q-CIV-4 his "2+3". The numbers (`rules.yaml` `mvp.arms_per_attack`, `mvp.combat_terrain_percent`, `mvp.combat_table`), the loss order, the retreat order and the attack's standing are agent defaults asked on #747 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/747#issuecomment-6297295556) and accepted by the owner on 20 October 2026, all six, as design values for the balance pass #813 (https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/747#issuecomment-6299184133). The truce refusal (Q-WAR-3) is PR 11, prisoners PR 12, artillery PR 9 and the enemy's orders PR 10.

R-COMBAT-001: `UnitOrder(unit, attack, q, r)` makes one of the player's military units attack the hex (q, r) at once, alone, when the order runs (the owner, 23 October 2026: "Instant, like Civ" and OQ-1: "One unit per attack, Civ"). It replaces the unit's order (a fortify is lost), spends the rest of its movement points for the turn and leaves no attack order. The hex must be next to the unit (artillery: within its range, R-COMBAT-007) and hold an enemy military unit; enemy units on a hex with a locality and its residents may be attacked, and the residents are never a target (Q-CIV-2). The order is refused with the state unchanged and a reason for an unknown unit, a halutz, a unit of another side, no hex, a hex not next to it (artillery: beyond its range), no enemy unit, no movement points left or a stock below the Arms this attack needs. Fortify and hold name no hex: one that names a hex is refused. The order costs `attention_cost.unit_order` (0, D-A6). A save writes it as `order <unit> attack <q> <r>`; a scenario takes `q` and `r`.

R-COMBAT-002: Resolver step 2 resolves the enemy's attacks in ascending attacker id: the lowest-id unit with an attack not yet in an engagement leads one, with every enemy unit attacking the same hex. A player's attack is fought when its order runs (R-COMBAT-001), as one engagement with that unit alone and one draw. Each unit's strength is first multiplied by its type's `combat_percent` (R-COMBAT-007, R-COMBAT-008). The attackers' strengths are summed; each defender's strength is multiplied by its order's defence percent (`Orders.DefencePercent`: fortify 125, R-ORDER-002) and by `mvp.combat_terrain_percent` for its hex's biome (real values: hills 125, mountains 150, every other land biome 100). The ratio is attack over defence in whole percent, rounded down, in integers only; the row of `mvp.combat_table` is the last whose `min_ratio_percent` it reaches (real rows: below 1:2, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, 3:1). Each engagement draws exactly one RNG value, a face of the row (six in the real table), whatever its outcome; a step 2 with no engagement draws nothing (docs/determinism.md, "Combat draws"). `Combat.Preview(c, s, unit, q, r)` gives the ratio and row the UI shows (Q-UI-1; spec §2.6), counting that unit alone for a player's attack.

R-COMBAT-003: The face gives the population units each side loses and whether the defenders retreat. Losses fall one unit at a time on the side's strongest unit in the engagement (a tie on the lower id; the attackers' losses spare their artillery, R-COMBAT-007); a unit at 0 is destroyed and leaves the state, and its id is never reused (`NextId` does not move). When the face says retreat, each defender left, in id order, moves one hex to the first neighbour in the fixed order (R-MOVE-002) that is land, holds no unit of the other side, has room for its side under `mvp.stack_limit`, and, for a player's unit, is not an enemy-held locality (R-MOVE-005). A defender with no such hex surrenders and leaves the state: the only way a unit is captured (impact plan §4.3 item 1; its people are counted as prisoners or as the player's soldiers held, R-POW-001, R-POW-005). The attackers never advance into the hex (Q-COMBAT-3). A player's unit's first loss stores the strength it had as its `Full`; only replacements (R-TRAIN-004) restore lost strength.

R-COMBAT-004: Each of the player's units pays `mvp.arms_per_attack` (real value 1; artillery pays its `arms_per_fire` instead, R-COMBAT-007) from the Arms stock when the attack is given; an attack the stock cannot pay for is refused. Enemy units pay nothing (Q-ENEMY-4: no supply).

R-COMBAT-005: Combat changes only combatant units, the Arms and, through a surrender, the counts of prisoners and of the player's soldiers held (R-POW-001, R-POW-005). No city, locality, resident, building, job or other resource, and no score, changes because of a combat outcome (Q-CIV-2; impact plan §4.3: kills and enemy losses never score). Halutzim are never attackers or targets. Lost strength comes back only through replacements (R-TRAIN-004), which are training, not a combat outcome.

R-COMBAT-006: Each engagement's Why line names the hex, the units on each side and the ratio, and states "the attackers lost N people and the defenders lost M people", the strength each side lost times `people_per_unit`, in the same words whichever side attacks (Q-CIV-4; impact plan §4.2). A destroyed unit gets "Unit N was destroyed", a retreat the hex it went to, a surrender the people it surrendered with. No running count of losses is kept; the war's Israeli total is PR 13's.

Phase 5 PR 9, part 1 (#748): artillery's combat role and the armoured car. Q-ARTY-1 and Q-ARTY-2 are the plan's recommendations (owner, 15 October 2026, #375: "Take all recommendations"). The numbers in `unit_types.yaml` are agent defaults proposed on #748 and accepted by the owner as design values on 21 October 2026, together with three of R-COMBAT-007's mechanics that the Q-ARTY-1 text does not settle: the attackers' losses sparing the guns, the hold refused as "never holds ground", and the 200% counting on defence too (owner, 21 October 2026, #748, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/issues/748#issuecomment-6317339304). R-COMBAT-008: A military type may carry a `combat_percent` (at least 1; none is 100): its units' strength counts at that percent in the ratio, attacking and defending, in integers (R-COMBAT-002). ## Objectives

R-OBJ-001: An objective (`objectives.yaml`) names its condition, `completes_when: { site_built: <site> }` (R-OBJ-004's `absorbed` has no site, so the site rules here apply only to `site_built`), a designated site of kind yishuv whose `founded` date must equal the objective's `reference_date`. R-OBJ-002: Recording a standalone objective adds `objective_points + min(objective_bonus_cap, objective_bonus_per_month * monthsEarly)` to the score, once. `monthsEarly` is `max(0, monthsFromHistory)`, and `monthsFromHistory` counts calendar months from the completion's month to the reference date's month, `(y2 - y1) * 12 + (m2 - m1)`: 0 in the same month ("on time, as in history"), negative when late (owner's card wording, #379, 14 October 2026). Late costs nothing. R-OBJ-003: An event with `objective:` is that objective's card. It is eligible only once its objective is recorded, so it fires once, at the end of the turn the objective is completed in, and never in the legacy loop. A card has exactly one option, and an objective and its card name each other (the loader checks both). An unanswered card resolves to its option without drawing from the RNG.

R-OBJ-004: An objective may instead name `completes_when: { absorbed: { population_at_least, overflow_at_most, employed_at_least } }` (D-B4; owner, 18 October 2026, #738, option (a)). Recorded and scored as R-OBJ-001 and R-OBJ-002 say; its card follows R-OBJ-003.

R-EFX-003: An option's `grant_units` entry `{ type, n, at }` adds `n` (at least 1) units of a `unit_types.yaml` type of kind `unit`, free, taking the next ids in turn. Military types are refused: this schema does not specify military strength or a sourced formation, and must not create a default-side, zero-strength soldier. Recruits enter through training (R-UNIT-003); a military event grant needs a separate schema decision (owner, 19 October 2026, https://github.com/eyalgolan/statecraft-israel/pull/1130#issuecomment-6269103829). Granted units stand at the settlement hex of the city `at` names or, with no `at`, at the settlement hex of the one city whose `facilities` include `military_camp` (Q-HFX-2). With no such single city, `at` is required. A site is not yet a target (R-EFX-006).

R-EFX-004: An option's `city_effects` entry `{ city, field, delta }` changes only that city's `housing_capacity` or `job_slots`, by a whole, non-zero `delta`, or its housing `condition` by a non-zero `delta` from -1 to 1. Housing capacity never drops below 0, and job slots never drop below the city's employed, so a larger cut applies only to what is free. Capacity may fall below the city's civilians, as overcrowding already can. The condition stays from 0 to 1 (R-HOUSE-001). The other fields of the city, its condition included, do not change. The cause carries the change that applied.

R-EFX-005: An option of an event carrying any sensitive tag (`holocaust`, `terror`, `massacre`, `war-casualties` or `displacement`) carries no unit grant and no `city_effects` gain, whether capacity, slots or condition, and no project unlock (Q-HFX-3; extends R-EFX-002 and R-EFX-007). A cut stays allowed.

R-GATE-001: An event tagged `displacement` must have `status: sensitivity-reviewed` before a shareable build. The release gate lists every such event whose status is not reviewed and refuses the build (Q-GATE-1(c), owner ruling of 18 October 2026).

R-EFX-006: The structured effects apply after the numeric effects, grants first, then city effects, then project unlocks, each in the option's list order. A target that does not exist on the date (an unbuilt site, a city not yet in the game) is refused at load with file and field, never skipped at run time; so is an unknown type, city, field or project kind. Every `cities.yaml` city is in the game from the first turn. A site has no window and starts unbuilt, so a site id as `at` is refused until sites carry a date. The legacy loop, which has no cities or units, refuses them. The preview (R-EVT-005) lists them in the same order.

R-EFX-008: A `projects.yaml` kind may say `locked: true` (default false). `StartProject` refuses a locked kind, like the other R-PROJ-001 refusals, until an option whose `unlock_projects` lists it has applied; from then on it is an ordinary kind for the rest of the game. The unlocked kinds are game state, hashed as `unlocked[...]` only when one is unlocked (R-LOOP-002). The loader refuses an `unlock_projects` entry that names an unknown kind or one that is not locked, since it would do nothing.

## Events

D-H1 in the GDD loop (GDD Phase 7 PR 1, #805). The owner took the plan's recommendations for Q-EVT-1 to Q-EVT-4 on 17 October 2026 (#784). As the plan's Decided rows record them (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-15-gdd-phase-7.md`), that keeps windows, hard anchors and the cap of 3 soft events a turn, keeps delegation, adds no delayed effects in the MVP, and shows every effect of every option from the sim's preview. These rules state what the code already does; no behaviour changed. On 18 October 2026 the owner added loyalty to delegation (#107: "Yes: minister loyalty changes a delegated event's outcome, with labelled values in the PR"); R-EVT-003 states it, with two design values (`delegation_loyalty_floor`, `disloyal_effect_share`).

R-EVT-001: An event fires on the first turn whose span overlaps its window `[from, to)` (or at the new game, when the start date is in it), once its `requires` is met and its conditions hold; an objective's card also waits for its objective (R-OBJ-003). Every eligible hard anchor fires. At most 3 soft events fire a turn (`EventSystem.MaxSoftEventsPerTurn`); they take the slots the hard anchors leave, `max(0, 3 - hard anchors)`, and the rest wait for a later turn (Q-CUT-6).

R-EVT-002: The soft events take the free slots in order: consequence cards first (R-ALT-008), then the rest by `from`, then id (ordinal). The decided place of an early armistice ahead of the other soft events (Q-EVT-1, through `front_quiet`, Q-OBJ-1) lands with that key in Phase 5 PR 13 (#752), and this rule is reworded then.

R-EVT-003: An event the player leaves unanswered at End Turn is delegated to its office's holder (Q-EVT-2): with probability competence/10 the holder picks the recommended option (else the first), otherwise a random option. In Historical mode a hard anchor takes its historical option, and an objective's card its one option, with no RNG draw. A holder whose loyalty is below `delegation_loyalty_floor` (5) applies only `disloyal_effect_share` (0.5) of each numeric effect of the option drawn; the grants, city effects, policies and security level apply whole, and so do a hard anchor's historical option and an objective's card. Why: competence decides which option a minister picks, loyalty how hard he works to carry out a decision the player left to him (#107, owner, 18 October 2026: "minister loyalty changes a delegated event's outcome"). Both are design values (R-LOOP-005), required by the loader: the floor 0 to 10, the share 0 to 1. Every minister in `people.yaml` is rated 5 or more today (5 is the deputies' neutral rating), so the rule changes no shipped game until a rating goes below the floor.

R-EVT-004: An option's effects apply when it is chosen or delegated, on that turn, and never later; an option has no delayed form, and the loader refuses an unknown key such as `delay` (Q-EVT-3).

R-EVT-005: One sim helper, `EventSystem.OptionEffects`, lists every effect of an option in the order choosing it applies them: the numeric effects, the unit grants, city effects and project unlocks (R-EFX-006), the policies it sets (by id), then the security level, then the way back to the record (`rejoin`, R-ALT-009), each with the option's own amount (Q-EVT-4). The event card will show this list (GDD Phase 7 PR 8), so no number will be computed in Unity.

## Alternate timelines

The alternate-timeline mechanism ([the plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-17-alternate-timelines.md); the owner's answers, 17 October 2026, #945).

R-ALT-001: An event's `requires` is a list, and the event is eligible only when every entry holds. An entry `{ event, option }` holds when that event was resolved (chosen or delegated) with that option. A list names each event at most once.

R-ALT-002: An entry `{ event, diverged: true }` holds when that event was resolved with any option except its historical one. An event with no historical option cannot diverge, and the loader refuses `diverged` on it.

R-ALT-003: A non-historical option may carry `alternative: { basis, by, date, sources }`. `basis` is `proposed` or `debated` (a body or office, in words, is recorded putting it forward on that date; at least one https source with a checked date) or `design` (nobody is recorded proposing it: a design assumption, which names nobody and no date; sources optional). The historical option carries none.

R-ALT-004: Every non-historical option of an event that has a historical option carries an `alternative` record (R-ALT-003).

R-ALT-005: An event of `kind: consequence` is soft, has no historical option, has at least one requirement naming a road not taken (`diverged: true`, a non-historical option, or an option of another consequence card), carries an `alternative` record on every option, and lies at most 2 cards deep from the record (`EventRules.MaxConsequenceDepth`, a labelled design value, Q-ALT-5). It is never an objective's card, and only a consequence card may require one.

R-ALT-006: A consequence card may follow only an option whose `alternative.basis` is `proposed` or `debated`; with `diverged: true`, every non-historical option of that event must be (the Ferguson test, Q-ALT-3).

R-ALT-007: A consequence card carries every sensitive tag (case aside) of each event it requires, so the no-gain rule (R-EFX-002, R-EFX-005) covers it.

R-ALT-008: Consequence cards take a turn's soft slots before the other soft events, then by `from` and id (Q-ALT-14). A `front_quiet` armistice (Q-EVT-1, Phase 5 PR 13) will come before them. With no consequence card eligible the order is unchanged.

R-ALT-009: An option of a consequence card may `rejoin` an event whose road the card follows (and which has a historical option). Once it is chosen, a requirement on that event's historical option holds, so the record's later cards fire again if their windows are still open. The event's own chosen option is not rewritten, and `diverged: true` on it still holds.

R-ALT-010: An event of `kind: reconvergence` has a historical option and may carry `variants`. The first variant whose every `when` entry holds supplies the card's title (if it gives one) and text; options and effects never vary. Each variant follows a road not taken, carries sources, has its placeholders checked over the event's window, and names only roads whose windows close on or before the event's `from` (for a road's historical option, also every consequence card that can `rejoin` it, R-ALT-009). Only a reconvergence event carries variants.

R-ALT-011: A shareable build refuses while any consequence card, or any event with variants, has no `reviewed_by` (a person's sign-off), alongside the sensitivity gate (Q-ALT-9). `Timeline.Entries` lists each resolved event, in window order, with the player's option beside the record's, and `Timeline.Origin` names the road a consequence card follows.

R-ALT-012: The historical run (the reference player's orders from 15 May 1948 to 15 January 1952) raises no consequence card and diverges nowhere.
