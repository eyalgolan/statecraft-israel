**Goal:** Build the GDD's "Military actions and settlement control" for the MVP. A player unit captures an enemy-held settlement on the curated target list (D-F5). The player then chooses Raid and withdraw, Occupy or Deport. After X turns of occupation the player chooses a puppet regime or annexation. Residents and combatants are distinct states. Deport follows the owner's ruling "honest cost, no reward" (GDD owner ruling 1; CONTENT_POLICY §4; impact plan §4.1). A new sensitive tag, `displacement`, joins the release gate. Sourced follow-up cards state what happened, under D-F1.

**Architecture:** Phase 1 built `TurnResolver` with step 3 ("capture") and step 10 ("occupation timers: Phase 6") left as comments (`sim/Statecraft.Sim/TurnResolver.cs`). Phase 5 fills steps 1 and 2 (enemy orders and combat) and leaves step 3 to this phase. Phase 6 adds a new `SettlementControl.cs` for steps 3 and 10, and a new settlement-state collection in `GameState`, ordered by id and hashed only when non-empty (spec §2.2, §2.4). There are two new commands, `ResolveCapture` and `EndOccupation` (spec §2.3), each with the five command touch-points. `EventRules.cs` and `ReleaseGate.cs` learn the `displacement` tag. The capture dialog reads the options and a cost preview from a public sim helper (spec §2.6). It holds no rule of its own.

**Tech stack:** as Phase 1: C# netstandard2.1 sim (`sim/Statecraft.Sim`), xUnit on net10.0 (`sim/Statecraft.Sim.Tests`), YamlDotNet content, Unity 6000.3.25f1 UI Toolkit (`unity/`), Python tools and policy checks under `uv`.

**Spec:** [the impact plan](../specs/2026-10-12-gdd-v0.1-impact-and-plan.md): §1.7, §2.2, §2.3, §2.4, §2.6, §3 (the `mvp-deport-cost` and `mvp-occupy-cost` scenarios), §4.1, §4.2, §4.3, §5 group F (D-F1 to D-F5) and §6 "Phase 6". The format follows [the Phase 2 plan](2026-10-14-gdd-phase-2.md), [the Phase 3 plan](2026-10-15-gdd-phase-3.md) and [the Phase 5 plan](2026-10-15-gdd-phase-5.md) with its PR 0 table (#578). ## Binding constraints (decided; not questions)

These rules are already decided. Every PR is built inside them, and no owner answer below is read as loosening them unless the owner says so in words.

1. **Honest cost, no reward** (GDD owner ruling 1, 12 October 2026; CONTENT_POLICY §4). Deport "stays as a historical possibility but never pays. It carries large Social cohesion and International Support penalties, creates refugees, has permanent consequences, and is shown soberly through sourced event cards." CONTENT_POLICY §4: a player choice that harms civilians "may exist only as a historical possibility that never pays: it carries its real costs (refugees, Social cohesion, International Support), gives no reward, and is shown soberly through sourced events." The same section bars "Mechanics or text that reward or celebrate violence against civilians, from any side."
2. **No mechanic may turn a depopulated place into housing, jobs or score** (impact plan §4.1 item 4, the approved plan). A deported settlement becomes `depopulated`. It adds no housing capacity, jobs, Food, Money, Arms or score. It cannot be puppeted or annexed for gain. It counts towards no objective. It ends the Occupy timer. Whether its buildings stand is a separate rule (D-F3). Either way, they are not usable housing in the MVP. The Phase 3 plan applies the same rule to start values: no rule reads `localities_1948.yaml` or the `arab_town` rows as a source of capacity.
3. **The no-gain rule covers all five resources** (Q-EFX-2, "Extend it"). A sensitive option cannot raise Money, Food, Arms, Social cohesion or International Support (or foreign currency). Impact plan §4.1 item 5 adds score for the `displacement` tag, and states it with no exception: "Event options tagged `displacement` ... cannot have a positive effect on any of Money, Arms, Food, Social cohesion, International Support or score." (Whether Phase 2's matching-cost exception applies to this tag is Q-DEP-6.)
4. **Residents are never the player's Refugee units** (D-A5). They are never labour, housing occupants or score after a Deport (§4.1 item 3).
5. **Residents are never prisoners** (impact plan §4.3 item 1): "Residents can never be captured, and capture is never an action aimed at a settlement's people." Prisoners come only from a surrender in combat (Phase 5, R-POW-001).
6. **Combat changes only combatants** (Phase 5, R-COMBAT-005). No locality's residents, housing, jobs or buildings change because of a combat outcome. Any change to residents in this phase comes only from a settlement-control rule that the owner has answered.
7. **Enemy combatants and settlement residents are distinct states** (the GDD). Deportation and razing are separate rules (the GDD; D-F3 decides how).
8. **D-F1 and D-F5** as quoted above. Cards state displacement factually, from sourced mainstream Israeli historiography (CONTENT_POLICY §1). Targets come from a short, curated, sourced list tied to sourced operations.
9. **The release gate** (CONTENT_POLICY §2.1; DECISIONS D11; `ReleaseGate.cs`). Every event with a sensitive tag must be `sensitivity-reviewed` before any build leaves the maintainer's machine. `Statecraft.Cli --release-gate`, the `-shareable` build and `tools/package_release.py` refuse otherwise. The `displacement` tag joins that set (§4.1 item 5). Sensitivity tags and the release gates are left to the maintainer (AGENTS.md).
10. **Deport never gets a video** (docs/event-videos.md), and it has no sound sting, heroic text or celebratory text (§4.1 item 6).
11. **Sourcing and wording** (CONTENT_POLICY §2; AGENTS.md). Every place, date and number is sourced, with a `checked:` date. There are no invented quotes. Event text uses office placeholders. Wording is plain and historically precise, with no euphemism, no melodrama, no softening and no loaded adjectives. Dates show as "15 May 1948".
12. **Abandoned Arab housing** is not named in Phase 3 (Q-SENS-1). Whether and how any Phase 6 text names it is an owner pick (Q-SENS-1b), with no recommendation.

## Global constraints

- The GDD wins over every older spec (AGENTS.md). Anything it marks Open, Proposal or TBD is built only as the owner's answer. **No PR in this plan decides an open D-row or an open sensitive question.** Every question that touches civilians, residents, depopulation, deportation, occupation or annexation is an **owner pick**: it lists the options with one neutral line of context, and carries no recommendation. Only technical questions (data structures, plumbing, test strategy) carry a recommendation.
- **Sourcing.** No target, date, population figure or card text is invented. A figure from `localities_1948.yaml` (VS 1945, draft) keeps its draft status until it is checked. One value per fact, chosen by CONTENT_POLICY §1. AI-assisted prose is a draft, and a human checks every claim before `fact-checked` (CONTENT_POLICY §2.6).

- **Determinism** (docs/determinism.md): one RNG, no trigonometry, no unordered iteration into state, invariant culture. **No PR in this phase adds an RNG draw.** Counts and timers are integers (spec §2.2). Settlements iterate in ordinal id order.
- **Goldens.** Phase 6 runs after the cutover. Each PR that changes an MVP rule re-pins every golden it moves, in the same PR, with a rebaseline-history row and the reason in the commit and the PR (spec §3, "After cutover"). ## Review focus

1. **Deport that pays, directly or indirectly.** The direct paths are housing, jobs, Food, Money, Arms, Social cohesion, International Support, score, objectives, Attention and freed units. The indirect paths are fewer future enemy units at the place, a quieter front that opens an armistice early (Phase 5 Q-OBJ-1), a hex that no longer counts towards a war loss (Q-WAR-5), and a site a pioneer can build on. Each one is either closed by a rule or listed as an owner question (Q-DEP-7, Q-SENS-2). Tests: PR 8 (`DeportNeverDominates`), PR 12 (the sweep).
2. **Residents who become something the player can use.** Residents are never refugee units, prisoners, labour, recruits, housing occupants or score, except as the owner's answer on annexation sets (Q-ANNEX-1), and that answer is reviewed under constraint 1. Tests: PRs 3, 7 and 8.
3. **A depopulated place that becomes capacity.** No command, project, site or event raises housing, jobs or a resource from a `depopulated` settlement or its hex (constraint 2). Test: PR 8, `ADepopulatedPlaceAddsNothing`, as a reflection test over every command kind and effect target.
4. **Deport made easy.** It is never the default, first in the list, pre-selected, cheaper in Attention, or chosen by `HistoricalAI`, the reference player or delegation (§4.1 item 1). Tests: PRs 5 and 10.
5. **Unsourced or unreviewed text in a shareable build.** Every `displacement` card is refused by the release gate until `sensitivity-reviewed`, and so is any other surface the owner puts under the gate (Q-GATE-1). Tests: PRs 2 and 9.
6. **Wording.** Plain, sourced, with no euphemism, melodrama, softening or loaded adjectives, in English and Hebrew (constraint 11). Review: PRs 9, 10 and 11, by the owner.
7. **Determinism.** Settlements resolve in ordinal id order. No RNG draw is added. The capture check and the timers are integer rules. Tests: PRs 4 and 6.
