This document says what the player does from the formation of Begin's government in June 1977 to the eve of the Oslo
signing in September 1993, and how each system of today's sim serves that period, by reuse or by a stated new piece.
It begins where the 1957–1977 chapter ends (20 June 1977) and ends where #52 begins. It writes no code and no content.
The work items are listed in §12 and drafted as issues in "Detailed issues".

Facts quoted here size the design. Each one names its source and the day it was checked. A fact marked *to verify*
has no checked source yet, or its sources disagree. The research issues (§12) settle it before any content uses it.

**Decisions.** Fourteen owner decisions, D1–D14, are listed with their recommended defaults in §13. The rest of the
document assumes the defaults. Decision numbers without a prefix are this document's; the 1957–1977 design's are
always written "1957–77 D*n*". Three bear on work that is already filed and should be decided before it is built:
D9 (the map box of #129 stops at 33.5 N, short of Beirut; #129 should wait for this era's item 20 so that both edges
are fixed in one renumbering), D10 (money: the chapters design's D5 rule for money on screen cannot show this era's
prices, which rose about 6,000-fold; D10 names the part of chapters D5 it would overturn and asks the owner) and D3
(this era needs 1957–77 D3 and 1957–77 D12 adopted as written; it adds no amendment of its own to a pinned golden).
This revision (2026-10-11) follows a staff and historian review; facts added or changed in it were checked on
2026-10-11.

## 1. The rules this design keeps

1. **The spec's perspective rule** (spec §1; `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` §1). Narration and terms are Israeli-perspective,
   from mainstream Israeli historiography, with one sourced value per fact and no opposing-narrative panels. Egypt,
   Syria, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon and its militias, the PLO, Hezbollah and Hamas, the superpowers and the UN are AI
   actors with their own goals.
2. **What holds regardless** (CONTENT_POLICY §2): every fact is sourced; no quotes are invented; nothing rewards an
   atrocity; the Holocaust is reacted to and never played; real people appear in their public roles; living people
   stay out of a shareable build until a legal review is recorded (spec §5; chapters design §13).
3. **No reset.** The 1957–1977 chapter carries into this one by the chapters design's deviation carry (its D1).
4. **Earlier goldens do not move.** `GoldenRun.Hash1950`, `Hash1952`, `Hash1957` and `SMOKE OK 5 turns` (the Founding
   goldens), and `Hash_sinai_to_upheaval_1967`, `Hash_sinai_to_upheaval_1977`, the 1957–1977 chain golden and
   `carry/sinai_to_upheaval.reference.yaml`, stay the same through every item in §12 (§9.2).
5. **Data decides, code implements** (chapters design §1.4, §5). This era adds **no new `systems` switch and no new
   engine abstraction.** It reuses the chapters items (#56–#67), the M4 systems (#21 war mode, #22 defence depth,
   #105 actor standing, #111 dated rules, #19 legacy report, #23 map actions) and the 1957–1977 design's
   `turn_anchors` (1957–77 D12, which this era needs but does not change). Where a new piece could be argued for, it
   is an owner decision with the smallest option recommended: a larger map (D9, data and tools only), a map action
   for the Sinai withdrawal (D6, not built), and money in an era of hyperinflation (D10). **D10's recommended option
   holds the one new engine rule this design proposes:** ratio carry for treasury and debt at a boundary (the mode
   #59 already uses for population), with a sourced price factor when the reference is zero (§5.3). The chapters
   design §4.3 names a different remedy for the same problem (money as a share of a scale quantity), and its D1
   carries money additively. It is built only on the owner's yes. D10 also names, for the owner, every part of the
   chapters design it would overturn (§5.3). Inflation itself adds no rule: it is an indicator with ordinary
   effects (§5.3).
6. **The reference run is history.** A policy's `default` is its level at the start of its window. Each later
   historical change is a hard event's `set_policy` on a sourced date, so the `HistoricalAI` reproduces history
   without sending policy orders (the Yishuv design §1.7; the 1957–1977 design §1.6).
7. **The sensitive-content rules extend here** (M4 D8; the 1957–1977 design D7; §10). Massacres and terror against
   Israelis are react-only. Israeli operations are decided at the level of policy, doctrine or operation, never at the
   level of civilian harm. Israeli acts that killed civilians, or that Israeli inquiries found wrong, are on an owner
   include/omit list, and nothing is omitted without the owner.

### 5.3 Economy, inflation and money (D10)

The sim keeps no price level today: money is three accounts in one internal unit (`model.yaml money`: treasury and
debt with the prefix `IL `, foreign-currency reserves with `$`). The chapters design's D5 makes money on screen
**display only**: "the UI shows each period's currency, converted by sourced redenomination dates and factors", and
its §11 adds "Inflation is a calibration question for #51, not a currency mechanism". This era is where that rule
meets its limit. Consumer prices rose about **6,000-fold** from 1978 to 1993 (compounded from the IMF yearly rates,
§2.5; about 1,400-fold by the end of 1985), while the redenominations divide by 10 (24 Feb 1980) and by 1,000
(4 Sep 1985), 10,000 in all. A sim with no price level, shown through redenomination factors only, would show
amounts about 1,400 times smaller than the period's nominal figures by the end of 1985 and about 6,000 times smaller
by 1993; event texts that quote sourced nominal budgets would contradict the screen.

**Recommended (D10 A): keep chapters D5's principle, and amend its factor rule.** Each period's currency stays on
screen and the sim never converts. What changes is named here, item by item, as an overturn of the chapters design
for the owner:

1. chapters D5 and §11 (the Money row): "converted by sourced redenomination dates and factors" becomes
   "redenomination factors and a sourced price index";
2. chapters §11: the sentence "Inflation is a calibration question for #51, not a currency mechanism" is withdrawn
   for display;
3. chapters D5 ("the sim keeps one internal unit for each money account in every era") and §11 ("its stored amounts,
   rules and hashes are in one unit forever"): the unit stays one *kind* of number, but its meaning becomes **the
   prices of each era's sourced start**, so it changes at every boundary;
4. chapters D1 and the §4.3 carry table ("additive: `start + (player − reference)`" for money): treasury and debt
   carry instead by ratio, with a price-factor fallback (below), a new engine rule in #59. Chapters §4.3's own
   foreseen remedy ("carry money as a share of a scale quantity") is a different mechanism and is not proposed.

In detail:

- **In the sim, money is real in every era: amounts at the prices of the era's sourced start, with no price level
  inside the era.** This is what the code already does (no price rule exists), stated as a rule:
  - *Founding (1948–1956):* unchanged; its calibration anchors are 1950–51 pounds.
  - *1957–1977 (#50):* unchanged by this decision: its amounts are pounds calibrated to its own anchors, with no
    price level. Its prices also rose (most after 1973: 34.62% in 1977 alone, §2.5); whether its display needs the
    same index is a question for its economy research (#116), raised in §9.4, not decided here.
  - *This era:* amounts at the prices of 20 June 1977 (the start file's sourced values).
  - *#52 (from 13 September 1993):* amounts at its own start's prices; it inherits the rule and decides its own
    display (inflation was about 11% in 1993, §2.5).
- **On screen, the period's currency at the period's prices.** `content/currency.yaml` (#66) holds, for this era, the
  pound until 23 Feb 1980, the shekel from 24 Feb 1980, and the new shekel from 4 Sep 1985 (the old shekel stayed
  legal tender to 31 Dec 1985; the display switch date is D11), each with a dated factor = redenomination factor ÷
  the CBS consumer price index relative to 20 June 1977, monthly in 1979–1985 and quarterly otherwise (series chosen
  by item 4). The factor is display data, read by `PlayerText.Money`, and is never hashed. If #66's schema allows one
  factor per currency only, it gains a dated factor list: a change to #66's data shape, not to the sim.
- **What the player must know:** the screen applies history's price level, not the player's. A player who ends
  inflation early still sees nominal amounts grow as history's did; the player's own inflation is the
  `price_stability` indicator. The legacy report (#19) states this.
- **Foreign-currency reserves stay in US dollars, nominal,** as `fx_reserves` is today: no price index on screen and
  no scale at the boundary. Reserve flows and aid in this era's rules and events are sourced nominal dollar amounts,
  and the calibration compares them with the Bank of Israel's nominal dollar figures. US prices also rose over the
  period (*to verify* the multiple); that drift is accepted, not modelled.
- **Across a boundary, treasury and debt carry by ratio, with a price factor when the reference is zero.** Chapters
  §4.3 carries money additively, `start + (player − reference)`, which adds amounts in two eras' different price
  bases. This design asks #59 for, per money quantity in the carry file:
  - if `reference > 0`: `start * (player / reference)` (the ratio mode #59 uses for population);
  - if `reference == 0`: `start + (player − reference) × k`, where `k` is a sourced price factor in the carry file
    (the CBS price index at the boundary ÷ the index at the previous era's price base, from item 4), and `k = 1` if
    the file gives none.

  The second case is the usual one for treasury, not an edge case: `EconomySystem` (lines 45–49) sets the treasury
  to 0 and borrows whenever it would go negative, so in a deficit economy such as 1977's the reference run's treasury
  is likely to be exactly 0. Debt is above 0 on any such run and carries by ratio. Both cases return `start` exactly
  when `player == reference`, so the chain invariant holds, and both use only `+ − × /` (chapters §4.3's exact
  arithmetic). It applies at 20 June 1977 (item 8) and is the default for #52's boundary. Reserves carry additively,
  as now (nominal dollars, no factor). This is a new engine rule and is built only on the owner's yes; without it,
  item 8 uses additive carry and the chapter card's money deviation is off by the price change between the eras'
  bases.
- **Inflation is the `price_stability` indicator** (§5.1), moved by `deficit_spending`, `exchange_rate_regime` and
  `wage_price_agreement` through ordinary effects. It acts **only through ordinary indicator effects that exist
  today**: an effect's `source` is a policy or an indicator and its `target` an indicator
  (`sim/Statecraft.Sim/ContentLoader.cs` lines 413–414). So `price_stability` moves `morale` and `employment` (and
  through `morale` the voter groups' support, by their weights), and events may condition on its bounds. It does
  **not** move emigration (a dated series, #102) or the economy's growth or tax rules (`EconomySystem`): either link
  would be a new engine rule, and none is proposed.
- **The `hyperinflation` situation is a flag** with two thresholds (`on_below` < `off_above`), as `food_crisis` is
  today: `EffectsSystem` turns it on and off and writes the Why line; no content situation has an effect of its own
  in today's sim (only the hard-coded `fx_crisis` in `EconomySystem` does). It labels the period for the player and
  the legacy report.
- **The 1985 plan is a policy package.** Its hard event on 1 July 1985 sets the three policies together (`set_policy`
  is a map of policy to level, `docs/WRITING_EVENTS.md`). Its decision scene (scene 7) offers the cabinet's real
  choices of the time. #51's "hysteresis test" is item 10's: on a fixture and on the reference run, **once the
  situation is on, it stays on while `price_stability` lies between `on_below` and `off_above`, and turns off only
  when `price_stability` rises above `off_above`**; and on the reference run it is on before the package and off
  after it.
- **Calibration** `UpheavalToOsloCalibrationTests` on a fresh start: foreign-currency reserves (nominal dollars),
  and treasury flows and debt at June-1977 prices, against the Bank of Israel annual reports for 1979, 1984, 1986 and
  1992 (deflated by the same index; anchors chosen by research); the years the situation is on, not the inflation
  rate.

### 10.4 Sabra and Shatila

This is the era's most sensitive event and is fixed by design, subject only to the owner's review of its text:

- **React-only, Israeli-perspective.** The killings were carried out by the Lebanese Forces militia (§2.4). The
  player never orders, permits or prevents them by an option, and no war-mode order touches the camps (M4 D4(e)
  extended). The event fires on its sourced date as a hard event.
- **Fully history-locked in Historical mode, independent of the player's choices**.
  - *Firing.* The September 1982 sequence (Bashir Gemayel's assassination, the IDF's entry into West Beirut, the
    militia's entry into the camps, the killings, the inquiry's establishment, the report, and its implementation;
    the dates not in §2 are *to verify* in item 5) is a chain of hard events with **no `requires` and no
    `conditions`**, so each fires on its date whatever the player chose in scene 5, set in `lebanon_posture` or did
    in any earlier chain event. Scene 5's Beirut option is the June decision only, and its text says that later
    events follow history.
  - *Options.* In Historical mode **every event of the chain has only its historical option**, the inquiry's
    establishment (28 Sep 1982) included: there is no option to delay or refuse an inquiry, so no player can refuse
    the inquiry and then see its report. The chain's events are records the government reacts to, not choices.
  - *People.* The chain's texts **name the historical people literally** (Begin, Sharon, Eitan and the others the
    report names), **never through office placeholders** such as `{defense_minister}`, which `Cabinet.Fill` would fill
    from the player's own appointments (`Cabinet.cs`); each name appears only after its person passes the
    living-people check (#62), and until then the text names the post as a literal string. Sharon's departure from
    the Defence Ministry (14 Feb 1983) is narrated as the historical record, whoever holds the office in the player's
    game.
  - *Tense.* The texts narrate, in the past tense, what the government and the IDF of September 1982 did, and present
    the Kahan findings as findings about those historical decisions, never about the player's.
  - *Test.* Item 18's Done-when includes a test: on runs with each of scene 5's options, with `lebanon_posture` at its
    extremes, with a different defence minister appointed before September 1982, and with any attempt to send a
    non-historical option to a chain event (refused: `EventSystem.Choose` returns "That event has no such option" for an option the event does not have), the chain fires on the same dates,
    with the same single option and the same text.
  - In Open mode (#10) the chain is out of scope until the owner rules on it. The owner's sensitivity review covers
    this framing.
- **The Kahan Commission's findings are sourced fact.** The event and the follow-on events state the commission's
  dates, members and findings (indirect responsibility; Sharon's personal responsibility and the recommendation that
  he leave the Defence Ministry; Eitan; others named in the report, *to verify* from the report's text) in the
  commission's sourced wording, never as an invented quote. Officers named in the report who are alive pass the
  living-people gate (#62) before their names appear in any text; until then the text names their posts.
- **Linked events.** The protest of 25 Sep 1982 and the grenade at the Peace Now march (10 Feb 1983; its victim named
  by role, §10.6) are linked events outside the locked chain, with the government's real responses as options.
- **Nothing rewards it.** No option anywhere in the chain or its linked events raises treasury, support or a legacy
  score by minimising or concealing; the chain offers no such option at all.
- **The death toll** is one value from a mainstream Israeli source (the commission's report is the candidate, *to
  verify*), not the article's range; D11.

### 10.6 People (D8)

Many people of this era are alive: ministers, chiefs of staff, intelligence chiefs, officers, the perpetrators and
victims of sensitive events. The chapters design's gate (#62) keeps any person with `living: true` out of a shareable
build until a legal review is recorded on the record. In this era that gate covers a large share of the office
holders, so:

- **Research item 1 sets `living` for every person** from a sourced death date or its absence (no guessing), and
  adds a content test that every office holder of 1977–1993 in `people.yaml` has `living` set.
- **People who held no public office are named by role only** (perpetrators, victims, hostages, the accused in the
  Bus 300 and Jewish Underground cases, the Peace Now activist killed in 1983, the nuclear technician of 1986, the
  agent arrested in 1985): in event ids, titles, text and anchors alike. The owner may rule otherwise for one person,
  and only with a recorded legal review. This design's §2 names some of them only to identify the source.
- **Portraits** of living people are not made before their legal review (spec §5, as amended: public-domain, CC0, CC BY 4.0
  or CC BY-SA 4.0; a human checks the likeness).
- **A shareable build of this chapter** needs the owner to commission the legal reviews early; until then, dev
  builds show the chapter with the DRAFT stamp (spec §5).
